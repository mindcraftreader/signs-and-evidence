CENTRAL REPLIES AND THEIR PRESENT ASSESSMENT
1. Bradley's demonstration concerns the wrong measurement if treated as a categorical disproof. Dalleur uses projected rays through corresponding object/shadow features to estimate a vanishing point, followed by camera geometry. He does not infer illumination just from a shadow's pixel length. This is corroborated by Dalleur's paper, especially figures9–12 and equations1–12. Generic changes in apparent shadow length do not refute that construction. Sloping receiving surfaces are not themselves a disproof. Correct correspondence and feature geometry still matter.

2. Real-photo checks exist. The earlier audit independently confirmed Dalleur's real examples as well as his synthetic scene. Therefore a claim that he validated only with CGI is factually wrong. Existing examples demonstrate successful operation in those scenes. They do not themselves quantify a matched D115 error distribution. At approximately1:05–1:06 Muse announces additional analyses of period photographs and a new archival scan as forthcoming; do not count that announcement as completed validation results shown in this video.

3. Camera uncertainty receives a substantive answer. Dalleur's paper expressly varies focal length and analyzes pitch/roll. The range discussed uses modern35mm-equivalent focal lengths, not an assertion that the original large-format camera physically had a31–46mm lens. The original numerical table supports the approximate11-degree minimum discrepancy under its selected geometry at30mm equivalent. Unknown exact make/model is not automatically fatal if all relevant plausible models can be bounded tightly enough. Conversely, whether those bounds are established is an empirical question, not settled solely by asserting a preferred lens.

4. Muse offers numerical distortion bounds based on plausible historical lenses and image straightness. This is a more specific reply than dismissing the possibility of lens error. The lens inventory, exact distortion limits, endpoint-displacement calculations and joint uncertainty bound were not independently verified in this turn. Do not record the claimed numbers as our independently reproduced measurements. Note also that a rectilinear perspective projection preserves straightness/collinearity, not arbitrary Euclidean angles; its image directions require the calibrated/geometric conversion that Dalleur models.

5. Several representation corrections are supported by the paper. Dalleur describes the second source as probably a bright cloud, not a second extraordinary orb. His map includes witnesses west of Fatima. The original paper does not propose western invisibility; Muse attributes the quoted remark to a separate discussion of Ghiaie, Italy. Checking the paper confirms the Fatima-side correction. A full independent audit of the Italian event and the exact external quotation is separate.

6. The remaining technical issue is narrower than Bradley's broad impossibility claim: how securely the actual faint image features identify corresponding shadows and constrain a joint error region excluding the real solar direction. Muse argues that spatially distributed cues converge and discusses interpretive uncertainty. This reply must be evaluated, not ignored. This transcript review did not produce a new endpoint coordinate table, uncertainty fit or independent solar reconstruction. Thus neither an unexplained generic distortion nor a bare appeal to missing camera metadata is a successful refutation.

The response materially undercuts Bradley's categorical objections about CGI-only validation, necessary physical shadow lengths/common level ground, and the absence of camera-parameter analysis. These should not be recycled as unanswered objections. It does not by itself establish that every positive uncertainty bound or historical lens assertion has been independently verified.
