FATIMA PHOTOGRAPH D115: LIGHT-SOURCE GEOMETRY AND WET/DRY PATTERNS
Research assessment, 27 October 2026

Conclusion

Dalleur's analysis is an actual geometric argument with successful worked
examples. It should not be dismissed as an impression of the crowd's gaze.
I reproduced its principal numerical result from the published inputs. I have
not demonstrated that ordinary sunlight fits all the relevant D115 shadows.
The remaining issue is the reliability and joint uncertainty of the image
measurements, especially object-to-shadow correspondences and the off-frame
vanishing point. The worked examples support the technique, but do not by
themselves supply a measured error distribution for this historical picture.

The wet/dry pattern can corroborate the witness evidence. It does not provide
an independent measurement of the drying time or the infrared spectrum. The
well-supported preceding rain, wet ground, and numerous drying reports must
remain part of the assessment; they are not unsupported premises to discard.

2. Correct numbers and calibration examples

D115 inferred elevation: approximately 25-32 degrees above the horizon.
Sun at solar noon: approximately 42.72 degrees. Central inferred value:
28.24 degrees. The discrepancy is about 11-18 degrees, centrally 14.48.
The remembered comparison of 56 degrees and 82 degrees is not the paper's
principal elevation result. Do not mix elevation, zenith angle and azimuth.

Dalleur's own examples page supplies specific checks:
- Castel Gandolfo: true solar elevation 16.59 degrees; estimates 17.07 at
  assumed 35 mm and 15.75 at 38 mm; an alternative zero-roll 35 mm fit gives
  16.93. Absolute deviations 0.48,0.84,0.34 degrees respectively.
- Saint Hubert: true 50.82 degrees; inferred 50.246; deviation 0.574 degrees.
- Synthetic scene with specified light elevation 30 degrees and relative
  azimuth 45 degrees is supplied as a template demonstration.

These are meaningful checks of the method, including real photographs. They
are author-conducted examples, not a blind independent laboratory validation
with a reported sample size and error distribution for D115-like scenes.
Muse describes accuracy within tenths of a degree; the displayed real examples
support subdegree accuracy on those images, not automatically that accuracy
on every image. Some example camera parameters are adjusted using scene cues.

3. Independent arithmetic check and sensitivity

check_geometry.py implements equation 5 using a=2.4207, e=-1.9927, h=0.31,
scaled image width 3.6, f=FD/10. It gives:
FD 30 mm: 31.8593 degrees
FD 33 mm: 30.2417 degrees
FD 37 mm: 28.2386 degrees
FD 45 mm: 24.7624 degrees
This agrees with the paper's table except a negligible approximately 0.01
degree difference in its last row. It is not an explanation of the anomaly.

At 37 mm, a one-degree pitch error has a first-order effect of about 0.824
degree on elevation, and a one-degree roll error about 0.565 degree. Dalleur
does examine camera uncertainty; saying he ignores it would be incorrect.

If all other inputs stay fixed at 37 mm, raising the inferred elevation to
42.72 by moving only the horizon requires a pitch of about 23.22 degrees
instead of 4.79, and a horizon shift about 35.5% of image width. Moving only
the vanishing point vertically requires about 43.3% of image width. These
are large conditional changes: a vague appeal to a slightly misplaced
horizon is not a demonstrated answer. They do not bound simultaneous changes
in several parameters, nor directly quantify endpoint measurement error:
small changes in a short projected ray can move a distant intersection far.

The 25-32 interval varies focal length under the selected geometry. It is
not a statistically calibrated 95% confidence interval for every uncertainty.

4. What remains open in the photographic inference

Projected lines through corresponding object and shadow points legitimately
estimate the direction of parallel illumination. A sloping ground surface
does not invalidate this geometry by itself. A curved cloth surface does
not invalidate a correctly matched point pair either; tracing an arbitrary
curved tonal edge is a different operation and may not identify a light ray.

The decisive empirical question is whether the selected points really are
matching object/shadow features, and whether independent selections keep
the fitted source well below 42 degrees. Several convergent ray estimates
would strengthen that claim. Common feature-selection or processing errors
cannot be treated as statistically independent just because there are many
lines. A reproducible endpoint table with uncertainties is more informative
than counting overlaid rays alone.

Figure 9 explicitly uses denoising and contrast processing. That does not
prove the features are artifacts. Quantitative measurements should remain
traceable to the unprocessed scan. The original 6K scan was not independently
obtained and remeasured in this pass; inspecting PDF figures is a limitation.

Grant corrected an early misreading of the hat. His lapel-based alternative
and subjective face-orientation estimates do not establish a solar fit to
Dalleur's selected correspondences. His bright-cloud simulation, as described
in the later exchange, raises a real issue about mixed illumination, but
Dalleur replies that it removes the very sharp shadows still visible in
D115. I have not verified a simulation satisfying all those constraints.
Neither an acknowledged obsolete objection nor a generic possibility of
uncertainty is a successful refutation.

5. Wet/dry and the cumulative evidence

The photographed trousers have a pronounced upper dark zone and lighter
exposed regions. Dalleur's interpretation connects the upper boundary to
the jacket's prior coverage, and the irregular transition to orientation
relative to drying rays. This is a scene-related shape with visible fabric
texture, not simply an arbitrary blotch. Muse is right that calling it an
unknown development defect does not explain the garment-related structure.

The observation, wetness interpretation, drying history and IR explanation
are nevertheless distinct steps. Ordinary black-and-white brightness is not
a moisture meter. A single exposure does not record the earlier buttoned
jacket, the moment it opened, initial water in each region, elapsed drying
time or wavelength of the heating. Even if wetness and very rapid drying
are accepted, the photograph does not independently measure the IR flux.

The known rain and wet conditions and repeated reports of rapid drying
give this interpretation evidential context. They should be assessed
together, rather than pretending each photo must re-establish the weather.
The defensible claim is that the picture is consistent with, and may
corroborate, differential drying under a covering. The stronger claim that
the picture alone proves a short intense infrared event is not established.
No physically measured alternative drying history is established here either.

6. What would resolve the most important photographic dispute

Use the original scan, publish every selected feature pair and its coordinate
uncertainty, have analysts select features without seeing the target answer,
and jointly fit plausible camera geometry and ordinary solar illumination.
Compare that fit with the alternative source, retaining all sharp shadow
constraints. If the ordinary solar model cannot fit those independent data
within justified errors, the anomalous-direction claim becomes substantially
stronger. This is a concrete test of the remaining uncertainty, not a claim
that an unidentified error has already explained the result.

Follow-up clarification from the counterargument research pass (same date)

The successful Castel Gandolfo example has the sun visible and, explicitly,
no shadows. It validates the angle-reconstruction pipeline but does not test
the difficult identification of faint object/shadow pairs in D115. The
Saint Hubert and synthetic examples provide further checks, but the set
is not a matched blind validation sample furnishing an error distribution
for D115. See ../fatima-photo-counters-20261027/research-report.txt for the
direct critiques by Grant, Fodor, Bradley and Ray, plus the latest replies.
