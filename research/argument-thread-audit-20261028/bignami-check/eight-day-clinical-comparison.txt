BIGNAMI CHALLENGE: EIGHT-DAY EXCLUSION VERSUS WOUND-HEALING EVIDENCE
28 October 2026

Proposition challenged: eight days of persistent lesions and bleeding under the reported controls rules out every ordinary chemically maintained wound explanation. This is a proposed exclusion inference being examined, not a claim Daniel has asserted every version of it.

For this challenge accept the attested controls: no covert access, no broken seals, no medicine added during dressings. The possible reason for persistence considered here is residual injury, not assumed tampering. Bignami's specific rapid-healing expectation can fail without every chemical-injury explanation thereby being excluded.

15 adult patients total, five per treatment group. Phenol group: five people, six nails. Procedure included partial nail avulsion, curettage, then88%phenol applied for3minutes. Antiseptic dressings and five days of oral antibiotics were used; it was not untreated skin exposure. No continuing applications of phenol are described. Phenol-group mean postoperative drainage14±4.85days; mean reepithelialization40.5±3.7days. Pain8.6±4.56days. Other cauterant groups also had closure times beyond eight days. The text reports weekly follow-up for two weeks then monthly; thus the precision and ascertainment of quoted healing times should be treated cautiously. Small sample and different surgical site prevent a matched explanation of Pio.

What this demonstrates: ordinary injured tissue can drain and remain unclosed beyond eight days without a further chemical application. Neither removal of the initiating chemical nor an intact dressing implies immediate reversal of the resulting tissue injury. An eight-day threshold needs lesion-specific clinical justification before it can exclude all such mechanisms.

What it does not demonstrate: a lesion unchanged in appearance throughout eight days; Pio's specific blood flow; fifty-year persistence; scarless closure; lack of inflammatory changes; fragrance; phenol application by Pio; an ordinary cause of the whole case. Surgical nail-matrix wounds are not chemically treated hand lesions, and drainage is not necessarily whole blood. Never turn14days of postoperative drainage into14days of bleeding.
