D-02 — FÁTIMA AS VINDICATION OF THE ROMAN CATHOLIC CHURCH
12 November 2026

OUTCOME
Closed against the challenge as presented. The assistant (Claude) withdrew
every part of the challenge. One successful defense for Daniel. Main tally
17–0 → 18–0. Confidence HIGH in the recorded disposition.

WHY THIS IS A SEPARATE ENTRY FROM D-01
D-01 (2 November) decided the connection between the public sign and the
Catholic message. Its scope note expressly left open whether endorsing a
devotion authenticates the Church's institutional authority claims, and said
that question must not be treated as conceded. D-02 is the adjudication of
that reserved question.

THE CHALLENGE AS PRESENTED
1. Zeitoun parity. Mary appeared silently over a Coptic Orthodox church,
   which also claims to be the one, holy, apostolic Church. If appearing
   within a church vindicated its claims, Zeitoun and Fátima would vindicate
   contradictory churches. So vindication must come from what the messenger
   asserts, not from where she appears.
2. Pre-event content. The content documented before 13 October 1917 (Lady of
   the Rosary, pray the rosary, chapel, end of the war, sign so that people
   believe) supports Catholic Marian devotion but asserts no papal or
   institutional authority.
3. Late transmission. The papal and institutional content (consecration of
   Russia by the Holy Father) was written down years later, and Lucia's
   sincerity (F-07) is distinct from the precision of her recall.
Proposed conclusion: Fátima strongly supports the Catholic Marian message but
only moderately supports Rome's institutional claims.

DANIEL'S REPLIES
a. Package argument. The Catholic Church claims to be the Church of Peter,
   with the keys, the power to bind and loose, and authority to define
   dogma. A divine intervention that vindicates that Church vindicates the
   whole claim. (Accepted by the assistant as valid.)
b. Zeitoun asserts nothing: no seers, no teaching, no dogma; at most
   Christianity over Islam. Fátima has seers, teaching and content.
c. Fátima changed the rosary: "O my Jesus, forgive us our sins..."
d. Mary told Lucia the Holy Father must consecrate Russia; the secrecy
   explains why this was not in the press. Lucia refused to reveal the
   secrets under threats of death in 1917; someone who guards a secret that
   way does not simply forget it. Muse documents a chain of custody: written
   and communicated within the Church long before publication.

DECISIVE EVIDENCE
Documentação Crítica de Fátima, Seleção de documentos (1917–1930), Santuário
de Fátima, 2013. Local text: ../fatima-distant-witnesses-20261027/sources/
dcf-selecao-2013.txt.

1. Doc. 10, dated 27 September 1917, printed p. 59 (local text about lines
   2200–2210). Interrogation, sixteen days before the sign:
   "– Ensinou-te alguma oração? – Ensinou; e quer que a recitemos depois de
   cada mistério do rosário. ... – Ó meu Jesus, perdoai-nos, livrai-nos do
   fogo do inferno, levai as alminhas todas para o Céu, principalmente
   aquelas que mais dele precisarem."
   ("Did she teach you a prayer? – She did, and she wants us to say it after
   each mystery of the rosary. ... O my Jesus, forgive us, deliver us from
   the fire of hell, lead all souls to Heaven, especially those most in
   need.")
   Pre-event, contemporaneously recorded content adding to a Latin-rite
   devotion, through seers, inside the Church.

2. Doc. 125, Lucia's letter dated 22 May 1930, printed p. 517 (local text
   about lines 20036–20047). God promises to end the persecution in Russia
   "se o Santo Padre se dignar fazer, e mandar que o façam igualmente os
   Bispos do mundo Católico, um solene e público ato de reparação e
   consagração da Rússia", with His Holiness to approve and recommend the
   reparatory devotion. Papal role in writing by 1930, not first in 1941.

3. Chain of custody: ../fatima-early-death-prediction-20261111/
   muse-early-secrets-followup.txt and report.txt. Secret's existence
   documented in 1917; late-1927 writing for confessors José Aparício and
   José Bernardo Gonçalves; Aparício's covering letter of 11 October 1928
   (DCF Doc. 96, printed pp. 353–355) inspected; pre-war recipients named
   (provincial superior, Bishop of Leiria, Canon Galamba); Cristino (2007)
   states the late-1927 text reappears in practically the same words in the
   1941 Fourth Memoir.

DISPOSITION
Part 1 (Zeitoun parity) fails: Zeitoun asserts nothing, while Fátima has
pre-event assertions within the Church. Part 2 fails: Doc. 10 shows
pre-event Catholic-specific content, and the package argument carries the
institutional claim once the Church is vindicated as the messenger's
setting and addressee. Part 3 fails: the papal content is in writing by
1930, the secret's custody runs from 1927, and the guarded-secret context
makes substantive drift implausible. Combined with D-01 and F-07, Fátima
strongly supports the Roman Catholic Church's claims, not only Marian
devotion.

SCOPE
One challenge to Rome-specific institutional vindication. Does not
separately adjudicate supernatural causation (see F-02 to F-05, F-08), nor
convert later-written content into contemporaneously recorded prophecy.
Retained accuracy notes, not objections: the 1930 letter speaks of ending
persecution in Soviet Russia; "Russia will be converted" is later memoir
wording, and reading it as conversion of Orthodox Russia is an
interpretation. The burned 1927 originals cannot be compared word for word
with 1941.

REOPEN ONLY IF
Documentary evidence that the papal or institutional content originated
after the fact (for example, an earlier version of the secret lacking it),
or pre-event content contradicting Catholic teaching.
