L-04 | LOURDES | SINCERE MISTAKE ACROSS THE APPARITION SERIES
7 November 2026

Challenge

Could Bernadette have sincerely mistaken a recurring perceptual experience
for an encounter with Mary? The strongest case examined combines an initial
misperception with reinforcement through return visits, prayer, expectation
and attention from others. This is one proposed explanation of a linked
series, so the eighteen reports should not be modeled as independent trials.
The test concerns Bernadette's account of the Lady, including the direction
to the spring. The separate healing record remains part of the cumulative
Lourdes case. The test does not require any claim about miraculous geology.

Contemporary case for the objection

1. Balencie, Lacrampe and Peyrus examined Bernadette at the hospice school
   in late March 1858, after the mayor commissioned the inquiry on the
   prefect's request. The mayor forwarded their conclusions on 1 April.
   They found no demonstrated intent to deceive. They suggested a reflection
   of light as the initial stimulus and, in the longer text translated by
   Trochu, proposed that attention and public excitement could reinforce the
   initial experience into recurring ecstasy. The examination and proposed
   explanation are historical evidence. The report supplies no observed
   reflection on 11 February and no observed change from reflected light
   to a recurring figure. I inspected the published excerpts in Estrade,
   Part II, ch. III, and Trochu's English translation. I did not recover
   the signed original. Trochu is a Catholic biographer and his narration
   must be distinguished from the report he quotes.

Empirical comparisons

Luhrmann et al. 2013 randomly assigned Christian volunteers to four weeks
of guided imaginative prayer or Gospel lectures. Among participants who
initially reported unusual auditory or visual events at most about once a
year, 7 of 34 in the prayer group and 0 of 16 in the study group reported
one on return. These included brief sounds, perceived figures and ordinary
objects momentarily misidentified. The comparison supports the possibility
that repeated, specifically imagery-based practice can affect reported
experience. Bernadette is not documented using the experimental practice,
and the trial does not document a comparable series of encounters and
conversations. Luhrmann et al. 2021 found associations between absorption,
cultural expectations and reported spiritual sensory experiences, which
likewise do not estimate a rate for Bernadette's sequence.

Rees 1971 interviewed 293 bereaved spouses. Some described experiences of
the same deceased person over long periods. The aggregate duration includes
sensed presence, not just repeated visual encounters. The study shows why
recurrence alone is not a basis for multiplying single-episode odds. The
population and circumstances are different from the Lourdes case.

Adjudication

The first encounter occurred before the Lourdes-specific expectation that
could reinforce the later visits. The doctors inferred an optical trigger
without observing it. The subsequent prayer and social context give a
possible route to recurrence. They do not identify what was operating in
this particular series. A model tied to ritual or place also has to allow
for the negative visits, the changed viewing position in July, the changing
content and the direction that preceded uncovering the spring. An
intermittent experience could accommodate some variation, so the negative
visits alone do not exclude it. Neither the 1858 comparison nor the modern
studies supply a match to the joint pattern. No case-specific ordinary
account strong enough to sustain the objection was established.
