What is accepted
If the Marian events genuinely authenticate Catholic teaching authority, Smith's incompatible claim to restored authority cannot also be divinely authorized. The conclusions of the Marian investigation must inform, rather than be erased from, subsequent comparisons. Smith's documented credibility problems make an unsupported demonic dismissal of Fatima evidentially different from a source-specific challenge to Smith.

What is not conceded
The four descriptions as worded are not an exhaustive set of mutually exclusive historical explanations. Sincere perceptual or interpretive error does not imply stupidity. Localized fraud does not require an intergenerational central conspiracy or every witness's complicity. Human error and deliberate deception can coexist, within an episode and across different cases.

Candidate fifth framework
Mixed origins: different events arise through different combinations of sincere observation and interpretation, localized fabrication, and physical or clinical processes, with shared religious traditions influencing attribution and preservation. This is a distinct cumulative pattern outside the user's all-good-faith versus all-coordinated-fraud descriptions, rather than a fifth fundamental kind of agency.

Evidential status
Merely naming this mixture does not establish that it adequately explains any retained core component, still less all four cases. This note supplies no newly established ordinary mechanism, no diagnosis of historical participants, no allegation that named participants committed fraud, and no new material reasonable doubt. A successful alternative must be tied to the actual records and survive the existing defenses. The fact that a taxonomy is not exhaustive also does not refute the Catholic explanation.
