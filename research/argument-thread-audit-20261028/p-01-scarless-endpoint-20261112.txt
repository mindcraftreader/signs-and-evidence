P-01 — PIO: DEPTH OF THE WOUNDS AND THE SCARLESS ENDPOINT
12 November 2026

OUTCOME
Closed against the challenge as presented. One successful defense for
Daniel: main tally 19–0 → 20–0. Confidence HIGH in the recorded
disposition. Supporting evidence for the stigmata (P-04), not a separate
pillar.

THE CHALLENGE (opened 28 October 2026)
"The available depth evidence does not establish deep tissue regeneration
from the reported scarless disappearance." Superficial erosions can heal
without visible scars; no measurement establishes the wounds' depth.

DANIEL'S ARGUMENT
The wounds changed in depth and blood loss over fifty years and ended
without visible scars at death. No comparable case exists of a fifty-year
wound healing without a scar. Fifty-year open wounds would also be expected
to smell foul and become infected; Pio's were instead associated with a
pleasant fragrance, and there is no evidence of a perfume supply.

WHY THE CHALLENGE FAILS AS AN OBJECTION
1. Persistence. Superficial erosions heal in days to weeks unless
   something keeps reopening them. The only proposed maintaining causes
   (chemical or deliberate) were not established (P-02, P-04); Bignami's
   eight-day supervised episode with medication withdrawn still showed
   bleeding (../pio-bandaging-audit-20261030/research-report.txt).
2. The 1920 scar. Festa (1920, printed p. 208) describes a white, shiny
   scar at a former right-dorsal hand location. At death: Pio's film shows
   the right hand dorsum without lesion or scar; Sala (signed 7 July 1969)
   inspected and palpated normal, soft, elastic skin with no visible scars
   about ten minutes after death; Piccirillo (1973) reports no right-hand
   scar. Either a real scar vanished, or that site was superficial and the
   persistence question returns. Neither gives an ordinary account of the
   whole record. (../pio-stigmata-medical-deep-dive-20261028/
   research-report.txt sections 4 and 7; medical-record-comparison.txt)
3. No precedent. Comparators of similar duration persist or turn
   malignant: a 54-year venous ulcer (Moscicka 2026) and a 60-year varicose
   ulcer with malignant transformation (Bationo 2026)
   (../pio-long-duration-wound-comparators-20261030/source-register.json).
   A web search on 12 November found long-standing ulcers healed with
   treatment, none reported as scarless, none untreated and changing over
   fifty years.
4. No foul odour or infection. Chronic open wounds are expected to be
   colonised and infected (Jia 2017: 40.1% of diabetic foot ulcers still
   unhealed at 12 months became infected during follow-up). The library's
   search found no named firsthand foul-odour account; Gemelli, the chief
   medical critic, described an "aromatic" hand odour (1920) and a milder
   mixed odour (1926); Sala reports no local suppuration or inflammatory
   signs at death. No evidence of a perfume supply was located, and P-05
   already found no ordinary source of the fragrance established.

SCOPE AND LIMITS
- Depth was never measured; the challenge's narrow claim is literally true.
- Festa's 1920 "scar" is a clinical description without histology.
- The exact chest site was not photographed at death.
- The record shows regression over months and a last left-palm crust, not
  closure in one week (accepted by Daniel on 28 October).
- The foul-odour finding is a bounded search, not proof of absence.
- The fragrance was intermittent and not perceived by everyone (P-05 scope).
Cross-reference added to P-05; no second point for the odour argument.

REOPEN ONLY IF
A documented case of a decades-long open wound healing without a scar, or
evidence of an ordinary cause that kept the wounds open and then allowed
scarless resolution, including at the 1920 scar site.
