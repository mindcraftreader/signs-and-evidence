L-05 — LOURDES: BASE RATES AND THE DECLINE IN RECOGNISED CURES
12 November 2026

OUTCOME
Withdrawn; logged without a point (closed_unscored_withdrawn), at Daniel's
preference. Main tally unchanged at 20–0.

THE CHALLENGE
1. Lourdes has received very large numbers of sick visitors (Dowling 1984
   estimated over two million sick pilgrims by that date).
2. Rare unexpected recoveries (spontaneous remission, misdiagnosis,
   functional illness) should yield a steady trickle of dramatic cases
   among millions of sick people regardless of the shrine.
3. Recognised cures follow diagnostic weakness rather than visitor numbers:
   of 72 recognised cures in ../lourdes-expanded-audit-20261103/
   baseline-roster.json, 64 recovered in 1858–1959 and 8 from 1960 on,
   while visitor numbers rose greatly.
Proposed conclusion: the healing record alone does not show an excess over
base rates.

DANIEL'S REPLIES
a. He grants that healings occur in all traditions; his Lourdes claim is
   that Mary directed Bernadette to a spring and healings have been
   associated with it since, not that the healing count beats chance.
b. Recognition takes many years (e.g. Moriau recovered 2008, recognised
   2018; Raco recovered 2009, recognised 2024).
c. Post-1960 pilgrims are nearly all medically treated, and recognition
   criteria (from Benedict XIV / Lambertini) require no treatment or
   clearly ineffective treatment, so most recoveries are excluded by rule.

DISPOSITION
Reply (c), with (b), answers the decline argument as well as the
diagnostic-artefact reading does, without implying fewer healings. The
base-rate argument targeted a claim Daniel does not advance (reply a).
Agreed scope: the Lourdes record's weight comes from the quality of the
strongest cases (L-02) and their association with the directed spring, not
from raw counts. Withdrawn as an objection; no point by Daniel's choice,
because the challenge largely missed his actual claim.
