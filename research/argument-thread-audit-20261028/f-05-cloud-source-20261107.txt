F-05 | FÁTIMA | SUNLIT CLOUD AS THE LOWER LIGHT AND THE MOVING DISC
7 November 2026

Challenge
One compact sunlit cloud patch produced the lower-angle shadows in D115 and
the disc reported spinning, changing color and plunging at the Cova and from
outside it. Daniel agreed to test this as one tally point. This model accepts
Dalleur's lower light direction and assigns it to a physical cloud patch.
F-04 tested whether ordinary direct sunlight with cloud illumination could
produce a misleading shadow reading. That earlier result remains separate.

Result
The challenge is not sustained. The fixed-height drifting patch misses the
reported motion. A changing illumination pattern can evade the simplest drift
test, but no evidenced version fits the shadow direction, sharpness and
contrast together with the disc and the reports from separate locations.
Daniel receives one successful defense. These tests do not determine the
identity of the source or exclude every form of atmospheric optics.

1. Direction and illumination

Dalleur's central estimate is 28.238 degrees and his solar-noon calculation
is 42.72 degrees. The 14.482-degree elevation gap is a lower bound on angular
separation from the Sun at that modeled instant. A different azimuth increases
the separation. The published relative azimuth is camera-relative.

The hypothesized scene has the direct Sun obscured, a sunlit patch at the
lower angle, and broader overhead illumination. Dalleur describes the
overhead component as diffuse and roughly comparable in brightness. Figure 6
identifies separate shadows on a sloping surface. I inspected the existing
public D115 crops showing the hat and stone boundaries. They do not supply
metric geometry or a calibrated plate response for a historical radiance fit.

For a uniform circular patch of angular diameter D, a perpendicular surface
receives E = L * pi * sin(D/2)^2. At Dalleur's illustrative typical cloud
luminance of 3,000 to 10,000 cd/m², a 1-degree patch supplies 0.72 to 2.39 lux
and a 5-degree patch supplies 17.93 to 59.77 lux. At 28.238 degrees the
corresponding horizontal values are about 0.34 to 1.13 and 8.48 to 28.28 lux.
These are model inputs and outputs, not measured historical illuminances.
The witness comparisons to the Moon give no measured angular diameter.

For a simplified perpendicular cross-section, full penumbra width at caster
gap g is 2*g*tan(D/2). At 5 mm spacing, 1- and 5-degree sources give widths
of 0.09 and 0.44 mm. At 100 mm spacing those become 1.75 and 8.73 mm. This
shows why a sharp near-contact edge is compatible with an extended source
and why the same patch must be tested against edges at several spacings.
Actual surface inclinations, caster geometry and image blur would need to be
included. No observed D115 width is equated to these illustrative widths.

2. Drift and the sightlines

At fixed height h and horizontal range r, the elevation is atan(h/r).
For a fixed projected physical width W the angular diameter is
2*atan(W/(2*sqrt(h²+r²))). A patch moving lower at fixed height and width
therefore appears smaller. Maria José de Lemos Queirós reported the globe
swelling as it seemed to plunge. Garrett reported the sharply edged disc
continuing to spin during the blood-red approach and describes seconds of
terror. The accounts supply no measured path, size or exact exposure phase.

The archive's representative Alburitel location is 13.16 km east and 1.92 km
north of the Cova. Garrett describes clouds moving west to east. A fixed-
height patch moving east while west of the Alburitel observer gets closer
horizontally and rises in that observer's sky. To be far enough east to recede
at a Cova elevation of 28.238 degrees, the patch would have to be at least
13.16 * tan(28.238 degrees), about 7.07 km above the Cova in the flat local
model. The 25 to 32 degree range gives 6.14 to 8.22 km. At 2 km high, the
patch can be no more than 3.72 km horizontally from the Cova at that instant.
Inácio Lourenço's later published Alburitel account describes a zigzag
descent. This is a conditional conflict for the same low patch and event phase
moving with that wind. It is not triangulation, and Garrett's reported wind
direction need not describe every layer. No terrain masking is modeled.

For scale only, assume a fixed-height patch passes from 42.72 to 28.238 degrees
in ten seconds. The required outward radial speed would be
h * (cot(28.238 degrees) - cot(42.72 degrees)) / 10 seconds, or about 78 m/s
at 1 km and 156 m/s at 2 km. These are conditional speeds. The start angle,
interval and assignment of D115 to the end of the descent are illustrative.

3. Changing illumination and colored cloud optics

A brightness pattern can change size or shift without the cloud following it.
A simple sunlight opening in one flat horizontal layer projects onto a second
parallel layer with a constant horizontal offset at fixed Sun angle. The
projected patch then follows the opening's drift. A deforming opening or
changing, tilted or multilayer clouds can give different motion. The available
weather and image evidence does not constrain those extra inputs enough to
give a predictive fit to the movement or the shadows.

Published cloud-edge enhancement is real. Yordanov's favorable cloud-gap
example models strong forward scattering within 1.5 degrees of the Sun and
reports an experimental extent of about 8 degrees. This is evidence for that
particular mechanism and geometry, not a universal scattering cutoff. The
example does not supply a compact bright patch at the required minimum
14.48-degree separation. Diffuse cloud light can arrive from farther off-axis.
Cloud iridescence can form colorful bands and sometimes swirling patterns,
so color alone does not exclude a cloud. A standard solar corona is centered
on the astronomical Sun. An isolated segment of a 22-degree ice halo can
reach 28.238 degrees at about 20.5 degrees of azimuth offset from a Sun at
42.72 degrees. This geometric possibility supplies neither the needed
shadow contrast nor the motion. It does not establish that such a halo
segment was present.

The separate Minde recollection says the fall was in Fátima's direction,
roughly north from the representative Minde location, with the Sun south.
If a patch is seen toward the northern sky when the Sun is due south at
42.72 degrees, its angular separation is at least 47.28 degrees, attained
only at the zenith. The bound follows from the dot product of the two sight
directions and holds for any north-half azimuth and above-horizon elevation.
It makes near-Sun forward scattering an inadequate common explanation if
the reported direction is retained. For illustration, the same 2 km high
patch placed due south of the Cova appears 14.5 degrees from the Sun at
the Cova, 67.6 degrees from representative Alburitel and 124.5 degrees from
representative Minde. The Minde location is approximate and its recollection
was collected decades later. No measured witness bearings or synchronized
observations were recovered. The geometry does not rule out a patch visible
by diffuse scattering, but that alternative still owes a fit to the reported
disc and its motion from more than one direction.
