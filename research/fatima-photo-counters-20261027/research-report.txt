COUNTERARGUMENTS TO DALLEUR'S FATIMA PHOTOGRAPHIC EVIDENCE
Research pass completed 27 October 2026

Finding

I found substantive criticisms, especially of shadow identification, mixed
illumination, and transfer of validation accuracy to D115. I did not find a
completed, independently reproduced, peer-reviewed counteranalysis showing
that ordinary sunlight at approximately 42 degrees fits all of Dalleur's
selected D115 shadow constraints. This is a scoped search result, not a claim
that no such work exists anywhere. Most direct discussion found is in blogs,
videos and an evolving Google document. Counting those discussions as several
independent experimental refutations would misrepresent the evidence.

The strongest numerical counterexample located is Mark Grant's mixed-light
render. The best methodological counterargument is that successful worked
examples do not establish the error distribution for faint shadows in D115.
Several categorical claims that no useful geometry is possible are too broad.

software developer. Institutional affiliation not verified. The document
explicitly flags parts as outdated and some mathematical appendices unfinished.

Primary critique:

Previously archived PDF and text:

Later render and description, published in Matthew Adelstein's review footnote:
https://benthams.substack.com/p/a-clash-of-titans-the-ethan-muse
Original render image saved here as grant-toy-render.png.

Claim: selected faint edges may not correspond to the cast-shadow points
needed by the geometric method. In his published toy render description,
both scenes have a sun at 42 degrees elevation and 70 degrees relative
azimuth, camera field of view 62 degrees and downward pitch about 5 degrees.
The clear scene yields a roughly correct inference. Adding a bright,
partly transmissive overhead square changes the apparent hat-shadow boundary;
using that intuitive boundary reportedly produces a 15-20 degree error.

What was checked: read original description, inspected both published renders,
and read Dalleur's detailed reply quoted in Muse's current response. I did
not obtain the scene file or independently remeasure the reported 15-20 error.
The rendered cloud contribution visibly changes the shadow information.

Dalleur's answer: the cloudy render loses the crisp directional contours that
he identifies under the hat, on the finger/hat and stick/knee, and under rocks
in D115. He requests inclusion of those features in the countermodel. A model
that reproduces a soft hat boundary while losing other relevant directional
shadows does not reproduce the full evidence he uses.

Assessment: this is a meaningful failure-mode demonstration and the most
concrete numerical counter found. It is not yet a demonstrated ordinary-sun
reconstruction of D115. Multiple cues must be fitted together. Nor does one
failed countermodel itself validate all of Dalleur's selections.

Historical correction: Grant acknowledged misreading a hat feature. Early
objections based on that mistake should not be presented as current knockouts.
His lapel-based alternative is not a quantitatively established solar fit to
Dalleur's correctly matched feature pairs.

2. James Fodor: unpropagated line errors and radial distortion

Background verified from University of Melbourne's institutional biography:
former PhD student in Decision, Risk and Financial Sciences; graduate studies
in physics/economics at Melbourne and a neuroscience master's at Australian
National University. The page now identifies him as a Centre for Brain, Mind
and Markets alumnus. This establishes relevant scientific training, not a
specialty in historical photographic metrology.
https://www.unimelb.edu.au/cbmm/about-us/team/james-fodor
The page was indexed but direct retrieval returned 404 during this pass.

Primary presentation, 15 June 2026:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YPDEfgnGPCM
A Skeptical Analysis of the 1917 Marian Apparitions and Miracle of the Sun.
Retrieved automatic English captions and metadata; reviewed 1:13:00-1:30:50,
especially 1:20-1:28. I did not review the entire presentation.

Main objections:
- Only D115 receives the principal quantitative angle reconstruction; the
  six historical pictures are not six independent solar-angle measurements.
- The intersection of hand-selected shadow lines needs its own uncertainty
  calculation, separate from focal-length and camera-tilt sensitivity.
- Lens distortion can move those lines and their inferred intersection.
- Dalleur himself discusses 5-20 degree errors in automated light estimation
  on other images; Fodor treats that as reason to reject the smaller anomaly.

Assessment: the line-selection uncertainty objection is legitimate and
directly relevant. Camera-pitch derivatives do not quantify errors in where
the vanishing point was placed. Fodor does not propagate those errors himself,
publish corrected endpoints, or establish a 42-degree fit in this section.

The automated-method comparison is not a measured error bar for Dalleur's
manual procedure. Different estimators need not have equal errors; one cannot
simply attach the cited 5-20 degree range to his result. Fodor's categorical
claim that the result is just noise goes beyond the calculation presented.

Lens distortion is a real concern. Dalleur's current website specifically
addresses likely camera/lens types and cites historical optical measurements.
That narrows the objection but does not supply a calibration of the exact
1917 camera. The page's current form cannot be assumed to have existed when
Fodor recorded his critique; the chronology of revisions was not established.

Reviewed the entire available automatic-caption transcript and description;

At 8:56-16:17 he emphasizes unknown lens distortion, calibration and vintage
lens variability. At 16:17-22:13 he discusses perspective, distance, apparent

only through a computer-generated scene. These claims were compared with
Dalleur's actual mathematical model and current worked examples.

Assessment:
- Unknown residual lens distortion merits a quantified sensitivity analysis.
- Demonstrating that an image changes with camera position/focal length does
  not refute a method explicitly modeling perspective and focal length.
- Pixel shadow length alone is not Dalleur's principal measurement. It is
  object-to-shadow correspondence, ray convergence, then camera geometry.
- Needing complete depth for every object or level ground for every shadow
  is not a general requirement for this ray-convergence calculation.
- Validation only in CGI is inaccurate as a description of the examples
  currently published: Dalleur also supplies real photographic examples.

Bradley advertises two follow-ups on development/processing and lighting.
Searches and an inventory of the channel's 30 listed videos found only this
Fatima installment. I did not locate published parts 2 or 3; promised future
experiments must not be counted as completed demonstrations.

His allegation about the paper's reviewers being exclusively humanities
reviewers was not substantiated. The journal is listed by DOAJ as using
double-anonymous peer review. Its science/religion scope is not proof that
this paper received specialist optical review, but neither does it establish
that peer review never happened. Rhetorical claims about dishonesty are not
evidence for a geometric error and were excluded from the assessment.

4. Georgia Ray: data accessibility and ambiguous assumptions

Background: microbiology undergraduate at The Evergreen State College,
master's in biodefense at George Mason University; independent biosecurity
researcher, according to her own biography.
https://eukaryotewritesblog.com/about/

Primary critique, 30 September 2025:
https://eukaryotewritesblog.com/2025/09/30/miracle-of-the-sun/

Her most useful points are the limits of public low-resolution copies,
ambiguity of candidate shadows/reflections, and the uncertain horizon and
camera settings. She does not claim to have replicated the full calculation.
The mismatch between accessible images and the original 6K scan is a real
reproducibility issue; a difficult-to-access scan is not proof of a false result.

Her suggested ambiguities in the rock's double shadow and umbrella-handle
highlights deserve examination, but are alternative readings rather than
demonstrated replacements. A level-ground objection would not by itself
invalidate the point-correspondence method. Her observation that some generic
uncertainty might bridge the gap is not a quantitative bridge of that gap.

5. Matched validation: the strongest general methodological objection

Mouthy Infidel's primary essay, 9 September 2026:
https://confrontingcapital.substack.com/p/fatima-apologists-need-to-show-their
Explicitly disclaims the technical expertise to adjudicate first-order optics.
Its useful proposal is to validate on photographs comparable to D115 in scene,
clothing, camera, terrain, shadow faintness and mixed lighting. This proposes
a better empirical test; it does not supply its result.

Dalleur's current examples:
https://sites.google.com/pusc.it/elevation-azimuth/examples

Important clarification to our earlier discussion of the control group:
- The Castel Gandolfo picture has the sun visible through cloud and visible
  rays. Dalleur explicitly says there are no shadows. Its accurate angle
  recovery checks other parts of the pipeline but not extraction of an
  off-frame light direction from faint clothing shadows.
- The Saint Hubert example uses a statue/pedestal, lamp posts and a camera
  with an approximately known focal length; it adds a useful real-scene test.
- The synthetic example gives controlled ground truth and verifies the
  computational geometry. It does not include all unknown historical-image
  processing and feature-identification errors.

The successful examples are genuine positive evidence for the method. They
are a few author-conducted demonstrations, not a reported matched blind
validation sample with a D115-specific error distribution. It was too broad
to imply that subdegree errors there establish the same error bound here.

6. Independent technical check of the broad objections

Hany Farid, Dartmouth computer science affiliation on his Digital Image
Forensics teaching material, section 5.7, PDF page 94:
https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dyer/cs534/papers/farid-digitalimageforensics.pdf
The material explicitly explains that correctly matched object and shadow
points locate the projected light source regardless of the receiving
surface. It also specifically warns about matching errors and unstable
intersections when projected lines are nearly parallel. This supports both
the validity of the basic geometry and the narrow empirical uncertainty issue.
I inspected the entire relevant page, not the full 199-page course text.

Antonio Criminisi, Ian Reid and Andrew Zisserman, Oxford Engineering Science:
Single View Metrology, IJCV 40(2),123-148 (2000), plus 1999 conference version.
https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A9b99df78-df75-49a9-a1b8-1b38e1b173a8
https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/publications/1999/Criminisi99/criminisi99.pdf
This establishes that useful measurements from a single perspective image
without complete pre-known camera calibration are established computer vision,
provided the necessary scene constraints are present. It does not establish
that D115 provides those constraints accurately enough for the claimed result.

Arthur A. Magill, US National Bureau of Standards, 1955:
Variation in Distortion with Magnification, Journal of Research NBS 54(3).
https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/54/jresv54n3p135_A1b.pdf
Provides measured behavior for historical symmetrical lens designs, including
Dagor types. It is relevant primary evidence against treating every old lens
as an arbitrarily warped fisheye. It is not the recovered 1917 lens calibration.

7. What this changes

The countercase is strongest as an objection to a proved error bound and to
correctly identifying the particular light-source shadows. It is weakest when
it substitutes generic camera demonstrations, speculation or ridicule for a
fit to the actual image. The control examples answer whether the procedure
can work; matched tests are needed to establish how reliably it works under
the difficult conditions invoked in this dispute.

No new conclusion about the preceding rain is drawn here. Rain and widespread
wet conditions are well supported background. Questions about the specific
wet/dry photo's causal interpretation do not erase those facts or the reports
of rapid drying. This pass primarily concerns the directional-light claim.
