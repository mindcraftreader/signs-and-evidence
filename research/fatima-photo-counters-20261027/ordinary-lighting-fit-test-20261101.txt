FATIMA: CAN AN ORDINARY-LIGHTING MODEL FIT THE COMBINED EVIDENCE BETTER?
1 November 2026

Question requested by Daniel Kosco
Establish an ordinary lighting explanation that fits the combined evidence
better, rather than merely mentioning possible errors in Dalleur's analysis.

Result
Not established in this review. The strongest concrete countermodel located
demonstrates a possible ambiguity in shadow interpretation. It does not yet
reconstruct D115's relevant features together, or demonstrate a better fit to
the complete historical case. This is a finding about the available analysis,
not a proof that every ordinary explanation is impossible or a calculated
probability for supernatural agency.

Candidate ordinary account tested
The real Sun is attenuated by cloud; cloud/sky illumination contributes to the
scene; faint, combined illumination makes some apparent boundaries unreliable
as exact cast-shadow correspondences. Visual adaptation and expectation might
contribute to apparent colors and movement. Regional observers might encounter
related viewing conditions. The advance announcement prompts observation on
the appointed day, but the occurrence of a sufficiently striking experience
then requires an additional account.

These are constituent hypotheses, not a successfully fitted model. In
particular, neither altered perception nor expectation is a substitute for
explaining a physically established non-solar shadow direction.

1. What the concrete lighting demonstration establishes
Mark Grant, who describes himself as a mathematics graduate student (institution
not verified), constructed two toy rendered scenes. The reported settings are
solar elevation 42 degrees, relative azimuth 70 degrees, camera field of view
62 degrees and downward pitch approximately 5 degrees. Introducing an overhead
partly transmissive luminous square reportedly makes an intuitive hat-shadow
selection produce an elevation error of 15-20 degrees. The clear-light scene
reportedly gives approximately the correct result.

That is a relevant failure-mode demonstration. It is stronger than simply
saying old photographs are uncertain. However:
- The published account does not provide an independently reproduced fit to
  all the D115 object-shadow correspondences.
- Dalleur replies that the cloud rendering loses directional contours retained
  in D115, including features around the hat, finger and stick/knee, and rocks.
  This remains a dispute about the image, not a demonstrated counter-fit.
- We inspected the published rendering previously and rechecked its written
  description in this pass. We did not obtain its scene file, rerun it or
  independently verify the stated 15-20-degree error.

2. What ordinary refraction can and cannot do here
Dalleur's central published reconstruction is approximately 28.24 degrees;
the expected solar elevation near solar noon is approximately 42.72 degrees.
That is a gap of approximately 14.48 degrees. Using the standard refraction
approximation reproduced on his current page at 42.72 degrees gives
0.01745 degrees, or about 1.05 arcminutes. The gap is about 830 times that
correction. Standard atmospheric refraction is therefore not an adequate
numerical explanation of this gap. This calculation does not test every
possible atmospheric optical phenomenon.

His published focal-length sensitivity also does not by itself reach the
Sun's expected direction. Our earlier script reproduces the equation's
approximately 31.86 degrees at a 30 mm equivalent focal length and 28.24 degrees
at 37 mm. These are conditional calculations using his selected vanishing
point and horizon, not independent verification of those selections and not
a full joint confidence interval.

Philippe Dalleur holds doctorates in applied sciences (UCLouvain) and philosophy
(Pontifical University of the Holy Cross). His current technical page addresses
focal length, pitch, roll and plausible period lens distortion; these cannot
be treated as simply omitted. The exact historical camera remains uncalibrated.

3. Basic physical distinction
Under an idealized fixed geometry, adding diffuse fill light changes the
contrast of a direct source's cast shadow. It does not move the actual
occlusion boundary for that source. Combined illumination can nevertheless
create other gradients or apparent edges that an analyst mistakenly chooses
as that boundary. Consequently the countermodel must identify which actual
D115 boundaries were misidentified and fit the remaining cues. It cannot
claim that clouds physically moved the real Sun's ray direction merely
because an apparent boundary changed in a toy scene.

An ordinary non-solar source, such as a bright cloud patch, is another possible
hypothesis. Inferring a non-solar direction is not by itself identifying a
supernatural agent. But no calibrated cloud-source reconstruction fitting the
required directional features, illumination and historical circumstances was
established here. Its mere availability as a possibility is not a better fit.

4. Combined-evidence check
Pale, readily watchable apparent disc:
Cloud attenuation is a plausible constituent explanation. We have not recovered
contemporaneous optical-depth measurements or a verified exposure history for
individual viewers. Reports do not establish continuous unattenuated Sun
exposure by every person, or clinical confirmation of healthy retinas for all.

Colors and apparent movement:
Perceptual effects can explain components. Previously reviewed comparisons do
not establish a measured match to the full reported sequence and duration.
They are not counted as a newly successful rebuttal in this pass.

Photographic geometry:
The toy cloud render demonstrates vulnerability in principle; no joint fit
to the historical photograph has been established. Dalleur's anomalous result
therefore remains an unresolved positive part of the case, conditional on
the correctness of the feature correspondences and camera reconstruction.

Contemporary public testimony and reporting:
Ordinary accounts can allow sincere witnesses. They still need to explain
what produced their reported experience, rather than treating sincerity as
either proof of supernatural agency or proof of perceptual error.

Distant reports:
There are genuinely dispersed accounts, including contemporary indirect
reports preserved in 1917 letters. Our existing source audit also distinguishes
later interviews from contemporary letters, and geographic connector lines
from measured observation bearings. No completed triangulation has been
established. These limits prevent treating the map as surveyed rays, but do
not supply an ordinary explanation of the reports. A model must address their
actual wording and provenance individually.
See ../fatima-distant-witnesses-20261027/research-report.txt and witness-records.json.

Advance announcement:
An ordinary account can explain why a crowd was observing that day. That
does not independently explain why an exceptional public experience occurred
on the appointed occasion. No defensible coincidence probability or model of
the incidence of comparable experiences was established in this review.
Expectation cannot simply be stipulated to have produced the entire event.

Drying:
Black-and-white garment tones do not independently quantify water content or
drying rate. Equally, pointing out this limit does not explain away the
historical drying testimony. Neither a calibrated photographic moisture
measurement nor a time-resolved ordinary drying reconstruction is supplied.

Overall judgment
The ordinary account has physically plausible ingredients and identifies a
real inference problem. Its strongest available demonstration does not yet
meet the user's requested combined-fit standard. I cannot responsibly claim
that it explains the assembled evidence better than supernatural intervention.
Nor have we obtained likelihoods sufficient to turn this failed counter-fit
into a numerical probability for the religious explanation.

Scope and tally
This pass rechecked Grant's current critique, the published cloud-render
description, Dalleur's current technical response, our prior equation check,
and the existing distant-witness provenance audit. It did not independently
remeasure the original 6144 x 4348 archival scan, render a full historical scene,
or clinically assess the crowd. No peer-reviewed solar-consistent D115
reconstruction was located in the targeted searches. That search result is
not a claim that no such study can exist. No tally has been silently changed.
