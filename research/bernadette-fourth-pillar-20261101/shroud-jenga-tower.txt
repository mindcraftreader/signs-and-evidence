The Shroud Jenga tower
Cumulative argument and evidence ledger
This document records your case that the Shroud is more plausibly the burial cloth of Jesus than a medieval manufacture. Each numbered block is a constraint a competing explanation must address. It also records my concessions and the limits that remain. It does not assign numerical probabilities to the blocks or imply that you have accepted every qualification below.
The newest argument is cumulative miracle evidence
Blocks 25-32 develop the modern miracle argument and the Fatima debate. Block 33 adds the comparison across religious traditions completed on 28 October 2026. Block 34 adds Bernadette Soubirous as the fourth pillar, centered on bodily preservation. The sequence is modern public and personal miracles, earlier miracles, then the Shroud with that background evidence carried forward.
Sampling representativeness remains a central block
Documented repairs, three carbon laboratories sharing one corner strip, and a competing mechanical result from an interior collection strengthen your objection to treating the corner as representative of the whole cloth. Agreement among the laboratories cannot remove a sampling limitation they share. [1–6]
The interior location is preferable for avoiding a corner-specific repair. However, the mechanical study used loose fibers vacuumed from between the cloth and its backing. The collection site does not by itself establish where each fiber originally belonged. Substantial replacement in the dated strip remains disputed.

Sampling map at the cloth’s approximate aspect ratio. Collection regions are schematic. FTIR and Raman were described as corner threads without identifying which corner; the alternatives marked on the map are not additional actual samples. [1, 4, 7, 8]

Sampling and the repair argument
01  One sampled region is not three regions
The 1988 carbon measurements came from three laboratories, but their Shroud portions came from one corner strip. They provide independent laboratory measurements with a shared assumption about the cloth sampled. [1]
Where we stand  Conceded. Calling this three independent samples of the whole cloth overstates the evidence. It remains stronger than one laboratory measurement of that region.
02  The cloth has a documented repair history
The Shroud suffered fire damage in 1532. Patches and a supporting backing were sewn on in 1534; conservation in 2002 replaced that backing and removed old patches. Human additions are part of this object’s history. [6]
Where we stand  Established. This makes repair a concrete issue to investigate, rather than a repair invented solely to evade the date. It does not locate a medieval replacement in the exact 1988 strip.
03  Material differences support an atypical region
Los Alamos chemist Raymond Rogers reported cotton, coatings and chemical differences in material attributed to the Raes and carbon regions. Western Connecticut State University chemist Alan Adler also reported differences between carbon-sample fibers and comparison fibers. These observations support investigating whether the dated region was atypical. [2, 3]
Where we stand  Positive evidence exists for your objection. Neither study measured the replacement fraction needed to produce the medieval date or mapped a complete repair boundary. Rogers also stated that the soluble gum would be removed by the carbon laboratories’ pretreatment; gum alone is not his full explanation.
04  The interior mechanical collection matters
University of Padua engineer Giulio Fanti and colleagues selected eight fibers from filter H, collected from the reverse beneath the buttocks image. This collection is away from the carbon corner. Its ancient estimate therefore deserves consideration in the sampling comparison. [4]
Where we stand  Conceded as a collection-location advantage against a corner-specific repair. Loose fibers can move between cloth and backing. Better location, secure fiber identity and a well-validated dating method are three separate questions.

The image that an artificial account must explain
05  The body image is not ordinary painted linen
The STURP investigations associated the body image with changes in the linen’s cellulose and did not identify an ordinary applied paint medium that explains it. A proposed process has to match the material evidence, not merely make a similar picture. [9]
Where we stand  A substantial constraint on simple painting explanations. This does not mean that no pigment exists anywhere on the cloth, or that every proposed artificial process uses paint.
06  The coloration is remarkably superficial
Microscopic work reports coloration concentrated at very shallow levels of image fibers. A convincing reproduction must address the depth and distribution of coloration as well as the visible outline. [10]
Where we stand  Supported, with measurement limits. Fiber-wall depth, thread penetration and fabric penetration are different quantities. A local microscopic estimate is not a measured statistical distribution for the entire image.
07  The negative appearance is the cherry on top
Reversing the tones reveals a strikingly recognizable human image. You have treated this as an additional constraint rather than the whole case: why would an alleged medieval maker produce such an unusual faint image? [9, 11]
Where we stand  A real visual feature requiring explanation. It need not imply knowledge of photography; some transfer processes can produce negative-like tones. Our research has not established a near-contemporary artwork matching the entire Shroud combination.
08  Anatomy and depth deserve a serious account
Image intensity has been studied in relation to body shape and cloth-to-body distance. Your argument is that the apparent human form and plausible draping are unusually demanding constraints on a medieval manufacture. [11]
Where we stand  There is published work on the relationship. It does not establish a unique, anatomically perfect three-dimensional scan. A fitted head or cloth model imports assumptions; our custom model supplies no independent authentication.
09  No complete manufacture has been agreed upon
A similar-looking reproduction does not settle whether the same process accounts for the chemistry, superficiality, anatomy, tonal behavior and other relevant observations together. [9–12]
Where we stand  Conceded: the literature does not supply an agreed complete medieval production account. “We lack a demonstrated complete method” is defensible; “every artificial method is impossible” exceeds what has been demonstrated.

The newer dating results
10  Several approaches point toward antiquity
FTIR, Raman, mechanical testing and WAXS produced results interpreted by their authors as favoring an ancient cloth. These are affirmative findings to assess, rather than evidence that can be dismissed merely because carbon dating is more established. [4, 7, 8, 13]
Where we stand  Conceded. Agreement across different kinds of measurement is relevant. These were not four independent laboratories testing four securely mapped, pristine locations. FTIR and Raman were reported as corner threads; WAXS used material near the carbon and Raes corner.
11  Agreement needs an explanation if the cloth is medieval
Your challenge is fair: if the cloth is medieval, why do different recent approaches give older estimates? A skeptical account should examine the specific calibrations, sample histories and environmental effects instead of assuming that all these results are simply wrong.
Where we stand  No single demonstrated explanation presently disposes of every ancient estimate in our review. Conversely, related cellulose aging, overlapping reference materials and uncertain histories can make errors dependent. Possible dependence is a concern to test, not proof of a shared error.
12  Published precision is not a universal error curve
The decisive statistical question is how often each method misdates independently dated ancient linen under realistic conditions. That requires external prediction errors, including failures, not only a fitted formula or a few favorable checks.
Where we stand  Our reviewed comparisons are too small and heterogeneous to establish trustworthy far-tail probabilities. A reported mechanical check about 82 years from its reference and selected WAXS checks averaging about 40 years are not validated error distributions for all ancient linen. See the local dating workbook and probability audit.
13  The astronomical odds were not established
Your proposed calculation asks how far AD 1300 lies from each ancient estimate, then multiplies the tail probabilities. This is a valid calculation only under explicit, justified error models and a justified dependence model.
Where we stand  The earlier conditional figure of 9 × 10⁻²⁰¹ is not a defensible actual chance of a medieval cloth. Our leave-one-out mechanical recalculation also produced one error about 1,330 years too old. That is a calculation from published data, not a new lab experiment; it shows why the favorable checks cannot define a universal narrow bell curve.

The cumulative argument and what could change it
14  The relic interpretation has an existing framework
Your Christian explanation is not a religion invented to account for one object. It connects the Shroud to an existing case involving Jesus, Paul and Luke, the disciples’ conviction and courage, the Temple prophecy, the Passion and Resurrection, and philosophical arguments such as Aquinas’s. The relic would act as an anchor joining those lines of thought.
Where we stand  That is a coherent cumulative argument. We have not independently audited every philosophical, prophetic and historical argument mentioned in the chat. Christianity’s cultural importance explains the stakes but does not itself authenticate the cloth.
15  The binary needs a precise definition
The cloth either is the burial cloth of Jesus or it is not. Your preferred comparison is the Christian relic against an exceptionally anomalous medieval manufacture.
Where we stand  The first statement is a true binary. The narrower pair does not exhaust every possible history: for example, an ancient cloth with a later image would fall outside it. Such possibilities are not established explanations, but cloth age, identity and image cause remain distinct questions.
The counterweights remain part of the tower
The medieval carbon result is a real measurement, obtained with controls and cleaning. University of Arizona researcher A. J. Timothy Jull and textile researcher Rachel Freer-Waters reported retained-fragment observations against substantial replacement. Their 2010 and 2026 examinations have limited coverage and share material; they are not two independent surveys of the whole cloth. [1, 5, 14]
Conservator Mechthild Flury-Lemberg argued that a fine repair should leave observable textile traces. Her inspection could not include material already consumed in 1988. Rogers’s attributed fibers and the retained Arizona pieces are not an identical sample set. Their disagreement should be resolved through the actual specimens and observations. [2, 5, 6, 14]
A practical standard for concession
The strongest next test would combine textile mapping, documented custody and independent carbon measurements from several well-separated original areas. Repeated medieval dates from demonstrably original cloth would seriously weaken your dating objection. A reproducible old date from secure original areas, or demonstrated replacement of the old strip, would seriously weaken the current carbon-based medieval case.
Your present judgment favors relic over manufacture pending better sampling. My concession is that the sampling objection is substantive and the combined artifact is demanding to explain. Our research has not established a numerical 98 to 2 result, a fatal invalidation of the 1988 measurement, or a measured joint probability for the newer methods.

Earlier history and the competing art account
These blocks were added after comparing the first draft with the wider local research record. The earlier draft omitted these strands or compressed them too far.
16  Relevant descriptions predate Lirey
The historical inventory contains twelve pre-1350 texts about preserved burial linens or images on cloth. The tenth–eleventh-century Latin Tractatus describes a whole-body impression; Gervase repeats that tradition before 1215. Robert de Clari reports a shroud displaying Christ’s figure in Constantinople before the 1204 sack. These provide possible antecedents for Turin. [15]
Where we stand  A substantial omitted supporting strand. Twelve texts are not twelve independent eyewitnesses or a continuous custody chain. Face-only accounts must remain face-only; the Pray Codex’s proposed Turin connection is disputed. John 20 supplies burial-cloth context without explicitly identifying the Turin image. [15, 16]
17  The medieval allegation deserves equal attention
The 1389–1390 controversy includes Bishop Pierre d’Arcis’s allegation of manufacture and an earlier artisan’s admission. This is documentary evidence favoring a medieval account, not simply a modern speculation based on carbon dating. [17]
Where we stand  It must be weighed rather than omitted. The surviving account is retrospective; it does not supply a named maker’s signed confession or a manufacturing recipe. Its transmission and the papal texts require source criticism. Permission for restricted display was not scientific authentication. [17, 18]
18  A capable workshop is not an identified maker
Your “beyond da Vinci” and “greater than Satoshi” comparisons express the scale of the alleged achievement and the missing attribution. Our art-history research found relevant medieval sculptural skill and large devotional linens, but no surviving medieval counterpart matching the whole Shroud combination.
Where we stand  General medieval incapacity is too strong; an exceptional anonymous maker is historically possible. However, the proposed Évrard d’Orléans connection has no recovered Shroud commission or Charny link. Painted linen comparisons also supply no representative microscopic-depth distribution from which to calculate the Shroud’s rarity. See the local workshop and linen reports.
19  Skilled historical reweaving was real
The CNRS and University of Lorraine’s medieval French dictionary records specialist repair vocabulary. Cotgrave’s 1611 dictionary explicitly includes linen mending. Southampton textile-conservation scholar Frances Lennard documents skilled historical tapestry reweaving; the Poor Clares’ 1534 work provides direct monastic sewing on this cloth. [19–21]
Where we stand  Conceded: sophisticated repair is not a modern invention. We did not establish a documented convent transmission chain or an exact period demonstration of microscopically invisible repair in this fine herringbone. Patches, linings, tapestry repair and replacement of the original weave are different operations.

Preservation and biological provenance
20  Preservation and handling must be interpreted locally
Your point is that a well-preserved textile can still contain worn, contaminated or repaired regions. Fr Andrew Dalton proposes that bare-hand exhibition could have worn the corners. A lining was added in 1534, and later conservation supported the cloth. [6, 21, 26]
Where we stand  Overall condition does not prove uniform original material. The lining date does not establish that every earlier display was unsupported or that handling stopped in 1534. Fire and water marks document exposure, not uniform heat or continuous damp storage. The 1997 rescue involved a protected enclosure, rather than the bare cloth surviving another direct burning.
21  Pollen and DNA are a separate provenance strand
Published botanical and genetic work includes Near Eastern associations. University of the Balearic Islands botanist Marzia Boi interprets pollen as compatible with burial ointments. University of Padua geneticist Gianni Barcaccia and colleagues report geographically mixed DNA. Such findings deserve evaluation as possible evidence of eastern contact. [22–24]
Where we stand  They do not uniquely establish a first-century Palestinian origin. Pollen identification and deposition dates are disputed; mixed DNA includes later contamination. The 2026 study also carbon-dated two loose reliquary threads to repair-era ranges. Those are additional carbon tests, but not new dates of securely located original image-bearing linen. [24]

Forensic pathology and blood evidence
22  The injury pattern and blood add constraints
The relevant discipline is forensic pathology, including the medical interpretation of patterned injuries; bloodstain pattern analysis examines the behavior of the blood marks. The argument concerns the combined distribution of scourge-like marks across the body, head punctures interpreted as thorn injuries, nail-site marks and the side wound. Fr Andrew Dalton cites approximately 360 scourge marks, about 240 dorsal and 120 frontal. A medical reconstruction by Bevilacqua and colleagues refers to more than 370 scourge wounds. These are attributed interpretations and counts, not an agreed exact census of separate injuries. [26–28, 34]
Where we stand  The numerous marks and their distribution raise the explanatory burden for a statue or relief account. A model matching the body outline still needs an account of the detailed wound pattern, blood behavior, materials and sequence. The number of marks alone does not quantify how much less likely a statue becomes: a proposed sculpted source could itself depict wounds, and related marks are not independent diagnoses. Forensic authors also disagree on some anatomical reconstructions. [27, 28]
New England Institute researcher John Heller and Western Connecticut chemist Alan Adler reported blood-related chemistry. Their selected-fiber observations also underpin the inference that blood preceded the body image. A reproduction with surrogate blood added afterward therefore does not settle the original sequence. Selected observations do not establish the composition of every red mark. [25]
22a  Reported AB correspondence with Lanciano
University of Turin forensic physician Pierluigi Baima Bollone and colleagues reported blood group AB on sampled Shroud threads. Their study included unstained Shroud fibers and known-group blood controls. Odoardo Linoli’s 1971 investigation of the Lanciano material also reported group AB in the examined flesh and blood. This is a documented correspondence between reported results, with Lanciano as the specific Eucharistic case used here. [29, 30]
Where we stand  The supported wording is reported AB, not established AB-negative. The cited Shroud study tests the ABO system and does not establish Rh status. Shared ABO type cannot establish a common individual donor. Kearse challenges the specificity of old-material serology because microbes can express A/B-reactive structures; Serafini defends the historical tests and their controls, and Kearse responds. The correspondence remains a conditional supporting observation pending stronger validation, rather than an independently authenticated blood match. [29–33]

Historical continuity and explanatory unity
23  Occam asks about explanatory unity
Your central argument is that one authentic relic could connect many observations to a worldview already defended independently, whereas a manufactured object seems to require an exceptional maker, unusual process and coincidentally old proxy dates. You ask that their combined burden be assessed, rather than resetting each issue in isolation.
Where we stand  Conceded as the right cumulative challenge. A single process explaining several features deserves credit; correlated features cannot be multiplied as independent improbabilities. Neither an unnamed genius nor an omnipotent creator is a complete discriminating explanation by itself. The Christian explanation must remain eligible without a zero prior, and both sides must state which observations their proposed history actually explains.
24  Historical checkpoints predate modern investigation
The Lier painted copy dated 1516 and the Poor Clares’ account of the 1534 repair document the image before modern photography. The copy records the earlier appearance and damage; the repair account describes the face, crossed hands, side wound, scourging and rear image, together with devotional interpretations. These are historical checkpoints for comparison with the surviving cloth. [21, 34]
Where we stand  The basic image and recognizable features existed centuries before Pia photographed the Shroud in 1898. Photography revealed its striking negative-like appearance and made some details easier to recognize; it did not first make the body image visible. This block supports continuity across the documented period. Its reach does not extend by itself to a first-century date or to identifying an earlier ambiguous relic.

Modern miracles and the return to the Shroud
25  Modern Christian miracles can change the starting assessment
Your starting position includes the belief that several modern Christian miracles are already well supported. On that understanding, a supernatural Shroud belongs to a world in which divine action has relevant precedents. Those conclusions are proposed evidence for the background framework, not merely arbitrary personal preferences.
Where we stand  Conceded as a legitimate cumulative argument. Evidence from a modern event must be allowed to change the assessment of another event. The record does not yet contain an agreed finding that Fátima, Zeitoun or Pio has a supernatural cause. Their proposed common Christian explanation remains eligible.
26  A public promise was connected to belief in the apparition
The July 14 and September 27 records precede October 13 and explicitly link the promised sign with people believing the apparition. Your claim is that a public fulfillment corroborates the reported speaker and her message. It is stronger than an unexplained occurrence with a religious interpretation added afterward. [35]
Where we stand  Conceded that the evidentiary purpose is explicit in the pre-event record. The date and place matter even though a detailed description of every later solar effect was not supplied in advance. You argue that this promise was sufficiently specific to make the subsequent event a strong fulfillment; that comparative inference remains disputed.
27  The public reports have force without the photographs
Your independent historical case combines the promise, a gathering of tens of thousands, contemporary newspaper reports including secular or anticlerical outlets, and testimony from geographically separated observers. The attendance estimate is not a count of separately preserved depositions. [36] [41]
Where we stand  Conceded that the historical case survives a failed photographic reconstruction. Images can add support, but they are not a required condition for a historical miracle inference. Newspaper publication establishes a contemporary public record; each article’s firsthand status and substantive observations still need assessment.
28  Distant descent reports add a distinct constraint
Portuguese records distinguish Inácio Lourenço’s Alburitel school group and Joaquim Lourenço’s hill group. Both describe apparent descent; Haffert’s published English interview with Albano Barros near Minde also describes a fall in the direction of Fátima. These localities are about 13 km from the Cova. A next-day letter reports apparent rotation at Granja, about 157 km away. [38] [39] [40] [45]
Where we stand  Conceded that interaction within the Cova crowd cannot by itself explain experiences elsewhere. Geographical separation is established more securely than numerical convergence of sightlines. The clearest distant descent narratives retrieved are later recollections; most records lack measured bearings. The Minde wording remains verified only in the English interview publication.
29  Variation in experience does not defeat a common event
Your common event is a luminous phenomenon, called the Sun by observers, behaving extraordinarily. Some observers describe rotation, others descent, colors or changes in landscape illumination; some report religious figures. Your claim does not require everyone to have the same experience or see every phase.
Where we stand  Conceded that omission of a feature is not a denial of it. Accounts must be tested for meaningful compatibility under different viewpoints and attention, rather than identical wording or experiences. Individual religious visions and publicly described light effects remain distinguishable claims within the same reported occasion.
30  The physical corroboration deserves its own assessment
Dalleur infers a light source elevation of about 25–32 degrees, against a solar elevation of 42.7 degrees, a discrepancy of about 11–18 degrees with a central estimate near 14.5. Witnesses describe comfortable viewing of a pale disc, and many accounts describe extraordinary drying after rain. These would be physical constraints beyond crowd excitement if established as interpreted. [36] [37] [43]
Where we stand  Conceded that these are relevant positive arguments, not disposable because the event is religious. The photograph does not itself measure a dive. The source reconstruction and alleged drying pattern remain disputed inferences; comfortable viewing and absence of identified injury reports are not a clinical demonstration of no retinal injury throughout the crowd.
31  A possible natural cause must earn explanatory weight
You argue that a natural account must address the advance promise and the combined pattern, not explain one visual feature and declare the whole event settled. The remaining natural contender combines viewing conditions, visual effects, expectation and later recollection. Pinto Coelho’s contemporary comparison gives it some positive support for colors and rotation. [42]
Where we stand  The assistant conceded that it has not demonstrated a natural explanation convincingly covering the complete record or shown natural alternatives to outweigh the cumulative case. It also retained the distinction between a plausible incomplete natural hypothesis and no plausible hypothesis. Daniel judges miracle to meet a preponderance standard; the assistant remains uncertain about whether that threshold has been crossed.
32  Independent cases must accumulate toward concession
One sufficiently strong event could justify a supernatural conclusion. Several independently supported events can jointly justify it even when each is individually inconclusive. A rule that resets every case to zero after naming a possible natural cause prevents the cumulative argument from ever being heard.
Where we stand  Conceded as the governing rule. There is no fixed required number and no permanent veto based on mere natural possibility. Shared sources, selection and causal dependence must still be considered. The next inquiry is Zeitoun, then Pio; older claims such as Lourdes and Joseph of Cupertino follow before returning to the Shroud with the revised background assessment. [44]
The religious distinction and the next inquiry
Daniel distinguishes explicit authentication of a Catholic message at Fatima from silent appearances at the Coptic Orthodox church in Zeitoun. Together with Pio, he treats these as proposed evidence for supernatural action within apostolic Christianity. The targeted comparison now completed is recorded in block 33; it did not establish the universal uniqueness claim.
Modern public claims are to be assessed before older claims, then carried back into the Shroud comparison without double counting. Joseph of Cupertino is a pre-photography case. Lourdes began in 1858, after photography existed, so it belongs to an earlier evidentiary setting rather than a strictly pre-camera category. None of those older cases has been adjudicated by this addition.

33 Comparable modern miracle claims require comparable evidence
The comparison strengthens the cumulative argument. The targeted search of modern Hindu, Jewish, Buddhist, Muslim and Sikh cases did not establish an evidential equivalent to the complete Fatima, Zeitoun and Padre Pio combination. It also did not establish a Jewish counterpart to Zeitoun's reported repeated mass visibility. Lists of other religions' miracles therefore do not, by themselves, answer this cumulative case. The search did find serious public reports and published bodily observations outside Christianity; their strengths and weaknesses must be compared individually.
Daniel's proposed explanation. The cases contribute different kinds of evidence. Fatima joins private Marian messages to an advance announcement and a public event. Zeitoun supplies reported repeated public Marian appearances across 1968-1971. Pio supplies bodily signs and fragrance reports across roughly fifty years, with identifiable medical examinations and close observers. Daniel argues that this diversity, persistence and religious coherence support intentional supernatural action within apostolic Christianity. A competing explanation must address the strongest supported features of each case, rather than dismiss all three because similar stories exist somewhere else.
The concession this search warrants. The assistant has not produced an equally documented modern counterpart to the combined case, or established an adequate ordinary explanation of these Christian events. A small-circle levitation story does not answer decades of public exposure; a large following is not a count of close observers of a miracle. Nor does a documented bodily anomaly in another tradition automatically explain away a Christian apparition. The same evidential standards apply in both directions.
The wording the evidence supports. The supported finding is that this targeted search did not establish a comparably documented match. The universal statement that zero other religions or worldviews have any comparable event has not been established by this bounded search. Pio's near-constant miraculous state is Daniel's interpretation of persistent signs and recurring reports; fragrance was not measured continuously for fifty years. Fatima and Pio are Roman Catholic cases, while Zeitoun is Coptic Orthodox. These settings fit his broader apostolic-Christian formulation, but their institutional setting alone does not establish exclusive doctrinal vindication or falsify Protestantism. The argument for divine agency and religious meaning remains explicit, rather than being silently converted into a measured fact.

34 Bernadette Soubirous as the fourth pillar
Bernadette Soubirous is the fourth pillar of Daniel's cumulative argument, alongside Fatima, Zeitoun and Padre Pio. Her contribution here is the medically recorded preservation of her body. The earlier Lourdes apparitions remain a separate historical inquiry.
Bernadette died on 16 April 1879, aged 35. Doctors David and Jourdan examined her remains on 22 September 1909, thirty years later. Doctors Talon and Raoul Comte examined them on 3 April 1919 and again on 18 April 1925, forty-six years and two days after death. Comte, a surgeon in Nevers, reported preserved muscles and an unexpectedly soft liver when he removed tissue in 1925. Andre Ravier's archival account reproduces these medical observations; the original Comte report was published in the Lourdes medical association's May 1928 bulletin, pp.33-46.
The reports also describe shrinkage, rigidity, discoloration and some skin loss. The claim is enduring preservation, including internal tissue, rather than unchanged living appearance throughout the body. Wax coverings were added to the face and hands for display after the examination findings; those coverings cannot explain the earlier findings.
Daniel argues that the four cases reinforce one explanation of purposeful supernatural Christian action through different evidence: the announced public sign at Fatima, repeated public Marian appearances at Zeitoun, Pio's sustained bodily signs and fragrance reports, and Bernadette's postmortem preservation. Bernadette adds a distinct evidentiary strand; it does not require her Lourdes apparition account to have been independently established first.
The cited Tibetan case reports delayed decomposition over thirty-seven days, ending with deterioration. It has not supplied an equivalent record of enduring preservation. This comparison favors Bernadette on duration and repeated medical examination. The cumulative causal inference remains an argument to assess; adding this pillar assigns no numerical odds or automatic debate point.
