SHROUD REASSESSMENT AFTER THE MARIAN INVESTIGATION
4 November 2026

PURPOSE AND STARTING POINT

Daniel asked for a new audit of the Shroud after our investigation of Fatima,
Zeitoun and Lourdes favored intentional Marian intervention among the
explanations actually developed and examined. The fourth case, Padre Pio,
supports the broader Christian-intervention argument but is not a Marian
apparition. The local cumulative statement records the exact scope of that
assessment. This report carries it forward instead of restarting with a
presumption that Christian intervention has negligible credibility.

That is the conclusion of this investigation, not a claim that the wider
scholarly community has established Marian intervention by consensus. The
relevant apparitions and seer history principally concern the nineteenth and
twentieth centuries; the present research and some analyses are later.

REVISED JUDGMENT

The Marian findings increase the credibility of a Christian supernatural
interpretation of the Shroud. Authenticity therefore deserves more weight
than in our initial discussion. An argument that implicitly excludes Christian
intervention cannot serve as a neutral assessment after reaching that Marian
conclusion. The image's religious character and technical constraints now
have a more credible proposed supernatural context.

The surviving material evidence still does not warrant a confident finding
that this particular cloth is Jesus's burial cloth, or that its image was
formed at the Resurrection. The medieval radiocarbon result remains strong
direct chronological evidence for the sampled material. Its extension to the
whole cloth has a substantive, unresolved representativeness dispute. My
assessment is increased confidence in authenticity relative to the initial
discussion, with the overall artifact question unresolved. If asked only to
rank the currently measured chronological evidence, I still give the AMS
result more weight than the aging-proxy dates. That narrower ranking is not
a demonstration of a medieval image-making process.

No numerical posterior has been established. This audit neither maintains
confidence unchanged nor invents a percentage to express its change.

HOW THE BACKGROUND EVIDENCE CHANGES THE ARGUMENT

1. Christian intervention is a live, positively supported causal category in
   the framework reached here. It should not be introduced only after every
   imaginable ordinary process has been excluded.
2. Existing Christian historical and philosophical arguments can contribute
   to that background. Their full validity has not all been audited here.
3. The same religious framework can connect Marian events, the Passion and
   an authentic Shroud. Explanatory unity is a strength of the proposed case.
4. That connection is not a guarantee that every attributed Christian relic
   is genuine. Christian devotion also gives ordinary makers a reason to
   depict Christ; religious subject matter alone is a weak discriminator.
5. Accepting one Christian intervention is not the same as accepting a
   specific physical history for every Christian object. The additional
   claims still make predictions about this cloth's age, custody, material
   composition, stains and image.

In probability terms, evidence for Christian intervention can change the
background odds of an authentic Resurrection relic. It does not by itself
measure the chance that the 1988 strip is replacement material. That chance
must be assessed using the sample evidence, potentially together with other
relevant Shroud observations. Nor can the Marian evidence be counted once
as a changed prior and again as an independent new Shroud measurement.

ARGUMENT AUDIT

1. THREE LABORATORIES, ONE REGION — SUSTAINED SAMPLING OBJECTION

Arizona, Oxford and Zurich made separate laboratory measurements, but their
Shroud material came from one strip. These are independent laboratory checks
of that region, not three geographically independent checks of the whole
cloth. Multiple labs reduce some laboratory-specific error risks; they do
not eliminate a shared material-selection error.

The original report documents controls, microscopy and differing cleaning
procedures, and reports AD 1260–1390 at its stated confidence level. It is
incorrect both to describe the protocol as careless testing of uncleaned
dirt and to say that the shared-region objection is answered by three labs.
The report does not directly date image formation.

2. REPAIRS AND THE DATED STRIP — POSITIVE EVIDENCE, UNRESOLVED CONFLICT

Documented fire repairs, patches and linings establish that this textile has
human additions. Historical skilled reweaving is real. They make the repair
question historically grounded, but do not identify the missing dated strip
as a particular later replacement.

Los Alamos chemist Raymond Rogers reported cotton, dye/gum coatings and
chemical differences in Raes material and threads attributed to the carbon
strip. It is a mistake to reduce his evidence to nearby Raes threads alone.
His paper says that attributed carbon-strip threads were supplied by Gonella.
The custody attribution and small specimen set remain relevant limitations.
Rogers's explanation concerns replacement material, not merely soluble gum
that escaped pretreatment; he says that gum would have been removed.

Textile researcher Rachel Freer-Waters and University of Arizona radiocarbon
researcher A. J. Timothy Jull report compatible flax, weave, yarn twist and
thread count in retained fragments. Their 2026 work found no cotton in the
areas examined, and SEM of about 2 by 1.5 mm of A1A did not reveal substantial
surface contamination. They expressly acknowledge that the small SEM sample
may not represent the entirety of either fragment. This is actual material
counterevidence, rather than a general appeal to carbon dating's reputation.

Matching linen and weave weigh against certain mixed-material repair claims;
they cannot alone exclude every carefully matched linen repair whose join
falls outside the retained pieces. Conversely, coatings/cotton observations
do not map or quantify the entire consumed strip. Neither side has supplied
a conclusive spatial reconstruction of the proposed replacement.

The saved isotope-mixing calculation illustrates the scale required: with
AD 30 original linen and sixteenth-century replacement carbon, approximately
three-quarters of the measured residual carbon must be replacement in the
chosen examples. That is not a measured repair fraction. Small stray-fiber
contamination should not be substituted for the substantial-replacement claim.

3. AN INTERIOR COLLECTION — A LOCATION ADVANTAGE, NOT SECURE ATTACHMENT

Padua engineer Giulio Fanti and colleagues used selected loose fibers from
an interior vacuum collection, away from the carbon corner. This reduces
exposure to the particular corner-repair hypothesis if the fibers originated
in the collection area. Loose fibers collected between cloth and backing
can have moved. Collection location, original fiber attachment and reliability
of the dating calibration remain separate evidential questions.

WAXS did not independently test a secure center-body location: the published
sample was a tiny thread near the Raes/radiocarbon corner. FTIR and Raman
corner-thread descriptions do not provide four securely separated sites.

4. FOUR AGING-PROXY DATES — CONVERGENCE COUNTS; MULTIPLIED ODDS DO NOT

FTIR, Raman, mechanical measurements and WAXS are different observations and
their ancient-compatible interpretations deserve positive weight. They are
not four independent laboratories with four independently validated calendar
clocks. Some reference textiles, investigators and cellulose-aging assumptions
overlap. Dependence is not proof that their results are wrong; it prevents
assuming independent complete error distributions without evidence.

The saved review found four non-anchor WAXS checks against carbon references,
with central differences of 10, 40, 0 and 110 years: mean absolute difference
40 years. This is favorable agreement in one small published set, not an
established universal 40-year standard deviation.

The mechanical paper's selected withheld reference differs by about 82 years.
Our earlier reproducible leave-one-out calculation, using the rounded published
reference data, also withheld an AD 575 reference and predicted approximately
758 BC: about 1,330 years too old. That is our reanalysis, not a new independent
laboratory experiment or a published universal failure rate. It is a concrete
reason not to treat the selected 82-year success or a narrow Monte Carlo
propagation interval as general real-world prediction accuracy.

For WAXS, Liberato De Caro and colleagues at Italy's CNR Institute of
Crystallography, with Fanti at Padua, explicitly condition the ancient-age
interpretation on historical temperature and humidity. Their calculations
argue against an ordinary seven-century history under their model. A rebuttal
must engage that model, not merely say heat might matter. Nevertheless, the
model and its applicability do not have a large independent validation set
establishing its extreme probability tails for the Shroud.

The previously discussed approximately 9 x 10^-201 joint number is not an
actual probability that the cloth is medieval. The Marian conclusion does
not repair the missing statistical validation, covariance or alternative-
hypothesis likelihoods. The convergence remains evidence without that number.

5. IMAGE CHEMISTRY AND SUPERFICIALITY — A SERIOUS MANUFACTURING CONSTRAINT

Los Alamos researchers Larry Schwalbe and Raymond Rogers summarized STURP
evidence as favoring cellulose oxidation/dehydration rather than an applied
pigment as the body-image explanation. This conflicts with microscopist
Walter McCrone's interpretation; it is not unanimity about every fiber.
The existence of pigment somewhere is different from demonstrating that
pigment produces the main image.

A candidate process must address shallow fiber coloration, spatial pattern,
chemical behavior and the relationship of body image to red marks. Matching
the photograph alone is insufficient. Claims that every later proposed
process has been excluded, or that no laboratory can reproduce any relevant
property, go beyond this evidence.

6. NEGATIVE TONES, THREE-DIMENSIONAL CORRELATION AND REPLICAS

The striking negative-like appearance is real. Jackson at Kaman Sciences,
Jumper at the Air Force Institute of Technology and Ercoline at the US Air
Force Academy published body-distance/shading work that deserves more weight
than dismissing it as merely software producing a relief. Their 1984 paper
also considers candidate mechanisms and identifies limitations. It does not
demonstrate a unique anatomically perfect recovered body or prove identity.

University of Pavia chemist Luigi Garlaschelli's physical replica demonstrates
that some negative-like and relief effects can be produced without knowledge
of photographic technology. It is a partial achievement, not a complete
match to the original. Fanti and Heimburger's published critique addresses
fiber coloring and other differences. The correct retained manufacturing
obligation concerns the conjunction of features, not just one visual effect.

Fresh check: Archaeometry published Casabianca, Marinelli and Piana's 2026
critique of Cicero Moraes's digital low-relief study, alongside his response.
The critique disputes its adequacy; Moraes, affiliated with Arc-Team Brazil,
defends a limited methodological purpose centered on morphological deformation.
Neither abstract establishes a successful complete material reconstruction.
The original Moraes article was read in the earlier investigation. Only the
publisher abstracts and reference lists of the 2026 exchange were retrieved
in this pass; do not represent their full arguments as newly read in full.

7. INJURIES AND BLOOD — SUPPORTIVE COMBINATION, NOT A MEASURED AUTOPSY

The detailed distribution of injury-like and blood-related marks adds a
substantive burden to an artificial account. A body-outline reconstruction
does not account for all those marks, chemistry, placement and sequence.
The medical literature also contains disagreements among authenticity
proponents, and selected blood-transfer simulations on uninjured volunteers
show that certain patterns do not logically require an actually injured
model. Those simulations do not reproduce the whole Shroud.

Our earlier audit did not establish that a maker necessarily needed an
actually tortured and crucified person, or that each proposed diagnosis is
independently certain. The affirmative combined-pattern argument survives
without asserting those stronger claims.

8. HISTORY AND THE MISSING MAKER — BOTH DIRECTIONS RETAIN EVIDENCE

Earlier whole-body cloth-image descriptions and Robert de Clari's report
are serious potential antecedents. They should not be discarded merely
because the securely identifiable Turin custody record begins at medieval
Lirey. They do not yet provide an unbroken identification and transfer chain.
The Gospel burial-cloth references supply historical and theological context,
but do not explicitly describe this object's body image.

The medieval allegations of manufacture also survive the Marian update.
University of Turin historian Andrea Nicolotti's source treatment discusses
the d'Arcis material and the newly published Oresme text. These are relevant
historical counterevidence, not laboratory proof or a surviving named maker's
signed confession. Manuscript criticism by Emmanuel Poulle remains part of
the dispute. A credible Christian worldview does not imply every medieval
Christian accusation was false or every relic claim was true.

Our archival and workshop work recovered no Shroud commission, named maker,
payment, tool, mold or exact technical precedent. That leaves the artistic
account incomplete. Bounded archive searches do not establish that no such
record ever existed or that an anonymous maker was impossible. Relevant
sculptural skill existed; no recovered comparison matches the full object.

9. FANTI'S RADIOACTIVITY PROPOSAL — DISTINCT FROM REPLACEMENT

Fanti's reply to Daniel proposed additional C14 generated from nitrogen.
His 2024 paper includes preliminary radiation observations and controls,
but does not isotope-identify the reported beta activity as newly produced
C14 in purified original linen, determine its spatial distribution or
calculate a corrected age for the 1988 strip. Christian intervention makes
a supernatural origin more credible in the framework, but does not supply
these missing measurements.

Repair and irradiation are different proposals. One cannot assume a secure
original sample when convenient and a replaced sample when inconvenient
without a consistent material account. A neutron-production model has its
own quantitative and spatial predictions; it can be tested.

WHAT CHANGES AND WHAT DOES NOT

Changes: the credibility of Christian agency; the comparative seriousness
of a relic explanation; the explanatory unity of an authentic Passion relic
with the wider intervention case. It is inappropriate to demand that the
Shroud establish the existence of Christian intervention entirely by itself
after the other cases have been accepted as evidence for that possibility.

Unchanged observations: the measured corner dates; Rogers's reported material
differences; retained-fragment counterevidence; sample custody limitations;
the proxy methods' limited external calibration; the body-image constraints;
the lack of a complete physical replication or recovered maker's commission.

The interpretation can change while those observations remain fixed. Both
relic and manufacture hypotheses need the complete record. The true binary
is Jesus's burial cloth versus not Jesus's burial cloth. The narrower pair,
Resurrection imprint versus fourteenth-century artistic manufacture, does
not exhaust possible cloth/image histories. This logical point supplies no
positive evidence for a third history by itself.

HIGHEST-VALUE NEXT TEST

Separate two historical tests rather than request another undifferentiated date:

A. Test whether the 1988 region represents the main linen, through authenticated
   retained-fragment chemical/textile mapping and AMS measurements of securely
   original material from multiple separated areas. Pre-register sampling and
   pretreatment, include controls and release raw data and specimen photographs.
B. Test the image process through blinded microchemical and optical comparisons
   of proposed replicas against an agreed observational set. A similar outline
   and a failed complete replica answer different questions.

Concordant ancient dates from securely original separated linen would remove
the strongest direct chronological obstacle to authenticity, while leaving
identity and image formation to the other evidence. Concordant medieval dates
from such material would seriously weaken authenticity, even in a world where
Christian miracles occur. If irradiation is proposed, require independent
isotope-specific/spatial predictions rather than declaring every inconvenient
result compatible after it is obtained.
