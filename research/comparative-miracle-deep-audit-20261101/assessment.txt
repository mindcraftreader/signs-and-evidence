COMPARATIVE MIRACLE AUDIT: NON-APOSTOLIC TRADITIONS
Research assessment, 1 November 2026

CURRENT JUDGMENT

This review has not established a non-Apostolic counterpart to Fatima or Zeitoun with comparable combined public documentation. The strongest cases newly inspected are mostly individual clinical, bodily or information claims. Several deserve serious investigation. They cannot fairly be dismissed as anonymous legends, and their limitations are not automatically ordinary explanations.

The defensible comparative conclusion is bounded: no equally strong competing public religious sign has been established in the cases reviewed. A claim that every other religion has been exhaustively searched, or that none has any serious miracle evidence, would exceed the work done. There are still substantial language, archive and access gaps.

This is a deeper audit than the earlier candidate lists. Ten research papers or critical analyses were read through their substantive full text, alongside original clinical-report documents, selected book/thesis sections and a medical appendix. Some other cases retain only the earlier screening. This is not equivalent to a week of investigation into every candidate. Reading scope is recorded separately; unreviewed downloads are not evidence of completed investigation.

HOW THE COMPARISON WORKS

SCOPE CLARIFICATION FROM DANIEL, 1 NOVEMBER 2026
For this comparative argument, healing cases belong in the private-miracle category, including medically documented recoveries or recoveries that receive publicity. Daniel allows such miracles across religious contexts. They are not competing public/vindicatory signs merely because the recipient or person praying belongs to another religion. Retain their medical research as background, but give them no weight as counterexamples to the narrower public-sign claim. The Baptist recovery, Mozambique prayer-healing series, Jewish/Sikh healing leads and Tenrikyo recovery therefore do not answer that claim. This is the agreed comparison scope, not a finding that every reported healing is authentic.

The relevant questions are what happened, how it was recorded, which alternative explanations have actually been tested, and what religious conclusion the event supports. These questions apply equally to Catholic, Coptic, Protestant and non-Christian reports.

A competitor does not have to reproduce Fatima's exact features. Repeated observations can compensate for the absence of an advance announcement. Conversely, a large gathering does not establish how many people closely observed the claimed anomaly. A clinical recovery may be powerful evidence for an individual healing while remaining outside the agreed public/vindicatory comparison.

For public religious signs, we examine advance records where relevant, contemporary firsthand reporting, independence of testimony, repeated opportunities for inspection, visual records, investigations and explicit religious content. For clinical cases, we examine the original diagnosis, actual before/after measurements, treatment, timing and follow-up. A university affiliation establishes a researcher's identity and relevant competence; it does not settle causation.

Three distinctions are essential:
* A source gap or possible explanation is not a demonstrated disproof.
* A documented unusual event does not by itself identify its cause.
* An event occurring in a religious setting does not necessarily authenticate every doctrine of that religion. The asserted message or purpose matters.

No numerical miracle probabilities or new debate points are assigned. The existing 9-0 tally concerns the objections actually adjudicated in the conversation, not a statistical estimate or a global comparative result.

1. BAPTIST VISION RECOVERY: THE STRONGEST NEW INDIVIDUAL CHRISTIAN CLINICAL FILE

Clarissa Romez and colleagues include Kenn Freedman of Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center's Ophthalmology department and Joshua W. Brown of Indiana University's Psychological and Brain Sciences department, alongside Global Medical Research Institute affiliations. Their case report describes a woman with recorded bilateral foveal atrophy and juvenile macular degeneration in 1960 and severe impairment again in 1971-72. She reports immediate restored vision after her Baptist pastor husband prayed at home in 1972. The earliest retained objective post-recovery measurement is June 1974: unaided 20/100 in both eyes. Later corrected acuity reached 20/30-20/40. The specific Stargardt diagnosis is inferred, not genetically established. [1]

The supplementary document includes actual redacted historical record images, not just modern retellings. I inspected full-resolution pages containing the 1960, 1971, 1972/1974 and 2001 records; the remaining pages were inspected only in a contact sheet. This strengthens the evidence for real longstanding disease and substantial subsequent recovery. It does not provide an eye examination at the instant of prayer. [2]

Assessment: a substantial recovery case with genuine medical documentation, deserving more weight than an unsupported testimonial. No adequate ordinary explanation was established in this audit. The delay before the first preserved post-recovery measurement limits the independently measured timing and magnitude of the claimed immediate restoration. Organic disease makes dismissal as pure imaginary blindness inadequate; it does not logically exclude every mixed diagnosis. These are limits on inference, not proof of fraud or of a particular ordinary cause.

The investigators address objections concerning the unaffected identical twin and variable disease onset; those objections do not simply dispose of the case. Their answers establish diagnostic compatibility, rather than genetic confirmation. [3]

Religious consequence: this is an individual Christian healing claim in a non-Apostolic setting. It is not a public rival to Fatima or Zeitoun, and Daniel already allows that individual healings may occur outside the Catholic Church. It therefore does not refute that narrower position.

2. MOZAMBIQUE PROXIMAL PRAYER STUDY: ACTUAL TESTS, LIMITED CAUSAL IDENTIFICATION

Candy Gunther Brown, an Indiana University Religious Studies professor, and colleagues Stephen Mory, Rebecca Williams and Michael McClymond studied Charismatic Protestant prayer in Mozambique in 2009. This is Candy Brown, not Joshua Brown from the preceding case. Twenty-four consecutive participants reported hearing and/or vision impairment. After three hearing exclusions for false-positive responses, hearing analysis involved eleven individuals and eighteen ears; vision testing involved eleven people. The study reported improved hearing (p<.003) and vision (p<.02), including some large changes. [4]

The study was unblinded and lacked a control group. Diagnoses were not established by full specialist examinations. Testing occurred amid considerable background noise, and hearing was generally tested at a single frequency. Retesting prioritized those reporting improvement; two unretested participants were counted unimproved. Long-term follow-up was extremely limited. The authors themselves discuss learning, expectation and other possible influences. [4]

Assessment: these are actual before/after performance measurements, not just people saying they felt better. But a before/after significance test does not identify the cause of improvement or demonstrate structural regeneration. No ordinary mechanism was demonstrated to account for every measured change. Equally, the reported p-values are not probabilities that God did or did not intervene. The study supplies a research lead, not established Protestant doctrinal vindication.

3. SAI BABA: A SERIOUS PERSONAL-MIRACLE COMPARATOR, NOT AN AUTOMATICALLY DEBUNKED ONE

University of Iceland psychologist Erlendur Haraldsson and University of Hertfordshire psychologist Richard Wiseman examined the disputed 1992 video in their 1995 paper. The authors obtained the footage and arranged specialist image enhancement. They concluded that it did not clearly show a chain being transferred: a transfer could have occurred in an occluded interval, but the footage did not unequivocally prove fraud. Nor did it establish materialization. I read the archived paper through OCR; I did not independently analyze the original video. [5]

Haraldsson's own account describes repeated firsthand investigations, object productions, alleged dematerialization, healings and bilocation. His directly observed objects and named witnesses make this more than one visitor's isolated story. Sai Baba declined the requested controlled experiments. The daughter with a heart condition, guestbook-based bilocation chronology and disappearing ring have different evidential limitations; they are not interchangeable controlled demonstrations. [6]

Assessment: the earlier characterization of this entire corpus as a handful of easily dismissed incidents was insufficient. It is a relevant comparison to the type of claims associated with Pio. However, an enormous following is not an audited number of close observers, and no comparable medical/examination dossier or comprehensive exclusion of concealment has been established here. I have not demonstrated ordinary explanations for the complete Sai Baba corpus. I have also not established his supernatural claims or the truth of his theology. The disputed video alone cannot honestly do either job.

4. TIBETAN TUKDAM: SUBSTANTIVE PRESERVATION REPORTS, DIFFERENT CLAIMS ABOUT CONSCIOUSNESS

Tawni Tidwell, a biocultural anthropologist and Tibetan medical practitioner at the University of Wisconsin-Madison's Center for Healthy Minds, reports a 2021 case followed for thirty-seven days after death. Pandemic restrictions delayed formal observation until day seven. Daily visual documentation and attendant observations reported prolonged suppleness and limited decomposition, followed eventually by drying, discoloration, odor and other changes. Infrared readings did not confirm the heart-region warmth perceived by touch. The paper explicitly situates these events within Buddhist religious meaning. [7]

Assessment: this is a real research report of unusual preservation, not proof of permanent incorruptibility. No matched decomposition baseline or full biochemical/microbiological explanation was established. Neither 'a corpse was preserved' nor 'there was no control' settles supernatural causation. The raw daily photo/video series has not been inspected here.

A separate Wisconsin-associated EEG investigation led by Dylan Lott recorded thirteen postmortem cases, with seven usable datasets. The earliest recording was twenty-six hours after death. No recognizable EEG activity or evoked responses were detected. Living participants showed that the equipment could record signals. [8]

Assessment: this does not establish a living postmortem brain. It also does not disprove delayed decomposition or settle every philosophical account of consciousness. The preservation and EEG claims must remain separate. No Fatima/Zeitoun-level public sign is established by these studies.

5. SUFI PIERCING: A REAL CLINICAL PAPER, WITH A SUBSTANTIVE COUNTERLITERATURE

Howard Hall of Case Western Reserve University's Division of Behavioral Pediatrics and colleagues reported a skewer passing through both cheeks, radiologically documented. The published abstract describes closure/healing on one side within two minutes and three-quarter closure on the other after eight hours, with reported absence of pain. Immunological results were unchanged; EEG findings were inconclusive. The full 2001 clinical paper remains unrecovered, despite finding its indexed record and an author's later chapter. This case therefore remains incompletely audited. [9]

Hall's own account presents the practice in an explicitly Islamic/Sufi framework involving Allah and the sheikh; it should not be stripped of religious meaning merely to protect a Christian comparison. [10]

Independent Vienna parapsychology researcher W. Peter Mulacz gives a firsthand counteranalysis of different Sufi demonstrations. He describes observing roughly a hundred piercings, prior scars, skin contraction during withdrawal and minor internal bruising. He distinguishes closure of a narrow tract from regeneration of destroyed tissue. His discussion cites older Swiss medical observations, autopsy findings and animal experiments concerning Mirin Dajo; those underlying papers were not independently recovered here. [11]

Assessment: this is substantive contrary observation, stronger than an appeal to incredulity. It attacks claims of complete invulnerability and instant scarless healing. But different demonstrations cannot automatically settle the later clinical cheek experiment. Neither Mulacz's interpretation nor Hall's supernatural interpretation may be treated as a completed experimental finding for every case. These claims warrant a serious individual-phenomenon comparison; they do not yet establish a rival public vindicatory sign or explain Pio's chronic lesions.

6. PRAHLAD JANI: NAMED MEDICAL OBSERVERS, UNVERIFIED LIFELONG CLAIM

Sterling Hospital neurologist Sudhir Shah, identified in the 2003 report as an associate professor of neurology at K. M. School of PGMR, and physicians Urman Dhruv and V. N. Shah issued a detailed report claiming monitored absence of intake/excretion during a ten-calendar-day hospital observation. The report contains laboratory values. Between November 12 and 20, sodium rose from 139.8 to 155.9 mmol/L and blood urea from 33 to 77 mg/dL; hemoglobin and hematocrit also rose. Thus an account of entirely unchanged physiology would misrepresent their own data. Their conclusions explicitly do not verify decades without nourishment. [12]

The 2010 statement of the Defence Institute of Physiology and Allied Sciences, led by director G. Ilavazhagan with Sterling clinicians, reports fifteen days of observation and video monitoring, with no intake or excretion. It records gargling and bathing from day five. It proposes adaptation to starvation/water restriction and describes further analyses still underway. These are investigator claims in an institutional update, not the final analysis of an independently audited closed fluid balance. [13]

Assessment: dismissing this as merely an unsourced guru story would be inaccurate. Named clinical observers and published primary reports exist. Their full surveillance record, detailed protocol and complete final analysis have not been inspected. Gargling/bathing is a documented potential measurement problem, not proof he secretly drank. The changing lab values require interpretation rather than suppression. No adequate complete ordinary explanation or lifelong supernatural nourishment has been established here. Jani's religious interpretation remains a claim, distinct from the institute's physiological hypothesis.

7. REINCARNATION: THE STRONGEST NON-CHRISTIAN INFORMATION-CLAIM CORPUS INSPECTED

University of Virginia psychiatrist Ian Stevenson and Sri Lankan investigator Godwin Samararatne's 1988 paper reports three cases with statements recorded before family meetings and verification. This reduces the specific problem of families improving the story after meeting. The children's accounts contain particular matches and also errors. Some additional statements were recorded after an investigator had already located the candidate family, although before the families met. Thus 'before family contact' and 'before any investigator knew the answer' are not identical conditions. The parents had some prior connections to the relevant towns; the investigators found no actual information transfer. [14]

Assessment: these are significantly better than wholly retrospective anecdotes. They cannot fairly be rejected on the assumption that every case was written down only after identification. At the same time, unblinded interviewing, partial prior geographic connections, retrospective parental recollection and flexible matching leave questions that the prior-record design does not entirely remove. None of those possibilities has been demonstrated as the complete explanation of the three cases. Nor do the data supply an empirically calibrated probability of reincarnation.

Data note: visual inspection of printed p.236 confirms an apparent internal table error for Iranga. Forty-three total statements minus three unverifiable would leave forty verifiable, and thirty-seven correct plus three incorrect also gives forty; the printed verifiable row instead says forty-three. The reported 92% is approximately 37/40, not 37/43. This is not itself a disproof of the case, but the table should not be imported uncritically into probability calculations.

Haraldsson and Samararatne's 1999 paper examines three children claiming monk lives. One child's statements appeared in a newspaper before contact with the candidate monastery. The paper also preserves failed recognitions, a wrong hospital-death claim, reinterpretation of 'radio' as a gramophone and tentative identity matching. The authors were themselves cautious. One child came from a Roman Catholic family. [15]

Stevenson's 1993 birthmark paper reports forty-nine cases with relevant medical documents, forty-three judged to correspond and six not. Physical marks and records are meaningful evidence, but most families were previously acquainted or related. The author's illustrative chance calculation assumes a simplified spatial model; it is not a validated probability of reincarnation after selection and matching. [16]

Assessment: there is a serious investigative literature here, not a zero-evidence category. These studies neither authenticate all reincarnation stories nor establish an entire Hindu or Buddhist metaphysical system. I have not established reincarnation as the best explanation or produced ordinary accounts for every retained case. This is one of the best directions for a further competing-metaphysics investigation. It is not a public-apparition counterpart, and cases across different religious backgrounds should not be assigned automatically to one religion.

8. JEWISH RACHEL APPARITION CLAIMS: AN ACTUAL FIGURE CLAIM, MUCH SMALLER DOCUMENTARY BASE

Bar-Ilan University Political Studies researcher Elisheva Rosman's 2018 article identifies four recorded accounts: Avner Azulai's radio testimony and three filmed soldiers. Azulai describes a hovering luminous female figure that warned his group away from explosives; the other three accounts describe an elderly Arab woman. Rosman explicitly studies the stories' circulation and meaning, not whether the miracles occurred. The named broadcast was publicized months after the fighting. Four accounts cannot automatically be counted as four independent apparitions. [17]

Assessment: this is a genuine qualitative apparition comparison, not simply a metaphor or a scholar's invented example. But the reviewed record does not supply Zeitoun's scale, repeated public accessibility or independent contemporary investigations. The apparent visual differences and late publication are source questions, not proof all soldiers lied. Rosman's institutional standing does not establish an ordinary cause. Original co-witness identification and the underlying film/radio require further inspection.

9. NAIROBI 1988: CONTEMPORARY PRESS, BUT ANOMALOUS DISAPPEARANCE IS NOT THE REPORTER'S OBSERVATION

Original Kenya Times and Sunday Times page reproductions preserve an estimated six-thousand-person gathering, a robed visitor, light and photographs. The first reporter's own observations must be separated from the driver's reported disappearance claim and the foundress's healing claim. The follow-up supplies a transport/earlier-encounter account through another intermediary, with a date problem still unresolved. [18]

University of Nairobi researcher O. M. Jacob Nandi's 1993 MA thesis includes interviews with foundress Mary Akatsa and original journalist Job Mutungi. Its relevant sections reinforce the Christian founding/authority context and document controversy, but do not supply a verified supernatural arrival or disappearance. A later Maitreya interpretation is an appropriation of this Christian event, not its original religious identity. The thesis has now been recovered locally; only the relevant sections were read. [19]

Assessment: more substantive than a late anonymous legend, but not an established Fatima-equivalent sign. An indexed 2013 interview attributes a later denial to Akatsa; the full premium article was not accessible. That is a lead, not a completed finding of fraud.

10. BRAZILIAN SPIRITIST SURGERY: REAL INCISIONS, NO ESTABLISHED CURATIVE EFFECT

Alexander Moreira de Almeida and colleagues, affiliated with the University of Sao Paulo's Hospital das Clinicas Psychiatry Institute and the Federal University of Juiz de Fora's medical-school Pathology department, observed about thirty procedures in 1995. Six patients received closer clinical examination and ten tissue samples were examined. Actual incisions and ocular procedures occurred; extracted tissue generally matched its bodily site. Apart from a lipoma, samples were not the pathological lesions that would account for the claimed cures. Only one studied patient reported pain; no infection was detected in the brief direct follow-up. Four later reports included two improvements and two non-improvements, without independent treating-doctor reports. [20]

Assessment: the paper contradicts a blanket claim that every such operation was fake blood or hidden animal tissue. It does not establish miraculous healing. Absence of observed pain or infection deserves investigation but is not a numerical impossibility without an appropriate baseline and follow-up. The procedures cannot simply be equated to Pio's chronic wounds. The six closely examined patients were Catholic, and the healer operated in an eclectic Spiritist setting: this is not a clean experiment discriminating two mutually exclusive religions.

OTHER CASES: RETAIN THE EARLIER DISPOSITIONS

Hindu milk, 1995: remains excluded as a serious rival supernatural sign on the reviewed evidence. Ordinary-object replication and original-site runoff/dye observations are substantive specific counterevidence. It is nowhere near Fatima as a case for divine intervention. No new evidence in this pass reverses that judgment. [21]

Dhammakaya Thai solar claim: remains inadequate as the proposed Fatima counterpart. No new evidence reverses the earlier assessment of its press record, low-sun circumstances and fundraising context. This does not deny every reported visual experience. [21]

Badulla shrine lights, 1887; the Bab's execution, 1850; and Ahmad-Dowie, 1902-1907: preserve their actual early sources but mark them outside a strict last-110-years comparison. The first has an early secondhand letter and a later named testimony; the second a near-contemporary secondhand diplomatic dispatch; the third a genuine pre-outcome death-order challenge. None currently provides the relevant matched public physical evidence. [21]

Welsh revival lights, 1904-05; Rebbe sightings and healings; Sikh Vasu Bhardwaj's recovery; the 16th Karmapa, rainbow-body reports and Itigilov: retained as research leads, with the primary-source limitations in the earlier reports. Do not represent these as newly completed full audits. [21]

Branham's 1950 halo: the available promotional compilation quotes document examiner George Lacy as finding no manipulation and concluding light struck the negative. That establishes neither a supernatural light source nor all of Branham's teachings. The original signed report and a complete optical reconstruction were not inspected. [22]

Tenrikyo's Belgian teenage recovery, 2010-11: the religious organization's 2011 account reports intensive hospital care and a treatment precedent followed by gradual recovery. It does not provide a named hospital team or diagnostic records. Religious interpretation is explicit; independently verified miraculous causation is not established. [23]

Chinese qigong object-penetration claims: Kongzhi Song's original 1999 abstract describes sealed-container/high-speed-camera experiments, making this a potentially important laboratory lead. Repeated PDF downloads were incomplete. The full protocol and rebuttals have not been audited. Alleged trickery in other Zhang Baosheng demonstrations cannot be silently substituted for a controlled explanation of these particular experiments. [24]

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE ARGUMENT

1. The stronger research has not supplied an equally documented non-Apostolic public sign to set against Fatima or Zeitoun. I should not imply a tie merely because other religions tell miracle stories.

2. Claims of individual extraordinary recoveries, bodily phenomena and apparently anomalous knowledge outside Apostolic Christianity have serious primary literature. Sai Baba, Jani, the Baptist case, the Sufi experiments, tukdam and the prior-record reincarnation cases merit differentiated judgments. 'All debunked' would be false on this record.

3. A supernatural explanation of one of these individual cases would not automatically refute Christianity. An individual Christian healing outside Apostolic institutions is particularly different from public validation of a rival doctrine. Conversely, religiously meaningful Sufi and Buddhist interpretations should not be dismissed by definition.

4. The global exclusivity claim remains stronger than this research can establish. We have not exhaustively inspected vernacular archives or every serious modern case in Jain, Zoroastrian, Indigenous, Shinto, LDS and other traditions. Within the traditions searched, several original records remain inaccessible. 'No adequate counterpart found' is the supported finding; universal absence is not.

5. This comparison does not itself resolve the underlying causal questions at Fatima, Zeitoun or Pio. It also supplies no ordinary explanation of their core claims. The previous failures of specific objections remain part of the argument; they are not erased by listing unrelated stories.

NEXT DOCUMENTS WITH THE HIGHEST VALUE

The complete Hall 2001 clinical paper and its accompanying commentary; the original Jani monitoring/physiology records and any final publication; original 1970s Sai Baba investigation reports and unedited video; the original written lists/field notes in the strongest reincarnation cases; original ophthalmic correspondence and exact recovery timeline for the Baptist case; the raw tukdam visual record with comparable preservation cases; named Rachel co-witnesses and original recordings. These are targeted evidence needs, not an assumption that contrary explanations will eventually win.

DANIEL’S HEALING-MEASUREMENT OBJECTION — 2 NOVEMBER 2026

Accepted observation: Daniel does not dispute that a real cheek puncture occurred. Further evidence that the implement entered tissue does not answer his present objection.
