PAINTING AROUND THE SHROUD: INITIAL VISUAL RESEARCH COLLECTION
4 November 2026

RESULT
12 works or technical groups were selected, with 11 illustrated records and
four closer visual comparisons. This is a targeted quality comparison, not
a global census or an objective ranking of the greatest artists.

MOST RELEVANT STARTING POINTS
- Cimabue and Giotto: monumental complete painted bodies before Lirey.
- Jean Pucelle: named French royal artist before Lirey; very fine small grisaille.
- Giottino: a dead Christ on a burial cloth close to the Lirey chronology.
- Parement de Narbonne: a near-period monochrome French Passion textile on silk.
- Spinello: a later-fourteenth-century double-sided processional canvas.
- Withyham group: near-period linen paintings with a technical study.
- Dieric Bouts and Justus of Ghent: later, well-studied linen-painting specialists.
- Mantegna: a later Renaissance dead-body/foreshortening control, not a contemporary.

VISUAL ASSESSMENT
Giotto's close-up provides direct evidence of sophisticated modeling of flesh,
facial volumes and the torso in the late thirteenth century. Pucelle and
Giottino show strong grisaille and facial/figure work around the first half
and middle of the fourteenth century. This is evidence against a blanket
picture of medieval artists as unable to depict convincing human volume.
It is not a quantitative test of the Shroud's anatomical proportions, nor
a mechanism for producing its chemical image properties.

The selection has not recovered an equivalent to the entire Shroud combination.
Recognizing medieval artistic skill and recognizing an unresolved technical
Shroud problem are compatible. A wooden crucifix, silk altar hanging or
painted linen should not be treated as a match simply because it depicts Christ.

DATING AND MATERIAL CHECKS
Giotto: the current church page proposes 1288–89; other catalogues often use
1290–95. The church's archive discusses a later proposal but documents the
cross before June 1312. The gallery uses a broad late-1280s–1290s label.
Giottino: Uffizi record currently gives c.1350–55. The Bologna Zeri catalogue
and older exhibition references often give c.1360–65. Both are recorded.
Justus: current Met record gives 1472–74; the 2016 technical article uses c.1470.
Narbonne: around1375 is conventional; the inspected short Louvre entry does
not print a manufacture year. It identifies silk and gray wash, not linen.
Spinello: current face of Christ on the reverse is a photographic facsimile
of the detached original. Do not compare it as original medieval paint.
Cimabue: about60% painted-surface loss from the1966 flood; absence of pigment
in present photographs is not evidence for original image superficiality.

LINEN AND CONSERVATION SOURCES
National Gallery researchers David Bomford, Ashok Roy and Alistair Smith,
'The Techniques of Dieric Bouts: Two Paintings Contrasted', Technical Bulletin10
(1986),39–57. Identified linen, pigments, paint layers and differing media.
https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/research/publications/technical-bulletin/the-techniques-of-dieric-bouts-two-paintings-contrasted

Sophie Scully, Metropolitan Museum paintings conservation, and art historian
Christine Seidel, 'A Tüchlein by Justus van Ghent', JHNA8.1(2016).
The open article includes photomicrographs, infrared examination and views
showing thin paint and weave. Thin film or visible weave is not a measurement
of the depth of coloration inside an individual flax fiber.
https://jhna.org/articles/tuchlein-justus-van-ghent-adoration-of-the-magi-metropolitan-museum-of-art-re-examined/
https://doi.org/10.5092/jhna.2016.8.1.3

Caroline Villers, John Stevenson and Jennifer Sharp, 'The Technique of Four
14th-Century Italian Paintings on Fabric Supports'(1993). The earlier local
library contains the paper and reading notes. Public images not recovered
here; the entry is a technical lead with existing inspected material.
https://www.icom-cc-publications-online.org/2736/The-Technique-of-Four-14th-Century-Italian-Paintings-on-Fabric-Supports

SUSIE NASH: IMPORTANT REMAINING TECHNICAL SOURCE
'The Parement de Narbonne: Context and Technique', in The Fabric of Images:
European Paintings on Textile Support c.1330–1500(2000),77–87. Cited by Louvre;
full chapter was not obtained in the earlier investigation. Its existence
is not a claim that this chapter has been newly read.

ACCESS
Institutional object pages and selected technical texts were inspected. JSON
records and source HTML were saved locally; images are referenced remotely.
The displayed official images of Giotto, Cimabue, Pucelle, Spinello and a
Giottino detail were visually verified in the browser. The earlier source
inventory supplies the Withyham technical findings. No tally was changed.

Each object's source, dimensions, date, image URL and qualification are in
artworks.json. visual-index.html is the reading gallery.
