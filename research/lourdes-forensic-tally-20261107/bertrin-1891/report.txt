LOURDES MEDICAL EVIDENCE | BERTRIN'S 1891 REGISTER
7 November 2026

The 1891 section contains 65 entries. Bertrin's later annual table also
gives 65 for 1891. I chose this year because the previous audit had
already traced Clémentine Trouvé and adjacent entries gave an exact
Annales volume and page. This is a census of the printed section. The
late-century register mixes reports of recoveries and improvements.

Forty-two entries match published narratives or documents in the
Sanctuary's digitized Annales. Of the 35 claims, 12 have an issue
account with limited clinical evidence and 23 remain index only.
The 65 entries include one unnamed young religious. Two appear on
today's official roster, Amélie Chagnon and Clémentine Trouvé.
The 42 traced cases go into the selected forensic tally, with the
one anonymous report identified as such. The other 23 remain in the
year's tally. The main tally also includes a December 1891 case that
Bertrin filed under 1892, outside the count of 65.

The source map preserves the print order, date groups, reported
conditions and cited pages. There are 40 rows with Annales citations,
20 with Bureau-register citations and five without either. Eleven rows
name one or more certificate authors in the appendix. The Annales
also yielded two records indexed without an Annales reference.
The 1890–1891 Bureau register was unavailable. The open issue scans
preserve what the contemporary writers published, including the
medical detail and reservations available for each case.

The digitized accounts sometimes say partial improvement or explicitly
leave durability open. In the four fast cases the grade describes a
specific clinical change and its timing. It leaves the diagnosis
and cause to separate assessment. Two fast recoveries were reported
at the French shrine. The other two followed Lourdes devotion or
water use elsewhere.

1. AMÉLIE CHAGNON | SOLID BEFORE/AFTER, FAST | OFFICIAL NO. 14

The paired record is Dupont's certificate of 30 August 1891, printed
in Annales XXIV pp. 160–161 and reproduced in Boissarie, Les grandes
guérisons de Lourdes, p. 117 (PDF p. 147).
Dupont had treated her for months and visited on the eve of departure.
He records persisting drainage from a left foot fistula, significant
knee swelling and sensitivity, and confinement to bed at departure.
He had tried counterirritation, cautery and immobilization. The apparatus
was removed about six weeks earlier, with symptoms persisting. He
planned extraction of the second metatarsal and zinc chloride injections
at the knee and agreed to defer them for the pilgrimage.

Boissarie gives 20 August for the departure. Dupont's return
examination, documented by 30 August, describes a firm scar where
the tract had been and loss of tenderness around the joint. In that
reproduction the pre-trip tract measures about 2 cm. Bertrin's shorter
transcription on p. 174 reads about 1 cm. This discrepancy leaves the
size approximate. Gaillard, a second treating physician, issued a
5 September certificate reporting a firm scar, comparable knee size
on both sides and free joint movement. Boissarie recounts an exam at
the Bureau soon after the baths and a return visit in November 1893.
The named bath attendant reported seeing the open wound between two
immersions on 21 August and the scar after the second. The strongest
clinician bracket is roughly eleven days at most. The bath chronology
has a witness basis and a reported near-event Bureau exam.

The grade covers observed closure, pain, swelling and joint function.
The period diagnosis of tuberculous osteoarthritis was clinical.
The sources examined supply no pathology, cultures or imaging that
could establish the original lesion extent or how the underlying bone
changed. The case remains causally undetermined on this record.

2. CLÉMENTINE TROUVÉ | MID TIER | OFFICIAL NO. 15

Cibiel's 11 June certificate identifies a persistent calcaneal lesion
treated by incision and injections. His 1 September certificate
describes a painless scar with residual plantar enlargement and ready
weight-bearing. A hospital superior reports the wound still present
on 10 August. Delaigne's October 1891 statement describes an open
draining wound on 17 August. Roederer's 1895 deposition describes
drainage at the station on 18 August and during the journey, and walking
after the 21 August bath. An attendant's account is also printed in
the Annals of Psychical Science. The 1908 diocesan decree describes
the earlier records and continued recovery.

This supports wound closure and return of function. The doctor who
certified both sides brackets the change June to September. The closer
timing is supported by lay witnesses and an inspection afterward. In the
September 1891 Annales, the Bureau physician states that he could
see the remnant of the wound but could not determine precisely
whether it had been draining when she entered the bath. A later critical
interview alleged prior wound improvement and questioned the timing.
The observed scar and return of weight-bearing are distinct from
confirmation of tuberculosis or rapid healing of calcaneal bone.

3. LUCIE RENAULD | MID TIER | OUTSIDE THE OFFICIAL ROSTER

Boissarie reports a pre-trip certificate describing left-leg muscle
atrophy as a sequel of childhood paralysis and a shoe lift of about
3 cm. He describes her walking without the old limp barefoot after
the final bath and needing her shoe lifts equalized. He also explains
that the certifying doctor had only seen her at departure, leaving
the disease history and diagnosis unresolved. The date group in the
appendix is 23 August, while the August 1891 Annales, Boissarie and
Bertrin's prose give 24 August for the bath. The issue describes a
4 cm lift, while Boissarie's later account gives 3 cm. The available clinical record supports a
reported improvement in gait and shoe-lift needs. The lift is not a
direct measurement of bone length. Paired anatomical measurements
and a dated follow-up for Lucie were not located. Monnier's actual
July–October measurements in the next pages are for her sister
Charlotte in 1892. They cannot be applied to Lucie.

4. SŒUR LABADIE | SOLID BEFORE/AFTER, FAST | OUTSIDE THE OFFICIAL ROSTER

The July 1891 Annales reproduces a fuller report by Dr Pansini than
the extract Bertrin printed. It places the onset of severe fever
and chills on January 30. It records bloody sputum, cyanosis,
breathlessness, delirium and a nearly impalpable pulse during the
acute phase. On day 13 she ate and entered an afebrile convalescence.
The editors place the turn on the February 11 anniversary of the
first Lourdes apparition. The grade covers the documented rapid
change in systemic signs and oral intake. No temperature chart,
cultures or detailed serial lung exams were recovered.

The association is a novena in Molfetta, Italy. The physician
recognized the possibility of pneumonia resolving by crisis but
was struck by the apparent absence of sweating or expectoration
at the turn. A rapid clinical crisis was a known course of lobar
pneumonia, documented in pre-antibiotic clinical series. The case
record leaves the specific mechanism undetermined.

DATE AND INDEX CHECKS

The 65 are the literal 1891 section. Bertrin includes Marguerite Gaffié
under 1892 and describes a December 1891 recovery elsewhere. The
original Annales XXV account (August 1892, pp. 106–109) names her,
dates the improvement to December 8 and reproduces her father's
clinical account. He was also her treating doctor. He records
recurrent painful swelling of the right first metatarsal, earlier
remission and relapse, and another exacerbation during 1891.
After a novena at home, he found pressure and movement painless
and the prominence markedly reduced. The doctor was absent for the
initial reported change, and the date of his last comparable pre-event
exam is unclear. Grade mid for the examined findings and reported
durability. Keep the case separate from the 65 while dating it 1891
in the main tally. Bertrin's excerpt gives December 7.
https://lourdes.centredoc.fr/doc_num.php?explnum_id=3717#page=10

Amélie Gimard appears immediately above the 1891 heading under 1890
with an August date and an October 1891 certificate. A later
certificate alone cannot fix the event year. The 1909 book's agreement
with the literal count does not settle the number of events actually
reported for the calendar year. The August 1891 Annales is titled
Au Bureau des constatations and says its initial notes are preliminary.

CASE NOTES FOR ALL 42 REPORTS TRACED TO THE ANNALES

Sœur Labadie | fast | Annales 1891-07, 71–73
Novena to Our Lady of Lourdes in Molfetta, Italy.
Hospital physician Pansini records onset January 30, bloody sputum, cyanosis, breathlessness, delirium and a barely palpable pulse as the illness progressed. On day 13 she could eat and entered convalescence without fever. His report gives specific clinical findings on both sides across days. The editorial introduction puts the turn on the February 11 anniversary. The prose in Bertrin misplaces it in January and calls it day 12. No temperature chart or cultures were available.
Cause assessment — A crisis of lobar pneumonia is a plausible ordinary course. The specific mechanism remains undetermined.

Mme Compas | claim | Annales 1891-08, 115
French Lourdes baths.
The pilgrimage chronicle describes a woman aged 39 with longstanding ataxic symptoms and reports abrupt relief after immersion. It supplies neither a measured neurological change nor follow-up. It places the episode in the August 15 feast account, while the register groups it August 18.

Clémentine Trouvé | mid | Annales 1891-08, 93 and 1891-09, 127–129 and 1891-10, 163–165
French Lourdes baths.
Cibiel documents a resistant calcaneal lesion in June and a painless scar with weight-bearing on September 1. An August hospital account and eyewitnesses support a closer interval. The contemporary Bureau author confirms inspecting the mark afterward and explicitly says he could not determine whether it had still been draining at entry into the bath. The report gives a firm endpoint and a less secure timing bracket.

Mlle Loranchet | mid | Annales 1891-08, 101–102
French Lourdes baths.
A physician from Luxeuil supplied a Pott disease diagnosis. She arrived on a stretcher in an iron corset and reportedly walked after bathing. The Bureau physician saw the cautery marks after the event and explicitly favored a functional disorder over vertebral damage based on the history. Evidence concerns movement. Vertebral tuberculosis was disputed in the original report.

Georges Verdier | mid | Annales 1891-08, 100
French Lourdes baths.
An 18-year-old with right foot pain and inability to walk after a sprain wore a silicate splint. The account records restored use after its removal at the bath, with residual swelling much reduced two days later. Exact pre-trip clinical findings and later follow-up were unavailable.

Amélie Chagnon | fast | Annales 1891-08, 94 and 1891-10, 158–163
French Lourdes baths.
Dupont's August 30 certificate describes drainage and severe knee symptoms just before departure, then a firm foot scar and no joint tenderness on return. Gaillard documented free movement and symmetric knee size September 5. The grade covers wound and joint findings within about eleven days. The sources give different tract lengths and timing of splint removal. Bone repair and the historical TB diagnosis lack direct confirmation.

Marie Seguin | fast | Annales 1891-08, 98
French Lourdes baths.
The Bureau report records moist chest sounds on August 22 despite subjective improvement, then their absence with only slight residual left-sided reduction in breath sounds on August 24. That is a dated clinical pair for a rapid change in auscultation. The referred TB diagnosis lacks organism confirmation and the recorded change does not establish its eradication.

Berthe Petit | mid | Annales 1891-08, 94
French Lourdes baths.
A named patient from Paris arrived with reported paraplegia and a lower-back prominence that a bath attendant saw. The report describes walking afterward and residual cautery marks with no apparent prominence. The diagnosis of Pott disease and actual vertebral position lack paired measurements. This woman, aged 34, is distinct from the Belgian mystic of the same name.

Marie Rayon | mid | Annales 1891-08, 97–98
French Lourdes Blessed Sacrament procession.
A referral warned against bathing given her severe state, following reported pleurisy, hemoptysis and prolonged bed rest. The Bureau account describes standing August 21 and a subsequent chest exam without rales but with residual signs. A precise before auscultation and durable follow-up were unavailable.

Sœur Dorothée | mid | Annales 1891-08, 98
French Lourdes baths and Blessed Sacrament procession.
The Bureau account describes longstanding bed rest and vomiting, standing after immersion, walking after the procession, and visibly improved general condition on August 24. The pre-event diagnosis of anemia and spinal ischemia has no paired testing in this account.

Vve Menuau | mid | Annales 1891-08, 100 and 1891-09, 139
French Lourdes Blessed Sacrament procession.
The reports describe hospital treatment for suspected gastric ulcer, repeated hematemesis and recent inability to eat. She got up and ate after the procession. September's chronicle gives August 22 and identifies her as Mme Charles Menuau. There is a clinical history and observable functional change, with no paired study of the ulcer or later bleeding record.

Charlotte Bernard | mid | Annales 1891-08, 101
French Lourdes grotto washes and baths.
A Paris clinician reported loss of eardrum and ossicles and left-sided deafness. Lourdes physicians observed that the child could hear a watch at 30 cm after the washes and bath. The source gives no comparable pre-bath watch test, audiometry or post-event anatomical ear exam. The reported endpoint is hearing a sound.

Amélie Lefrançois | mid | Annales 1891-08, 99
French Lourdes baths.
The report describes a referring physician's chest diagnosis, symptomatic improvement after the third bath, a negative post-event chest exam and a recheck two days later. No specific pre-bath lung findings or durable follow-up were found.

French Lourdes baths.

Dr Chaplût's account describes right-sided paralysis, contracture and vomiting, initial improvement by the sixth bath and later independent walking and eating. French Lourdes baths.
