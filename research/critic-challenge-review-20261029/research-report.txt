1. A genuine comparative-religion objection

New York Times correspondent Seth Mydans's 13 August 1999 report, preserved on a Kenyon College religion course website, describes a Dhammakaya Buddhist gathering and interviews a named claimant, Chuleeporn Chungrangsee. She attributed the experience to tens of thousands. The reported features include a softened, cool-looking solar disc, rainbow rotation, and an image of the movement's founder. Mydans also reports Thai newspaper coverage. The reporter does not say he personally witnessed the preceding year's solar event.

Source: https://www2.kenyon.edu/Depts/Religion/Fac/Adler/Reln260/Dhammakaya.htm
Local: sources/dhammakaya-mydans-1999-kenyon.html and .txt

Argument: substantial public solar-miracle reports with religious imagery and contemporary secular reporting occur outside apostolic Christianity. Thus those properties alone do not uniquely identify Catholic supernatural agency. This challenges an absolute absence-of-comparators premise, rather than demonstrating that Fátima did not happen.

Important differences: this search has not established a months-ahead dated prophecy, independently enumerated observers, geographically separated unprimed accounts, a comparably rich primary dossier, or an external journalist personally reporting the solar event. It has not demonstrated the Buddhist event's natural cause. Similar descriptions are a reason for comparison, not proof of identical physical causes or equal evidential strength.

A 2002 paper by Rungrawee Chalermsripinyorat in Manusya: Journal of Humanities is catalogued in Chulalongkorn University's repository. Its indexed text quotes a different named witness, associate professor Saowalak Piempiti, via The Nation, 2 December 1998. This is a lead for recovering the original issue. The full PDF returned 403 at both the publisher and repository during this pass, so do not label it fully read or the underlying newspaper checked. The paper's author is not being presented as an eyewitness or clinician.

2. Pio: what the strongest new critique actually argues

Exegetical Drift's 9 October 2026 essay, The Exact Perception of a Void, is an extensive medical-source critique. The author's identity and medical credentials are unverified. Use its citations as leads; its length and medical language do not establish its diagnosis.
https://exegeticaldrift.substack.com/p/the-exact-perception-of-a-void
Local full article: sources/pio-exact-perception.html and .txt

Its most relevant argument is that a repeatedly renewed superficial-injury account can accommodate some observations without requiring all examiners to be conspirators. Many of its other targets—full-thickness perforation, quantified loss of blood, perfect symmetry, laboratory sterility—are not necessary premises of Daniel's retained case.

Primary check: Roman surgeon Giorgio Festa's 1920 report, document 53, printed p208, describes a shifted right dorsal lesion and a whitish glossy scar at the prior site. It immediately also describes enlarged, deeper palmar lesions, pain and normal surrounding skin. Printed pp211–212 report foot scars with continuing central lesions. These are observations of real injury and some repair, not a witnessed act of self-injury. They do not explain the whole chronology.
Primary edition: Gerardo Di Flumeri, Le stigmate di Padre Pio da Pietrelcina (1995).
https://www.imagomundi.biz/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/PBSO37-Le_stigmate_di_Padre_Pio_da_Pietrelcina-Gerardo_di_Flumeri.pdf
Local original is in ../padre-pio-muse-audit-20261028/sources/di-flumeri-stigmate.pdf; printed pp208–209 visually checked this pass and rendered locally.

The actual remaining question is whether a maintained/recurrent-injury hypothesis fits the complete serial record better than supernatural stigmata. Establishing ordinary healing at one site does not answer that question. Nor does failure to prove chemical exclusion establish application of a chemical. No doctor needs to be dishonest for an unresolved ordinary process to be misinterpreted, but that logical point is not itself evidence of such a process in Pio.

Clinical comparison, with its limits

University of Seville podiatry researchers Juan Manuel Muriel-Sánchez, Manuel Coheña-Jiménez and Pedro Montaño-Jiménez reported a 27-patient randomized phenol nail-treatment trial in 2021: mean healing around 15 versus 22 days, depending on application duration. It concerns nail surgery, subsequent dressing care and antimicrobial treatment, not untreated hand stigmata. The comparison shows why eight days of persistence alone cannot universally exclude a preceding chemical injury. It does not establish unchanged daily bleeding for that interval or explain Pio's longer reported withdrawal periods.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8508160/
Full text acquired and methods checked this pass. This study and the general eight-day objection were ALREADY in the prior audit. They must not be sold as a new challenge.

Independent literature corroborates the limited clinical premise: A. B. Aksakal and colleagues, Gazi University School of Medicine dermatology department, reported prolonged postoperative drainage as a known phenol-treatment problem. Only their 2001 abstract was read here; it is not a study of Pio.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11207690/

Clinical lookalikes must be audited with equal care. The critique cites an eight-year bilateral bleeding-sole case in Clinical Psychodermatology: A Casebook, edited by Mohammad Jafferany and Asmahane Souissi. The book and case title were verified; Google Books withheld the relevant full pages. The alleged razor discovery has NOT been independently checked in the chapter and is not promoted as verified clinical evidence. A freely accessible 2026 scalp-ulcer case by Xiaowen Wen and colleagues is only a three-year case, includes inflammation, and does not demonstrate Pio's pattern or catch a fraudster.
https://books.google.com/books?id=6H0_EQAAQBAJ

3. Another stigmata claimant: worth auditing, not established equivalence

A translated statement attributed to physician Nicola Ceglie, dated September 2009 and published by UFO magazine in 2010, asserts twenty years of daily bleeding stigmata in Giorgio Bongiovanni, with no visible infection or edema. It is translated by Pedro de Campos and transmitted through a supportive association; original signed case records and Ceglie's institutional credentials were not recovered. Bongiovanni's own movement presents a religious teaching involving reincarnation and extraterrestrial revelation, differing from Catholic doctrine.
https://ufo.com.br/2-medico-estudou-os-estigmas-de-bongiovanni-por-20-anos/
https://thebongiovannifamily.it/la-biografia-di-giorgio-bongiovanni/

This is a concrete competing claimant with named medical testimony, stronger as a research lead than an unspecified hypothetical stigmatist. It is not yet a documentation-matched rival to Pio. It does not establish either man's fabrication, and an extraordinary event would not automatically validate every teaching of its recipient. Do not convert the movement's assertions into independent clinical findings.

Coelho's partial comparison is not upgraded to a demonstrated repetition of the complete event. The new useful component is the Buddhist comparison above.
