PADRE PIO AND GIORGIO BONGIOVANNI: COMPARISON OF RECOVERED MEDICAL EVIDENCE
29 October 2026

Assessment
Pio has the stronger recovered documentary clinical record. Bongiovanni is a substantive comparative lead: contemporary published statements attributed to named examining doctors exist. Neither similarity of appearances nor favorable psychiatric impressions demonstrates a common cause. This comparison does not establish fabrication or a complete ordinary explanation for either man. No new debate tally is assigned.

SOURCE QUALITY
Pio: Gerardo Di Flumeri's 1995 documentary collection reproduces dated medical reports, correspondence, photographs, radiographs and later testimony. We have inspected published reproductions, not newly authenticated original manuscripts. The existing inventory's 20 classified records are not 20 independent medical examinations or 20 independent doctors.
Bongiovanni: Busqueda bulletin 50, September 1992, printed p5, reproduces Spanish translations of statements attributed to Federico Finatti, Maria Luisa Viel and Stanis Previato. All concern one joint visit on 16 November 1991, not three independent trials. Finatti's statement is dated 18 March 1992; Viel's 19 March 1992. Typed firma placeholders do not authenticate original signatures. Later clinical claims appear in supporter publications and translated statements; original lab worksheets and a complete original clinical dossier have not been recovered.

EXAMINERS AND WHAT THEY OBSERVED
Pio: Luigi Romanelli, chief surgeon at Barletta's civil hospital; Amico Bignami, professor of medical pathology at the Royal University of Rome; Giorgio Festa, Rome physician/surgeon; and later physicians including Luigi Pancaro and Giuseppe Sala. Favorable and skeptical examiners describe real visible signs; they disagree over depth and cause. Do not count Rossi, an apostolic visitor, as a doctor, or Gemelli as having completed a verified formal wound examination.
Bongiovanni: Finatti, a physician in Copparo; Viel, identified as an anesthesiology/resuscitation specialist; Previato, a psychiatrist with a psychosomatic-service role in Rovigo. The published statements describe crusted/hemorrhagic epithelial areas and recent bleeding. Reported chronic frequency and quantities are not all directly observed. Nicola Ceglie, independently identifiable as an anesthesiology/resuscitation clinician in Bari, is attributed a 2009 statement asserting twenty years of observations; no complete dated visit log was recovered. Current professional affiliation does not authenticate a historical statement.

LONGITUDINAL COVERAGE
Pio: clinical records span 1919 to the 1968 endpoint, with gaps. Repeated visits and different examiners strengthen longitudinal evidence but are not continuous surveillance. Changing morphology is preserved; invariant lesions are not the user's claim.
Bongiovanni: early 1991 examination material and later twenty-year claims provide longitudinal leads. A supporter book reproduces Carmen Recalde de Fernandez's 3 August 1993 report of repeated visits over five days, four bleeding episodes and blood testing. Her reproduced credentials concern clinical medicine/allergy/immunology, not an independently verified hematology professorship. Its cached text was read; the original signed report/lab worksheet and a currently downloadable scan were not recovered.

BLEEDING AND INFECTION
Both: named observers report bleeding and little/no obvious clinical infection. Such observations deserve weight; absence of visible infection is not a culture-based demonstration of sterility. Neither recovered corpus supplies a reliable measured daily whole-blood-loss series sufficient to prove physiologically impossible replacement. Do not invoke normal blood counts plus an unmeasured description of abundant bleeding as a quantitative impossibility.
Pio: Pancaro's 1954 cloth-extract haemoglobin test and blood/coagulation findings are recoverable, but dilution/extraction details do not permit a daily blood-loss calculation.
Bongiovanni: matching ABO type, normal-count claims and different coagulation times are attributed to examinations, especially Sergio Alba Briseno in Mexico in 1994, but raw methods and original worksheets remain missing. ABO matching is not unique donor identification. Differently collected samples need a standardized protocol before their coagulation-time difference can be interpreted.

DEPTH AND EXCLUSION OF MANIPULATION
Pio: depth descriptions conflict; some are inferential and others retrospective. Later radiographs concern bone integrity, not a complete map of soft-tissue channels. The 1919 dressing episode has sworn testimony to persistence, medicine removal and later testimony to intact seals. This is positive control evidence, with limitations: it is not a decades-long medically charted exclusion of every means of maintenance. Festa's iodine-directed tests do not exclude every irritant.
Bongiovanni: some reproduced statements use traversing or through-wound language, but no calibrated depth study, histology or verified channel tracing was recovered. No comparably documented sealed-dressing intervention was recovered. This is a retrieval limit, not proof that no such experiment ever occurred or that he produced the wounds himself.

RESOLUTION AND SCARS
Pio: Sala's direct postmortem inspection and palpation, coupled with hand photographs and predeath film, are a distinctive evidentiary strength. They support no visible scars/normal-looking skin at the endpoint. Festa described a white shiny scar at an earlier dorsal hand location in 1920; that does not settle its 1968 state. No recovered histology demonstrates full-thickness tissue regeneration.
Bongiovanni: Daniel Munoz's UFO article (published 1 May 2003) reports disappearance of the forehead cross on 11 February 2002 and a television appearance without it on 27 February. Despite its broad headline, the body concerns the forehead sign; it is not evidence that all limb wounds vanished. No equivalent recovered physician's before-and-after palpatory examination or histology establishes scarless restoration. It would be incorrect to claim no disappearance narrative exists.

INSTITUTIONAL AND THEOLOGICAL LIMITS
Harvard Medical School psychiatrist John E. Mack is a genuine university-affiliated lead for Bongiovanni, but favorable comments attributed to him in a 2010 UFO article are not a recovered Harvard wound investigation or an original signed clinical report. Psychological composure does not itself exclude deliberate wound manipulation, for either claimant.
Bongiovanni's own teachings retain Jesus and Mary while incorporating reincarnation and extraterrestrial intelligences. He is therefore not a clean example from an unrelated non-Christian religion. If the phenomenon were authenticated as supernatural, its theological significance would still require a separate argument.

Overall inference
The present record supports ranking Pio ahead on recoverable detailed reports, longitudinal documentation, limited control evidence and clinically observed endpoint. Bongiovanni cannot currently be presented as medically equivalent or as a demonstrated ordinary reproduction of Pio. Equally, his documented clinical leads should not be erased merely because their religious context is unconventional. A general statement that only orthodox Catholic saints have ever had doctor-observed stigmata would exceed this evidence.
