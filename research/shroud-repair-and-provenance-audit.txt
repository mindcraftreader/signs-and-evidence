SHROUD OF TURIN: REPAIR, OTHER CARBON-DATE EXPLANATIONS, AND BIOLOGICAL PROVENANCE
Research audit — 27 October 2026

QUESTION AND RESULT

Could the 1988 dating accurately measure a corner while misleading us about the
original cloth? Yes. Three laboratories do not overcome a common sampling failure.
The question is whether a failure large enough to explain a first-century cloth is
supported by the actual material, rather than merely possible.

My assessment after this pass: substantial replacement remains the strongest of the
reviewed ordinary mechanisms for reconciling ancient linen with the medieval carbon
result. It has positive chemical observations behind it, conflicting textile and
microscopic observations against it, and no established reconstruction of the repair
across the dated strip. It is neither a demonstrated repair nor a conclusively
excluded possibility. Ordinary dirt, a few cotton fibers, and a hypothetical
biological film should not be substituted for that substantial-replacement claim.

The pollen and DNA literature contains affirmative claims of Near Eastern contact.
It does not establish a unique, securely dated Palestinian origin. The best-known
pollen identification is disputed, and genetic analyses explicitly accommodate
later contamination. This pass does not justify treating an ancient age as an
80% measured result. The numerical section makes conditional odds explicit.

This is an audit of dating and provenance. It does not purport to calculate the
probability of Christianity or to settle image formation. The separate 26 October
dating assessment is preserved as a dated snapshot, not silently rewritten.

1. KEEP THE SAMPLES SEPARATE

1973: Raes removed textile next to the region subsequently used for dating.
1978: STURP collected tapes and vacuum dust from several parts of the cloth.
1988: one corner strip supplied all three radiocarbon laboratories.
2003: Rogers reports receiving warp and weft threads attributed by Gonella to the
      center of the 1988 strip, in addition to his earlier Raes material.
2010: Freer-Waters and Jull examined retained Arizona fragment A1B.
2026: the same authors examined A1A and A1B, with limited SEM sampling of A1A.

Nearby Raes threads are relevant evidence about the neighborhood. They are not
identical to the portions consumed by the dating laboratories. Conversely, it is
incorrect to claim that Rogers examined only Raes threads: he explicitly describes
receiving threads attributed to the radiocarbon strip. Attribution, handling
history and the limited material remain important to weighing that evidence.

Original dating report: https://www.shroud.com/nature.htm
Rogers: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tca.2004.09.029

2. THE STRONG REPAIR ARGUMENT, AND WHAT CHALLENGES IT

The positive argument has three components: reported compositional differences,
observations of atypical threads/coatings, and a historically proposed reweaving
operation. Rogers interprets gum/dye and cotton observations as evidence of foreign
material. Importantly, he says the soluble gum would have been removed by the
dating pretreatment. His proposed failure is replacement textile, not simply an
unremoved layer of that gum.

Brown independently examined selected Raes threads and documented coatings and
cotton. That corroborates local observations; it does not independently establish
the age or composition of the entire dating strip. The reviewed Benford/Marino
conference paper supplies historical and textile arguments for an unobtrusive
repair. Availability of a technique and royal resources show feasibility, not that
the particular intervention occurred.

Brown: https://www.shroud.com/pdfs/brown1.pdf
Benford/Marino: https://www.shroud.com/pdfs/ohiomarino2.pdf

Counterevidence is concrete too. Conservator Flury-Lemberg argues that reweaving
fine linen would leave detectable structural evidence and reports cohesive
remaining corner fabric. Her 2002 access was after removal of the 1988 strip; it
cannot substitute for a map of the missing material. Also, she objects to the
carbon date on contamination grounds: citing her against reweaving as though she
endorses the whole radiocarbon conclusion would misrepresent her position.

https://www.shroud.com/pdfs/n65part5.pdf

The 2010 Arizona study finds compatible linen and no overall dye/coating, but
observes cotton on three occasions, with uncertainty over whether one was internal
or surface contamination. The 2026 study reports compatible weave/count/twist and
no cotton in the areas examined. SEM covered approximately 2 x 1.5 mm of A1A.
The authors acknowledge limited, potentially non-uniform material. These are
related studies, not two fully independent replications. Matching flax and weave
weigh against some mixed-cotton repairs; they cannot by themselves exclude a
carefully matched linen repair whose boundary lies elsewhere.

2010: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200056277
2026: https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-026-02530-7

The mass-spectrometry disagreement is narrower than some summaries suggest.
Bella and colleagues challenge the interpretation of two published spectra; a
2016 response disputes their analysis. Neither abstract establishes a complete
corner reconstruction. Full texts were not retrieved in this pass, so those
arguments receive less weight here than directly inspected material reports.

Critique: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tca.2015.08.002
Response: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tca.2015.11.021

What would resolve this branch: authenticated retained threads, chemical maps,
documented joins or boundaries, and separate carbon measurements of suspected old
and added fibers. A convincing reconstructed repair must explain the individual
subsamples, not just fit one pooled average. Lack of detected cotton alone is not
a complete disproof; presence of a little cotton alone is not proof.

3. HOW LARGE A REPLACEMENT OR CONTAMINATION IS REQUIRED?

This audit calculated isotope mixing rather than averaging calendar dates.
The model is F_mix = (1-f) F_old + f F_added, where F = exp(-R/8033), R is the
conventional radiocarbon age, and f is the added share of measured carbon.

Inputs: hypothetical original linen grown in AD 30; the published 691 BP mean;
IntCal20 curve means for calendar-dated added material. Results:

  Added material grown AD 1500: 77.5% of measured carbon must be added material.
  Added material grown AD 1532: 74.6%.
  Added material grown AD 1600: 77.5%.
  Un-enriched modern reference carbon, F=1: 62.1%.

Rounded implication: about three-quarters, or more, of the residual carbon must
be sixteenth-century material in these examples. This could be replacement linen;
it need not all be cotton. A few stray fibers cannot supply that fraction.

These are illustrative required fractions, not measured contamination percentages
and not probabilities that a repair exists. They refer to carbon after cleaning,
not the initial dirty sample, DNA read counts or percentages of fabric area.
Full calibration uncertainty is not propagated. Post-bomb or otherwise enriched
carbon needs its actual isotope ratio and can require a smaller fraction.

Official curve: https://intcal.org/curves/intcal20.14c
Method publication: https://doi.org/10.1017/RDC.2020.41
Reproducible outputs: shroud-dating-audit/carbon-mixing.csv and
shroud-dating-audit/calculation-method.json.

4. ALTERNATIVES TO A FRENCH REWEAVE

A. Other replacement or reinforcing textile.
The dated strip could in principle contain later linen without the particular
French-reweaving technique being correctly identified. This preserves the genuine
sampling objection but still requires physical evidence for the added material,
its geometry and its large carbon contribution. A hypothesis should not be
declared proved just by changing the repair's name.

B. Surface deposits: handling, smoke, waxes, biological films or conservation products.
Such material can alter dates if enough survives pretreatment. Gove and colleagues
published an actual contamination investigation, including discrepant ibis wrapping
and tissue dates. But rewrapping and biological carbon sources complicate the ibis
inference. A microbe consuming old linen does not automatically add modern carbon.
No sufficient surviving Shroud coating is established by that experiment.

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-583X(96)00718-5

C. Carbon exchange during fire.
Heating does not automatically reset a radiocarbon clock. The proposed mechanism
requires carbon exchange or another isotopic change. Austin Long's experiment
heated old cotton in radiocarbon-enriched CO2 and found no measurable transfer.
It tests a cellulose comparator under specified conditions, not every fire/storage
combination. The reviewed publisher abstract supports a negative result for that
mechanism, not a universal proof about all contamination.

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200017884

D. Radiocarbon-enriched carbon monoxide.
Oxford explicitly investigated Jackson's proposal: enriched contaminant carbon
could have a large effect at much smaller mass fractions. Initial high-CO exposure
tests found no significant reaction with linen. Oxford leaves specific historical
conditions open but reports no direct evidence establishing the required effect.
This is an investigated alternative, presently without a demonstrated sufficient
effect on the Shroud.

https://c14.arch.ox.ac.uk/shroud.html

E. Irradiation changing the isotope content.
Neutron capture can create carbon-14. Phillips proposed this in 1989, and later
authors develop irradiation scenarios. Establishing the nuclear possibility is
different from establishing that this cloth experienced the required exposure.
The reviewed literature does not independently measure that historical event.
A model fitted to explain the carbon date cannot count as independent evidence
that the event occurred. Phillips's original correspondence was not obtained in
full; the proposal is described in NIST and later proponent papers.

Proposal: https://doi.org/10.1038/337594a0
Technical context: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4853109/
Recent proponent synthesis: https://doi.org/10.54026/ARS/1034

F. Laboratory, statistical or sample-identity problems.
The original report itself acknowledges excess scatter and changes its uncertainty
treatment. Later analyses identify heterogeneity and infer a spatial pattern from
incompletely documented sublocations. Different pretreatment effects are another
published explanation. This matters to the precision and interpretation of the
pooled date. It does not by itself demonstrate a thirteen-century bias or identify
the cause. No positive evidence establishing a sample substitution was found in
the material reports reviewed here.

https://doi.org/10.1111/arcm.12467
https://doi.org/10.3390/e22090926
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2019.102015

5. POLLEN AND OTHER ORGANIC MATERIAL: PALESTINE VERSUS FRANCE

Four questions must be answered separately: is the particle securely associated
with the cloth; is its identification reliable; is its distribution geographically
diagnostic; and when/how was it deposited? A verified eastern assemblage could
favor eastern contact over an exclusively local French history. That would be
positive evidence. Its weight depends on these questions, not merely on whether
later transfer is conceivable.

Frei's original report describes real dust sampling and claimed identifications
supporting an eastern itinerary. Danin and Baruch combine pollen with proposed
floral images and overlapping plant distributions. Danin's later conference paper
argues for Jerusalem/Hebron and springtime from interpreted plant images. These
are the affirmative claims to evaluate, not an argument invented by later popular
writers. Pollen under a microscope and a proposed flower outline on a photograph
are nevertheless different kinds of observation, not automatic independent
confirmation of one another.

Frei: https://www.shroud.com/pdfs/ssi03part3.pdf
Danin/Baruch: https://www.shroud.com/pdfs/daninx.pdf
Danin: https://ohioshroudconference.com/papers/p05.pdf

Boi's peer-reviewed reinterpretation identifies the supposed dominant Gundelia
type as Helichrysum and discusses Cistus, Ferula and Pistacia. She interprets this
as support for burial ointments compatible with ancient practices. That affirmative
interpretation belongs in the audit too. However, she compared published images
with modern reference grains, rather than collecting a new controlled Shroud set.
The pollen does not uniquely identify Palestine, directly establish chemical
residues of specific ointments, or date their application. The alternative
identification weakens a geographically narrow argument based on Gundelia; it
does not establish that the cloth never encountered the Middle East.

https://doi.org/10.1111/arcm.12269

Danin's 2010 book is located on the author's flora website, but its download was
unavailable in this pass. A contemporary review recounts reservations arising
from Thomas Litt's examination. Litt's original report was not independently
obtained. Those access limits are recorded rather than treating a secondhand
quotation as a newly inspected laboratory report.

https://flora.org.il/en/books/botany-of-the-shroud-2/

The 2015 DNA paper reports Near Eastern as well as European and South Asian
lineages, and mixed plant DNA. Its authors explicitly accommodate both an eastern
journey and European contact with geographically diverse visitors. Native range,
current range and the place a transported particle was deposited are not identical.
The study does not supply a clean Palestine-versus-France classification.

https://doi.org/10.1038/srep14484

The 2026 study uses different material and sequencing methods. It identifies a
predominant human lineage matching the modern sample collector, other lineages
including a Near Eastern-associated one, and mixed environmental organisms. Its
authors conclude that metagenomics cannot establish medieval versus ancient age.
The separately dated loose repair-associated threads do not locate a repair at
the 1988 strip. Detectable biological contamination is not evidence that enough
young carbon survived 1988 pretreatment to cause the measured shift.

https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-60684-7

Provenance conclusion: suggestive eastern associations remain in the literature,
but there is no independently dated, uniquely Palestinian biological signature
established by the reviewed studies. European traces do not refute an ancient
eastern history, and eastern traces alone do not establish a first-century one.
Blinded palynological re-identification, modern reference assemblages, controls
and contamination histories would make the evidence more discriminating.

6. THE ALTERNATIVE DATES ARE RELEVANT, WITH THEIR OWN SAMPLING CONDITIONS

FTIR/Raman, mechanical testing and WAXS are positive ancient-compatible findings.
They are not four independent replications by four laboratories. Mechanical work
reports loose fibers from vacuum filter h near the rear gluteal region, distinct
from the carbon-dating corner. WAXS used a tiny thread near the Raes/radiocarbon
area. The latter cannot simply be treated as unquestionably original cloth while
all neighboring material is assumed replaced. Neither is an exact map of the
material consumed in 1988. Calibration and environmental aging remain substantive
questions, not reasons to dismiss the results solely because the methods are new.

https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/20153601001
https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage5020047

7. NUMERICAL ODDS: A TRANSPARENT SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS

It was too categorical to say that odds cannot be attached. Bayesian odds can be
calculated under explicit assumptions; the papers do not supply all those inputs.
Giving a number without showing them would conceal the main disagreement.

For illustration compare A, first-century original linen, with M, medieval original
linen. This is a two-hypothesis illustration, not an exhaustive chronology and not
a probability that the cloth belonged to Jesus. Let E be a broadly medieval carbon
result. Let pi be the probability of A before adding E, using the other evidence;
q=P(E|A) includes whatever mechanisms could make ancient cloth measure medieval;
m=P(E|M). Then:

  P(A|E, A-or-M) = pi*q / [pi*q + (1-pi)*m].

Illustration with ASSUMED m=0.95:

  Assumed prior ancient       q=0.05       q=0.20       q=0.50
            50%                  5.0%         17.4%         34.5%
            80%                 17.4%         45.7%         67.8%
            95%                 50.0%         80.0%         90.9%

All inputs are assumptions, not fitted probabilities. In particular, m=0.95 is NOT
derived from the published 95% confidence interval. q is not the posterior chance
that a repair exists. If sample-inspection evidence enters the prior/conditioning,
it must not be counted again as an independent likelihood factor.

Thus an 80/20 ancient conclusion can be expressed coherently: for example, prior
95%, q=20%, m=95% gives posterior 80%. Defending it requires defending those inputs
and their dependencies. Neither 80/20 nor a reverse numerical conclusion follows
from counting papers, counting laboratories or multiplying unmeasured tiny odds.
Equal starting odds are one sensitivity case, not an objectively neutral default.

8. WHAT THIS AUDIT CHANGES

The repair objection has to be engaged as an empirical sampling claim. The original
three-laboratory result does not make the question disappear. Conversely, a
photographically subtle repair must be supported in the actual sample, at a scale
capable of producing the isotope measurements. The primary evidence is conflicting
and incomplete, rather than uniformly pro-repair or uniformly cotton-free.

The botanical evidence is weaker as a geographic discriminator than the simple
claim that Palestinian pollen rules out medieval France. That finding is about
identification, sample history and comparative likelihood, not a rule excluding
religious conclusions. Nor does failure of one repair account automatically prove
a medieval image-making process.

My qualitative preference on scientific cloth dating remains medieval, with a
material representativeness uncertainty that requires new sampling. This audit
does not produce a calibrated probability for that preference. It supplies the
physical constraints and explicit numerical assumptions needed to debate odds
without disguising judgments as measurements.

The highest-value next evidence remains independently characterized original-weave
samples from separated locations, plus authenticated retained-corner analysis.
Published raw data, sample photographs, chemical mapping and separately dated
suspected components would test the competing explanations directly.
