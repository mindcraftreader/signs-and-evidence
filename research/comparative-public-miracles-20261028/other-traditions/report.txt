MODERN MIRACLE COMPARISON: BUDDHISM, ISLAM, SIKHISM
Research pass: 28 October 2026. Scope updated to media/scientific-age claims only.

ASSESSMENT
This bounded search found serious modern non-Christian anomalous-body reports and actual medical investigations. It did not establish a counterpart combining Fatima's advance public sign announcement and large contemporary crowd, or Zeitoun's prolonged repeated public humanlike apparitions. This is an evidential finding from the sources inspected, not a proof that no comparable case exists anywhere. Nor does the existence of another tradition's reports establish equal evidential quality or incompatible supernatural agency.

The strongest next audits are the Tibetan 37-day preservation record and the medically monitored Sufi piercing case. Their small witness populations differ fundamentally from a public apparition. Crowds attending ceremonies, viewing a preserved corpse, or trying to see light are never counted here as independently documented observers of the underlying miracle. No modern levitation before 100,000 observers was established.

1. KHENPO A CHO: ALLEGED RAINBOW BODY, EASTERN TIBET, 1998
Francis V. Tiso, a Catholic priest and scholar of Tibetan Buddhism, publishes taped interviews conducted in Tibet in July 2000 and India in February 2001. Named witnesses include Lama Norta, Rinchen Tsering and Sonam Gyamtso. They describe pleasant fragrance, rainbows and music, shrinking beneath a robe, and an empty covering after seven days. About six were present at death; the disappearance was not observed by a mass audience. Tiso's actual witness interviews explicitly say no photographs were made of the body and it remained covered during the interval. The younger monks did not inspect it uncovered then. One reported two or three hairs remaining; another reported nothing. These are substantial modern testimony, including fragrance comparable in category to saintly-scent claims, but retrospective interviews and an unmonitored covered body do not document continuous bodily dissolution. No fraud is demonstrated by that limitation. The claim's Buddhist religious meaning is explicit; Tiso explores Christian parallels rather than simply denying it.
Sources: Tiso, Rainbow Body and Resurrection (North Atlantic Books, 2016), chapter 1, interview sequences beginning July 20, 2000 and February 11, 2001. Full text locally retained for checking; public mirror provenance is not publisher-authenticated.

2. TIBETAN TUKDAM: 37-DAY POSTMORTEM CASE, INDIA, MARCH 2021
University of Wisconsin-Madison biocultural anthropologist and Tibetan medical doctor Tawni L. Tidwell reports a senior monk's unusually prolonged preservation in Culture, Medicine and Psychiatry (online 2024; issue 2025). An attendant collected daily photographs/video and environmental/body-temperature data. The report describes supple skin, limited discoloration and marked resistance to decomposition across 37 days. This merits serious inspection, rather than being dismissed because it is Buddhist. The body generally remained at room temperature, approximately 70-74 degrees F; hand-perceived heart warmth was not confirmed by infrared readings. Drying began around week three, with clear decay signs and odor by day 37. Pandemic restrictions required remote/image-based monitoring through an attendant. This is observational preservation evidence, not a demonstrated return to life, levitation, or proof that consciousness sustained tissue. The paper does not furnish a completed matched forensic decomposition experiment establishing the probability of its course under all relevant ordinary conditions.
Paper: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12325478/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11013-023-09844-2
Author credentials: https://www.centerhealthyminds.org/people/tawni-tidwell
Project design/control plans: https://www.tukdamproject.org/about
Related EEG study by University of Wisconsin-Madison-led team: 13 cases recorded, seven usable in analysis; no recognizable EEG activity from the earliest recording at 26 hours onward. That negative result concerns a particular brain-activity hypothesis and does not itself refute preservation reports.

3. SIXTEENTH KARMAPA'S POSTMORTEM WARMTH, ILLINOIS, 1981
Physician Mitchell Levy personally reports warmth over the heart region after the Karmapa's clinical death and the body remaining in the hospital for three days. Levy's medical standing is independently verified by Brown University; he is also a Buddhist practitioner. Devotional witnesses and medical credentials are both disclosed rather than treating either as decisive. His narrative concerns a limited group at an American hospital, not thousands witnessing a miraculous physical event. The available account is retrospective, and no original contemporaneous temperature chart establishing persistent heat generation was recovered. Treat as a medically witnessed report deserving the original hospital documentation, not as an already measured violation of thermodynamics. It is closely related to tukdam, so it cannot be multiplied as an independent kind of miracle merely because it has a separate name.

4. DASHI-DORZHO ITIGILOV'S PRESERVED BODY, 1927-2002 ONWARD
New York Times reporter Steven Lee Myers documented the September 2002 exhumation shortly afterward, including a dozen witnesses, two forensic experts and a photographer. The preserved body is a continuing physical object. The burial predates the scientific inspection by 75 years; earlier openings are retrospectively reported. The same contemporary account records a previous reburial with salt, a concrete preservation variable rather than an invented accusation. Russian reports in 2004 describe examinations by Viktor Zvyagin, head of personal identification at the Russian Centre of Forensic Medical Expertise, and claims of flexible tissues and well-preserved skin/hair/nails. The primary laboratory protocol/data were not recovered in this pass. Preservation alone must not be called a living body; salt does not automatically explain every claimed feature either. Large pilgrimage attendance today is not the witness count for the original 1927 death or the full 75-year custody.
Contemporary report preserved on University of Pennsylvania site (NYT, October 1, 2002): https://rees.sas.upenn.edu/about/spotlight/lama-itigilov-ivolgin-datsan-buryatiya-russia
2004 local reporting: https://www.infpol.ru/167491-uchenye-priznali-fenomen-itigelova/

5. BUDDHA STATUE 'RAYS', SRI LANKA, AUGUST 2006
Contemporaneous PTI and AFP reports establish widespread public statue-gazing and reports of luminous outlines/rays. PTI's headline says millions, while AFP says thousands; neither is an audited count of unique people actually perceiving a miraculous light. PTI describes a white line at a colored robe/skin boundary after several minutes of gazing. AFP quotes electronics engineer R. Weerasekara saying he saw rays and regarded them as an optical illusion; monk Pitiduwe Siridamma rejected the miracle interpretation. Those are contemporary counterinterpretations, not a recovered controlled optical experiment covering every site. This is a genuine modern mass religious visual claim worth recognizing, but the inspected record does not document a predicted event or detached moving humanoid light resembling the detailed Zeitoun reports. No reported personal spiritual sincerity is equated with deceit.
PTI, August 2, 2006: https://www.buddhistchannel.tv/index.php?id=43%2C2986%2C0%2C0%2C1%2C0
AFP, August 2, 2006: https://wwrn.org/articles/22316/

6. SUFI CHEEK PIERCING / RAPID CLOSURE, CLEVELAND, REPORTED 2001
Howard Hall and colleagues at Case Western Reserve University's Division of Behavioral Pediatrics report a monitored Sufi practitioner's metal skewer passing through the cheeks. X-ray and CT confirmed its presence; the paper reports left puncture closure within two minutes, the right three-quarters closed after eight hours, and no subjective pain. EEG was inconclusive and immunological recordings unchanged. This is an actual medical publication with physical examination, not just a generic story of Islamic healing. It is one participant and a small professional audience. Wound closure, tissue elasticity and regeneration of destroyed tissue are different outcomes: the abstract does not supply a matched non-Sufi control experiment quantifying how extraordinary these closure times are. Full original paper was not recovered; only its original indexed abstract and publication metadata were verified, so detailed protocol conclusions remain provisional. No ordinary explanation was demonstrated in the material inspected. It is a stronger lead for sustained embodied anomalous-religious practice than for a Fatima-size public sign.
Hall et al., Advances in Mind-Body Medicine 17(3):203-209 (2001): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11572849/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1054/ambm.2000.0314
Peer-reviewed 2004 review by Hall and University of Arizona psychologist Gary E. Schwartz: https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/rapid-wound-healing-a-sufi-perspective/
Hall credentials: https://gsapp.rutgers.edu/howard-hall

7. IMAM REZA SHRINE PARAPLEGIA HEALING: PUBLISHED LEAD, PROVENANCE UNRESOLVED
Tehran University of Medical Sciences nursing researchers AkramSadat SadatHoseini, Habib Shareinia, Shahzad Pashaeypoor and Kermanshah colleague MohammadMehdi Mohammadi discuss a 50-year-old woman with eight years of T7-T8 paraplegia, improving after a shrine visit and subsequent physiotherapy. They mention pre/post records, MRI/NCV/EMG and shrine-committee approval. The crucial methodological qualification is that this appears under 'Constructing a model case' within nursing concept analysis, using ten qualitative interviews, not as a full neurological case report. No named patient, event date, raw diagnostic images or standalone neurological report are provided. This is therefore a serious lead to the Commission for Healed Affairs of Astan Quds Razavi, but the paper alone does not establish independently verified spinal-cord regeneration. Its genre/provenance must be clarified before comparison with an authenticated cure. Neither a hypothetical counterdiagnosis nor the journal's existence resolves that question.
BMC Nursing 22:252 (2023): https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-023-01404-8
Full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12912-023-01404-8

8. VASU BHARDWAJ'S SIKH PRAYER-ASSOCIATED CANCER REMISSION, 2002
Bhardwaj's published first-person narrative links dramatic recovery to Gurbani prayer at the Golden Temple, names physicians and gives May 2002 marrow/electrophoresis tests. It also explicitly records six chemotherapy sessions. The text inconsistently calls the disease 'multiple melanomas' despite describing marrow/electrophoresis and bone disease; the precise original diagnosis requires original records. There are also internal timeline discrepancies between the introductory 2001 diagnosis and the narrative's three-and-a-half-year illness ending in 2002. These are specific documentary issues, not grounds to dismiss Sikh experience wholesale. Juniata College religious-studies scholar Susan E. Prill's Oxford chapter identifies this as a prominent modern Sikh miraculous-healing narrative and points to scans formerly hosted by the promoting mission. No independently reviewed full oncology dossier was recovered here. This is one patient's cure claim with potentially retrievable records, not a mass public apparition. Chemotherapy is a documented co-intervention; its causal adequacy still requires medical analysis.
First-person text: https://sikhnet.com/news/story-vasu-bhardwaj
Prill's scholarly treatment (2011): https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198075547.003.0007
Prill's credentials: https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34369/chapter-abstract/291509867

BOUNDARIES AND RECOMMENDED NEXT WORK
This pass covered English-accessible primary reporting, the original Tiso interview corpus, peer-reviewed biomedical/anthropological papers and institutional texts. It is not an exhaustive search of Arabic, Persian, Tibetan, Sinhala or Punjabi archives. No ancient foundational case is used to answer the user's modern-age challenge.

Prioritize: (1) independent forensic review of the 2021 tukdam image/temperature protocol and control baseline; (2) Hall's original 2001 full paper, video and later replication history; (3) original Imam Reza commission files after resolving whether the published model case is a single actual case; (4) Bhardwaj's actual diagnosis/treatment/remission documents. A demonstrated ordinary explanation should be recorded as such only when a real test or adequately applicable case analysis establishes it. An unresolved case is not automatically a miracle; an uncompleted mechanism is not automatically a refutation.
