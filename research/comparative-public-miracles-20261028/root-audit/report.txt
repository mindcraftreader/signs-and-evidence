ADDITIONAL PRIMARY-SOURCE AUDIT
28 October 2026

Scope: modern/media-age candidates supplementing the Hindu, Jewish and other-tradition reports. A bounded comparison, not a census of all religious claims. A candidate's resemblance to a Christian claim does not establish equal documentation, authenticity, or a common explanation.

PUBLIC ACCESS AND NUMBERS
Separate attendance, opportunities to observe, claimed direct observation and independently documented observation. This is especially important for comparisons between a lifetime of ministry and one levitation performance. Neither the size of a following nor total Mass/darshan attendance establishes that all attendees closely inspected wounds or saw a purported object production. Conversely, limited clinical inspection does not erase broader reports of publicly visible wounds or fragrance. No comparison located here establishes a modern yogi levitating before 100,000 direct observers.

1. SATHYA SAI BABA: A PARTICULAR PREANNOUNCED PUBLIC EVENT
The first-party Sai Baba of India compilation preserves devotee accounts of 4 October 2007. It attributes to Prof. Anil Kumar an announcement of an extraordinary universal-form viewing toward the airport before 7 p.m. Thousands gathered. Named devotee Satish Naik describes expectations, confusion, Sai Baba remaining in his car, and departure around 7:40 p.m.; he concludes that the anticipated manifestation was perhaps postponed. This is significant because disappointment is recorded by a supporter, not inferred solely from an opponent's accusation.

The page separately preserves named claims of faces seen in the moon on other dates, including August and later October 2007. Those reports must not be silently merged with the airport event. Several illustrations are explicitly composites or depictions of what witnesses say they saw. They cannot be represented as authenticated photographs of an anomalous lunar image. This pass has not separately settled all those perceptions.

Thus this candidate resembles Fatima in advance gathering, but does not supply an equally documented successful sign at the appointed airport event. The page supports that restricted conclusion, not a blanket verdict about every Sai Baba claim.
Source: https://www.saibabaofindia.com/news4oct_sai_baba.htm

2. SAI BABA AS A LONG-RUNNING PERSONAL COMPARATOR
University of Iceland emeritus psychology professor Erlendur Haraldsson's own 2013 Goldsmiths abstract describes direct close observations, interviews and reports of apparent materializations, distant fragrance and other phenomena. It also records refusal to participate in the investigators' proposed scientific investigation. This is a substantive investigative lead, not merely a large following. Institutional affiliation establishes who investigated; it does not certify a paranormal outcome. The source does not provide an audited population of observers who clearly saw each phenomenon. The Hindu report follows Haraldsson's retrospective account further.
Source: https://www.gold.ac.uk/calendar/?id=6705

3. THE BAB'S EXECUTION, TABRIZ, 1850
An unusually valuable contemporary non-Christian comparison is British envoy Justin Sheil's 22 July 1850 dispatch to Lord Palmerston, reproduced by Moojan Momen in The Babi and Baha'i Religions 1844–1944 (1981), printed pp.77–78, PDF pp.72–73 (zero-based viewer numbering). It reports the Bab's disappearance after the first volley, broken ropes, discovery in a recess and subsequent shooting. The archival reference is FO 60/152, dispatch88.

This establishes that the remarkable first-volley story circulated within weeks, beyond later devotional literature. Sheil was reporting information, not personally witnessing it. The same collection identifies the British consul's absence and reproduces a Russian consular account without this particular detail. The dispatch does not verify the later full bundle of crowd size, precise number of soldiers, or a recorded advance prediction. It records a physical escape and an immediate religious interpretation, but does not by itself establish supernatural disappearance. This early press/diplomatic-era case is relevant, while not matching 20th-century photographic coverage or years of repeated public appearances.
Source: https://bahai-library.com/pdf/m/momen_babi_bahai_religions_1844-1944.pdf

4. LEVITATION LEAD: SUBBAYAH PULLAVAR, 1936
The Journal of the American Society for Psychical Research, vol.30 (1936), contains Nandor Fodor's letter reproducing P. T. Plunkett's account and discussing photographs published by the Illustrated London News on 6 June 1936. This is a genuine near-contemporary documentary trail, not only an internet legend. Visible support involved a cloth-wrapped stick; the original description and procedures must be audited before calling the event either authenticated levitation or demonstrated fraud. The Illustrated London News publication date is not automatically the performance date. A renewed web-tool fetch failed because the annual PDF is over18MB; the initial search recovered the pertinent text, but no complete local visual audit was completed in this root pass. Therefore no precise audience or duration figure is admitted here. It does not establish a 100,000-observer levitation.
Source: https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/aspr_proceedings/aspr_journal_v30_1936.pdf

INFERENCE
These sources supply actual modern non-Christian extraordinary claims and some identifiable investigators. Their existence is not a rebuttal of Fatima, Zeitoun or Pio. Equally, one should not infer that alternative traditions have only ancient or solitary undocumented anecdotes. Whether a particular event establishes a supernatural cause, and whether that cause vindicates an entire religion, remain distinct inferences. A successful bodily anomaly investigation would support an anomaly; identifying its theological significance requires additional argument.
