COMPARATIVE PUBLIC MIRACLES: HINDUISM
Research date: 28 October 2026
Scope: bounded first research pass; strongest modern public candidate, a sustained personal candidate, a medical-observation candidate, all within the media/scientific age. This is not an exhaustive proof of religious uniqueness.

ASSESSMENT
CURRENT DISPOSITION, 1 NOVEMBER 2026: The 1995 milk event is excluded as a serious rival supernatural case in the cumulative Fatima/Zeitoun/Pio comparison. It is nowhere near Fatima in evidential strength for supernatural intervention on the reviewed record. Give it no positive weight as an authenticated competing divine sign, and do not use a bare mass-witness analogy to offset the Christian cases. The earlier 'partial match / not equally strong' wording understated the disparity. Reopening requires new substantive evidence and an explicit argument addressing ordinary-object replication and original-site runoff/dye reports. Preserve the historical documents and limits; this is not a claim that every witness was dishonest or every subsidiary report was separately tested. No new tally point or numerical probability is assigned. Full follow-up: ../../comparative-public-miracles-followup-20261101/research-report.txt.

The 21 September 1995 milk event is the strongest public-scale Hindu comparison located in this pass. It is a real mass religious episode supported by contemporary reporting, named witnesses, press photography and international spread. It should not be dismissed as a solitary late legend. It does not currently match Fatima's documented advance gathering for a dated promised public sign. Moreover, ordinary liquid-flow mechanisms were demonstrated contemporaneously in this case; that is a stronger objection than merely proposing that large groups can be mistaken. This does not establish that every extraordinary claim in every temple was explained.

Sathya Sai Baba is a more relevant comparison for Pio's sustained personal phenomena, including fragrance claims. He has a serious firsthand investigator and substantial original literature; declaring all his phenomena conclusively fraudulent would outrun the evidence examined here. Conversely, firsthand observation without controls is not a successful controlled materialization experiment. Prahlad Jani supplies an unusually explicit named medical-institutional report, but its authors themselves sharply restrict what they established.

A collection of comparable FEATURES is not an evidential tie. None of these comparisons by itself disproves Fatima, Zeitoun or Pio. The proper outcome is to identify where the documentation actually overlaps and where it does not. The broad claim that no other tradition has modern public miracle claims with large numbers, cross-group observers and contemporary reporting is too strong. The narrower claim that no equally well-supported counterpart matches the complete Christian combination has not been falsified by this bounded pass, nor exhaustively established.

1. THE 1995 MILK EVENT: CONTEMPORARY POSITIVE RECORD
UPI's 22 September 1995 dispatch reports nationwide crowds and milk shortages, with named believer Gopal Prasad recounting the spoon emptying for his family. It also names Damodar Yadav, who saw milk dripping down an idol. The dispatch provides contemporary opposed observations, not an audit of the total number who personally observed an anomalous disappearance. Its millions figure concerns claims, not independently verified case files. [H01]

Hinduism Today's November 1995 coverage (reprinted in Loving Ganesha) preserves reports from multiple countries and named observers. Edmonton barrister Aran Veylan estimates sustained consumption over 51.5 hours, says garments became wet but that this could not account for the offered milk; other witnesses challenge porous-stone explanations with metal objects and lack of apparent runoff. These are positive claims requiring investigation, not observations to erase because witnesses are religious. December coverage includes Nairobi businessman Vijay Bhagani's recollection that Morari Bapu predicted, two years earlier, a miracle and avatar that year. No original pre-September-1995 record was located, and the recalled prediction specifies neither date, place nor milk. [H02, H03]

2. WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DEMONSTRATED?
The Times of India, 22 September 1995, reports reporters Ravi Bhatia and N. Suresh's office experiment: photographer K. K. Laskar emptied a spoon against a clay Ganesha, then repeated disappearance with water and a plastic jug. The same report describes CSIR scientist Biman Basu's team visiting three Delhi temples, photographs of recovered runoff, and NCSTC scientists colouring milk to trace it down an idol. It also quotes Delhi University science professor A. M. Gangadharan Nair supporting a miracle after his own inspection. This source gives concrete tests and disagreement, rather than generic skepticism. However, the original dye videos, lab notebooks and quantitative recovery measurements were not recovered. The newspaper's own preservation status is a reprint created 23 September 1995 on a TIFR-hosted page, not an authenticated newspaper scan. [H04]

T. Jayaraman of the Institute of Mathematical Sciences, Madras, explains wetting, surface tension and gravity-driven runoff. He reports personally witnessing a demonstration by T. V. Venkateswaran of the Tamil Nadu Science Forum. He proposes measuring input milk against plate recovery, including on smooth metal; he does not publish a completed numerical original-temple recovery experiment here. His polemical insistence that contradictory claims must be fantasy is not itself a measurement. The evidential contribution is the demonstrated appearance and testable mechanism. [H05]

INTERPRETATION OF THE MECHANISM
The hypothesis is not simply that a small statue stores gallons inside itself. A thin flowing film can leave the spoon and continue to the base or drainage. Therefore metal composition and finite internal volume alone do not refute it. The strongest remaining claim would be substantial measured missing milk with the statue and all outputs isolated. The named no-runoff reports matter, but estimation amid continual offerings is different from closed input/output accounting. A proposed mechanism is strengthened by observed runoff and a dye-tracer test; it remains inappropriate to assert an exact balance was established in every cited case. A claimed sudden ending also needs same-object, same-angle, same-wetting before/after trials; no such systematic series was recovered.

3. SAI BABA'S SUSTAINED PHENOMENA
Erlendur Haraldsson, University of Iceland emeritus psychology professor, described his research at Goldsmiths, University of London, in October 2013. His abstract lists materializations, multiplication of food, alleged resurrections, distant ash, fragrance and apparitions; it explicitly records Sai Baba declining scientific participation. Goldsmiths verifies his doctorate at Freiburg and University of Iceland affiliation. This is the investigator describing his work, not a university endorsement of miracles. [H06]

Haraldsson's own retrospective Society for Psychical Research article describes direct 1973-74 observations with Karlis Osis: objects apparently appearing, a rudraksha subsequently assessed as gold and ruby, and the unexplained disappearance of a ring insert. It includes supportive interviews with former devotees, alongside criticisms. It reports Haraldsson and psychologist Richard Wiseman finding the 1992 television handoff footage suspicious but insufficient to prove an object transfer. The 1995 underlying paper was identified but not recovered in this pass, so the article is the direct investigator's retrospective account rather than a fresh audit of the video. That caution must apply symmetrically: unexplained observations do not equal authenticated paranormal creation; suspicious footage does not establish that every episode was fraudulent. [H07]

The multi-decade duration and several types of reported phenomena make Sai Baba relevant to Pio, rather than a precise Fatima substitute. The scale and independence of fragrance testimony need their own source audit; they have not been measured here. His reputation cannot be counted as the number of people who closely saw a specific production.

4. PRAHLAD JANI: A NAMED MEDICAL OBSERVATION
Neurologist Sudhir Shah of Sterling Hospital and physician Urman Dhruv of Medisurge Hospital host an original report attributed to the director of the Defence Institute of Physiology and Allied Sciences (DIPAS), within India's DRDO. It concerns observation from 22 April to 6 May 2010, using CCTV and clinical/laboratory examinations. The report says Jani consumed nothing and passed no stool, while acknowledging bathing and gargling from day five. Its no-urine claim was still to be scientifically substantiated. Crucially, the doctors expressly say 15 days cannot establish his claimed years without food or water and that publication would follow further analysis. This is meaningful institutional firsthand documentation, not a peer-reviewed complete physiology dataset. No complete published independent replication or open raw surveillance/physiology record was recovered. It cannot support the stronger statement that decades-long inedia were medically verified. Neither does the limited record demonstrate fraud. Pages 2-3 were visually inspected. [H08]

PUBLIC EXPOSURE CAUTION
These are different units: a person waiting at a temple, a person personally offering milk, a person seeing a nearby spoon empty, a close observer of a Sai Baba object production, and an adherent attending darshan. None should silently become another. UPI and the Times of India show many direct participant claims; the global numerator is not audited. Sai Baba's enormous following and public assemblies cannot establish how many clearly saw a production close enough to exclude concealment. Haraldsson's direct sessions provide identifiable observations; a global direct-witness number was not established. Fragrance reports in his corpus establish a relevant claimed phenomenon, without establishing a denominator comparable to the broad public access claimed for Pio's scent and wounds. Geographic spread can support replication of the observable spoon-emptying appearance while phone/TV transmission remains the documented way participants learned to try it. This is neither proof that all witness reports were copied nor proof that every site was a wholly independent unprompted discovery.
