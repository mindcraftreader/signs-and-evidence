ARGUMENT CHAINS

Identifying the man
Premises: Crucifixion, a thorn crown, a pierced side, scourging and facial injury are interpreted as a distinctive combination.
Conclusion offered: Dalton identifies Jesus as the best historical candidate.
Dependency: The identification depends on the wound interpretations and surviving historical comparisons.
Entries: 110, 111, 268

Blood before body image
Premises: Investigators are said to find no body-image coloration beneath blood. Dalton expects a painter to draw the body first.
Conclusion offered: The reported sequence is presented as unexpected for deliberate artwork.
Dependency: The physical sequence and assumptions about a maker’s procedure are separate premises.
Entries: 100, 101

Departures from devotional convention
Premises: A cap of thorns and wrist-area wounds differ from familiar artistic depictions.
Conclusion offered: A conventional devotional forger would have made different choices, Dalton argues.
Dependency: This is an argument about expected choices; it does not exclude every possible maker.
Entries: 121, 125, 133, 271

Chemistry and the failure of replicas
Premises: Superficial coloration, absent brushwork, transmitted-light behavior, fluorescence and claimed microscopic differences are combined.
Conclusion offered: Dalton concludes that available artistic or photographic replicas do not explain the complete image.
Dependency: A convincing visible replica is acknowledged; his objection concerns the combination of properties.
Entries: 014, 017, 018, 020, 024, 028, 274, 275, 276, 287

Disagreement among alternative models
Premises: Critics propose different mechanisms, including painting, rubbing, scorching and photography.
Conclusion offered: Dalton treats their lack of agreement as an additional weakness.
Dependency: The alternatives need not agree for a particular one to be testable; disagreement is not an experiment.
Entries: 270

Dating a sample versus dating a whole object
Premises: All laboratories sampled one region, which Dalton says had anomalous chemistry.
Conclusion offered: Even accurate measurement could misdate the whole cloth if that region were unrepresentative.
Dependency: Whether the corner was actually unrepresentative in age is the contested empirical premise.
Entries: 046, 047, 048, 051, 277

Invisible repair and the medieval result
Premises: Cotton, purported gum, splice observations and historic reweaving are proposed as evidence of mixed-age material.
Conclusion offered: Old linen combined with newer repairs could yield an intermediate radiocarbon date.
Dependency: Dalton calls this his preferred hypothesis, not a proved repair. No first-century date follows without further premises.
Entries: 069, 070, 071, 073, 074, 075, 076

Statistical disagreement and a local date gradient
Premises: Different measurements and a proposed gradient across the sampled strip are cited.
Conclusion offered: Dalton questions treating the sample as homogeneous and asks for renewed dating.
Dependency: The critique is distinct from a retraction and does not by itself supply a first-century date.
Entries: 054, 057, 058, 059, 060, 062

Geographical provenance
Premises: Particles allegedly match Jerusalem limestone; pollen is identified with local spring flora.
Conclusion offered: Dalton argues that these traces favor a Palestinian setting over an entirely European origin.
Dependency: The argument depends on identification, geographic specificity, custody and exclusion of later contamination.
Entries: 166, 167, 168, 169, 170, 171, 172, 173

The Sudarium as an earlier chronological anchor
Premises: The facial stains are said to correspond and the Oviedo cloth is assigned an early provenance.
Conclusion offered: If the donor and event were the same, the Shroud would be at least as old as that earlier anchor.
Dependency: Both the same-donor inference and the chronological anchor require independent support.
Entries: 161, 162, 163, 164

Images and manuscripts before Lirey
Premises: Earlier icons, the Pray Codex and descriptions of the Edessan image are compared with the cloth.
Conclusion offered: Dalton argues that the object may predate the medieval carbon interval and the secure Lirey record.
Dependency: The comparisons are proposed identifications, not a continuous documented custody chain.
Entries: 175, 180, 189, 190, 192, 195, 196, 198, 199, 200, 202

Actual death rather than survival
Premises: The side wound, proposed blood separation and serum halos are interpreted as postmortem features.
Conclusion offered: Dalton uses them against survival and subsequent resuscitation accounts.
Dependency: Authentication and the medical interpretation are necessary premises.
Entries: 147, 148, 151, 152, 108, 109, 285, 295

A short interval in the cloth
Premises: Rigor, no visible decay and alleged fibrinolytic transfer timing are combined with Friday-evening burial.
Conclusion offered: Dalton places separation within roughly thirty to thirty-six hours, compatible with early Sunday.
Dependency: The exact timing depends on unestablished biological and environmental assumptions.
Entries: 155, 156, 157, 158, 159, 279, 280, 281

The burial cloths as a sign in John
Premises: Plural cloths, their position, the separate face cloth and repeated narrative attention precede seeing and believing.
Conclusion offered: Dalton proposes intact collapsed wrappings as the intelligible sign that led John toward belief.
Dependency: The text does not explicitly identify the Turin cloth, describe its image or state a physical disappearance mechanism.
Entries: 207, 208, 210, 211, 212, 213, 214, 215, 216, 218, 288, 289, 290

The cumulative case and the final step
Premises: Dalton combines the preceding lines rather than resting everything on one measurement. He distinguishes unexplained image formation from a proven miracle.
Conclusion offered: His personal conclusion is a physical image caused in connection with the Resurrection, while Christianity does not depend on this relic.
Dependency: The interview offers a cumulative judgment and theological interpretation, not a calculation proving every component independent or a repeatable Resurrection experiment.
Entries: 268, 269, 272, 273, 079, 221, 222, 251, 252, 253
