OUR LADY OF GHIAIE DI BONATE — INITIAL SOURCE AUDIT
Prepared 1 November 2026. Public sources only; no correspondence or account access.
Source identifiers below resolve to exact URLs and provenance in source-register.json.

FINDING
Ghiaie is a substantial Italian Marian-apparition case, not merely a recent internet story. A real 1944 pilgrimage, contemporary ecclesiastical notes, early publications, and attributed contemporary crowd footage are recoverable research leads. However, the alleged apparitions have not been recognized as supernatural. The strongest primary documents recovered here explicitly preserve that distinction. This report neither establishes supernatural agency nor demonstrates fabrication.

1. THE CLAIM AND ITS DATES
Bishop Francesco Beschi of Bergamo's 2019 letter identifies Adelaide Roncalli, aged seven, as the child who reported seeing Mary, Joseph and the infant Jesus on 13 May 1944; twelve further alleged appearances followed through 31 May. The messages, reportedly delivered in local dialect, concerned prayer, conversion and penance. [S02]
The usually listed dates are 13–21 May inclusive, then 28–31 May inclusive: thirteen occasions. These two groups also appear in the presentation of contemporary Bishop Adriano Bernareggi's diary. [S06] The apparition site was the Torchio locality at Ghiaie, in Bonate Sopra near Bergamo. A minute-by-minute original chronology has not been established in this pass.

The claimed Holy Family appearances to Adelaide must be distinguished from what surrounding crowds allegedly saw. Later narratives describe public solar rotation, colors, colored light on surroundings, and other signs; these are not evidence that an entire crowd saw the same Holy Family figures as the child. The six often repeated solar dates are 20, 21, 28 and 31 May, and 13 June and 13 July 1944. That complete list currently rests on later accounts, not a recovered original six-event dossier. [S12]

2. THE BEST EARLY TESTIMONY FOUND
Marco Roncalli's Corriere Bergamo article (12 November 2013) reproduces Bernareggi's 20 May–27 July 1944 notes. The original manuscript/full Studium edition remains a target. [S06]

For 21 May the bishop records his secretary reporting about ten minutes of solar rotation and rapid colors, colored surroundings, and comfortable viewing. The bishop himself initially noted yellow-lit houses. At a second episode around 19:45 he stared at the dazzling sun, perceived apparent rotation and redness, and judged that episode optical. These are differentiated observations, not a blanket explanation or disproof of every reported sign. The same article records reported healings and a police chief's concern on 30 May about enormous crowds blocking wartime roads. It does not establish individual diagnoses or precise headcounts. [S06]

3. PREDICTIONS: WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED
Modern devotional accounts attribute instructions to return for further evenings and an announced public sign to Mary. An original document written before the proposed sign is still needed to establish exact wording, dissemination and timing. Do not import Fatima's documented advance-announcement argument automatically into Ghiaie.

A war-end prophecy requires particularly careful comparison. Bernareggi's 20 May note already records two-month versus two-year versions in an exchange with Adelaide and her cousin. [S06] Later conditional formulations therefore cannot simply be treated as unchanged contemporary quotations. Alberto Lombardoni's pro-apparition comparison identifies several Adelaide diaries and says the earliest surviving materials include the Sacramentine sisters' daily 1944 questionnaire and report. These originals are the priority witnesses to wording, not the later summaries alone. [S11]

The locally saved handwritten diary facsimile is introduced by its host as WRITTEN IN 1953. It is valuable attributed firsthand retrospective testimony; it is not a diary demonstrably written during May 1944. I have not fully transcribed its handwritten pages. [S07]

4. PRESS, PHOTOGRAPHY AND FILM
A specific original-press target is Enrico Caffi, 'Il sole delle Ghiaie,' L'Eco di Bergamo, 26 May 1944. The title/date are repeated in later accounts; an original issue was not recovered, so neither its wording nor its eyewitness basis has been verified. Caffi's institutional standing is independently confirmed: the Bergamo civic natural-history museum identifies him as its first director and a natural-sciences graduate of the University of Pavia. His credentials are relevant background, not authentication of the article or its conclusions. [S12,S18]

Another target is La Gazzetta del Popolo (Turin), 15 July 1944, reportedly on the 13 July solar episode. Original text not obtained. [S12] The Lanfranchi collection inventory independently lists an L'Eco di Bergamo issue of 22 May 1944 and an item titled I fatti delle Ghiaie, without supplying the latter's date or contents. A holding is not proof of a specific eyewitness article. [S16]

The devotional portal La luce di Ghiaie embeds two videos attributed to Vittorio Villa, made during 28–31 May 1944 with a 9.5 mm camera. [S08–S10]

They are described as historical crowd/pilgrimage footage. The actual videos could not be fetched in this pass and HAVE NOT BEEN WATCHED or frame-audited. The original reel's chain of custody and camera format need confirmation. No authenticated moving film of Mary or a transforming solar disc was established. Their presence on YouTube is not such proof.

Storylab also hosts a historical postcard captioned as published in 1944 by Crimella, Milan, with photograph by G. Meli, Bergamo. This is an image-provenance lead; the user-supplied caption does not certify a supernatural image. [S13]

5. CROWD SIZES
Large attendance has substantially better support than any precise total. Historical notes, pictures and the attributed crowd film are useful evidence of mass pilgrimage. Common later numbers include hundreds of thousands at particular gatherings and around 1.5–1.75 million cumulative visits. They have not been traced here to contemporary enumeration with a reproducible method. Different dates, attendance totals and unique visitors must not be merged. No verified total of independently documented eyewitness accounts was established. Retain the impressive scale as a research question, not an exact headcount.

6. ACTUAL CHURCH DECISIONS
The original signed decree recovered is Bishop Beschi's decree dated 1 January 2019, effective 11 February, publicly announced 13 February. It cites Bishop Bernareggi's decree no. 2424 of 30 April 1948, saying the reality of Adelaide's alleged apparitions/revelations was not established. It also quotes the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith's letter of 20 November 2018, protocol 240/1944-67408: the prior discernment remains in force while Marian devotion may be regularized. [S01,S04]

The 2019 act authorizes and supervises devotion at the parish chapel of Mary Queen of the Family while expressly excluding references in that worship to alleged messages, apparitions and other unapproved supernatural phenomena. This is neither an apparition approval nor a declaration that fraud was proven. The original 1948 decree and full investigation dossier have not been obtained; their disposition is known here through the later signed primary decree. [S01]

A 2025 diocesan shrine-directory paragraph says the Church never officially pronounced on the apparitions. That sentence is inconsistent with the explicit 1948/2019 legal record; a tourism/pastoral blurb should not silently override the signed acts. No subsequent reversal was located. [S05]

7. THE INVESTIGATION: DOCUMENTS TO COMPARE, NOT A VERDICT BY REPUTATION
The early investigator Don Luigi Cortesi published Le visioni della piccola Adelaide and Storia dei fatti di Ghiaie in 1944, followed by Il problema delle apparizioni di Ghiaie in 1945. The devotional bibliography's linked early PDFs returned 404. These publications and the commission depositions are essential before judging his reasoning or allegations about coercive interrogation. [S14]

Padre Agostino Gemelli was founder and first rector of the Catholic University of the Sacred Heart in Milan, independently verified by university records. Later narratives place him and his collaborator Sidlauskaite in the investigation. Recover their actual signed clinical/psychological reports; do not convert the finding that a child was psychologically normal into proof of supernatural vision. [S17,S20]

Psychiatrist Ferdinando Cazzamalli is another principal figure. Pietro Martini's 2018 historical profile in Insula Fulcheria, hosted by the Municipality of Crema, documents his Pavia medical training and Como psychiatric-hospital career. His 1951 La Madonna di Bonate is a primary interpretive target, not a report fully read here. Partisan accounts of his role, Gemelli's opposition and Adelaide's treatment need confrontation with originals. [S15,S20]

The reported retraction and later reaffirmations are similarly disputed. A retraction by a child under adult control is not self-interpreting proof of fraud; a later reaffirmation is not self-interpreting proof that the early retraction was coerced. Both documents, interview conditions and independent corroboration are needed. [S19]

8. COMPARISON TO THE EXISTING FATIMA/ZEITOUN RESEARCH
As an evidence TYPE, Ghiaie resembles a child-vision case with reported public signs, rather than the claim of repeated publicly visible personlike apparitions at Zeitoun. Its solar-sign claims make comparison to Fatima worthwhile. It also offers a promising historical film lead. But advance predictions, crowd figures, distant reports and newspaper coverage must each be independently reconstructed before assigning equal evidential strength. The Church's still-unfavorable recognition status is an important genuine difference, not proof that every witness was mistaken.
