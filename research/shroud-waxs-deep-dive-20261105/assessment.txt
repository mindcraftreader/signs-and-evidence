WAXS DATING OF THE SHROUD: VALIDATION, CLIMATE AND THE MEDIEVAL ALTERNATIVE
5 November 2026

JUDGMENT

WAXS is meaningful conditional evidence for an ancient cloth. The original
program includes successful checks on other dated linens and a quantitative
argument against an ordinary medieval European history. I independently
reproduced that argument's central monthly-climate calculation. It cannot
fairly be dismissed merely by saying that temperature and humidity matter.

The stronger claim, that this method reliably distinguishes first-century
from medieval cloth despite an unknown exposure/treatment history, has not
been established by the validation located here. Four within-study
non-anchor checks are useful but do not establish reliability for a displayed,
handled, fire-exposed relic. A single measured aging index also cannot
independently determine both calendar age and past aging rate.

I did not establish a documented ordinary process that explains away the
Shroud WAXS result. Conversely, the uncertainties do not justify treating it
as an independently validated calendar date with a known catastrophic-error
probability. The distinction is between substantive positive evidence and
the present limits of its chronological interpretation.

1. PEOPLE, MEASUREMENT AND INFERENCE

The principal method paper is by Liberato De Caro, Cinzia Giannini, Rocco
Lassandro, Francesco Scattarella and Teresa Sibillano of Italy's National
Research Council, Institute of Crystallography, with Emilio Matricciani of
Politecnico di Milano and Giulio Fanti of the University of Padua [1].
The 2022 Shroud application is by De Caro, Sibillano, Lassandro, Giannini
and Fanti [2]. The later temperature paper adds independent researcher
Cesar Barta and Matricciani [3]. These are related investigations, not three
independent WAXS dating laboratories.

WAXS measures how X-rays scatter from the structural organization of
cellulose. The authors derive a numerical aging factor from selected
diffraction intensities, then normalize it to an aging parameter AP.
AP estimates a fraction of the maximum cellulose-chain breakage in their
model. It is not simply the percentage of visibly damaged fibers.

The conversion to years uses reaction kinetics, estimated temperature,
humidity and the premise that natural aging dominates. Established
X-ray instrumentation, cellulose degradation science and the particular
calendar-dating calibration are three separate levels of validation.

The scientific foundation extends outside Shroud research. For example,
Catherine H. Stephens and Paul M. Whitmore of Carnegie Mellon University's
Art Conservation Research Center studied cotton, linen and kozo papers at
90 C and 50% relative humidity for thousands of hours [7]. H.-Z. Ding and
Z. D. Wang of the University of Manchester developed cellulose degradation
models against several experimental datasets [8]. Those results support
the kinetics background; neither is an independent WAXS calendar-date trial
on ancient linens. Only their publisher abstracts were reviewed here.

2. WHAT THE 2019 VALIDATION ACTUALLY CONTAINS [1]

The main kinetic parameters are obtained using the first-century Masada
sample FII and sample D from Fayyum, dated AD544-605. Modern raw flax and
very old saturated linen supply normalization references. The authors
explicitly identify four other radiocarbon dates not used to set the two
main kinetic parameters. Those four are useful within-study checks.

Sample       Carbon-reference age     WAXS age          Absolute gap
             (years, as reported)     (years)
DII/Jericho        950                   940                  10
NII/Engedi        2310                  2350                  40
E/Egypt           2390                  2390                   0
HII/Thebes        2880                  2770                 110

All four reported approximately 95% intervals overlap. Their mean absolute
central-estimate difference is 40 years. Their WAXS intervals are much wider
than that figure:

DII: 870-1010 years; NII: 2060-2860; E: 2160-2670; HII: 2410-3370.

The mean total interval width is 602.5 years. These intervals are asymmetric
near saturation. The 40-year mean gap is not a universal +/-40-year error
bar. Four comparisons do not define a reliable extreme-tail distribution.

This is not a trivial calibration-only exercise: the four ages were not
used to determine the two main kinetic parameters. However, it is also not
a blind external test. Regional climate estimates, parameter choices and
the same research program are involved. Their controls mostly concern
preserved Near Eastern/Egyptian material rather than the exposure history
of an exhibited European relic.

3. THE SHROUD MEASUREMENT [2]

The measured thread was approximately 0.5 x1 mm, attributed to the area
near the Raes/radiocarbon corner. Thus this WAXS test is not an independent
sample from the central body image. Its detailed documentary chain of
custody is not supplied by the paper's brief sampling description.

The authors report aging factor AF=10.0 +/-0.2, compared with Masada's
AF=10.3 +/-0.2. Their normalized Shroud AP is 0.60 +/-0.02. The pattern and
aging factor are comparable to Masada under their analysis; that does not
mean that equal degradation guarantees equal calendar age under different
exposure histories.

The Shroud was measured after instrument work, including anode-filament
replacement and optical realignment. The authors repeated a modern fabric
measurement to establish a common intensity scale across measurement
sessions. This is a disclosed calibration step, not evidence of fabrication.
An interlaboratory/raw-data replication would help validate this sensitive
comparison. I did not independently extract AP from raw detector counts.

4. THE STRONGEST RESULT: THEIR MEDIEVAL CALCULATION WORKS [2,3]

The April 2022 paper predicts AP about0.027 for seven centuries at a
constant12.5 C and75% relative humidity. The September paper improves the
calculation by using monthly temperatures, a nonlinear moisture-sorption
relation and the different European locations.

I transcribed its Table2 climates and implemented Equations1,2,4,5. For
European histories ending in2021, my results are:

European start    My reproduced AP     Published rounded AP
AD1204                0.05162                  0.052
AD1354                0.04071                  0.041

The measured AP is0.60 +/-0.02. Thus the measured normalized aging is
roughly12-15 times the central predictions of those European-only scenarios.
That is a real discrepancy under the adopted model. A vague invocation of
ordinary temperature variability does not close it: the authors already
model seasonality.

I also reproduced the endpoints for the proposed ancient routes:

Alleged long sojourn      My reproduced range       Published range
Beirut                     0.5661-0.5989             0.566-0.598
Edessa                     0.4859-0.5200             0.485-0.519
Memphis                    0.6856-0.7136             0.685-0.713
Camulia                    0.1900-0.2149             0.190-0.215

Rounding of printed coefficients/inputs and year conventions can explain
small differences of order0.001; they do not alter the model comparison.
The Beirut scenario is compatible with the measured aging. That is a
successful compatibility check on a proposed route, not independent proof
that the cloth actually followed it. The authors explicitly acknowledge
that other unknown routes could give the same result.

5. HOW LARGE AN ENVIRONMENTAL CHANGE WOULD BE NEEDED?

I added the same hypothetical temperature offset to every month in every
European location, keeping the authors' relative humidities unchanged.
Within that deliberately simplified counterfactual, reaching AP0.60
requires approximately:

  AD1204-start European history: +9.21 C in every month/location.
  AD1354-start European history: +10.36 C in every month/location.

These are not documented past storage conditions and not a proposed
history of the Shroud. They show the scale of the change needed if one
tries to close the discrepancy using only this uniform temperature change
inside the published model. A modest one- or two-degree adjustment does
not do it. Other mechanisms or model errors would require their own tests.

A separate illustrative calculation holds a cloth at one climate throughout
its entire lifetime, using the 2019 reaction rate0.0023/hour at90 C:

Constant temperature    Years required to reach AP0.60
20 C                               2102
22.5 C                             1392
25 C                                928
27.5 C                              623

These uniform-lifetime examples do not respect the documented European
sojourns and must not be substituted for the European route comparison.
They explain why AP alone is not a unique calendar reading. The full
temperature/humidity history determines which age conversion is applicable.

6. A CONCRETE LIMIT TO THE NATURAL-AGING SCREEN [1]

The 2019 paper excludes a known modern bleached sample A because its
chemical treatment caused non-natural aging. That is a legitimate exclusion
for a method explicitly intended for naturally aged material.

However, sample A's maximum/minimum diffraction ratio is8.1, above the
paper's preliminary greater-than4 natural-aging screening threshold.
Therefore that threshold alone is not sufficient to distinguish all
chemically altered material. The sample's known bleaching history matters.

Using its reported AF8.1, modern-raw AF7.56 and saturated AF11.6 gives
AP about0.134. If I inappropriately force that modern bleached sample
through the natural-aging equation at21 C, it acquires a counterfactual age
of about741 years. This is my diagnostic calculation, not an age published
by the authors. It does not demonstrate that the Shroud was bleached or
that its much larger AP0.60 resulted from a similar process. It demonstrates
why suitability screening is part of the dating inference rather than an
optional detail.

The oldest saturated sample also cannot supply a finite WAXS date. That
is a declared measurement limit, not a failed finite prediction hidden
from the comparison. The four usable checks remain affirmative evidence.

7. THE FIRE/WATER OBJECTION AND THE AUTHORS' RESPONSE [2-4]

Marco Ricci, Paolo Di Lazzaro and Alfonso Sanchez Hermosilla, writing as
members of the International Center for Shroud Studies' scientific
committee, question environmental ambiguity, sampling provenance,
calibration and whether dry heating represents the Shroud's historical
fire-and-water exposure [4]. Their commentary is not a blinded laboratory
rejection or a demonstrated medieval-age explanation.

The original authors did perform an experiment: 200 C for30 minutes with
uncontrolled, falling relative humidity. They report unchanged WAXS
profiles despite substantial yellowing. Their September follow-up also
calculates that30 minutes at100 C/100% RH produces only about0.006 aging,
and a year at room temperature/100% RH less than0.001. Therefore it is
incorrect to say they simply ignored heat or moisture.

The remaining gap is whether those tests/calculations adequately bound
the actual thread's material and exposure history. Neither a dry-oven test
nor an assumed hot/wet scenario is a measurement of its1532 thermal history.
I found no experiment here showing that the actual Shroud WAXS observation
is explained by the documented fire. Fire remains an applicability question,
not an established disposal of the result.

8. INDEPENDENT ACCEPTANCE AND VALIDATION

On5 November2026, OpenAlex listed23 citing records for the2019 method
and10 for the2022 Shroud paper,29 distinct records in their union.
The saved index records include reviews, structural studies, diffraction
processing, unrelated theoretical citations, duplicated chapter editions
and one retracted cellulose-fiber application. Citation is not equivalent
to independent validation of calendar dating.

I screened all29 by title/metadata and inspected promising accessible
primary material. I did not read all29 full texts. Recent examples include
Massimo Ladisa's2025/2026 CNR diffraction-processing work: useful signal/
background methods, not new independently dated archaeological-linen
calendar trials. A2020 structural review includes members of the original
WAXS team. These cannot be counted as independent replications.

The2026 dating synthesis by Padua's Fanti reuses the WAXS program rather
than reporting a second WAXS lab dating a separate Shroud specimen [5].
It describes the alternative methods as still requiring confirmation.

No blinded interlaboratory calendar-validation study of the specific WAXS
method was located in this bounded search. That is a search finding, not
proof that none exists or that every citation has been exhaustively read.
The founding authors themselves propose such a repeatability/blinding
campaign. Their CNR institute continues to present the result and calls
for measurements by multiple laboratories on multiple samples [6].

9. UNCERTAINTIES THAT MUST NOT BE CONFUSED

  Instrument repeatability: how closely a repeat measurement agrees on a
  particular point of a particular thread.

  Conditional age uncertainty: propagation through the assumed model,
  including specified parameter/climate uncertainties.

  Applicability uncertainty: whether that model, starting state and exposure
  description correctly apply to this particular thread.

The first two do not automatically measure the third. The large difference
between0.60 and about0.05 is many units of the reported0.02 measurement
uncertainty. It does not follow that the historical medieval-age hypothesis
has a Gaussian probability computed from those units. Such a probability
would require a validated model-error distribution for comparable objects.
The current four-check dataset cannot supply that far-tail distribution.

10. WHAT THIS DOES TO THE JENGA BLOCK

Retained: the measured WAXS result quantitatively challenges the modeled
ordinary medieval European history; it resembles an ancient comparator;
four non-anchor reference checks agree with radiocarbon; a valid non-carbon
dating result would matter greatly to the overall Shroud assessment.

Not established: a reliable first-century-versus-medieval decision for an
object with unknown treatment history, independent of suitability and
environmental assumptions; a general +/-40-year accuracy; astronomical
joint probabilities with the other aging methods.

Not established in the opposite direction: a specific ordinary exposure
that actually accounts for the Shroud's measured AP or a decisive
experimental refutation of the WAXS program.

The evidential block therefore remains, with conditional strength. This
audit identifies concrete validation limits rather than defeating the whole
alternative-dating argument. These are analyst assessments, not concessions
attributed to Daniel. No modern-miracle numerical tally is changed.

An especially useful external test would set the model in advance, blind
the ages, and include securely dated European textiles with differing known
conservation/treatment histories, alongside Near Eastern tomb controls.
Cross-laboratory repeat measurements of the same threads would separately
test the scattering-to-AP step. The failure rates of these two steps should
be measured separately before deriving authenticity odds.

REPRODUCIBILITY
calculate_sensitivity.py uses Python's standard library and writes
sensitivity-results.json. It includes source-derived climate inputs,
reference checks, route predictions and explicitly labeled counterfactuals.
Assertions check the reconstructed European and ancient-route outputs
against the published rounded values. It does not process raw X-ray data
or calculate a posterior probability of authenticity.
