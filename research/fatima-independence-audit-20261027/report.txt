FÁTIMA: INDEPENDENCE, TRANSMISSION, AND PREDICTION AUDIT
27 October 2026

Judgment
The advance promise and substantial contemporary public response survive this audit. A generic claim that the prediction or whole event was created by much later storytelling is untenable against the sources inspected. However, there is concrete contemporary evidence of shared attention, discussion during/after the event, and later re-use of the same witnesses. The most geographically decisive distant narratives are generally late rather than independently recorded in October 1917. This warrants a specific discount against counting every account as an independent replication. It does not establish that expectation caused all observations, or that no supernatural event occurred.

1. What was recorded before October 13?
Parish priest Manuel Marques Ferreira's interrogation dated July14 records Lúcia requesting a miracle so everyone would believe, followed by the Lady's reported promise to make everyone believe in three months. Manuel Nunes Formigão, then a teacher at the Santarém seminary and lycée and later canonical rapporteur, recorded on September27 that on the coming October13 the Lady would make people believe she really appeared. The July and September records are different interviews with the same principal claimant, not independent revelations or independently predicted phenomena. They nevertheless establish genuinely advance attestation of the claim. Relevant printed pages were visually checked in the official DCF selection, pp.32 and58.

The precise predicted *date and evidentiary purpose* are stronger than a post-event coincidence. The inspected wording does not specify rotational motion, three descents, colors, duration, an angle, or an emitter’s location. Do not strengthen it into a prediction of that complete physical signature. Conversely, do not weaken it into a mere general hope something nice would happen. Its stated object was public belief in the apparition.

The pre-event September27 account also associates October13 with peace for the world. That additional wording should remain visible in a complete prediction audit, rather than selecting only the successful-looking components. Whether peace was immediate, conditional, or differently intended is a separate interpretive question; this audit does not use it to erase the well-attested public-sign prediction.

An especially useful pre-event context document is Raio de Luz, September1 (DCF III-1 doc20). It publishes an August letter describing the crowd's reactions, temperature change, a cloud near the Sun reported by others, and a bystander's remark about astronomical appearance. This shows attention to sky/solar phenomena already circulated before October. It does not show that they were illusions. The writer carefully distinguishes her own experience from what companions said.

2. What the contemporary journalist actually contributes
Avelino de Almeida, O Século's special correspondent, is a genuinely important external eyewitness, not merely a later Catholic reteller. His private postcard dated October13 says he saw extraordinary things and estimates40,000 present. The DCF editors describe the actual postcard and postmarks; my inspection is of the printed critical edition, not the physical postcard. The private early statement supports a sincere impression but contains no detailed solar inventory.

His O Século article was composed/dateline October13 and published October15, pp.1–2 (DCF III-1 doc87). It reports the matte disc, extraordinary spectacle and reactions, while distinguishing what different people said they saw. In its clearest passage, people ask each other whether/what they saw; the majority describe trembling/dancing, others descent, colors or a Marian face. This is direct evidence that people exchanged descriptions. Difference of experience is not a refutation: the user does not require identical perception, and an external event can be perceived differently.

His October29 Ilustração Portuguesa article, II(610), pp.353–356 (doc228), explicitly presents dancing and colors as what he personally saw and leaves the explanation open to science and Church. Therefore a skeptical claim that Almeida only witnessed the crowd and never personally saw an unusual solar appearance would overstate the record. Equally, his postcard, O Século article, Ilustração article and later canonical quotation are one witness appearing repeatedly, not four independent observers. Publication in an anticlerical paper does not itself prove the journalist was an atheist, or that he endorsed a supernatural cause.

The Diário de Notícias October15 report, datelined Ourém October13 (DCF III-1 doc89 pp.245–246), offers another contemporary reporting stream. It explicitly attributes descent to what some persons told the reporter, and describes these people explaining it to others, followed by fearful cries. The reporter labels this suggestion. The descriptions of communication count as historical evidence; the reporter's attribution of causation does not become a controlled psychological experiment. This report should not be sold as a second clearly self-attested observer of every optical feature, nor dismissed simply because it is skeptical. I visually checked the printed relevant p.246.

Other papers repeating these reports can document rapid publicity and editorial willingness to print them. They do not add a new observer merely because a masthead or language changes. A future count of genuinely independent journalistic witnesses must trace authors and copying chains. This pass did not finish that whole-press census.

3. The distant witnesses, by evidential grade
Granja, ~157km straight-line: a letter by Maria Joaquina Tavares de Proença de Almeida Garrett to Formigão, October14 (DCF III-1 doc79 pp.214–215), relays unnamed locals' rotation/pink-cloud sightings. She attended the Cova herself. This is genuinely next-day and predates the October15 major reports. There is no demonstrated newspaper-copying chain. But it is indirect, does not report descent, and gives no measured direction, exact position or clock time. It rebuts 'all remote reports appeared decades later'; it does not independently triangulate an emitter.

Torres Novas, ~21km: Adelaide Grego's letter dated November24 (doc319 p.740) relays something appearing in the Sun at the same hour, explicitly saying local descriptions were not comparable with Cova. It is a contemporary distant report, not a precise duplicate. No proven copying or fabrication emerged.

Leiria, ~17km: an early November newspaper claim is quoted by University of Coimbra mathematician Diogo Pacheco de Amorim in his1960 article, appendix p.202. He dates O Mensageiro to November8, where Muse gives November5. Original issue not retrieved, so its exact content/date remain a concrete gap. Guilhermina Lopes da Silva's much later interview in John Haffert's Meet the Witnesses, pp.41–42, explicitly says she knew the prediction, watched toward the mountain because her husband prevented attendance, and summoned two workers. Her later narrative includes relatives returning from Cova. Informational isolation is affirmatively absent in this account; the workers are not separate full interviews in the inspected passage. Her address at interview time is not proven to be her1917 observing address.

Alburitel, ~13km: Portuguese material distinguishes Inácio Lourenço's school group from Joaquim Lourenço's hill group. De Marchi says he questioned teacher/villagers in corroboration. Amorim reproduces Maria do Carmo Lopes da Fonseca's June10,1958 letter, relaying Joaquim's recollection told to her in1957. Fonseca was the schoolteacher's daughter. She recalls inhabitants preparing smoked glass because they already expected a celestial sign and a hill group waiting for solar noon; she explicitly does not recall seeing the phenomenon herself. The account's own information contradicts treating these remote witnesses as universally unaware of the expected sign. It does not contradict their observing something. Haffert's English version attributes the school narrative to Joaquim; keep the source discrepancy visible, rather than count two identical independent testimonies.

Minde vicinity, ~13km: Haffert's interview of Albano Barros, pp.40–41, directly states that the Sun fell in the direction of Fátima. This is the clearest retrieved directional-descent testimony. It is, however, published decades later (1961 work, consulted2006 reprint; interviews generally1960); no earlier contemporaneous account or original Portuguese recording was retrieved. Haffert does not establish that Barros had been uninformed about Fátima. Absence of an early record does not prove invention or borrowing. No measured azimuth or exact village/field is supplied.

São Pedro de Moel, ~34km: poet Afonso Lopes Vieira's veranda experience is preserved in later Portuguese authors and his widow's1960 deposition in Haffert, p.40. Haffert narrates it as over the mountain at Fátima; the inspected Portuguese passages do not give a measured bearing. No evidence here establishes actual borrowing. Late documentation limits the independence that can be demonstrated.

Santo Amaro de Oeiras, ~119km: Amorim p.197 preserves an August17,1958 correspondence chain, Fonseca forwarding Berta Leite's account of her father José Leite. Berta explicitly was not with him that day. This is later secondhand testimony, not her direct sighting. It reports rotation, not measured descent toward Cova.

Distances are modern locality-to-Cova great-circle approximations from the prior map audit, not surveyed1917 stations or historic travel distances. The map is evidence of geographical distribution. It is not in itself a map of measured intersecting light rays.

4. Direct evidence versus live possibility
DEMONSTRATED IN THE INSPECTED RECORD:
- Pre-event prediction with public evidentiary purpose.
- Sky-focused expectations already in pre-October publications.
- People exchanging descriptions at the Cova in contemporary journalism.
- Some distant observers explicitly knew and anticipated the sign.
- Multiple published narratives recycle the same witness, family, interview or correspondence chain.
- Important late accounts were recorded after decades in which the event's interpretation was public.

NOT DEMONSTRATED:
- That these processes generated the entire October13 spectacle.
- That all distant witnesses learned their accounts from Cova returnees.
- That Barros, Vieira or José Leite knowingly fabricated their testimony.
- That a specific ordinary optical process reproduces all strongest observations.
- That a nonordinary external event could not coexist with expectation, discussion or varied perception.
- That the surviving corpus yields a defensible numeric independence coefficient or likelihood ratio.

5. Strongest competing interpretations after this audit
The supernatural case retains a real advance dated promise followed by striking experiences sufficiently public that an external journalist privately and publicly recorded his own amazement; it also has contemporary remote reports and later detailed dispersed testimony. Mere blanket 'mass hallucination' fails to address that combined record. A handful of qualified caveats cannot establish the probability of an ordinary cause.

The strongest ordinary proposal is a combination of unusual viewing/weather conditions, perception during directed solar observation, and rapid interpersonal interpretation, later reinforced in memory and publication. This pass documents the historical conditions and some communication, not a complete tested physical/psychological mechanism. The broad wording of the predicted public sign gives that proposal more room than an exact prior prediction of every subsequent optical detail would. This is a comparative evidential point, not a demonstrated complete natural explanation.

The best justified adjustment is narrower and concrete: retain substantial weight for the dated prediction, the early personal testimony and the fact of public experience; reduce the extra multiplier claimed for numerous wholly independent exact-match reports and a completed distant triangulation. That reduction does not reduce their weight to zero. Nor can one add every person's reaction as an independent experiment.

For the combined Fátima–Zeitoun argument, within-Fátima dependence is not itself a demonstrated dependence between the two events. That needs its own evidence. One must not convert a real local transmission issue into a generic dismissal of a later event in a different society.

6. Scholarly context and source limits
Bruno Cardoso Reis, writing from Universidade Católica Portuguesa's Centre for Religious History Studies in2001, examined early Catholic newspaper reception and disagreement. His Análise Social article explicitly excludes adjudicating the supernatural cause. Its value here is historical press relationships and the fact that Catholic reception was not monolithic. It cannot be cited as an experimental disproof of the event. The official DCF selection is a scholarly critical edition issued by the Sanctuary with Portuguese Catholic University theological/CEHR scientific sponsorship. The modern fatima-1917.com index is a third-party AI-assisted finding aid. I treated original Portuguese passages and printed page images as evidence; its automatic relation/feature tags are not independent historical verification.

This is a focused audit of the strongest already cited chains, not an exhaustive rereading of3,811documents. No numeric odds were derived. Remaining high-value retrievals are original early Leiria issue, unabridged original Haffert interview notes/recordings and Portuguese edition, earliest editions/depositions for Vieira and the Lourenço brothers, and a deduplicated contemporary journalist ledger tracing reprints. Those are specific tasks, not a demand that believers supply impossible certainty.
