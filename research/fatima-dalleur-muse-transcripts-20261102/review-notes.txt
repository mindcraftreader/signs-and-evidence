1. THE AUTHORS' ACTUAL PHOTOGRAPHIC DEFENSE

The Bradley response distinguishes synthetic implementation checks from real
photographic worked examples and defends caster-shadow ray correspondences,
including the hat tangency. It does not require the caster's physical height or
all receivers to occupy one plane. A longer visible ray segment can improve
selection precision, but physical shadow length is not the trigonometric input.
The contemporary geometry literature reviewed in the adjacent audit supports
the validity of appropriate common supporting tangents. The earlier isolated
contact-hardening calculation does not test the complete set of D115 cues.

3. VERIFIED EARLY COLOR-OBSERVATION SOURCES

Carlos Silva, letter dated18October1917, DCF III/1document122, printedpp317–318.
The printed page was downloaded and visually inspected. It reports a progression
of colors on faces and a white felt hat and says he chose not to fixate the Sun
to avoid attributing the effects to optical illusion. That supports Muse's relevant
claim and is a record five days after the event. It does not establish a measured
zero-light-exposure protocol. Silva also reports noticing that he had dried and
declines to call that a miracle; his description and causal judgment should both
be preserved. His theological caution is not a denial of the observation.

4. GARRETT: PRECISE OBSERVATION VS SPOKEN PARAPHRASE

Jose Maria de Proenca de Almeida Garrett's18December1917report, DCF III/1doc345,
states that after fixating the disc he saw widespread purple coloration, turned,
closed/covered his eyes, reopened them and still saw purple. The eye-closing
passage does not explicitly state that he saw no afterimages while his eyes were
closed. That stronger spoken paraphrase should not be inserted into a quotation.
The report also describes a later brief Sun-viewing comparison producing irregular
moving yellow patches, which he distinguished from the uniform Fatima appearance.
Retain that favorable detail rather than presenting him as never testing an
ordinary perceptual comparison. Neither step is a controlled clinical experiment.

6. OBSERVATIONS, INTERPRETATIONS AND RETAINED CONTEXT

The transcript arguments connect the preannounced public sign, reporter/witness
evidence and remote testimony before adding photogrammetry. This is the structure
Daniel has repeatedly advanced. It must not be replaced by a photographic-only
case, a requirement for identical experiences, or a generic unexplained-event
argument. The religious-context claim is affirmative evidence about purpose and
agency and must be evaluated as such.

Some technical claims remain interpretations: uniform perceived color is not a
measured spectrum; a monochrome garment patch is not by itself a measurement of
water content, elapsed drying time or infrared irradiance. These distinctions
do not erase testimony or supply an ordinary countermodel. Similarly, hearing
multiple speakers repeat the same article or documentary report does not create
new independent witnesses. The conditional-prophecy defense remains closed as
previously scored; this review does not reopen it by recycling an earlier source.
