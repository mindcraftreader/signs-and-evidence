PADRE PIO'S STIGMATA: CONSOLIDATED MEDICAL-DOCUMENT ANALYSIS
28 October 2026 | Second, visit-by-visit audit

ASSESSMENT

The available medical documents establish a real, recurrent bodily phenomenon examined across decades, not merely a legend about concealed hands. They contain positive evidence for the authenticity claim: actual lesions and blood-stained discharge, repeated absence of marked surrounding inflammation or suppuration, persistence despite reported withdrawal of treatment, and a physician's direct inspection and palpation of normal-looking skin without visible scars after death. Several examiners explicitly favored an extraordinary or supernatural interpretation. Those conclusions are part of the evidence and must not be presented as neutral or skeptical findings.

They do not establish that all five sites were continuously open, anatomically identical, full-thickness perforations throughout fifty years. Depth, crusting, shape, position and function varied; some descriptions conflict; important anatomy was inferred rather than measured. The strongest surviving question is how the whole documented sequence, including late apparent depth and the final skin examination, fits together.

This pass has not demonstrated either deliberate fabrication or an ordinary mechanism explaining the full retained sequence. It has identified specific limits in some physicians' arguments for excluding natural causes. A weakness in an exclusion argument is not evidence that the underlying observation was fabricated, nor is it a substitute for a complete causal explanation.

ACCESS AND METHOD

Three separate audits reread the original Italian as reproduced in Gerardo Di Flumeri's Le stigmate di Padre Pio da Pietrelcina, second edition, 1995. The central medical documents are45-61, with earlier dressing and later attendant/photographic testimony. Giorgio Cruchon's 1972 conference chapter was checked against rendered pages. These are published reproductions/transcriptions; the APP manuscripts, original laboratory sheets and negatives have not been newly inspected.

The source inventory currently classifies20 records, including letters, joint visits, later reviews and nonmedical testimony. It is not a count of20 independent physicians or20 experiments. The visit-audits folder provides more granular classifications. A doctor's observation, the patient's reported history, and the doctor's causal inference are treated separately.

Rossi's full inquiry, the complete canonization Positio and several1987 conference chapters remain unavailable in full. Rossi's presently accessible excerpts are included with that limitation. This is a complete audit of the recovered core corpus, not a claim to have read every medical document in existence.

VISIT-BY-VISIT FINDINGS

1. LUIGI ROMANELLI: 15-16 MAY1919
Chief surgeon, civil hospital of Barletta. Document45, printed147-151; PDF75-77.

Romanelli directly inspected hands, feet and chest. The extremity signs were membrane-covered, with normal-looking surrounding tissue and marked pain on pressure. At the initial palm inspection he expressly described no external opening and no fluid issuing at that moment. Opposed finger/thumb pressure produced what he interpreted as a void under the membranes. The chest lesion was approximately7cm and bleeding; he did not probe its depth. Limb movements were then described as normal.

His conclusion was affirmative: he regarded the cause as supernatural. It would misrepresent his report to reduce this to a physician who merely could not decide. The anatomical support is nevertheless inferential: the record does not document a visibly open hand channel, probing or instrumented depth measurement. His later explanation explicitly says pain prevented confirming whether his fingers met through the membranes.

2. ROMANELLI: LETTERS AND REPEATED CONTACTS1919-20
Documents46-50, printed152-172; PDF78-88.

The correspondence documents repeat contact, devotion and continued observation. Romanelli later describes five examinations over roughly fifteen months, but the source does not supply five separately dated complete medical reports. The June family visit should not automatically become an additional clinical examination. His August1919 letter about the eight-day dressing result is information received through a Capuchin source; he himself qualifies its accuracy. Its early date matters, but it is not another witnessed trial.

His November1920 synthesis retains his conviction of an unusual, unclassifiable surgical problem and describes the absence of suppuration despite ordinary washing and coverings. It also acknowledges modifications. His devotion may affect interpretation; it is not a reason to presume that his sensory observations were false.

3. AMICO BIGNAMI: JULY1919, REPORT26 JULY
Professor of medical pathology, Royal University of Rome. Document51, printed173-178; PDF88-91.

Bignami described superficial eschars on the hands, normal deeper structures on palpation, very superficial dorsal-foot lesions, sole pigmentation and a superficial, nonbleeding chest abrasion at his visit. He also recorded little or no surrounding gross inflammation. Thus absence of visible inflammatory reaction is corroborated by an examiner who favored an ordinary explanation.

Pio acknowledged iodine application and described intermittent bleeding and film/crust shedding. Bignami considered artificial production, pathology, or a mixture. He expressly declined to assert deliberate simulation without proof and favored a mixed pathological/chemical-maintenance proposal. His report did not observe Pio producing the wounds, and its historical neuropathological category is not a present-day established diagnosis.

The significant disagreement is about anatomy and cause, not whether any signs existed. Different examination dates allow genuine change; they do not make either examiner's actual snapshot disappear. No retrieved signed Bignami retraction establishes that he later accepted supernatural causation.

4. THE EIGHT-DAY DRESSING EPISODE
Paolino, Basilio and Ludovico: document14, printed64; Paolino's later account: document25, printed82-84.

The short sworn report describes daily blood and persistent signs, with greater redness/blood at the end and no medicine applied during dressing changes. Iodine was removed. Paolino's later narrative adds removal of medicines, sealed coverings and checks that seals remained intact. No positive evidence of broken seals or covert application has been recovered.

This is meaningful supervised persistence evidence. The later detail should be identified as later testimony rather than silently inserted into the short attestation. The original report is not a physician's daily measured wound chart. The result challenges Bignami's reported rapid-healing expectation; it does not by itself establish that every chemically injured wound must heal within eight days.

5. GIORGIO FESTA: OCTOBER1919
Rome physician and surgeon. Examination9-10October; report composed28October, delivered15November according to the editor. Document52, printed179-200; PDF91-102.

Festa documented actual lesions, crusts, tenderness and blood or serosanguineous fluid. He inferred subcutaneous hand involvement partly from impaired fist closure. The chest lesion was explicitly very superficial, yet exuded fluid. The surrounding tissue showed no marked edema, infiltration or redness. His conclusion favored an unexplained origin beyond his current knowledge.

He followed cloth changes over an interval, but did not supply measured volume or haemoglobin mass sufficient for a daily whole-blood-loss calculation. His first report states that nothing had been applied for more than three months. Because the examination and composition dates differ, that duration must be attributed to the report, not treated as an independently timed three-month interval directly observed at the first examination.

6. JOINT ROMANELLI-FESTA EXAMINATION: 16 JULY1920
Romanelli's9August letter, document49; Festa's31August report, document53. Printed162-164 and200-221, particularly206-213; PDF83-84 and105-108.

They observed larger/deeper-appearing palmar lesions, blood and painful impaired movement. After crust loss, dorsal/plantar foot sites displayed tissue breaks. Festa palpated the underlying metacarpal as intact. Romanelli interpreted matching foot openings as communicating; the channel was not traced or probed. Two observers provide corroboration of one episode, not two independent experimental replications.

Their alcohol/starch procedure undermines the claim that the foot signs at this visit were simply iodine coloration on normal skin. It was not a validated assay excluding phenol. Festa's account of persistence approximately a year after iodine withdrawal is a substantial obstacle to iodine alone as a sufficient explanation, while remaining reported treatment history rather than a year-long continuous seal log.

The same report contains an important finding sometimes omitted from favorable summaries: a white, shiny area called a scar at the former right-dorsal lesion position, with an active lesion nearer the joint. It also describes pink scar-like areas and changing foot appearances. These observations show healing/change during life. They do not establish whether any visible scar persisted in1968.

7. FESTA: APRIL1925 REVIEW AND OCTOBER1925 RE-EXAMINATION
Document54, printed222-294; document55, printed295-296.

The7April third report is a review, not a new April examination. Festa explicitly says a fresh examination requested inDecember1924 had not been permitted. On5October1925, during an episode of postoperative collapse after hernia surgery, he did directly re-examine Pio and described similar signs and a fresh red chest lesion after crust loss. That supplies genuine later observation, including partly unexpected access; it does not constitute five years of controlled observation.

His review defends the case vigorously. Some exclusions, however, exceed the demonstrations supplied: sterility is asserted without a provided stigma-site culture protocol; chemical injury is assumed necessarily to worsen or cause destructive consequences; and an argument about bleeding through intact barriers must be reconciled with his own descriptions of tissue breaks. Normal clotting does not by itself make bleeding from an actual lesion impossible. None of these criticisms proves an ordinary origin; they identify where an exclusion argument needs additional evidence.

Festa explicitly lacked the sense of smell. His accounts of others detecting fragrance from retained cloth are relevant testimony, but he cannot be counted as another direct smelling witness. His reported brief luminous emanations at the1925 chest examination are a separate unmeasured observation.

8. LUIGI PANCARO: 1954 AND1955
Physician working in the Casa Sollievo setting. Documents56-57, printed296-300; PDF150-152.

The1954 investigation concerned illness, medicines, reduced wound fluid and hematology. A cloth extract produced a historical Sahli haemoglobin reading, but the report lacks extraction volume, recovery yield and other details needed to calculate daily blood loss. Microscopy was unsuccessful because cells had deteriorated. The period blood/coagulation results were described as unremarkable, but historical Hb percentages cannot simply become exact modern concentrations.

In1955 Pancaro directly inspected the left hand and openly says he did not inspect the remaining sites. This is useful later observation but not an anatomically measured five-site survey.

9. ALBERTO CASERTA'S1954 IMAGING AND FRANCESCO LOTTI'S RETROSPECTIVE
Caserta: Foggia radiologist. Lotti: chief pediatrician, Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza. Document58, printed300-303; appendix17-21.

The images were dated13October in Lotti's narrative and14October in captions. They should be treated as one session with a minor unresolved date difference. Lotti says he did not personally read the limb films; he reports Caserta's answer that no abnormal metacarpal/metatarsal change was apparent. Reproduced plates supply a distinct modality, relevant to bone destruction. Intact bones do not exclude every soft-tissue passage, and a1954 image does not establish1919 anatomy.

Lotti's overlapping laboratory retelling differs from Pancaro's reproduced figures: WBC10500 versus10750, bleeding time2min versus2min16sec, and final clot retraction54min versus2h50min. Without original laboratory sheets, do not average these or count them as independent confirmatory trials. These textual differences do not negate the general report of unremarkable blood findings.

The broader1950s history includes systemic antibiotic treatment for other illnesses. A literal claim of no medication or antibiotics anywhere in Pio's entire fifty-year history is not supported. This does not establish a treatment explanation for the stigmata.

10. LOTTI: FILM OF THE FINAL MASS, 22 SEPTEMBER1968
Document59, printed303-308; PDF153-156.

Lotti's own film showed an intact-looking right-hand dorsum and a2-3cm red-brown left-palm eschar. He checked repeated frames and Elia Stelluto's photographs, providing negative numbers32450,32453 and32460. This gives specific photographic corroboration of a residual sign and differing states at different sites.

His postmortem premise was supplied by other witnesses; he did not conduct that inspection himself. He judged the final course extraordinary if their account was correct. A remaining surface crust does not alone measure the amount of still-absent deep tissue underneath it. Gradual repair beneath a crust is an observational possibility, not a demonstrated explanation of Pio's whole history.

11. ANDREA CARDONE: DECLARATION9 NOVEMBER1968
Pietrelcina physician. Document60, printed308; PDF156.

Cardone reports approximately1.5cm through-holes, transmitted light and fingers meeting on compression. That is specific eyewitness testimony for communication through the hands and must not be omitted. The observations themselves are undated. The editor and Cruchon explicitly flag the late recollection's limitations. Old age or uncertain dates do not establish dishonesty, but without dates the testimony cannot settle every visit's anatomy or the condition immediately before death.

12. GIUSEPPE SALA: POSTMORTEM EXAMINATION23 SEPTEMBER1968, STATEMENT7 JULY1969
Pio's personal physician; University of Naples medical-semiotics assistant according to the editorial biography. Document61, printed309-312; PDF156-158.

Sala's strongest evidence is direct: about ten minutes after death he reports inspection and digital palpation of the sites, with normal-looking, soft, elastic, mobile skin, no visible scars and no depressions on pressure. He explicitly regarded the disappearance as outside ordinary clinical patterns. This is substantial medical endpoint testimony, not merely a secondhand claim or distant photograph.

He disclaims a formal direct examination of the living wounds, although he describes incidental views and touch. Saying he never saw or touched anything is incorrect. The editor distinguishes the value of his endpoint from the inadequacy of his living description as proof of original depth. Sala also describes swelling of hands/feet during life: absence of a localized inflammatory margin must not become absence of all swelling.

No recovered histology or calibrated late-depth sequence establishes microscopic regeneration of previously missing full-thickness tissue. Missing histology is a limit on that stronger claim, not contrary evidence to his visible/palpatory observation.

13. PHOTOGRAPHER AND ATTENDANTS
Giacomo Piccirillo, document38, printed131-133: direct postmortem photographer. He describes no right-hand or foot scars and a small transparent residue at the left palm. The residue must not be mistranslated as a fibrous scar. The editor says the chest photographs miss the old lesion's exact site. That materially limits those particular images, not the hand photographs or Sala's direct chest observation.

Alessio Parente's February1968 care episode, preserved by Cruchon1972, describes late hand lesions appearing about1cm deep. This is an uncalibrated visual estimate, but it must be retained in any reconstruction. The published text/footnote disagree on whether his later statement was signed in1968 or1969. The event is consistently placed inFebruary1968; the original statement is needed to settle its record date.

Eusebio Notte's account of5July1964 describes a deep-looking, cross-shaped chest lesion while helping with care. Pellegrino's account describes late regression over months/years and no visible scars. They are care witnesses rather than additional physicians. Their evidence counters an assumption that all reported deep-looking injury belonged only to1919.

14. ROSSI, GEMELLI AND LATER EXPERT REVIEWS
Rossi, Bishop of Volterra and apostolic visitor, inspected the signs and collected sworn testimony in1921. Published excerpts support a favorable judgment of Pio's truthfulness and authenticity, and the edition describes him as favoring divine origin. His report also describes signs without open tissue breaks at that visit. These findings must be read together; the full report remains pending. The agreed half point for this favorable investigation remains recorded; no complete stigmata verdict follows from it alone.

Agostino Gemelli was a physician/psychologist and influential critic, but no complete formal wound examination by him has been verified in the recovered core corpus. Festa explicitly says he lacked the authorization to inspect the wounds. His title cannot create an examination that has not been established, and his historical diagnostic labels are not a modern proven diagnosis. His original reports remain a retrieval target.

Giorgio Cruchon's1972 paper is a source-based favorable synthesis and preserves important early postmortem interviews. It is not another direct medical examination of the living Pio. The1987 conference papers by Valli, Capuano, Gusso, Lotti and Stefaneschi, and the Positio studies by Marcozzi and an unnamed psychological assessor, must not be counted as read or as new independent patient examinations until obtained.

Some popular accounts attach an iodine/phenol observation to Vincenzo Tangaro and Il Mattino30June1919. Luzzatto's archival history instead attributes closely matching text to Enrico Morrica's article on that date. Neither original newspaper text nor an independently verified Tangaro clinical report was recovered here. This is an unresolved attribution lead, not another validated physician's report or evidence one author lied.

15. PIETRO GERARDO VIOLI: RETROSPECTIVE MEDICAL OPINION, 2018

In Riccardo Maccioni's19September2018 Avvenire interview, Violi is identified as former head of the microcythemia/thalassemia centre at Casa Sollievo and an investigator in the cause. He graduated in medicine at Pavia in1971, after Pio's death: this is a later expert assessment, not another living-wound examination. He explicitly argues that scar-free disappearance after tissue loss is scientifically unexplained. This supplies a named modern physician supporting the scar concern, beyond the earlier Sala/Lotti statements.

Some premises in that interview need correction against the original corpus. The wounds did change shape/size, and Festa describes impaired hand closure. Violi's unqualified invariance/normal-function wording cannot override those observations. His scar argument remains worth assessing on accurately specified anatomy and timing. The interview does not provide new histology or a new wound-healing experiment.

MODERN MEDICAL COMPARISONS: WHAT THEY ACTUALLY ADDRESS

SCARRING. Christopher Dunkin and colleagues, Stoke Mandeville Burns and Reconstructive Surgery Research Trust/Hospital, studied113 human subjects with a graduated-depth hip wound. Deep portions scarred; superficial portions left no visible residual mark after18weeks. Their threshold estimate was0.56+/-0.03mm in that specific hip-skin model. Abstract inspected, full paper not retrieved in this pass. This experimentally supports taking depth seriously; it does not prove how deep Pio's lesions were or make the hip threshold universal for palms, soles and all causes.

VISIBLE INFECTION. Sue Gardner, Rita Frantz and Bradley Doebbeling, Iowa City VA/University of Iowa nursing and medical research, compared signs with quantitative cultures in36 chronic wounds. No single sign was necessary for infection. Publisher abstract inspected; full paper not retrieved. Accordingly, no pus/red margin cannot be upgraded to documented lifelong microbiological sterility. This does not establish that Pio had a hidden infection or explain his sustained course.

Juan Manuel Muriel-Sanchez, Manuel Cohena-Jimenez and Pedro Montano-Jimenez, University of Seville's Department of Podiatry, randomized27patients/54feet with108nail folds to30/60second88%phenol applications. COMBINED EXPLANATORY ASSESSMENT

Mere staining: inadequate as a complete account. The retained record includes tissue breaks, crusts and discharge, not color alone.

Iodine alone: has a real historical basis in acknowledged use but is substantially challenged by subsequent iodine-directed testing and reported longer withdrawal. A later negative test does not prove no iodine was used earlier; earlier use does not explain everything that followed.

Repeated injury or chemical maintenance: not physically excluded simply by an eight-day result or by normal mental presentation. But no act of production, sustained application record, validated matching reproduction of the full course, or collusive medical fabrication was demonstrated. It remains a possible mechanism family with significant unfulfilled obligations, especially controls and the skin endpoint.

Spontaneous pathological or psychophysiological process: no modern established diagnosis reproducing the full retained record was recovered. An old diagnostic name is not enough. Absence of obvious psychiatric disturbance does not logically exclude every involuntary mechanism; equally, that observation cannot be turned into a positive diagnosis of one.

Supernatural stigmata: receives positive contextual, testimonial and clinical support, including explicit interpretations by Romanelli, Festa, Rossi and Sala. It can accommodate changing signs; changes are not an intrinsic falsification. Compatibility does not create measured likelihoods for either hypothesis, and a miracle interpretation cannot itself determine disputed anatomy.

WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT MOVED

Sustained: actual bodily signs; real lesions at several visits; recurring blood-stained discharge; multiple observations over decades; repeated lack of gross local inflammatory reaction; supervised short persistence and reported longer withdrawal; substantial no-visible-scar endpoint testimony; favorable judgments by several investigators.

Qualified: continuous identical wounds; unanimous clinically demonstrated full-thickness perforations; complete microbiological sterility; exact enormous daily whole-blood loss; all sites healing simultaneously in hours; an iodine-directed assay excluding phenol; counting reports/reviews/repeated quotations as independent trials.

Unresolved: ordinary cause of the complete course; exact depth at each site/date, particularly near death; how visible/palpatory normal skin relates to earlier tissue loss; full Rossi reasoning; original late laboratory/photographic records; full expert assessments in the canonization dossier.

This audit has not falsified Daniel's central stigmata case. It does supply substantive documentary challenges to some stronger subsidiary formulations. They are recorded for discussion, not automatically scored as concessions. The previously agreed tally remains Daniel1.5, assistant0 in this explicit review, with the two existing challenges open.
