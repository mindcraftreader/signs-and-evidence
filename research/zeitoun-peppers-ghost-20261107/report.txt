ZEITOUN, APRIL 30, 1968
One-pane Pepper's Ghost audit, 7 November 2026

FINDING
A flat reflector can give several observers a moving, human-sized virtual
image with ordinary parallax. The difficulty at Zeitoun is placing the lit
source and the pane so the light can reach the witnesses without exposing the
source. The modeled original roof provided no such path in the tested cases.
I withdraw the one-pane challenge as presented. The result is conditional
on the site model and the recovered witness locations.

WITNESSES AND LOCATIONS
02:45, Bishop Athanasius: outside with the crowds. The May 7 Al-Gomhouria
interview, preserved in an Arabic excerpt and translation, has him losing
and regaining sight as the crowd pushed him away and back. At 04:00–05:00
he watched from the garden of a house west of the church. The house is
unnamed. This is the best localized position for this episode. The original
May 7 page was not recovered here. The account's 02:45 and 04:00–05:00
sequence matches the committee booklet. In the Gregorios compilation, the
bishop describes a life-size form above the domes, between the central and
southern domes, moving, turning west and gesturing. The description does not
measure height, travel or bearing. His May 5 Al-Gomhuria account describes
the full form and movement, along with a luminous mist and two doves.

02:45, Major Asaad Iskandar: among the crowd. Watani prints his description
of a full figure moving and turning toward crowds around the church. This
is evidence of the crowd's distribution and his reading of the gesture. It
does not place him or other named observers at different surveyed bearings.

02:45, Seti and Makram Shenouda: near the church, no precise position given.
Their father Zaki reported in Watani that they saw the form between the two
rear domes for about two hours. The Arabic transcript explicitly says
القبتين الخلفيتين, the two rear domes. Their father's own sighting from a
side street was April 28. Two possible rear roof edges were tested as
alternative image locations because 'rear' does not identify a compass edge.

About 03:00, Louis Marcos: at the church, position unpinned. Watani reports
he described a luminous form at the press conference and had submitted a
written testimony. The original written account was not recovered here.

Dawn to 05:00, Dr. Sobhi Farid Shawqi: position unpinned. The Gregorios
compilation has him describing a white cloud above the figure. This is
supporting evidence about the same morning and is outside the body ray test.

Jerome Palmer's 1969 interview has a different vantage, a little building
south of the church, and puts the form five or six metres above the central
dome. The bishop's own narrative begins April 6, despite the archival
page's April 29–30 introduction. April 6/7 is also Dumsday's reading.
The southern office and the extra five or six metres are excluded from the
April 30 reconstruction. The separately reported April 30 evening cross
is likewise kept separate.

CHURCH AND RAY TRACE
The parish lists five domes, 17 m and 12 m summit heights, and a 250 m²
church. The digitized roof parapet is roughly 19 by 12.5 m (about 237 m²).
It is the small apparition church west of Toman Bay, separate from the
larger cathedral on the former garage plot. Coordinates come from manual
digitization of a 190 x 190 pixel crop of the archived Esri image,
enlarged to 950 x 950 for tracing. Crop upper left is (901, 898) of the
2000 x 2000 image, with the original geographic extent in the same archive.
Perspective and the roof edge limit the precision. The OSM church polygon
is much larger than the parapet. The 6 May Al-Ahram map fixes the 1968
street/garage topology and has no usable scale. The west garden is a sector.
All eye coordinates in the code are hypotheses at 1.65 m above ground.
The street and garage samples are comparisons, not dated April 30 stations.

Feet heights of 12.5, 15 and 17 m bracket scenarios based on the dome
heights. The nominal deck is 8.5 m. An alternate test uses 10 m, extends
the roof polygon by 2 m at every edge and increases the ceiling opening's
radius from 2.5 to 3.2 m. These are modeling choices, not surveyed dimensions.
The four small cupolas are floored at the church ceiling according to the
parish. The main dome communicates with the nave. The Gregorios compilation
describes fixed colored glazing. For the under-deck light path, the model
gives the central drum fully open sides and retains the opaque dome caps.
For the elevated performer the same model asks whether the caps or wall
can hide the source from the viewers. It does not reconstruct the actual
columns, window mullions or undocumented scenery.

For every pane, the real source A is the mirror reflection of the virtual
body I in the plane n·x = d. A = I − 2(n·I − d)n. Eye E and source A must
be on the same side of the pane, across from I. The line E–I must hit a
finite reflecting patch in the allowed plan. The source ray A–patch and
viewer's ray patch–E must both clear masonry. The grid covers all azimuths
in 3° steps, mirror normal elevations in 1° steps and perpendicular
image-to-pane distances from 0.15 to 24.9 m in 0.15 m steps. The body is a
1.7 x 0.9 x 0.7 m envelope. This gives a compact image without fully
extended arms and allows a tilted or reclining source. The movement check
uses two endpoints 3 m apart westward as a sensitivity case, not measured
travel. Grid counts are neither probabilities nor a continuous proof.
Patch dimensions describe the range of required ray intersections along
two pane axes. They omit the frame and clearance.

RESULTS
West garden alone, central–south image, 17 m feet, nominal deck.
One representative station about 22 m west has 772 sampled configurations
with the pane and source in plan, the source below the deck, and a clear
eye-to-pane path. Of those where the source rays also avoid the caps, the
best requires at least a 7.81 m radius from the central drum axis to admit
all source rays through the ceiling. That is far larger than the central
opening. In the illustrated example the body center is at 4.2 m, the
reflecting point at about 10.0 m, and the central source ray strikes the
roof 7.0 m from the axis. The roughly 1.9 x 0.55 m patch in this example
shows why pane size by itself is insufficient to rule the trick out.
Varying the western bearings and using the expanded roof produced a more
favorable case at a modeled southwest station with 17 m feet. It still
required a 3.65 m central opening radius with all drum sides open, compared
with the generous modeled 3.2 m. This sensitivity is why the result cannot
be promoted into a surveyed impossibility for every spot in the garden.

Toman Bay alone.
From the central street sample with a nominal 15 m target, several tilted
panes fit the footprint and clear the eye path. Even the best surviving
source-ray trace needs roof passage as far as 8.66 m from the axis.
The enlarged geometry permits a single pane for three street/garage
positions in the geometric screen, but none has a clear under-deck
source path. A separate rooftop-source pass did not find a source hidden
by the modeled masonry while retaining the required light paths.

West garden plus Toman Bay/garage as a conditional simultaneous test.
No under-deck one-pane fit even in the geometric screen at 15 or 17 m
feet for the central–south target, even using the widened plan. At 12.5 m,
30 candidates pass the widened geometric screen but fail the masonry test.
Roof-level source trials do not pass the full test either. The alternate
rear-roof targets at 12.5 m also give no working path in the under-deck
model. These checks do not assert an April 30 observer at a specific
garage position at 04:00–05:00.

A horizontal reflector can in principle serve viewers on opposing sides.
As an illustration, at height 11 m with feet at 15 m, the reflected source
would occupy about 5.3–7.0 m height upside down. The same three west/street/
garage eyes would require ray hits spread over about 24.8 m east–west and
12.6 m north–south, with the farthest ray hitting about 6.4 m east of the
roof's easternmost point. This is an example, not a universal screen-size
minimum. A nearly vertical pane can reduce some offsets but makes it harder
to hide a human source high above the ground. Moving the apparatus outside
the roof, adding a masking stage or using multiple reflectors changes the
tested setup. No evidence of such an installation was recovered.

INDEPENDENT SITE CHECK
In its May 6 issue Al-Gomhuria reported climbing onto the church and
inspecting the five domes, with two bells mentioned and otherwise empty
interiors. This is evidence from a different day, not a contemporaneous
equipment inventory for the April 30 appearance. It gives a proposed
temporary apparatus a further historical burden without substituting for
the geometry.
