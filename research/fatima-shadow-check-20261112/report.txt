These values are upper limits conditional on the selected trace being a valid projected beam; they are not measured source elevations or a statistical confidence interval.

The decisive unresolved empirical step is semantic: the detector measures a real brightness edge near the author's indicated common-tangent region. A line fitted to a silhouette, material edge or occlusion cannot be substituted for a physical light ray. Consequently this result must not be promoted to independent confirmation that the Sun was at the wrong angle.

THE NEW SINGLE-RAY PLANE TEST

For a calibrated perspective camera, an image line backprojects to a plane through the optical center. If n is that plane's unit normal and u is world-up in camera coordinates, the largest possible elevation of any ray in that plane is:

  maximum elevation = acos(abs(dot(n, u))).

This maximizes over all directions in the plane. It does not assume the source's particular horizontal bearing or Dalleur's fitted shadow VP. Because it even admits directions that may later be physically excluded, it is an upper bound, not a preferred elevation.

For image line y=m*x+b, focal length in pixels f, and principal point (cx,cy), a proportional plane normal in camera coordinates (right, up, forward) is:

  n proportional to (m, 1, (m*cx + b - cy)/f).

The adopted frame restoration gives cx=834.5, cy=555.125. Dalleur's h=0.31 is retained; with equivalent focal length FD, the adopted world-up is proportional to (0, FD/10, -0.31). The 30,33,37,45 mm settings remain conditional camera choices, not a newly established joint camera uncertainty region.

Identity checks confirm that the selected image line lies in the calculated plane and that projecting world-up into it attains the stated maximum. The old raw OLS fit and old two-feature arithmetic are also reproduced.

INTERPRETATION

The directional result is not produced simply by typing Dalleur's VP into the new plane-cap calculation. An independently computed image trace constrains a plane. Under the retained camera, its conditional maximum is below the noon Sun. That is a useful consistency result and should receive its proper weight.

The calculation still begins with a proposed physical interpretation of an image trace and retained camera assumptions. The result therefore supports the narrower conditional statement; it does not complete the missing independent joint authentication. The seven-cue audit does not establish that Dalleur's assignments are wrong. Limited recovery from the smaller reproduction is not counterevidence to what he reports from his 6K scan.

This pass identifies no demonstrated material error closing the discrepancy and supplies no full ordinary-solar fit. It also does not independently authenticate the source elevation, a trajectory, the source's identity or supernatural agency. Those are different inferences and remain connected to the historical and testimonial case at their existing scope. No new score is assigned.
