LOURDES AND PADRE PIO — ADVERSARIAL CLINICAL CRAWL
11 November 2026

OUTCOME

No substantive new rebuttal of the retained Lourdes or Pio core was established. The best genuinely new document is Gemelli's 6 April 1926 account claiming an earlier, two-day 1919 visit and inspection of Pio's wounds. Three original-typewritten-page facsimiles were obtained and visually checked. This survives as a correction to dismissing Gemelli on the categorical ground that he never saw the lesions. It does not establish an ordinary cause, fabrication, or a successful challenge to the carefully bounded CP1 claim. The distinction matters: a new historical document deserves preservation without being promoted into a core defeat.

Nine candidate challenges were screened. Three received deeper comparison: Gemelli's asserted inspection, Micheli's structural recovery/treatment record, and the new Carrel/Bailly archival reappraisal. Additional checks covered Bigot comparators, Cirolli diagnostic dissent, Perrin, the reviewed-cure cohort, decades-long self-injury, and contemporary clinical stigmata. Existing seal, chemical, rapidity and scent objections were not recycled as new points. No tally was edited.

CLAIMS ACTUALLY TESTED

Lourdes CL2 concerns repeated substantial recoveries with documented clinical histories, differing in diagnosis, speed and treatment. CL3 concerns the long investigation record. The positive case also includes the shrine's Marian origin and religious meaning. A flaw in one diagnosis can reduce that case's weight without explaining away other cases. The retained claim does not require every cure to be instantaneous or every patient to have anatomically pristine restoration.

Pio CP1 concerns actual recurrent, medically observed bodily lesions over approximately fifty years. It does not require five invariant full-thickness perforations, microbiological sterility, specified blood quantities, or scent/fever corroboration. Contemporary physicians' observations must be distinguished from their causal judgments. Absence of a complete ordinary theory would not rescue a genuinely falsified inference; conversely, an unmatched mechanism family cannot itself establish the cause.

DEEP CHECK 1 — GEMELLI'S ASSERTED 1919 EXAMINATION

Target inference: a critic's account can be dismissed because he lacked direct access to the physical phenomenon. The current consolidated audit already uses the more careful formulation that no complete formal wound examination by Gemelli was verified. Some surrounding sources use the stronger assertion that he never examined or even saw the wounds. Only the stronger dismissal is challenged here.

New primary material [S01]: a typescript attributed to Agostino Gemelli, dated 6 April 1926, responding to Giorgio Festa's 1925 submission. Flavio Peloso publishes the first two and a half pages, as three image scans, from ACDF, Fondo della Sacra Congregazione del Sant'Uffizio, Padre Pio da Pietrelcina, Prot.255/1919,VIII,131. Page1 carries the date/title and places the alleged visit in1919. Page2 describes two days at the friary, conversations with Pio and Benedetto, and an inspection of the wounds. Its short sentence is: “Esaminai anche le piaghe del P. Pio” — “I also examined Padre Pio's wounds.” Page3 disputes Festa's no-access premise. These exact features were read from the images, rather than inferred from a modern summary.

The supplied excerpt is not signed within the three visible pages. Attribution and archival location come from Peloso's scholarly publication and institutional repost, not from a personal archive inspection or an independently authenticated signature. The full thirteen-page document, including most of its substantive answer to Festa, was not acquired. Downloaded images total1,350,872bytes; SHA256 values are in source-manifest.json.

Peloso's research [S02], originally published in Bollettino dell'Archivio per la storia del movimento sociale cattolico in Italia LI(2016),71–86 and reposted22September2021, argues for two visits. He contrasts the1926 account with the known19April1920 encounter at which medical access was refused. He also cites Gemelli's1952 correspondence defending his claim. The account of another visit is detailed, but the1952 statements repeat the same claimant; they are not independent eyewitness corroboration. Peloso reports that the other affirmative witness, Domenico Palladino, learned the story from Gemelli. No new military itinerary, friary register, contemporary independent witness or1919 examination sheet was obtained here.

Strongest defense: Gemelli's own excerpt admits no neurological examination, unsuitable clinical facilities and no exhaustive psychological assessment. His1920 report explicitly denied performing a medical examination; its first-page facsimile was also downloaded and visually verified [S17]. A displayed lesion seen in conversation is different from a complete clinical workup. The purported1919 visit is recounted seven years later in a defensive dispute with Festa. These limitations prevent treating Gemelli as a verified fourth equivalent technical examiner or importing his causal conclusions as observed facts.

The direct reply by Stefano Campanella, director of Tele Radio Padre Pio [S16], specifically disputes Peloso. He asks why the alleged1919visit left no trace in the convent chronicle and why Gemelli omitted it from his1920submission to the same Holy Office; secrecy from the public cannot itself explain silence to that recipient. The chronicle was not independently obtained here, so the asserted absence remains his archival counterclaim. Campanella also cites a reported later denial passed through Zavalloni, Aurilia and Fabiano; that multi-step retrospective chain is weaker than direct signed testimony. His either/or accusation of lying is stronger than the logic requires: no formal medical examination and some visual access can coexist. Nevertheless the visit's date and occurrence remain seriously contested. He identifies the1926text as previously transcribed in Di Flumeri's2001biography, pp421–466. This is new access to the facsimiles for this crawl, not a document previously unknown to scholarship or the cause.

Surviving impact: categorical claims that he could have had no firsthand visual access should be replaced by an explicitly contested account of possible earlier access. His dissent cannot fairly be discarded solely by equating all his claims with the unsuccessful April1920 visit. The already careful consolidated formulation remains defensible. The document supplies neither an observed act of injury nor new diagnostic testing; the fifty-year clinical chronology survives. This is the strongest new documentary qualification found, with low core impact.

DEEP CHECK 2 — MICHELI: DOES BONE REMODELING REMOVE THE HARD CASE?

Target inference: destruction followed by structural recovery proves that ordinary biology could not have produced the outcome. The strongest version concerns regression of a destructive malignant pelvic lesion plus later repair, associated with a pilgrimage; the retained ledger already separates symptoms from structural remodeling.

The actual before/after image evidence remains material. Garner's1974 CMAJ reporting reproduces films dated1August1962 and23August1963 [S05]. Its text reports an abandoned radiological treatment and two months of chemotherapy, without naming drugs/doses. Neilan's Salmon-derived account denies specific anticancer treatment [S06]. This genuine contradiction was already recorded on2November. No original administration chart settling it was recovered in this pass. It remains a reason to qualify “untreated,” not new evidence of an effective anticancer course.

Salmon's full report remains a catalogue target [S04], not an acquired original. His quoted discussion distinguishes tumor disappearance from recalcification. Delourmel reproduces report-specific passages describing a displaced new socket, residual anatomical changes and progressive repair [S07]. Those facts reject instantaneous recreation of a perfect original hip. They do not erase regression of the destructive lesion. Delourmel's psychosomatic interpretation is an interpretation, not a demonstrated mechanism. He is a physician/psychoanalyst quoting Salmon, not a second examining orthopaedic committee.

The most useful genuine clinical comparator is Dissing, Heerfordt, Schiødt and Sneppen, Acta Orthopaedica Scandinavica49(1978),49–53 [S08]. Their Copenhagen Finsen Institute/Rigshospitalet case had a histologically malignant destructive pelvic tumour, favored as chondroblastic osteosarcoma with a differential retained. No radiotherapy or chemotherapy preceded spontaneous clinical and radiographic improvement after two years of worsening. It therefore defeats a universal assertion that such natural regressions with structural improvement never occur. This comparator, however, was already fully read and acknowledged in the earlier audit and is cited by Neilan herself. It is not a fresh adverse discovery.

Strongest defense: the comparator differs in timing, histology and pilgrimage association. Its authors did not identify a cause, and it cannot yield a rate from one published patient. Bone remodeling over months also does not explain why the prior destructive process stopped. Conversely, its rarity does not make it medically unreal or permit dismissing only its diagnostic uncertainty while overlooking Micheli's.

Surviving impact: no new core challenge. The substantial recovery persists; exact untreated status and exact histological subtype remain open. No independently documented operation supporting May's later reconstructive-surgery allegation was recovered. Catalogue records, reproductions and repeated publications are not independent clinical trials. Next decisive records remain Salmon's full film/treatment appendix, the original1962 medication sheets, biopsy material and any actual operative record. The claimed1971 orthopaedic proceedings were not newly obtained; a reported conference presentation is not a personally verified blinded rereading.

DEEP CHECK 3 — A GENUINELY NEW ARCHIVAL STUDY OF CARREL/BAILLY

Ginier-Gillet and de Franciscis, American Journal of the Medical Sciences371(3)(March2026),201–207, DOI10.1016/j.amjms.2025.11.010, is original archival work rather than a polemical retelling [S03]. The accessible abstract/introduction describes fifty original documents, eleven transcriptions and additional collections, including Carrel's notes. It raises tuberculous peritonitis versus pseudoperitonitis and retrospective misinterpretation. Ginier-Gillet's article affiliation is graduate of Grenoble Alpes Faculty of Medicine; de Franciscis is identified as the then Lourdes Bureau president. OpenAlex's Lourdes University label is not adopted.

Only abstract/introduction and bibliographic/author information were accessible; the complete article and archive items were not obtained. The diagnostic question cannot be converted into the authors' final diagnosis. Even a securely established error would concern Bailly, not the strongest modern cases. Status: worthwhile new primary-archive lead, no demonstrated rebuttal yet. No email/request/purchase was sent.

OTHER CANDIDATES AND THEIR STRONGEST REPLIES

4. Bigot: diagnostic instability and spontaneous visual recovery. Iraci and colleagues' Padova series contains82 surgically verified optochiasmatic cases and warns that traditional preoperative radiology cannot reliably establish the diagnosis [S09]. That is relevant historical clinical evidence, but Bigot has a reported operative finding of posterior-fossa adhesions, and posterior-fossa disease is not automatically an optochiasmatic diagnosis. The1976 Bojarski/Drozd/Rylek spontaneous-remission paper remains metadata-only [S10]; severity, duration, treatment and hearing findings were not recovered. Prior actual untreated optic improvement is acknowledged in the Bigot audit. None newly matches prolonged bilateral deaf-blindness and her full course. This weakens universal irreversibility language, already rejected, rather than supplying a new actual explanation.

5. Cirolli: an infection mistaken for tumour. Dowling's first-person CMIL account remains affirmative evidence of biopsy assessment and serious disagreement over tumour subtype [S14]. The earlier Pritchard-Jones/Equinox diagnostic-sufficiency challenge is substantive but already recorded. No new physical slide review or written expert opinion was obtained. A possible bone infection cannot become established tuberculosis. This remains a live limitation on Cirolli's exact diagnosis, not a new refutation of the Lourdes recovery pattern.

6. Perrin: prior recurrent or partly functional deficits. The newly accessible material had already been read in the3November expanded audit: the angiographic thrombus interpretation is disputed, emotional blackouts contain a functional component, and Bartoli nevertheless records ocular deficits with residual disc pallor and later functional recovery. These concern different endpoints. Calling the whole case psychogenic does not follow from one functional component; calling it proven carotid-thrombus reversal also overstates the source. No new contrary neurological record appeared.

7. The clinical-review cohort and shrinking cure counts. François, Sternberg and Fee [S13] reviewed411 reports from1909–14 and25 later acknowledged cures, raising specific reservations about eight of the latter and two exclusions. Those are denominators for selected reviewed reports, not disease-matched visitors or untreated natural-history controls. The reasons differ: treatment exposure, diagnostic evidence, follow-up, relapse and timing cannot be collapsed into a single remission mechanism. The existing95-case forensic selection separately grades10 fast and4 staged clinical pairs; these are documentary grades, not a cure-incidence estimate. The heterogeneous cohort supplies real case-specific cautions already integrated into Ganora, Bouley, Rams, Cirolli and other entries. It does not compute the expected number of retained outcomes under ordinary causes. No new denominator-based refutation was established.

8. Pio: decades of recurrent self-injury. Alcolado and colleagues, East Birmingham Hospital, reported forty years of excoriation of an old surgical scar followed by malignant transformation [S12]. Thus longevity alone cannot make recurrent self-injury impossible. Their scarred fatal ulcer is not the reported Pio endpoint, and this patient supplies no causal link to Pio. The clinical dossier includes actual lesions and withdrawal/control observations to be answered, rather than merely a duration claim. This comparator supplies a narrow possibility boundary, not evidence that Pio used that mechanism.

9. Pio: contemporary clinical religious stigmata. Bonamonte and Bari colleagues report a42-year-old woman with three annual episodes, erosions/ulcers and bleeding, followed by cessation after police involvement [S11]. The authors infer artificial production; their detailed narrative does not observe the act and calls specific means probable. Their patient lacked palm/sole involvement and had a short seasonal course. This shows real physical wounds and a religious narrative can coexist with an ordinary explanation, but does not explain Pio. It supplies no new breach of the audited controls or50-year matching course. The chemical/seal records [S15] were used as the strongest defense, not rescored.

RESEARCH LIMITS AND NEXT DECISIVE OBSERVATIONS

This was a broad targeted scholarly/primary-source crawl in English, French, Italian and Polish-title searches, plus a review of the established local reports. Primary clinical reports and document facsimiles were preferred; generic skeptical/devotional recitations and recent pages apparently recycling the same literature were finding aids only. Searches covered new medical reviews, Micheli's hip repair/histology/treatments, Carrel's archive study, Bigot cranial comparators, Gemelli's archive chronology and medical stigmata comparators. This is not a census of every archive or a new physician diagnosis.

The best new acquisition warrants a precise change in source description: Gemelli's possible1919 direct access must be recorded as contested, with its explicit self-reported limitations. Obtaining the remaining10.5pages of his1926 reply and independent evidence of the1919 itinerary is the most concrete Pio follow-up. Carrel's full2026 article and archive-document schedule could test an older clinical diagnosis. For Micheli, new contemporary treatment/biopsy records would matter more than another summary. No new evidence here warrants reopening the settled chemical or recovery-timing objections, and no complete ordinary account or probability estimate is claimed.
