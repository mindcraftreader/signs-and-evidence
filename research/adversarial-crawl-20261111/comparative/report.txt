PALMAR DE TROYA AND INSTITUTIONAL AUTHENTICATION — SECOND-WAVE AUDIT
11 November 2026

Result

Palmar is a substantive comparator for the kinds of reported signs: named early witnesses describe solar rotation, colors, a pale disc, fragrances and a vindicatory purpose. A contemporary newspaper and independent anthropological fieldwork make it more than an unsourced later list. This pass did not establish parity with Fátima's combined evidential package, a comparably precise independently documented public-sign prediction and fulfillment, a demonstrated ordinary explanation, or a supernatural endorsement of the later rival Palmarian papacy. No tally point or reopening follows.

The strongest sustainable result is narrower: the reported combination “Marian messages + solar signs + supporting marvels” is not exclusive to the cases accepted by Rome. That blocks using the bare presence of those categories, without examining source quality and message chronology, as a sufficient institution-identifying test. It does not block an inference from the stronger particular Fátima record. A non-equivalent comparator can test a proposed sufficient rule while failing to defeat a richer cumulative argument.

1. Strongest new early primary source

Francisco Sánchez-Ventura y Pascual's Las apariciones en el Palmar de Troya (Zaragoza: Círculo, 1970), printed pp.20–23, provides a personal account of a fast rotating, color-changing Sun on September 16, 1969, about 14:00; he connects that visit with fragrances and claimed photographs. Page20 reports a November15 request for proof answered by a solar halo/dance before about200. These are separate episodes. The author was an apparition advocate and publisher; this is not skeptical event-date journalism. The reproduction is scan OCR; exact publication day and original negatives were not recovered. At pp.89–90 he specifies six of eight seeing the September16 display while two could not look because of glare. He distinguishes earlier successful photos from three later blank Sun frames and withholds certainty that the pictures are miraculous. This is not photographic calibration.

This new source materially improves the comparator's provenance: a named near-contemporary author explicitly reports personal seeing, an approximate observing hour, and an evidential role for a solar sign. It still lacks Almeida's composition on the event date, his external reporting position, and the independently timed public expectation retained for October13. An immediate request and response, reported afterward, is not a months-ahead publicly documented prediction. Distinct Palmar dates cannot be assembled into one super-event by taking each account's strongest detail.

2. The original 1969 newspaper: what it establishes

El Diario Palentino–El Día de Palencia, August20,1969, carries “Las ‘apariciones’ en el Palmar de Troya,” Ana Aldaz of Agencia Cifra, Madrid dispatch August18. It interviews Jesuit Francisco Albarracín, identified as director of Madrid's Centro de Estudios Orientales. It attributes to him rotation, colors over people and a central silver disc, photographs, and a March17 visit; his August15 Mass is a separate event. Aldaz is not established as a firsthand solar observer. The astronomer/observatory reference is Albarracín's claim, not a retrieved instrumental report. Original-issue OCR is badly interleaved across columns; the image endpoint repeatedly returned429, so no exact page number or verbatim continuous reconstruction is certified here.

https://prensahistorica.mcu.es/es/catalogo_imagenes/grupo.do?path=8006897

This is genuine contemporary publication, but its strongest solar account is retrospective interview reporting months after the relevant visit. It does not solve the same immediacy/independent-journalist issue that defeated the Thai comparison. Nor should the book account, this interview and modern retellings be counted as three independently documented sightings of one episode. Their dates differ and their transmission histories require separate treatment.

3. Independent fieldwork: a crucial distinction between solar display and predicted climax

Anne Cadoret-Abeles, “Les apparitions du Palmar de Troya: analyse anthropologique d'un phénomène religieux,” Mélanges de la Casa de Velázquez17(1981),369–391, reports fieldwork for months each year during1968–1973. Printed pp.380–381 distinguish an April2,1970 solar rotation at dusk from a miracle announced for May15, when over10,000 gathered at18:00. The latter narrative describes announced healings and healing rumor, not a dated witnessed solar display. I retrieved ordinary page-viewer HTML and visually verified both page scans. Her article also quotes diocesan rejection; it is anthropological observation, not an optical experiment. Her opening chronology differs from the four-girls/March30 chronology of the1969 newspaper, so exact background agreement must not be invented.

This is an important adverse test of the comparator itself. It would be erroneous to cite her independent crowd estimate and prediction as confirming a spectacular solar fulfillment on May15. They do not describe that conjunction. An earlier solar episode and a later crowded healing occasion cannot inherit each other's evidential strengths. The frequently repeated much larger crowd and solar-acrobatics story for May15 needs its own original report.

William A. He does not there supply a date, optical measurements, or an assertion that he personally saw extraordinary solar motion. He also describes a predicted-climax solar account at Aldeamoret and other failed predicted climaxes. This is independent historical fieldwork, not a controlled reproduction of Fátima. 4. What the modern scholarly synthesis adds

Magnus Lundberg is Professor of Church and Mission History at Uppsala University, as the front matter of his A Pope of Their Own, second edition(2020), states. Printed pp.19–20 describe the early Catholic message and the sign bundle; p.21 discusses large pilgrim attendance. Footnotes20–24 cite earlier accounts and ABC1968. The abbreviation LBM is not an independent clinical report: the bibliography identifies a letter of April15,1972 signed by Manuel Alonso and341 pilgrims, preserved in a2011 revised Palmarian message collection. The book supplies a research map and critical institutional history, not scientific certification of the phenomena. Its bibliography gives Cadoret's page range incorrectly as369–378; Persée and the actual pages establish369–391.

https://magnuslundberg.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/palmar-second-edition.pdf

The sign categories are relevant to a proposed category-based authentication rule. The modern book does not supply a denominator, contemporaneous medical controls for the stigmata/fragrances, or a photographic reconstruction comparable with D115. The many kinds of claim belong to an interconnected movement and are not automatically independent likelihood multipliers. That is a source-specific observation: the principal evidentiary channels include the movement's own messages and petitions, overlapping advocates and later historical syntheses.

5. Roman rejection and later schism: establish sequence, avoid a false inference

Carlos Seco Caro of the University of Seville, writing in Anuario de Derecho Eclesiástico del Estado(1988), printed p.586, quotes and identifies the archbishop's May18,1970 adverse statement: Boletín Oficial Eclesiástico del Arzobispado de Sevilla1857,June1970,p.297. The original bulletin page was not recovered. This precise locator improves on loose modern assertions. The article discusses legal registration, not the physical cause of the solar reports; registration as a religious body is not supernatural authentication.

https://www.boe.es/biblioteca_juridica/anuarios_derecho/pdf/E/1988/ANU-E-1988-10058100600.pdf#page=6

The early solar reports preceded an independent Palmarian papacy by years. The early messages' Catholic setting therefore cannot simply be redescribed as public vindication of a rival pope. Neither the later organizational outcome nor later misconduct retroactively diagnoses the earlier sky experiences. Conversely, church condemnation itself supplies no physical explanation and cannot be treated as an experiment proving every early witness false. The institutional history is a reason to ask exactly which speaker, message and time a sign is said to authenticate.

6. Best challenge, strongest reply, outcome

Challenge: if publicly reported solar signs, messages, fragrances and stigmata suffice to identify an institution's divine authority, Palmar appears to satisfy the broad categories while eventually supporting an incompatible institutional claim. The challenger need not prove an ordinary mechanism merely to test that proposed sufficient rule.

Strongest reply: the real argument is about evidentially strong particular events, not a checklist of labels. Palmar lacks the retrieved full Fátima package. Its earlier Catholic content may support an apostolic-Christian thesis, and no contemporaneous comparable public sign was recovered specifically authenticating the later schismatic papacy. A genuine earlier experience could coexist with later error or appropriation. There is therefore no established contradiction in accepting Fátima while rejecting later Palmarian authority.

Assessment: that reply succeeds against the complete comparator as currently developed. The bare-category sufficiency rule is overbroad, but it is not Daniel's full defended rule. The new book and fieldwork make a focused research lead viable; they do not establish the intended equivalent counterexample, ordinary causation, or falsehood of a divine institutional endorsement at Fátima. Preserve D-01 and C-02 and all eight scored comparative outcomes. This candidate is unscored, with no proposed point.

7. Specific gaps that would change the result

• An original dated announcement defining a public sign, followed by independent event-date solar eyewitness journalism, rather than after-the-event description of an immediate proof request.
• Provenance-bearing original photographs/negatives and an actual analysis of what they record, rather than the fact that someone reported taking pictures.
• The actual doctrinal message at the same event: specifically whether the proposed authenticated proposition contradicts Rome, or merely presents ordinary Catholic devotion before later schism.
• Independent early direct witnesses with precise dates, hours, sites, and access to one another specified. A fieldworker's presence should not be changed into their endorsement of extraordinary motion.

These are distinguishing evidence targets, not demands for impossible certainty or reasons to dismiss all present testimony. In this bounded follow-up no complete survivor against the combined Marian/institutional argument was found. The meaningful gains are the1970 named firsthand account and the primary fieldwork that prevents conflating the best-looking Palmar claims.
