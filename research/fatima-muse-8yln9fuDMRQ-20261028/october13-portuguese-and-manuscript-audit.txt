Diplomatic transcription of relevant answer, joining printed line breaks, preserving older orthography:
Disse que se emendasse a gente, que não offendesse a Nosso Senhor, que estava muito offendido, que rezasse o terço e pedisse a Nosso Senhor perdão dos nossos pecados, que a guerra acabaria hoje e que esperassemos os nossos soldados muito breve.

Literal reading offered by this audit, not a certified translation:
She said that the people should amend their ways, that they should not offend Our Lord, who was deeply offended, that they should pray the Rosary and ask Our Lord's forgiveness for our sins, that the war would end today, and that we should expect our soldiers very soon.

Linguistic analysis
The first se can naturally be the pronominal element of emendar-se (amend one's ways), attracted before the verb in a subordinate clause introduced by que. It need not be the conjunction if. Emendasse, ofendesse, rezasse and pedisse are consistent with reported instructions using the imperfect subjunctive. The later acabaria can express future relative to a past speech act (would end), not necessarily the consequent of an explicit condition. A gente takes singular grammatical agreement and can refer semantically to we; the later esperassemos does not by itself prove a conditional construction.

An illustrative explicit conditional would be: Disse que, se a gente se emendasse, a guerra acabaria. That example is an explanatory reconstruction, NOT a transcription from Formigao.

A contextual conditional interpretation remains possible. The grammar alone does not establish the claim that the October13 passage unambiguously records an if-then prophecy. This audit disagrees with that strong linguistic claim; it does not claim to have interviewed native speakers or refuted the reported private consultations.

Countervailing early testimony
Jacinta's October19 answer in Formigao's interrogation explicitly has que se o povo se emendasse acabava a guerra, followed by the world-ending clause and later further questions/answers. Official DCF selection, document18, printedp104. The conditional conjunction and pronominal se are separate here. This is real early support for a conditional tradition; it prevents treating the defense as only a late invention.
