LOURDES SHRINE COMPARATORS — FIRST PASS
12 November 2026

DANIEL'S TEST FOR A LOURDES EQUIVALENT
A shrine in another religion or church where (1) its divine figure directed
a seer to dig, find or build at a specific place, and (2) there are
documented medical before-and-after healings at that site for a century or
more, comparable to the Lourdes Medical Bureau (examining cures since 1883).
Daniel grants that healings occur in all traditions; the claim concerns the
directed site plus a long, medically documented record.

RESULTS
Tinos, Panagia Evangelistria (Greek Orthodox, 1823)
  1 ✅ In visions the Virgin told the nun Pelagia to have them dig for an
       icon and build a church; a matching dream of Michael Polyzoes is
       reported; the icon was found on 30 January 1823.
  2 ❌ Strong healing reputation; no physician records or case files found.
  Fails 2. Limit: English-language sources only; Greek church archives not
  searched.
Our Lady of Kazan (Russian Orthodox, 1579)
  1 ✅ The Virgin appeared to ten-year-old Matrona in dreams; they dug in
       the ruins where shown.
  2 ❌ Traditional healing accounts (a blind man at the translation); no
       medical records.
  Fails 2.
Imam Reza shrine, Mashhad (Shia Islam)
  1 ❌ A tomb shrine, not founded on a divine direction to a seer.
  2 ❌ The library's 3 November audits found a documentary trail but no
       recovered case with Lourdes-equivalent clinical records
       (../lourdes-comparative-healing-shrines-20261102/
       comparative-review-pending-20261103.txt); no century-long medical
       record found.
  Fails both.
Mazar-i-Sharif (tomb said to be Ali's, found through a dream)
  Not established; no sources recovered in this pass. Not scored.

FINDING
No candidate meets both parts. What distinguishes Lourdes is the
century-long physician-examined record, not only its founding story.

NOTE ON THE ROME QUESTION
On part 1 alone, two Orthodox Marian shrines (Tinos, Kazan) match Lourdes
closely. They fail only on documentation, which measures attestation
rather than content. Against Orthodoxy specifically, what points Lourdes
to Rome is the 25 March 1858 statement "I am the Immaculate Conception".
