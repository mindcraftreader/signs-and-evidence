The report says no sign of conversion neurosis found during this assessment; that is a clinician's observation, not a universal exclusion of functional contributions throughout the past illness.

Pages18–19 distinguish recurrent partial blackouts from classical epilepsy or true syncope and connect their occurrence to distress; page19 explicitly concludes that an important functional component is plausible. It does not extend that conclusion automatically to every motor or visual finding.

Page20 says the carotid thrombosis hypothesis was not confirmed by angiography. Apparent interrupted circulation is attributed to intramural contrast injection and possible resulting arterial spasm. An additional hidden thrombosis/stenosis could not be verified; the precise carotid-thrombosis diagnosis cannot be retained. This is an explicit diagnostic limit inside the favorable medical report, not a later invented objection.

Page25: DrDominique Bartoli interprets residual disc pallor as evidence of genuinely organic optic-pathway injury, and calls its rapid recovery medically inexplicable. It records26November1973acuity10/10right with correction,10/10left without correction, normal visual field and persistence of recovery six years after the Lourdes event. These are reproduced clinical conclusions, not our new ophthalmological examination or histological proof of optic-nerve regeneration.

Overall: Perrin has a more concrete medical record than the summary previously in hand, including named hospital examinations and objective ophthalmic findings. Its anatomical vascular diagnosis is less certain than simplistic descriptions imply. Both findings must remain together.
