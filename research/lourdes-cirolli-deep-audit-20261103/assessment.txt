DELIZIA CIROLLI — DEEP AUDIT, 3 NOVEMBER 2026

Working synthesis; original slides have not been independently examined here.

1. Substantial affirmative medical record
St John Dowling, MB MRCGP, a Much Hadham general practitioner and CMIL member, described the case in Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine77(1984),634–638. Printed pages636–638 were visually inspected in this pass. Mollica at Catania's university orthopedic clinic obtained a biopsy. Cordaro called the lesion metastatic neuroblastoma. Payan, Nezelof and Mazabraud/Curie colleagues favored Ewing tumor, while retaining the alternative. Dowling reports no anticancer treatment: proposed amputation and irradiation were declined. The CMIL judged the recovery medically inexplicable in1982.

2. Timing and actual anatomical outcome
August1976 pilgrimage/baths did not produce immediate recovery. September films reportedly showed extension. Around Christmas in Sicily she began walking without the former pain; she was weak and weighed22kg. The family continued prayer to Our Lady of Lourdes and Lourdes water. Later films showed bone repair. Knee angulation remained. Neither pain improvement nor subsequent tumor remission should be converted into instantaneous regrowth of a perfectly normal limb. Conversely, retained deformity does not alone demonstrate retained active tumor.

3. New counterevidence and recovered documentary captions

He names Dr Kathy Pritchard-Jones, consultant pediatric oncologist at the Royal Marsden. May reports that she considered infection, including tuberculosis, possible instead of tumor;2022 wording strengthens this to probably no cancer. 4. How much this changes the assessment
The recovered captions support a substantive concern about diagnostic adequacy. They do not establish infection, much less confirmed tuberculosis, or support treating May's stronger2022 wording as an independently demonstrated histological finding. Earlier specialists' malignant diagnoses remain positive evidence. Resolving the actual specimen identity, methods, tissue preservation, exact clinical differential and full specialist opinion could materially alter confidence. A diagnosis may be questioned without supplying a proven replacement; conversely a possibility must not be promoted into the actual cause of this illness.

5. Comparative cases
Y Mori et al., Department of Orthopaedic Surgery, Kanazawa University,1997,PMID9137504, described disappearance of metastatic Ewing tumor following infection. Their17-year-old had surgery and chemotherapy before the regression. The case is not an untreated match; infection causation was the authors' hypothesis. The comparator report is now complete. L. Carvalho, FRCS, St Paul's Eye Hospital Liverpool,1973, documented untreated biopsy-supported neuroblastoma with skeletal involvement and survivalto22, but onset was four months, regression unfolded across years, and deformity/visual deficit remained. It is not an adolescent-onset counterpart. No close untreated adolescent Ewing/bony-neuroblastoma analogue was located in this bounded pass; this is not proof none exists.

6. Judgment from presently retrieved evidence
Cirolli remains a serious, documented exceptional-recovery claim with a Lourdes/Marian connection. It should not currently be presented as an uncontested, molecularly confirmed untreated Ewing cure. No ordinary cause of her actual illness and recovery has been established in this audit. A specific diagnostic challenge has, however, been located and must be evaluated before assigning the case maximum evidential weight. This conclusion concerns Cirolli; it does not automatically decide other Lourdes cases or the cumulative religious argument.
