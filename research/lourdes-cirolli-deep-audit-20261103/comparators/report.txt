DELIZIA CIROLLI: NATURAL-HISTORY COMPARATOR AUDIT
3 November 2026. Bounded public-source pass. No purchases, emails or credentialed databases.

FINDING
This pass did not find a genuinely close, fully documented comparator consisting of biopsy-confirmed Ewing sarcoma or metastatic bone neuroblastoma beginning around age 12, receiving no anticancer treatment, then completely resolving as Cirolli reportedly did. That is a search outcome, not proof no such case exists. It does not turn an unexplained recovery into an established supernatural cause. It also means that citing “neuroblastoma sometimes regresses” or a treated Ewing case as a complete explanation would oversell the literature.

CIROLLI BASELINE ACTUALLY READ
St John Dowling's JRSM 1984 article, printed p.636, visually inspected in the existing local scan jrsocmed00220-0014.png. His description is a painful swollen RIGHT KNEE with abnormal X-rays, biopsy by Professor Mollica at the Orthopaedic Clinic, University of Catania, and Professor Cordaro's report of a bony neuroblastoma metastasis. Do not substitute an exact tibia/fibula lesion solely from this page: it does not specify that bone. Dowling calls her 12 in 1976; modern birth-date reconciliation is the parent's separate task. Amputation, cobalt irradiation and further Turin consultation were proposed but no treatment was given. Four days at Lourdes in August produced no immediate improvement. September films reportedly showed extension; she deteriorated to 22 kg. Shortly before Christmas she wanted to get up and go out; swelling disappeared and subsequent X-rays showed bone repair, with genu valgum remaining.

Cordaro's neuroblastoma interpretation was reviewed by Payan (Marseille), Nezelof (Paris), and Mazabraud and Curie Institute colleagues: Ewing was favored, although neuroblastoma was not absolutely excluded. CMIL judged Ewing the correct diagnosis in 1982. These are Dowling's reports of expert review, not our direct examination of the original slides. Parent's separate lead about a 1998 Equinox re-review raising infection/TB is not established here and must be resolved separately. This audit therefore treats malignancy and exact subtype conditionally, not as a diagnosis independently verified in 2026.

1. MORI ET AL., 1997: EWING WITH REGRESSION AFTER INFECTION — RELEVANT BUT TREATED
Y. Mori and colleagues, Department of Orthopaedic Surgery, School of Medicine, Kanazawa University, Japan. Anticancer Research 17(2B):1391–1397, PMID 9137504. Full indexed abstract and affiliation read; full seven-page paper not acquired. A 17-year-old male had a destructive left pelvic lesion, biopsy interpreted as Ewing. After an operation he developed spinal metastases and paraplegia. CHEMOTHERAPY WAS INITIATED; later bone scan suggested multiple metastatic lesions. An operative-site fistula developed purulent discharge and fever. During antibiotics and fistula irrigation the fever subsided and metastases nearly disappeared radiologically. Nine years after operation he was alive without evidence of tumor. Authors hypothesized infection-driven antitumor activity.
This proves a published Ewing regression report exists after Dowling's 1984 statement that none had been recorded. It does not demonstrate untreated Ewing regression or resolve chemotherapy's contribution. Age is adolescent, but site, infection, surgery, chemotherapy and metastatic course differ from Cirolli. No infection mechanism for Cirolli is established by this analogy. The abstract does not provide modern EWSR1 fusion testing.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9137504/

2. L. CARVALHO, 1973: ACTUAL UNTREATED NEUROBLASTOMA WITH BONY LESIONS — INFANT-ONSET, INCOMPLETE ANATOMICAL RESOLUTION
L. Carvalho, F.R.C.S., St Paul's Eye Hospital, Liverpool. British Journal of Ophthalmology 57:832–835, DOI 10.1136/bjo.57.11.832. All FOUR original page images downloaded and visually read, including clinical photos and skull radiograph. Baby boy, age four months at initial admission; abdominal mass, skin lumps and orbital/skull-region swellings. Axillary-gland biopsy on 28 January 1950 established neuroblastoma according to Dr W. K. McGinley. Because of extent, poor condition and mother's reluctance he was taken home without tumor therapy. After a year most secondary deposits had disappeared and primary swelling was about one-third original size, but skull films still showed secondary deposits; abdominal mass and orbital abnormality persisted in subsequent years. He remained untreated. At age 22 radiographs showed right orbital deformity and skull sclerosis compatible with prior metastases; impaired right-eye vision, swelling and ptosis remained. Author described apparent spontaneous cure with bony metastases and no therapy.
Important comparator: regression and very long survival without cancer treatment CAN be reported in biopsy-supported neuroblastoma with skeletal involvement. Important differences: onset at four months, gradual change across years, no repeat histologic verification of each bone lesion, persistent masses/deformity and visual deficit; not abrupt complete normalization in a 12-year-old. His age 22 at follow-up must NEVER be relabeled age 22 at tumor onset or proof of adult-onset regression.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1215227/

3. N. L. T. CARLSEN, 1990: DIRECT POPULATION SURVEY — NOT A 12-YEAR-OLD ANALOGUE
Department of Paediatric Surgery, State University Hospital/Rigshospitalet, Copenhagen. British Journal of Cancer61:441–446, DOI10.1038/bjc.1990.97. Original pp441–442 visually read, not entire paper. Survey250Danish cases: the five selected unusual/regressing cases were all diagnosed below14months. They are NOT five untreated cures:
Case1:8months, suggestive biopsy unavailable for re-review, vitaminB12 for years; mass gradually vanished over10months.
Case2:3weeks, primary disappeared during29Gy radiation. Later skin/bone/node lesions stabilized then partly regressed; biopsies8and12years later showed ganglioneuroma.
Case3:13months,25Gy radiation and resection; mature ganglioneuroma, potentially reflecting selective killing of undifferentiated elements.
Case4:10months, laminectomy removed neuroblastoma; no subsequent therapy, but prior surgery precludes wholly untreated comparison.
Case5:10months, stageIV bone lesions, initially stable5months; then cyclophosphamide/vincristine, complete remission after53weeks.
The paper distinguished two definite regressions from three unusual courses possibly involving regression. Its heterogeneous population's observed proportion cannot estimate untreated regression probability in a12-year-old with bone metastasis. A later abstract describes regression as extremely rare in older children.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1971306/
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1530115/

4. MODERN CLINICAL CONTEXT: DO NOT TRANSFER INFANT RATES TO OLDER CHILDREN
Garrett M. Brodeur, Children's Hospital of Philadelphia/University of Pennsylvania Perelman School of Medicine, 2018 Cell and Tissue Research review (PMCID PMC5920563): full public HTML saved; abstract and introductory natural-history sections read. It distinguishes the special infant liver/skin/minimal marrow pattern with favorable biology from older children with bone and extensive marrow disease and poor prognosis. Its introductory account says the latter historically never regressed; this is a review statement, not a proof of impossibility. It discusses plausible regression mechanisms (neurotrophin, telomerase, immune, epigenetic), not a validated individual explanation for Cirolli.
The NCI PDQ public clinical summary's observation section was read: selected infant small localized adrenal masses and infants with non-MYCN-amplified tumors can regress under observation. These are intentionally selected populations. GPOH's patient guidance also says regression can occur in older children with LOCALIZED stage1–3/L1–L2 tumors; it does not establish a corresponding rate or full comparator for a 12-year-old with a bony metastasis.

5. LEADS THAT MUST NOT BE COUNTED AS CONFIRMED UNTREATED MATCHES
A2020 “Ewing Sarcoma of Fibula ... Disease Regression and Bone Regeneration” report (PMC7445639) explicitly describes regression FOLLOWING ONCOLOGIC TREATMENT. Its title/word“regression” cannot support spontaneous untreated recovery.
A2001 EMSOS conference abstract by S. J. Strauss, A. McTiernan, J. S. Whelan, London Bone and Soft Tissue Service/Middlesex Hospital, describes an Ewing patient with lung nodules regressing or stable after prior surgery for local recurrence. Indexed passage read; original conference abstract PDF not visually acquired in this pass, no age verified and metastases lacked histological confirmation. It cannot bear the weight of a pathology-proven untreated primary-bone cure. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2395454/
The1976 Evans/Gerson/Schnaufer series (PMID1030781) has an abstract covering24 regressions after minimal, unusual OR no treatment, but full ages and treatment table were not recovered here; not24untreated matches. Noetic databases were discovery aids only, not substituted for original clinical reports or used to estimate incidence.

BOTTOM-LINE EVIDENTIARY CONSEQUENCE
If Cirolli's original disease truly was Ewing, the readily located documented regression comparator is substantially treatment-confounded. If it was neuroblastoma, spontaneous regression is biologically established, but age/site/stage matter and these full cases do not make her course routine or supply an individualized explanation. Most useful next step is the original Cirolli biopsy/material and later diagnostic re-review, not multiplying infant remission rates or assuming “spontaneous” already names a known mechanism. No numerical probability is defensible from these handpicked anecdotes.

Search scope: combinations of Ewing/Ewing's sarcoma + spontaneous regression/remission + untreated/without treatment; neuroblastoma + spontaneous regression +12-year/11-year/adolescent/older; reference chains from1973Carvalho,1990Carlsen,2018Brodeur and NCI. This was bounded, not an exhaustive systematic review.
