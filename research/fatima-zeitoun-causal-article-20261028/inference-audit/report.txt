FATIMA AND ZEITOUN CUMULATIVE INFERENCE AUDIT
28 October 2026

Purpose
Assess what a defensible article against the supernatural conclusion can actually claim, without treating naturalism as an axiom or retreating to an impossible standard of certainty. This report reads the existing local primary-source audits rather than repeating a complete event investigation. The official DCF publication and Nelson DOI were also checked through the web tool in this pass. Separate agents are assessing each physical/perceptual case.

Recommended conclusion boundary
There is a genuine position between a demonstrated complete ordinary reconstruction and assent to Catholic vindicatory miracles: reasoned suspension of the causal judgment. The user's proposed either/or is not logically exhaustive. However, the assistant must not use that third position as a way to avoid stating its own judgment or confronting positive evidence. An article can defend withholding assent, or rank an explicit ordinary reconstruction above Marian intervention, but must say which claim it is defending. An article that merely lists possibilities cannot honestly claim to have established ordinary causation as the more likely explanation.

The strongest fair thesis is: The surviving records contain serious positive evidence for a religious interpretation, particularly Fatima's pre-event promise, but the relation between the observations and that interpretation remains inferential. There is specific evidence of ambiguous perception and interpretive influence, not just abstract permission to imagine them. Those facts make an ordinary account a substantive contender. They do not establish a single complete ordinary mechanism for both events. Consequently the record does not compel assent, while the supernatural hypothesis remains a live explanation rather than a refuted one.

If the main author judges the evidence differently after the event audits, it should say so. It must not manufacture a naturalistic verdict to comply with the user's challenge; nor concede a theological proposition it does not actually judge supported merely because a complete alternative remains unavailable.

What must be conceded without hedging

1. Fatima's advance promise was not simply invented after October. DCF selection document 3 (14 July 1917), printed p. 32, and document 10 (27 September 1917), printed p. 58, record an evidentiary promise associated with the coming sign. The latter connects the coming October 13 appearance with making people believe the Lady appears. The Catholic vindicatory intention is in the pre-event record; it is not only a later theological gloss.
2. Fatima has genuinely early reporting from people outside the original visionary circle. Contemporaneous skeptical reporting matters. Neither 'the journalists were culturally Christian' nor 'they reported others' words somewhere' erases their own direct observations. Each passage must be classified separately.
3. Distant reports exist, including 1917 letters. Their existence cannot be dismissed as a recent internet invention. But direction, contemporaneity and firsthand status vary; the strongest triangulation formulation goes beyond the retrieved sources.
4. Zeitoun's record includes affirmative civil-official treatment and important lighting checks. The five excluded extensions do not erase the narrower observations. Nelson's observation that local streetlights had been disconnected for days is especially important because it is independent of church endorsement and inconvenient for a simple streetlamp-reflection explanation.
5. Different witnesses can perceive different aspects of one real event. No argument may require everyone to see the same thing, a literally displaced astronomical sun, or unanimous belief.
6. The cumulative argument survives the dependency audit. The located record does not establish that Zeitoun is copied Fatima, that the initial witnesses were primed by Fatima, or that the physical events have one ordinary cause. Both can contribute new evidence.
7. A supernatural cause would not have to be laboratory-reproducible or have a known physical mechanism to be a historical explanation. Rejecting it on that basis alone would beg the question.

The specific ordinary-side arguments with evidential content

A. Prediction is both positive fit and a cause of the observing conditions.
The strongest ordinary response is not 'coincidences happen'. The announced date led people to assemble, watch the sky and interpret an unusual experience as the promised sign. The Alburitel hill-group correspondence specifically recalls anticipation and preparation of smoked glass. Thus the observation date and attentional setting are not exogenous to the prediction under an ordinary model. This changes the chance comparison from 'an unanticipated miracle independently happened on a randomly specified day' to 'people expecting a public sign assembled and searched for it on that day'.
Limit: this does not explain every detailed solar report, make the prediction worthless, or render the promise unfalsifiable. Had nothing happened, it could have failed. No failed-prediction denominator has been established here, so do not invoke a large unmeasured universe of failed Marian predictions as though it has been counted. The event agent must show how a proposed visual/atmospheric mechanism fits the early descriptions.

B. The early record is stronger for experiences than for a particular reconstructed light geometry.
The distant-witness audit identifies early indirect reports and later firsthand interviews, not an instrumented multi-station observation. Granja is a next-day letter by someone at the Cova relaying unnamed Granja observers; Torres Novas is an early indirect report explicitly describing a less striking phenomenon. The clearest explicit 'toward Fatima' wording comes from a much later published interview near Minde, with the exact field unidentified. Several Portuguese sources provide no compass bearing. Therefore the claim that several independent measured rays converge on a local emitter should not be treated as established.
Limit: absence of numerical azimuths does not erase meaningful geographical corroboration or refute a real regional anomaly. It makes a particular strong geometric inference unproven.

C. Zeitoun has documented perceptual ambiguity in addition to documented unexplained light.
Cynthia Nelson (American University in Cairo), observing in 1968 and publishing in 1973, gives actual episodes of a white-shirted person briefly misidentified and then corrected, and an outline becoming nunlike when she sought that form and becoming light when she stopped that interpretation. That is positive evidence that interpretation altered at least some reported perceptions. It gives pareidolia/expectation a case-specific basis.
Limit: her own account acknowledges unidentified light and disconnected streetlights. Her observations are not a universal diagnosis of every night and do not automatically overturn the committee's more articulated moving-figure reports. A hypothesis of ambiguous light plus religious interpretation must confront the strongest earlier detailed reports, not simply choose her least vivid experience as the whole event.

D. Counting records is not counting independent repetitions of the strongest assertion.
Newspapers reprint committee statements and previous newspaper reports; witnesses sometimes read those descriptions before attending. Those paths are documented. Reprinting one blackout story in ten outlets is one underlying report. However, a named observer's new firsthand account in the same newspaper remains new evidence; calling the outlet partisan does not cancel it. The numerical census must be kept separate from the magnitude of evidential support.

What does not work as an article against supernatural causation

- 'Science only accepts natural causes, therefore this was natural.' That is a rule of procedure, not a causal discovery.
- 'We cannot exclude every alternative, therefore the miracle loses.' The user's standard is comparative evidence, not certainty.
- 'The supernatural explanation has no mechanism.' An intentional-agent account can be explanatory without a physical mechanism. Its problem, if any, is insufficiently discriminating evidence, not the absence of a machine blueprint.
- 'The accounts vary, therefore no common event.' Variation can occur under a common external stimulus or under different perceptions; the actual pattern must be assessed.
- 'One eyewitness did not see Mary, therefore no one did.' This ignores night-to-night and viewpoint differences.
- 'Mary was already culturally familiar, so they must have imagined her.' Familiarity is an interpretive condition, not proof of an error.
- 'A secular government would never endorse a real miracle' or its reverse. Government conduct establishes conduct; any causal inference needs content and source assessment.
- Calling the two cases one copied story. The existing audit does not support that.
- Treating many centuries of ordinary experience as a quantified prior without showing how it bears on these selected observations, or inventing extremely small supernatural priors.
- Treating modern source gaps as disproof of the broader event.
- Invoking medical gaps when the user's principal argument is about the public apparitions and not healings.

Positive fit of the supernatural account must be faced directly
Fatima's announced date and evidentiary purpose, followed by extraordinary publicly reported experiences, are more specifically fitted by an intentional religious agent than an entirely unconnected surprise. Zeitoun's reported gestures, relation to a Marian church and recurrence have religious specificity. This is a positive explanatory argument. To answer it, the ordinary case must show how the expectation/setting/reporting process can generate the particular religious pattern from more modest external stimuli with enough probability to outweigh that fit. It must not merely say 'unknown, therefore ordinary'.

There is also a real constraint on the supernatural model. A creator's ability to cause any event does not make every possible evidential record equally predicted by the claim that Mary intentionally supplied a public vindicatory sign. If articulated, shared, clear phenomena are its expected evidence, reports that track interpretive prompts more strongly than stable external properties count against it. The need to assess that expectation is not a demand that miracles be scripted to the skeptic's preferred design. It is the normal requirement that a hypothesis make some observations more expected than others.

Avoid a theological moving target
There are distinguishable conclusions: an event exceeded ordinary mechanisms; the agent was Mary; it authenticated a Christian message; it vindicated the Catholic message associated with Fatima. Distinguishing them is legitimate, but the article must not concede genuine Mary-given public authentication at Fatima and then demand a separate proof of every Catholic dogma before calling it a Catholic vindicatory miracle. That would misdescribe the user's actual claim. If Fatima's preannounced message-and-sign account is accepted as a true Marian act, the user's general vindicatory description is reasonable. Zeitoun's silence and Coptic location do not independently establish specifically Catholic exclusivity, but the user already acknowledges that distinction. Do not pretend that observation is a new rebuttal to him.

How to state the cumulative inference without arbitrary arithmetic
Use the conditional product rule: the evidential contribution of Zeitoun is its contribution after the Fatima record and relevant background have been considered. There is no universal 'dependence discount'. The events can strengthen an intentional Marian explanation even with some shared cultural interpretation. Likewise, a supported ordinary account need not use one physical trigger for a daytime solar observation and a nighttime luminous church figure. Distinct ordinary events are not themselves an ad hoc rescue if each mechanism has independent support; proposing unspecified mechanisms solely to preserve naturalism would be ad hoc.

At minimum the article must state what ordinary explanation is being compared, what source-level assumptions it makes, what it explains positively, and what it leaves unresolved. A qualitative ranking is permissible. If the ranking reverses with plausible changes in the reliability of the articulated-figure or remote-direction reports, label it fragile. This is more informative than either unsupported 98/2 odds or unqualified language that the issue is forever unresolvable.

Suggested article structure
1. Direct thesis: explain whether the author withholds assent or positively favors an ordinary model; acknowledge the genuine force of the evidence immediately.
2. Fatima: concede documented prediction and public reporting, then analyze attention/visual experience and the actual remote-record chains. Do not lean on literal sun motion.
3. Zeitoun: retain local lights-out evidence, set out Nelson's actual observations and strongest early articulated-figure accounts, and compare explicit physical/perceptual possibilities. State where a mechanism is not identified.
4. Cumulative challenge: explain how expectation and transmission have real documented effects without collapsing two cases into a single report or assigning an invented discount.
5. Own judgment: say exactly which conclusion the author reaches and which concession is due. If no complete ordinary case can be demonstrated, concede that rather than asserting victory for skepticism. If it still does not establish a supernatural cause in the author's judgment, explain the specific unresolved inference instead of saying only 'more evidence is needed'.
