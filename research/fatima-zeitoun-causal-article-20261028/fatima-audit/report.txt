Judgment
A defensible ordinary explanation is available as a historical inference: collective solar observation under unusual viewing conditions, ordinary visual adaptation and apparent motion, interpersonal interpretation, and later testimonial preservation. Several components have direct historical or experimental support. This is stronger than merely saying that an unknown natural explanation might someday exist. However, no retrieved study reproduces the complete Fatima event, and the historical application of the mechanisms is uncertain. The article must not call the event scientifically explained or claim that every extraordinary testimony has been refuted.

My comparative assessment is a modest preference for the ordinary composite account of the securely documented optical/testimonial core, rather than an established supernatural cause. This depends on distinguishing that core from stronger late reconstructions of a localized descending emitter. It is a qualitative historical assessment, not a calculated posterior or proof that all ordinary alternatives collectively exceed a measured 50 percent. A reader who assigns greater reliability to the detailed physical claims or stronger independent prior grounds for Marian action can rationally rank the alternatives differently. The adversarial article should make the underlying reasons visible rather than pretend those differences have disappeared.

The strongest affirmative case must be stated first
The advance public-sign promise is documented before October 13. Parish priest Manuel Marques Ferreira's interrogation dated July 14 records the request for a miracle so everyone would believe and the answer promising belief in three months. Manuel Nunes Formigao, then a Santarem seminary/lycee teacher and later canonical rapporteur, recorded the explicit October 13 promise on September 27. An anticlerical newspaper correspondent, Avelino de Almeida, sincerely recorded an extraordinary solar appearance, including in a private postcard dated the event day and a detailed personal account published October 29. There are remote reports recorded in 1917, not only much later retellings. It is historically untenable to dismiss the whole case as an invented late legend or a crowd which saw nothing.

The strongest ordinary answer to the prediction
The announcement supplies both evidence of a prediction and a possible causal organizer. Under the ordinary hypothesis the event date is not an unrelated day drawn randomly from a calendar: the announcement gathered observers, sustained attention, and defined the setting in which a striking experience would count as the promised sign. Early records already include sky-focused phenomena before October, and Almeida describes attention shifting toward the solar disc and people exchanging descriptions.

That argument respects the real evidentiary purpose of the promise. It does not reduce the promise to 'something nice might happen'. Its limitation is narrower: the prior records do not specify rotation, three plunges, colors, duration, light elevation or a local emitter position. A broad public-sign prediction that helps produce its own observation conditions is less discriminating between supernatural and ordinary hypotheses than a precise independently specified physical result would be. No fraud by the children is needed for that ordinary scenario.

The war statement and Muse's actual conditional-prophecy defense
Formigao's September 27 interrogation associates Joseph/Child and peace with October 13, but does not unambiguously specify that fighting would stop that very day. Indeed, Formigao's October 19 questioning expressly recalls Lucia predicting peace shortly AFTER October 13, not that day. It would be a strawman to call September 27 an unmistakably failed unconditional October 13 war prediction.

Almeida's October 15 article does report Lucia publicly announcing the war HAD ENDED and that soldiers would return; DCF editorial note15 clarifies the Portuguese past perfect rather than future tense. This is Almeida's report of her public words, not an independent hearing of the apparition. More directly, the October16 parish-priest interrogation and October19 Formigao interrogation record Lucia attributing 'the war ends today' to the apparition. In the latter she also acknowledges possible misunderstanding when challenged.

Muse already answers this in his original article section4.1. He quotes Formigao's 1927 account reconstructing an October13 conditional statement, Jacinta's October19 conditional formulation, and Lucia's1922 recollection that Jacinta corrected her understanding. The official DCF selection printedp104 indeed preserves Jacinta's conditional reform/war language, followed by an immediate-Heaven formulation and her expectation of Sunday. Thus the conditional variant is real in early testimony, not a wholly invented modern defense. I visually checked printedpp100 and104 in the official PDF in this pass; saved images dcf-selected-102.png and -106.png.

The legitimate adverse inference is limited: the witnesses and records show uncertainty about precise transmission of the reported message. An article should not present every wording as a perfectly verified divine announcement, or select only successful-looking components. But the conditional explanation prevents using this as a decisive failed-prophecy disproof. Nor does it refute the distinct advance public-sign promise or the solar experience. Under a supernatural account the children could misunderstand; under an ordinary account sincere errors and revisions are expected. This point modestly bears on confidence in reported divine authorship rather than supplying a complete cause of the public optical event.

What the crowd and newspapers establish
Almeida's October 15 article explicitly distinguishes what different people reported and describes people asking one another whether and what they had seen. His October 29 article unmistakably presents dancing and colors as his own observation, so saying he only observed an excited crowd is wrong. The newspaper documents a sincere public experience and crosses an important ideological boundary. It is not an optical calibration instrument or an institutional scientific finding. His postcard, two articles and later reprints remain one witness. The Diario de Noticias report dated October 15 attributes descent to what some people said and describes their explanations spreading; the reporter calls it suggestion, but that causal diagnosis is interpretation rather than a controlled demonstration.

Different perceptions do not refute a common external event. The ordinary case should instead say that variability plus documented interaction fits an interpretation/perception process and prevents treating all reported details as thousands of separately recorded identical measurements.

What the remote reports establish
The strongest retrieved early distant report is Maria Joaquina Tavares de Proenca de Almeida Garrett's October 14 letter relaying unnamed Granja observers' rotation/pink-cloud experience. It predates the major October 15 accounts. It is indirect, gives neither a falling disc toward Cova nor a measured bearing/time. Adelaide Grego's November 24 letter relays a Torres Novas solar experience and expressly says its description was not comparable with Cova.

Detailed descent toward Fatima appears most clearly in Albano Barros's published Haffert interview, recorded decades later, while exact location and original Portuguese interview notes remain unresolved. The Alburitel hill account affirmatively describes prior expectation and smoked-glass preparation; remoteness did not guarantee informational isolation. No retrieved early set provides surveyed observation points and measured convergent bearings locating a light source over Cova. That prevents using the map as a completed triangulation. It does not establish borrowing or invention by distant witnesses.

Positive physiological evidence and its limits
Auguste Meessen, Institute of Physics, Catholic University of Louvain, presented a physiological hypothesis in 2003, published in 2005 conference proceedings. His personal experiments reportedly produced a grey disc/rim and colors when looking at an electric lamp and the solar disc. He expressly says he did NOT personally see solar motion. Rotation, oscillation and approach/recession are extrapolations using motion after-effects and a zoom/loom proposal. Do not describe his work as an experimental reproduction of the entire Fatima sequence. His attribution of the Fatima sun to the zenith is also inaccurate. PDF pp.4-6 were text-read, rendered and visually inspected in this pass.

A more rigorous, narrower finding: Qasim Zaidi and Robert Ennis of SUNY College of Optometry, Dingcai Cao of the University of Chicago, and Barry Lee of SUNY/Max Planck, Current Biology 22 (2012), pp.220-224, demonstrated retinal-ganglion adaptation and afterimage signals far below photoreceptor-bleaching intensities. Their display was modulated about mid-grey 62.85 cd/m2. This supports ordinary color adaptation at comfortable everyday light levels. It does not reproduce the Fatima white-disc stimulus, changing environmental illumination, coherent crowd timing or the full motion sequence.

Muse's actual latest argument and the legitimate reply
The locally saved revised reply, section1.2, says the relevant sungazing effects require retinal overstimulation by an extremely bright high-contrast source, and that attenuating enough for comfortable fixation excludes the perceptual mechanisms needed for the rest of the data. He does not literally claim every afterimage requires photopigment bleaching. Accordingly the fair criticism is that discomfort is not a universal boundary separating all ordinary visual distortions from veridical perception. Zaidi directly demonstrates ordinary afterimage mechanisms below bleaching levels. But transferring those results to white-source solar rotation/descent remains unestablished. Do not pretend a colored-pattern laboratory experiment alone answers his complete objection.

Muse also cites witnesses claiming environmental colors without deliberately looking at the solar disc. Those reports, when accepted at their detailed accuracy, are genuine difficulties for a simple sun-gazing-afterimage account. One must either establish another mechanism or state what testimonial uncertainty one is invoking. Declaring them impossible or automatically explained by shared expectation would exceed the research.

Photographic source and rapid drying
The existing local geometric audit reproduced Dalleur's numerical result: inferred source elevation about25-32 degrees against actual solar elevation about42.72 degrees. It did NOT establish that sunlight fits all the selected D115 shadow correspondences. A critic must not claim a mathematically refuted result merely because image-feature selection and vanishing-point uncertainty remain open. The image is a real evidentiary challenge whose decisive empirical correspondences have not been independently remeasured from the original high-resolution scan with a calibrated joint uncertainty analysis.

Likewise, contemporary rain and reported rapid drying should be admitted as evidence. The photograph does not independently measure wetness, elapsed drying time or infrared flux. These distinctions constrain what can be concluded; they do not establish ordinary drying as the answer to all testimony. This audit therefore does not claim that its physiological account explains a verified local light source or verified extraordinary energy transfer. It argues that those stronger physical interpretations are less securely established than the public optical experience itself.

What the adversarial article can and cannot honestly conclude
CAN: Offer an evidenced ordinary model rather than a bare appeal to logical possibility; explain why the prediction creates relevant observation conditions; distinguish eyewitness amazement from physical measurement; correct the supposed early completed remote triangulation; identify concrete uncertainty in transmission of the reported message while acknowledging Muse's conditional-prophecy reply; reject a categorical rule that comfortable vision excludes all perceptual color effects; and decline assent to a proved Catholic vindicatory cause without presuming materialism.

CANNOT: Say every observation is explained, all witnesses copied a story, the advance promise was invented later, the crowd all hallucinated identically, the real sun had to physically leave orbit for the user's claim to work, late testimony is necessarily false, or modern methods scientifically disproved the entire event. No defensible numerical odds emerge here. The article should admit that the supernatural account has positive evidentiary fit, and that some strong physical interpretations remain unresolved.

The user's requested dichotomy is not logically exhaustive: failure to demonstrate every causal step of an ordinary account does not compel a specifically Catholic miracle conclusion. Conversely, inability to prove supernatural causation does not establish an ordinary winner. An explicit modest comparative preference is more honest than either claim of decisive victory.
