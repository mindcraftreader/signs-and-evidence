D115 head directions, 8 November 2026

Question
Do the spectators who appear to be watching face the lower light source inferred
from the shadows? Agreement in gaze would support identifying that source with
the light they watched in this exposure.

Method
I manually marked visible features on six faces selected as possible watchers,
plus two faces turned elsewhere, using the 1669 by 1181 Commons D115 JPEG.
I fit the landmarks to the MediaPipe canonical face with OpenCV PnP and used
Dalleur's 37 mm equivalent focal length, estimated top crop, and 4.79 degree
downward pitch for the nominal camera. I expressed the fitted 3D head axes in
a level camera frame, with azimuth positive to the right of straight behind
the camera and elevation measured in the vertical plane of each axis.
Dalleur's 90 degree turn is a side view of his 3D reconstruction.
The 400 perturbations per face test sensitivity to landmark picks, face shape,
focal length and principal point. Their percentiles are descriptive, not
confidence intervals. Synthetic projections recovered four known directions.

Result
Five of the six selected faces fit to an upward and rightward head axis.
Their nominal axes vary by about 21–75 degrees right and 8–19 degrees up.
The vector average of all six gives about 42 degrees right and 10 degrees up.
Dalleur's nominal lower source is about 35 degrees right and 28 degrees up.
This supports broad directional consistency. The profile fits can move by
tens of degrees when the inputs change, and the pupils are too indistinct
to estimate eye direction from this JPEG. The head-axis average therefore
cannot verify a gaze match at 28 degrees or discriminate between 28 degrees
and the roughly 43 degree solar elevation. The head fit shares Dalleur's
camera assumptions but uses different image features from the shadow fit.
D115 supplies one exposure. The witness sequence bears on whether it caught
the reported plunge.

Files

head-directions.csv and landmarks.json supply the numerical results and inputs.
