PAIRED DATING AUDIT: NEWER LINEN METHODS AND RADIOCARBON
27 October 2026


LATER 27 OCTOBER REANALYSIS
The 82-year mechanical holdout is not a general accuracy estimate. Our
reproduction of the published calculation gives an approximately 1330-year
older error when another included C14 reference textile is withheld. This is
our reanalysis, not a new published experiment. See shroud-probability-analysis.txt
and its reproducible data/script before interpreting Gaussian tail probabilities.

RESULT
The clearest verified non-Shroud carbon-versus-alternative dating dataset in the
citation set is De Caro et al. (2019), X-ray Dating of Ancient Linen Fabrics.
Four non-anchor WAXS checks differ from the published radiocarbon reference
central values by 10, 40, 0 and 110 years: mean absolute difference 40 years.
This is positive agreement in the reported sample set. It is not a +/-40-year
precision estimate or a broad population error estimate.

SCOPE AND INTERPRETATION OF THE USER'S REQUEST
The 85 citation links are 46 + 16 + 23 incoming links to three founding papers
(the first introduces both FTIR and Raman). They identify 68 distinct OpenAlex
records. At least three named Wiley chapters have duplicate edition records;
one item is an issue/paratext record, and one article is retracted. Therefore
85 should not be described as 85 independent experiments or even 85 distinct
papers. This audit screens all 68 records by title, metadata and available
abstracts, then inspects the promising accessible full texts. It is not a claim
that all 68 full texts have been read. Corpus-screening.csv records the access
level and unresolved gaps for every record.

To calculate a date difference, a paper must give two numerical calendar-age
estimates for the same material: a reference carbon age and an alternative
FTIR/Raman/mechanical/WAXS age. Neither method is required to be labelled the
primary method. Merely using FTIR to identify linen or contamination alongside
a radiocarbon age does not supply a second calendar date. Similarly, using
carbon-dated cloth to build a calibration chart is recorded, but not treated as
independent validation of that same chart. Shroud disagreements and reused
control data are recorded separately from non-Shroud benchmarking.

The phrase 'average data range' is interpreted in two useful ways:
1. Average absolute separation between the central age estimates.
2. Average width of the reported approximately 95% age intervals.
Averaging the ages themselves across different ancient objects would not
measure agreement between the methods.

WAXS: FOUR RADIOCARBON REFERENCE CHECKS
Source: De Caro et al. 2019, Heritage 2, 2763-2783.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage2040171
Table 1 identifies the four reference dates as radiocarbon dates. Section 6
states that these four dates were not used to determine the two main model
parameters; those were set using sample D and historically dated sample FII.
Table 3 reports aging times in centuries. Below they are converted to years.
These are aging-time values as reported, not uncalibrated radiocarbon BP ages.

Sample / place      Carbon reference         WAXS estimate          Gap
DII / Jericho       950 +/-80                 940 -70/+70           10
NII / Engedi        2310 +/-60                2350 -290/+510        40
E / Egypt           2390 +/-40                2390 -230/+280         0
HII / Thebes        2880 +/-140               2770 -360/+600       110

All four approximately 95% intervals overlap.
Mean absolute central-estimate difference: 40 years.
Median absolute difference: 25 years.
Observed difference range: 0-110 years.
Mean signed difference (WAXS age minus reference age): -20 years.
Root-mean-square difference: 58.7 years.
Mean carbon-reference interval width: 160 years.
Mean WAXS interval width: 602.5 years, or about 600 years total width.
The WAXS intervals are asymmetric for older samples; do not turn the average
width into a universal symmetric margin of error.

The source deliberately replaces potentially multimodal calibrated carbon
ranges with equivalent symmetric central values/limits for comparison. These
are rounded tabulated summaries, not full probability distributions. In Table 1
the calendar-range widths differ slightly from the rounded Table 3 widths.
The 160-year mean above uses Table 3 consistently with the age differences.
The mean width from the four Table 1 calendar ranges is 152.5 years instead.

The two model anchors are excluded from this average. The chemically bleached
modern sample and very old saturated sample do not produce comparable eligible
checks in this analysis. The four checks are from one research group's study,
with temperature/humidity conditions incorporated in its ageing model. They
are not four independent laboratories or four different alternative methods.

MECHANICAL METHOD: AN ADDITIONAL POSITIVE HOLD-OUT CHECK
Source: Basso, Fanti and Malfi 2015, Monte Carlo method applied to the mechanical
dating of the Turin Shroud. Section 6 and Table 7, PDF page 13.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/20153601003
The authors remove sample FII from the nine-sample calibration and predict its
age using the remaining eight. Reference AD 65 +/-12 years (95%); mechanical
prediction AD 147 +/-214 years (95%). Absolute central-date difference 82 years.
The intervals, AD 53-77 and AD -67 to 361 under the paper's signed-year
convention, overlap. This is one reference prediction, not three independent
samples because three mechanical readings contributed to it.

Important reference-provenance ambiguity: the companion 2015 combined-method
Table 1 labels FII radiocarbon-dated, while the 2013 spectroscopy paper and
2019 WAXS paper identify the Masada reference as historical. The WAXS paper
explicitly uses the Siege of Masada historical interval AD 55-74. No original
laboratory C14 determination for FII was traced in this pass. Therefore the
82-year check is retained as a successful known-age comparison but is not
silently pooled with the four unambiguous carbon-reference pairs. The +/-12
reference interval in this mechanical paper and AD 55-74 in WAXS are also
slightly different reported uncertainty conventions.

FTIR AND RAMAN: WHAT THE CITING PAPERS ACTUALLY PROVIDE
Fanti, Malfi and Crosilla 2015, DOI 10.1051/matecconf/20153601001:
Includes radiocarbon reference textiles for calibration and three Shroud age
estimates. The training references do not constitute a second independent
non-Shroud validation sample set. A fitted residual calculation would have to
be labelled as such, with exclusions and rounded inputs handled explicitly.
It is not supplied here as an estimate of independent method performance.

Baraldi and Tinti 2015, Molecular Spectroscopy as an alternative for dating
Textiles, DOI 10.1051/matecconf/20153601002:
Explains FTIR/Raman reference calibration and applies it to the Shroud. Does
not add a reported series of unknown non-Shroud textile dates independently
checked by radiocarbon.

Bonizzoni et al. 2016, DOI 10.1016/j.microc.2015.11.011:
Table 2 identifies a Fayyum sample at about AD 600 as radiocarbon-dated. The
paper studies spectral ageing signatures and relative chronology, rather than
publishing a separate Raman calendar age for that sample. Its new experimental
data are real, but no paired calendar-age difference can be computed from them.

Barta et al. 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.microc.2024.112502:
Full paper obtained via University of Akron library access. Uses new modern
irradiation/heating experiments and reanalyses existing Shroud data, including
the reused 1988 carbon date. It is not a new non-Shroud carbon-paired series.
For completeness, Table 5 gives modern unirradiated controls expected at
AD 2020, predicted by FTIR as AD 1598 and 1626: absolute gaps 422 and 394 years,
mean 408 years. They are pieces from one fabric, not independent ancient
textiles. These shifts lie within the paper's broad stated dating uncertainty.
They are not carbon-versus-FTIR pairs and are not pooled with the WAXS results.
The heated sample gives AD 1396 and tests treatment effects, not natural age.

OTHER CO-USE OF CARBON DATING AND MATERIAL ANALYSIS
Quiles et al. 2014, DOI 10.4000/archeosciences.4222:
Full publisher PDF obtained in the follow-up. Reports infrared detection of
bitumen and radiocarbon dating/pretreatment experiments on mummy textiles.
Table 3 supplies five uncontaminated C14 reference textiles with ATR-IR, but
no separately calculated infrared calendar age. The documented bitumen-related
C14 contamination errors are retained as relevant limitations of radiocarbon.
They do not demonstrate bitumen on the Shroud. See the follow-up report.

Marie Ferrant, doctoral thesis, 2021:
https://theses.hal.science/tel-03616167
Full thesis downloaded and text searched; relevant Chapters 3 and 5 inspected.
Infrared spectra diagnose degradation and contamination; radiocarbon provides
the calendar dates before/after cleaning. Two differently pretreated carbon
dates are not an independent FTIR-versus-carbon comparison.

Carbonaro et al. 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.culher.2023.08.004:
Measures stable carbon isotope composition in ancient and modern linen, not
paired radiocarbon and alternative calendar ages. A stable-isotope reading is
not automatically a radiocarbon date.

REVIEW STATUS AND LIMITS
Verified numerical non-Shroud comparisons are one WAXS series of four C14
reference checks and a mechanical leave-one-out check whose historical/C14
reference attribution needs clarification. There is no defensible combined
average across all four methods from the independent paired results located.
This does not establish that no other comparison exists anywhere, nor that
full texts marked unavailable contain none. Books/chapters and several papers
remain metadata/abstract-only in the screening table. The paper list is a
citation-based convenience set, not a systematic sample of all archaeological
method-validation literature. An average over four selected qualifying samples
is not a general error rate for all textiles, particularly excluded treatments.
