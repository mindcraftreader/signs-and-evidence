PADRE PIO: WOUND EXAMINERS AND WITNESSES
Research roster, 28 October 2026

Scope: named people identified in the sources reviewed, not an exhaustive census of everyone who saw Pio during fifty years. A person’s presence or professional title is not counted as proof of a complete medical examination or agreement about supernatural causation. Multiple documents by one person do not create independent witnesses. Monastic names are retained to distinguish people with similar religious names.

Principal source: Gerardo Di Flumeri (editor), Le stigmate di Padre Pio da Pietrelcina: Testimonianze – Relazioni, second edition, San Giovanni Rotondo, 1995. Sixty-one numbered documents, including Pio’s own letters, repeated letters by the same witness, contemporary reports and later recollections. Printed-page and document numbers below refer to that edition. Original archival references and biographical notes are printed alongside documents.
Primary-source PDF: https://www.imagomundi.biz/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/PBSO37-Le_stigmate_di_Padre_Pio_da_Pietrelcina-Gerardo_di_Flumeri.pdf



MEDICAL REPORTS/IMAGING (9 records)

Luigi Romanelli
  Credentials/role: Chief surgeon, civil hospital of Barletta
  Date(s): 1919–1920; five visits
  Observation and limits: Personally examined hands, feet and side; reports and letters survive.
  Source locator: 45–50; printed pp. 147–172

Amico Bignami
  Credentials/role: Full professor of medical pathology, Royal University of Rome
  Date(s): July 1919; report 26 July
  Observation and limits: Personally examined wounds; proposed an ordinary pathological/chemical explanation rather than endorsing a supernatural cause.
  Source locator: 51; pp. 173–178

Giorgio Festa
  Credentials/role: Physician and surgeon in private practice, Rome
  Date(s): 1919, 1920, 1925
  Observation and limits: Repeated direct examinations; examined side again during the 1925 hernia operation; rejected trauma/caustic injury as adequate explanations.
  Source locator: 52–55; pp. 179–296

Angelo Maria Merla
  Credentials/role: Physician; socialist mayor of San Giovanni Rotondo, 1915–1919; friary physician
  Date(s): 1919; assisted surgery 1925
  Observation and limits: Early limited inspection, reported through a government report and Placido; could not determine precise cause. Not equivalent to a recovered signed wound report.
  Source locator: 20–21 and 55; pp. 71–76, 295–296

Luigi Pancaro
  Credentials/role: Physician working in Foggia and with Casa Sollievo; described as an internist by Muse; later worked in Canada
  Date(s): 1954–1955
  Observation and limits: Signed reports; blood and clotting investigations, stained-cloth examination; directly inspected the left-hand stigma in 1955 and expressly did not inspect the others then.
  Source locator: 56–57; pp. 296–300

Alberto Caserta
  Credentials/role: Radiologist from Foggia
  Date(s): October 1954
  Observation and limits: Performed hand/foot radiographs; role establishes skeletal imaging rather than a complete skin-wound examination. Sources differ between 13 and 14 October.
  Source locator: 58; pp. 300–303; photographic appendix 17–21

Francesco (Franco) Lotti
  Credentials/role: Physician; chief pediatrician, Casa Sollievo della Sofferenza
  Date(s): Medical care 1951–1956; filmed 22 September 1968
  Observation and limits: Reported blood tests and radiology arrangements; filmed final Mass and analyzed visible hand signs. His conclusion about the later disappearance relies on others’ postmortem report.
  Source locator: 58–59; pp. 300–308

Andrea Cardone
  Credentials/role: Physician in Pietrelcina; treated Pio from 1910
  Date(s): Statement 9 November 1968
  Observation and limits: Reported seeing and palpating hand perforations. Statement does not date the particular examinations; editor explicitly flags limits of the late recollection.
  Source locator: 60; p. 308

Giuseppe Sala
  Credentials/role: Pio’s physician; assistant in medical semiotics, University of Naples; mayor of San Giovanni Rotondo, 1966–1974
  Date(s): Later life; postmortem 23 September 1968; statement 7 July 1969
  Observation and limits: Present for direct postmortem inspection and photography. Explicitly says he had not directly inspected the living wounds; do not count him as an independent confirmation of their original depth.
  Source locator: 61; pp. 309–312

SOURCE CAUTIONS THAT AFFECT EARLIER CHAT CLAIMS
1. The Capuchin editor himself recognizes disagreements over depth, dimensions and location. A roster is not unanimous endorsement.
2. Sala personally inspected the body after death but expressly had not directly inspected living wounds. His after-death observations and the earlier depth descriptions must not be collapsed into one longitudinal examination.
3. The editor says the postmortem chest photographs miss the precise former side-wound location. That limits those particular photographs; it does not erase hand/foot photographs or all testimony about the side.
4. The eight-day report has three named signatories; Placido separately reports participation. They should not be called four signatories.
5. The source contains a contrary secondhand seal-breaking account from Giuseppe Maria; editor notes lack of confirmation in the signed report and Paolino’s memoir. Preserve this conflict for assessment.
6. Cardone’s late statement is explicitly qualified by the collection’s own editor.
7. Dottoressa can denote a university graduate in Italian. Rina Giordanelli is not entered as a licensed physician without further verification.
8. No university appointment or specialist qualification is invented for physicians identified only as local clinicians. Historical professore is not automatically a university chair.
