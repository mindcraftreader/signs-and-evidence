ZEITOUN: ORIGINAL INVESTIGATION REPORTS, POWER CUTOFF AND HEALINGS
27 October 2026

WHAT CHANGED IN THIS PASS

The case now has inspected documentary support beyond the podcast: a six-page
Arabic Ministry report with a reproduced signature and date, a four-page Arabic
committee-report reproduction, and a signed individual physician's case report.
There are also eight selected medical summaries in the 1969 booklet and dated
examination accounts in contemporary newspaper reporting, available here through
a modern translation. These source types are identified separately below.

POWER CUTOFF: POSITIVE FINDING AND EXACT SCOPE

The Ministry of Tourism's report is dated Cairo, 30 April 1968. Its last page
names William Farid Basili, Director of Inquiries and Complaints, and shows a
signature. Its third page describes doubts about reflected electric light,
searchlights directed at the figure, nearby lamps being smashed and the lights
of the whole area being extinguished while the figure reportedly remained.

The key Arabic phrase is: اطفأوا انوار المنطقة كلها
A close translation is: they extinguished all the lights in the area.
The surrounding text says the figure appeared clearer/more radiant afterward.
This was checked visually in the actual Arabic scan, not only in a modern
English transcription. It is affirmative contemporary documentary evidence for
a lights-out intervention, not merely a story originating in a 2026 interview.

The wording does not specify a measured blackout radius, a power-grid switching
record, how long mains supply was disconnected, or an officer who performed
the intervention. Thus the later claim of a multi-hour, five-kilometre district
cutoff has more detail than this retrieved passage establishes. Nor does the
passage identify a particular crowd photograph or its exposure/flash settings.

Watani's modern English transcription for 5 May 1968 repeats the same sequence.
It appears within reporting of the Ministry's account, so this should not be
counted as a second independent cutoff experiment. The original Arabic newspaper
pages have been saved; the specific newspaper paragraph has not yet been
independently located and translated. The Ministry's own scan supplies the
directly checked Arabic passage.

The June 1967 wartime church-lighting precursor is a separate claim. It was not
independently established by these 1968 records. A separately described 15-mile
equipment search appears in a later Zaki copy/reprint; no original search log,
officer list or search inventory was found. Do not merge the search radius with
the blackout boundary.

FIRST CHURCH INVESTIGATION

The report dated 30 April 1968 describes visits beginning 23 April. It names
Guirguis Matta, Youhanna Abdel-Messih and Benjamin Kamel, all identified as
priests in the Arabic. Some modern English titles are mistranslated as bishop
or archbishop. The document reports conversations with named garage workers,
then the investigators' own repeated attendance and visual observations of a
luminous figure, movement, bowing, blessings and luminous dove-like forms.

This is stronger evidence than a present-day narrator merely saying that an
investigation occurred. The saved images are printed-page reproductions of the
report with typed names under a signatures heading. They are not inspected
handwritten original reports or three independently authenticated signatures.
No cutoff engineering record is appended to this four-page reproduction.

MATTA: A REAL SIGNED PHYSICIAN REPORT FOUND

The scan identifies Dr Zarif Bishara Rizk, FRCS, MRCOG, consultant surgeon and
gynaecologist at Manchiet El Bakry Hospital, Cairo. The report describes Matta's
admission after a motor-car accident on 27 June 1967: a serious open elbow
injury, muscle damage and fracture/dislocation. It records that the main artery
and median nerve were intact, that an attempt to approximate muscle failed,
and that the arm was immobilised in plaster. Serum and antibiotics were given;
the wound subsequently healed over 55 days. Later movement was very limited.

The clinician then describes finding practically full elbow flexion and
extension, and states he cannot explain that restored range medically. The
reproduced signature is followed by a handwritten date in May 1968; the day
appears to read 15, but the source image should govern any exact transcription.

This is a specific medical attestation of serious injury and unexpectedly good
later function. It merits investigation on its own terms. The report does not
describe the apparition or establish the exact instant of recovery. That link
comes from Matta's narrative and the committee's account. The hospital chart,
original operation notes, objective strength testing and sustained follow-up
have not been retrieved. An unexplained recovery reported by the treating
surgeon is evidence; it does not itself identify supernatural causation.

The modern page's twelve-year duration conflicts with the dated 1967 accident.
The twelve-year hand-paralysis history appears in a different case, Linda Kamal.
Keep those patients separate. The signed report supplies the firmer chronology.

OTHER MEDICAL INVESTIGATIONS

The 1969 booklet names the medical committee head, Prof. Dr Shafik Abdel-Malek,
and gives eight selected cases. The cases are individually registered below.
It says fuller files were kept by the medical branch; those files have not
thereby become inspected records in our possession.

Watani, 2 June 1968, has a more specific procedural account: patients were
invited to the committee office at Anba Ruweis, prior records collected,
specialist referrals arranged, and individual reports prepared. Its modern
French transcription reproduces Dr Shafiq Abdel Malek's examinations of
Dr William Rizk (29 May) and Iyad Malak Shenouda (28 May). It also describes
the reporter accompanying examinations. This supports an active contemporary
medical process; it is more specific than an undated claim that doctors agreed.
The French transcription contains date mistakes and has not been checked
against the underlying Arabic clinical paragraphs. Mark these two cases as
dated published examination accounts, below an inspected signed individual scan.

Watani's 26 May transcription calls the committee head a former professor at
Ain Shams Medical School. Some later summaries instead say Cairo University.
That affiliation discrepancy remains open. Dr Aziz Fam is identified as a Cairo
University urology professor in the committee's cancer-case summary; that is
the source's attribution, not an independent verification of his faculty file.

WHAT WE CAN DEFEND NOW

The power-cutoff narrative has contemporary governmental documentary support,
and Matta's recovery has a reproduced signed physician attestation. The claim
that no investigation or medical evidence exists is not supported by this pass.
The stronger claim that every narrated healing is fully clinically documented,
or that the precise blackout geography and illumination photographs are settled,
would outrun what has actually been recovered.

The next missing records are concrete: the utility/police/search records;
Matta's hospital chart; the cancer patient's pathology and serial examinations;
Atwa's named surgeon and original statement; and the committee's full patient
files and follow-up. Their absence from this public retrieval does not establish
that they never existed. No emails or requests to custodians were sent.

H01: Wagih Rizk Matta
Prior condition: Motor-car injury on 27 June 1967; open elbow injury, fracture/dislocation, reported avulsion of biceps and brachialis; later severely restricted elbow movement.
Record: Reproduced typed, signed physician report, May 1968.
Medical personnel: Dr Zarif Bishara Rizk; report names Manchiet El Bakry Hospital. Modern narrative also names other treating doctors.
Reported result: Surgeon reports later full elbow flexion/extension and says he cannot medically explain it.
Limits/next record: Report describes operative care, intact artery/median nerve, failed attempt to approximate muscle, plaster, serum and antibiotics. Wound healed over 55 days. Does not date the recovery to the apparition, document the instant of recovery, supply strength testing or establish long-term follow-up. Obtain hospital records and better copy of signature/date.
