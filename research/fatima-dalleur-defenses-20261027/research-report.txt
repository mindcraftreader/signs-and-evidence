DALLEUR: DEFENSES AND SUPPORTIVE AUTHORS
Research date: 27 October 2026

Scope
This report distinguishes the author's technical arguments, correspondence reproduced by Ethan Muse, and favorable discussion by other authors. No independent replication of the D115 reconstruction was located in this search. That is a search result, not proof none exists.

The core defense is that corresponding object and shadow points constrain a projected illumination direction. Sloping ground does not by itself invalidate that construction. Camera assumptions and the identification of the actual shadow features still matter. Dalleur varies equivalent focal distance, and discusses pitch and roll. His stated estimates are about 25-32 degrees, compared with the Sun's roughly 42.7 degrees. This is not a demonstrated joint confidence interval for all measurement and model uncertainties.

His current website addresses lens distortion using the probable historical camera/lens type and historical optical measurements. The exact camera and lens used for D115 have not been positively calibrated. Do not identify current website material as a dated, named reply to Bradley or Fodor without evidence of its publication history.

His examples include real photographs as well as synthetic scenes. Castel Gandolfo is a known-source example with the Sun visible and no shadows; it does not test ambiguous faint shadow matching. Saint Hubert supplies another worked example. These examples support the plausibility of the geometry but do not constitute a matched, blind validation dataset for D115.

2. Specific response to Mark Grant
Public source: Ethan Muse, It Wasn't the Sun!, section 3, including correspondence attributed to Dalleur.
https://motivacredibilitatis.substack.com/p/it-wasnt-the-sun

Grant's simulated bright overhead illumination illustrates a possible failure mode: selected boundaries can yield a light elevation well below the actual simulated solar elevation. Dalleur replies that the simulation washes out the directional information, while D115 contains more specific contours involving the hat, finger, walking stick/knee, and stones. He argues a countermodel must reproduce those constraints together, rather than merely a broadly similar illumination pattern. He asks for the scene and additional features. This is a substantive reply. This research did not obtain and refit Grant's scene, and did not locate a completed independent fit resolving the disagreement. The publicly accessible reply is correspondence published by Muse, rather than a separate journal rebuttal.

Assessment
Dalleur has offered specific defenses of his construction and of the features he identifies. Treating the method as automatically impossible because the ground is sloped, or saying all his examples are CGI, is inaccurate. Grant's simulation and questions about feature selection and combined uncertainty remain substantive issues. Other supportive writers strengthen the documented debate around the argument, but their agreement should not be counted as separate experimental replications.
