PIO’S TWO APPROVED HEALINGS: DOES THE PUBLIC MEDICAL RECORD MAKE THEM OPEN AND SHUT?
31 October 2026

ASSESSMENT
The published records substantiate two serious recoveries, actual canonical investigation, favorable medical-board judgments and formal papal recognition as miracles. They do not presently let this audit independently confirm that ordinary explanations are excluded. “Open and shut” is justified for the existence of the Church’s approvals; it is not justified as this audit’s own medical conclusion. Colella remains the more compelling case on reported clinical severity. Neither case has been medically refuted here. No debate score assigned.

METHOD
Rechecked the relevant complete printed decree pages against page images: AAS91(1999),588–589, and AAS94(2002),492–493. Reinspected reproduced De Martino hospital discharge and signed Basile attestation. Read the published reconstructions by nephrologist Pietro Gerardo Violi, who assisted Capuchin postulation. Compared the lymphatic case against the full saved Leuven2021 article, and Colella against the published1996 ICU-study abstract and the recovered Erasmus2010 full text. These studies do not directly investigate either miracle claimant. Published reproductions are distinguished from a complete authenticated hospital chart and signed medical-board relation.

1. CONSIGLIA DE MARTINO
What is positively supported:
• Salerno hospital admission1November1995 after painful/pressure symptoms and left supraclavicular swelling following physical effort.
• CT with and without contrast recorded substantial fluid across cervical, mediastinal and retroperitoneal regions. Estimated volume about2litres.
• Swelling decreased2November, absent3November; subsequent examinations documented fluid resolution. No medical/surgical treatment before improvement is stated in the decree and Violi’s reconstruction.
• The medical board30April1998 unanimously judged recovery very rapid, complete, stable and scientifically inexplicable. Theological and cardinal/bishop reviews were favorable; papal decree21December1998 declared a divine miracle through Pio’s intercession. This favorable expert judgment is substantive evidence, not merely an uninvestigated anecdote.

Diagnostic distinction:
The original-looking signed attestation by Dr Ferdinando Basile of Salerno’s S.Giovanni di Dio e Ruggi d’Aragona hospital explicitly calls lymphatic-duct rupture a presumptive diagnosis. The reproduced discharge form also uses suspected traumatic-infiltration wording. Violi reports the inference from fluid distribution and clinical history; no aspiration/biochemical confirmation or direct visualization of a tear is established by the recovered material. The fluid and its resolution are documented; the exact lesion’s demonstration is a separate question. The final decree nonetheless accepts thoracic-duct rupture. We have not recovered the board’s detailed reasons resolving that diagnostic issue.

Relevant primary clinical comparison:
Albrecht Betrains and colleagues at University Hospitals Leuven reported8women with spontaneous cervical swelling syndrome in Oxford Medical Case Reports2021,doi10.1093/omcr/omab029. Self-limiting episodes averaged3.8days(range1–7) among patients with symptom-free intervals; one patient had persistent swelling. Exertion was a reported trigger. All had left supraclavicular edema;7had mediastinal involvement;5had pleural fluid; one had ascites; acute lymphoscintigraphy demonstrated leakage in3examined patients. Thus rapid resolution of lymphatic-region swelling is a documented ordinary clinical phenomenon, not merely a suggested possibility.

Important mismatches:
Most Leuven patients had recurrent symptoms; one had a first/single episode and one persistent swelling. The paper does not establish a measured2litre collection identical to De Martino’s or demonstrate her diagnosis. A transient obstruction/leakage syndrome is not automatically the same as a traumatic structural rupture. Case-specific imaging, labs and exclusion reasoning are needed. We cannot assert the Vatican overlooked this diagnosis; detailed board reasoning is unread.

Earlier literature:
Claude Franceschi and colleagues, Vascular Surgery Department,Hôpital Saint-Joseph,Paris,reported4patients with recurrent swelling and thoracic-duct dilation/obstruction in Phlebology2012,doi10.1258/phleb.2011.011006; abstract inspected. Serena Preyer and colleagues at University of Tübingen described4women with recurrent supraclavicular lymphangiectasia in Lymphology1995,28:118–125. That earlier series has different morphology and surgical/histologic investigation: not an untreated duplicate. Its existence dates a related diagnostic literature to1995; it does not show what the1998 board knew or considered. Web-hosted article text inspected; attempted local PDF download returned HTML and was relabeled, not represented as a recovered PDF.

Verdict: documented recovery with real favorable expert review; a concrete differential prevents this audit from calling natural causes conclusively excluded. Not a diagnosed natural explanation and not a demonstrated overturning of the miracle decree.

2. MATTEO PIO COLELLA
What is positively supported:
• Severe meningococcal disease beginning20January2000, septic shock, abnormal clotting, renal/hepatic injury and respiratory/cardiovascular failure. The decree explicitly records cardiac arrest and prolonged hypoxemia.
• Intensive treatment, ventilation and resuscitation; treating staff expected death or serious residual damage. Violi’s published reconstruction quotes multiple clinical witnesses and chart entries, and reports16diocesan witnesses, two follow-up examiners and a5–0 medical-board decision. The full signed board minutes are not recovered.
• Improvement began21January. Sedation and paralysis were stopped31January; early-February notes describe alertness and recovery, and discharge was26February. This was not an untreated, instantly completed recovery. Continued treatment does not itself explain the unexpectedly favorable course.
• The medical board22November2001 judged recovery rapid, complete, lasting, without sequelae and scientifically inexplicable. Papal decree20December2001 recognized a divine miracle through Pio’s intercession.

Specific remaining medical question:
Why did his particular physiologic history, treatment response and complete recovery exceed plausible rare ICU outcomes? The observation that some children survive meningitis is too broad to answer. Conversely, the count of9affected systems does not independently establish a validated100%death probability; the organ definitions, severity, timing and underlying prognostic evidence must be checked. Di Raimondo’s strong prognosis is quoted by Violi; the original full medico-legal report and supporting prognostic references were not obtained. A reported expectation of death is evidence of severity, not mathematical proof of impossibility.

Relevant primary comparisons:
Quen Mok, Hospitals for Sick Children/Great Ormond Street,London, and Warwick Butt,Royal Children’s Hospital,Parkville,Victoria: Intensive Care Medicine1996,22:259–263,doi10.1007/BF01712247. In35children with meningococcal septicemia admitted1985–1992, many required ventilation/inotropes and21developedDIC,renal failure and/or skin/limb necrosis. Overall survival66%; surviving children described as functionally normal. Publisher’s abstract and affiliations read, not full subscription article. Cohort is not matched to Colella’s worst physiology; functional normality is not proof of absolutely no residual finding. The66%must not be applied as his personal survival chance.

Corinne Buysse and colleagues,Erasmus MC–Sophia Children’s Hospital,Rotterdam: Critical Care2010,14:R124,doi10.1186/cc9087. Full text recovered from university repository. Follow-up of120meningococcal septic-shock survivors admitted1988–2001 found73with at least one specified adverse outcome and47without those defined outcomes. Survivor-only sample; no overall survival probability or individual Colella estimate follows. Median ICU stays and severity differ from his reported course. This supports the limited proposition that favorable long-term recovery after meningococcal shock is documented, not that Colella’s exact recovery has been explained.

Verdict: a serious, medically grave and formally approved case, with stronger specificity than an anonymous healing anecdote. The current audit has not overturned the favorable board conclusion, but has not independently established biological impossibility or excluded treatment-mediated rare recovery.
