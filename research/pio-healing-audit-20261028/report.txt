PADRE PIO HEALINGS: PRIMARY-RECORD AND CANONICAL AUDIT
28 October 2026

The best starting cases are Consiglia De Martino and Matteo Pio Colella. Both have actual public Vatican miracle decrees, not merely a biography saying the Vatican approved them. Both events occurred after Pio’s death. They therefore support the wider intercessory claim but are not observations of bedside healings performed by the living Pio.

This pass adds two lifetime cases: Wanda Półtawska, with contemporary correspondence, and Gemma Di Giorgi, with public participant testimony but a much weaker recovered diagnostic dossier. It is a prioritized audit, not a census of all reported healings. No debate point is adjudicated.

1. WHAT THE PRIMARY VATICAN RECORDS ESTABLISH

Consiglia De Martino: the 21 December 1998 decree, AAS91(1999), pp.587–589, records acute symptoms 31 October 1995, Salerno admission the following day, two CT examinations showing extensive fluid, roughly 2 litres, and an inferred traumatic thoracic-duct rupture. No therapy was applied before improvement. Swelling reduced 2 November and was absent 3 November; later examinations found no collection. The medical board unanimously judged recovery rapid, complete, lasting and scientifically inexplicable on 30 April 1998. Theological and cardinal/bishop stages followed. John Paul II declared the healing a divine miracle through Pio’s intercession. This was the beatification miracle.
https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-91-1999-ocr.pdf#page=587

Matteo Pio Colella: the 20 December 2001 decree, AAS94(2002), pp.491–493, records 20 January 2000 illness/admission, grave deterioration despite medical care, ICU intubation/ventilation, hypotension, coagulation abnormalities and other organ injury. Improvement began 21 January. The medical board 22 November 2001 judged recovery rapid, complete, lasting, without sequelae and scientifically inexplicable; theologians and cardinals/bishops gave favorable judgments. The decree identifies hyperacute meningococcal sepsis with shock, cardiac arrest, prolonged hypoxemia, respiratory distress, disseminated intravascular coagulation and multiorgan failure. This was the canonization miracle.
https://www.vatican.va/archive/aas/documents/AAS-94-2002-ocr.pdf#page=491

These are official case-specific judgments. They do not amount to publishing the full clinical file or every medical opinion. Their existence warrants serious weight; access limits must not be recast as proof that no investigation occurred.

2. NEWLY RECOVERED CLINICAL MATERIAL: DE MARTINO

Three reproduced documents are saved and visually inspected: Dr Ferdinando Basile’s signed hospital attestation, the hospital discharge form, and the first page of De Martino’s handwritten account. They are publicly reproduced through Giovanni Caccioppoli’s compilation, not originals authenticated directly with the hospital. Basile describes a presumptive lymphatic-duct rupture and fluid along the mediastinal/retroperitoneal route, with reduction over subsequent days. The discharge diagnosis also uses suspected traumatic filtration language.

Physician Pietro Gerardo Violi’s 2010 clinical reconstruction, published under his own name, identifies Salerno’s S. Giovanni di Dio e Ruggi d’Aragona hospital, emergency physician Silvana Anzalone, initial noncontrast/contrast CT, then ultrasound and subsequent CT. It says no medical or surgical treatment was given. The primary imaging itself was not obtained. Later popular accounts shift dates or inflate the fluid amount; the decree and dated clinical records should control.
https://www.padrepioesangiovannirotondo.it/piosgr/il-miracolo-della-beatificazione-di-padre-pio/
https://www.caccioppoli.com/%27%2015%20Vita%20Consiglia%20De%20Martino%20e%20Matteo%20Pio%20Colella.html

Material question: was the precise lesion demonstrated, or was it inferred from the fluid’s distribution? Neither aspiration/biochemistry nor direct visualization of a rupture is established in the recovered documents. That is a real diagnostic limit, not evidence the fluid was imaginary.

A relevant comparison exists. Albrecht Betrains and colleagues, University Hospitals Leuven departments of Internal Medicine, Radiology, Nuclear Medicine and Otorhinolaryngology, reported eight women with spontaneous cervical swelling in Oxford Medical Case Reports(2021), doi 10.1093/omcr/omab029. Mean episode duration 3.8 days, range 1–7; left supraclavicular swelling, pressure/dyspnea, occasional mediastinal/abdominal involvement, and demonstrable lymphatic tracer leakage were described. This disproves a blanket assertion that this broad class of lymphatic swelling cannot regress spontaneously. It does not establish that De Martino had this syndrome: her estimated volume, laboratory details, single-event history and exact CT findings require comparison.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8143671/

Jacqueline O’Neill and Kevin Ranum, HonorHealth Internal Medicine/Scottsdale Thompson Peak Medical Center, published a primary case poster with acute neck and retroperitoneal fluid, chylous pleural fluid confirmed by analysis, conservative management, discharge day 4 and CT resolution day 10. Important difference: thoracentesis and MCT diet were used, so this was not an untreated duplicate of De Martino. It nevertheless challenges claims that all such leaks require surgical repair or are invariably fatal. The poster is institutional case reporting, not an independently peer-reviewed matched series.
https://www.honorhealth.com/sites/default/files/2020-09/internal-med-residency-follow-fluid-spontaneous-thoracic-duct-rupture.pdf

Assessment: strong documented recovery and real canonical examination; a serious ordinary differential exists that deserves professional comparison with the full file. Neither an established natural diagnosis nor a demonstrated rebuttal to the 1998 judgment was produced here.

3. MATTEO: THE DETAILED INVESTIGATOR ACCOUNT

Violi’s signed 2010 account states 16 diocesan witnesses were examined, including parents, treating physicians/nurses, a nephrologist, a physician uncle, teacher and friar. Two later examining experts reportedly confirmed lasting health; one was a cardiologist, another a Palermo University internal-medicine professor, unnamed in this passage. Do not invent their names.

Violi names the medical-board members Lorenzo Bonomo, Francesco Di Raimondo, Giovanni Battista Pignataro, Rodolfo Proietti and Giovanni Rocchi, with Ennio Ensoli as secretary, and reports a 5–0 conclusion. These details are an investigator’s published reproduction, not the recovered signed board minutes. He was a physician collaborating with Capuchin postulation, not himself the postulator and not a detached opponent.

His chronology includes treatment against infection, vasoactive support, CPR, ventilation, transfusion and continued ICU care. Sedation/paralysis stopped 31 January; the child was alert in early February, returned to pediatrics 12 February, and was discharged 26 February. He explicitly calls the prolonged coma pharmacologically induced. Rapid recovery means an unusually favorable clinical trajectory; it must not be retold as a brain-dead untreated child instantly restored.

His nine-system list includes neurological, cardiovascular, respiratory, renal, liver, gastrointestinal, hematological/coagulation, adrenal and skin involvement. A nine-item count is not itself a validated 100%individual mortality calculation: organ definitions, actual failure criteria, severity and duration matter. The exceptional features are the detailed physiology and recovery, not a number divorced from its clinical definition. No patient-specific prognosis calculation was reconstructed here.

The 2002 book by the mother, Maria Lucia Ippolito, Il miracolo di Padre Pio(Mondadori,349 pages), is catalogued as including medical/theological reports; a full copy remains a priority retrieval. Google Books lists a section on illness reports beginningp 48 and Giovanni Ippolito’s reportp 109. These are catalogue leads, not pages read.
https://www.padrepioesangiovannirotondo.it/piosgr/il-miracolo-della-canonizzazione-di-padre-pio/
https://books.google.com/books/about/Il_miracolo_di_Padre_Pio.html?id=y1DZAAAAMAAJ

The official biography gives diocesan process 11 June–17 October 2000, documents forwarded 23 October, theologians 11 December 2001. Violi gives 2 September/18 October/12 December variants. Prefer official dates; the variations do not undo the documented decree.
https://www.vatican.va/news_services/liturgy/saints/ns_lit_doc_20020616_padre-pio_en.html

Assessment: strongest medically grave case in this pass, with formal recognition. An ordinary recovery is not established just by noting that intensive care was given. Equally, its contribution needs the full medical reasoning rather than 100%death rhetoric.

4. LIFETIME CASES

Wanda Półtawska: the contemporary Wojtyła request and 28 November 1962 thanks letter are important. The latter visibly states recovery 21 Novemberbefore surgery; the cropped request does not show its date. The official Padre Pio portal dates request 17 November, not 18 Novemberas some compilations do. Półtawska’s own later account says a presumed growth and that surgeons allowed a 5%possibility of inflammation; she initially considered that explanation and later became convinced of Pio’s intervention. Preserve both statements. No histology was obtained establishing malignant disease. The letters substantiate contemporary belief and a reported recovery, not the specific assertion of pathologically confirmed terminal cancer.
https://www.padrepio.it/mons-wojtyla-a-padre-pio-pregate-per-wanda/
https://padrepiodevotions.org/pray-hope-and-dont-worry-issue-18/
Published provenance lead for her first-person piece: Lay Witness, October 1999; original issue not obtained. The pamphlet editor’s assertion this was a canonization miracle conflicts with the named official decrees and should not be repeated.

Gemma Di Giorgi: a major lifetime claim of restored sight with persisting abnormal eyes. Her public participant testimony is real as a testimony source, but the recovered corpus does not contain original before/after ophthalmology examinations. Names Bonifacio, Cucco, Contini/Contino and Caramazza are source leads, not authenticated full reports. No exact anatomical diagnosis, visual acuity, slit-lamp/fundus findings or optical examination has been established here. Popular versions differ in when sight began, including partial perception on the journey before meeting Pio. Neither declare a proven anatomical impossibility from photographs nor declare a diagnosis disproved without the records. This is not one of the two specifically accepted healing decrees; absence from those decrees is not proof the Vatican rejected it.
Priority early leads: Carty 1953 chapter VIII, pp. 159–162; Clarice Bruno 1970 pp.111–116; John Schug 1971 interview cited by Ruffin 1991 pp.333–334; reported 1947 Corriere d’Informazione and Sicilia del Popolo items, whose original scans were not obtained. Later public testimony 2005 is accessible through TRC search, but download failed.
https://staging.trcgiornale.it/?p=6445

5. CANONIZATION: SCOPE AND NEXT DOCUMENTS

Medical examination, theological attribution and papal recognition are separate stages, visibly present in both decrees. The medical board evaluates diagnosis/prognosis/treatment/recovery; theologians address invocation and attribution. The 2016 published regulations explain present procedure and confidentiality but postdate these cases; do not back-project the seven-member board rule onto the five-member boards recorded here.
https://www.causesanti.va/it/documenti/regolamento-della-consulta-medica-italiano.html

Highest-value next documents:
- Colella: Ippolito 2002 book’s reproduced reports; original Fattispecie Cronologica, Summarium, independent medico-legal reports,22 November 2001 board relation, hospital chart and long-term examinations.
- De Martino: Tommaso Contaldi, Il miracolo della Beatificazione di Padre Pio(Edizioni Padre Pio;2003/2014 editions listed), full clinical file, original CT/ultrasound reports/images,1998 board relation. Compare with current lymphatic differentials through a qualified clinician.

- Gemma: original ophthalmic records and early newspapers, then an ophthalmologist’s anatomical assessment.
