PUBLIC MIRACLE COMPARISON — FOLLOW-UP
1 November 2026

QUESTION AND RESULT
Daniel requests a documented non-Apostolic counterexample comparable in evidential strength to Fatima or Zeitoun, rather than a catalogue of weaker miracle stories. This follow-up does not establish such an equivalent. It identifies a substantive new non-Apostolic Christian public-appearance report (Nairobi, 1988), a newly traced Buddhist shrine-light report (Badulla, 1887), and a public Ahmadiyya prophecy claim. Their differences and documentary weaknesses are recorded below. None is promoted into an established supernatural event merely to produce religious symmetry.

STANDARDS APPLIED
1. What exactly was observed, and what physical/theological interpretation was attached?
2. Was there a specific advance announcement, preserved before the outcome?
3. Separate crowd attendance, claimed perception, close inspection and named surviving witness reports.
4. Prefer contemporary firsthand reporting; distinguish dispatches repeating another paper from independent observation.
5. Inspect original photographs/scans where accessible; photographs of people do not by themselves establish anomalous arrival or departure.
6. Identify actual tests or documented ordinary explanations. A conjecture, source gap or hostile opinion is not a demonstrated disproof.
7. Record religious content without assuming that every phenomenon associated with a religion vindicates all its doctrines.
No case is required to copy every detail of Fatima. Repetition can strengthen a case without prediction, and clinical observations can matter without a mass audience. Each is compared on its actual strengths.

NAIROBI, 11 JUNE 1988 — IMPORTANT NEW PRIMARY-SOURCE LEAD
Job Mutungi's Kenya Times report (22 June, pp.16–17) describes a gathering estimated at 6,000, a short advance announcement, a robed visitor, unusual light and photographs. The disappearance comes through driver Gurnam Singh's reported account, not Mutungi's direct observation. The healing claim is attributed to Mary Akatsa. The 26 June Sunday Times follow-up reports an earlier encounter with the visitor at Singh's house and transport to the meeting; its date differs from the first report. Neither article supplies clinical verification. The original setting is Christian; the Maitreya identification is a later New Age interpretation. These are substantive contemporary reports, but do not establish an equally strong rival miracle.
Downloaded both original page reproductions; visually read their relevant columns. The initial article also includes qualifications and doubts. The follow-up contains theological objections that are not physical disproof. Its arrival account is reported through follower Moses Iyavo, not a separately recovered signed statement by Singh. A different event date could be an error or another episode and remains unresolved. Source preservation is Ilias Chrissochoidis's personal Stanford-hosted page; university hosting is not institutional authentication or endorsement. A 1993 MA thesis by O.M. Jacob Nandi at the University of Nairobi is identified, but its full PDF was not recovered. No claims about its conclusions on supernatural agency are made here.

BADULLA, SRI LANKA, MAY 1887 — NEW BUDDHIST PUBLIC-LIGHT LEAD
High priest H. Sumangala's letter dated 4 June 1887, reprinted in The Buddhist Ray (August 1888, printed p.64), reports shrine radiance on 7 May lasting about two hours and subsequent repetitions, citing pupils and others. He does not present himself as a direct observer. In February 1890, The Theosophist supplement (printed ci–cii; PDF pp.51–52) publishes monk Rambuppola Pannasara's signed account. He dates his arrival to 6 May at 7:30 a.m., estimates about 200 pilgrims and describes rising bright points and a rainbowlike form following the shrine's outline. His duration is visually 1.5 hours; OCR drops the fraction. Henry Steel Olcott's commentary rounds it to two hours and describes thousands in the introduction. Do not silently replace the witness's 200 with thousands, his 6 May with 7 May, or his 1.5 hours with two.
This is more than an anonymous modern internet legend: an early letter and a named eyewitness statement survive. The testimony is religiously interpreted and linked to an expected recurring festival phenomenon. However, no pre-event appointed prophecy document, independent contemporary secular eyewitness report, original photograph, measurements or full scientific investigation was recovered. Olcott's optical exclusions are reasoning, not documented calibrated experiments. No specific ordinary mechanism is demonstrated here. This is a serious historical lead, not an established Fatima/Zeitoun equivalent. It is within the nineteenth-century press/science era, but not within a strict last-110-years window.

HINDU MILK EVENT, 21 SEPTEMBER 1995 — PUBLIC / VERIFIABLE / VINDICATORY?
CURRENT DISPOSITION — 1 NOVEMBER 2026
Excluded as a serious rival supernatural case in the present cumulative comparison. On the evidence reviewed, this event is nowhere near Fatima in evidential strength for supernatural intervention. The central spoon-emptying effect was reproduced with ordinary objects, and investigators reported runoff and dye tracing. The earlier phrase 'not equally strong' understated the disparity. Give this case no positive weight as an authenticated competing divine sign or as an evidential peer of Fatima/ Zeitoun; do not use its crowd size or religious interpretation alone to cancel or discount those cases. Daniel explicitly requested this disposition, and the assistant agrees on the reviewed evidence. Reopen only with new substantive evidence and an explicit argument addressing these findings. This does not declare every witness dishonest or every exceptional subsidiary claim individually tested. Historical sources remain preserved. This is a qualitative evidential disposition, not a calculated probability. No new debate point is assigned by this note.

Public: yes. Contemporary UPI and AP reports record widespread direct participation and substantial crowds. Verifiable appearance: spoon-emptying was available for observation and testing. Supernatural consumption: not thereby verified. Vindicatory intent: some participants and religious advocates explicitly treated it as divine self-disclosure; others disagreed. The religious interpretation cannot simply be denied.
The earlier source audit contains contemporary Times of India reporting of office replication with a plastic object and original-temple runoff/dye tests. UPI also includes a named observer who saw dripping. This is concrete ordinary-mechanism evidence rather than a bare suggestion of mass hysteria. No quantitative closed accounting of every exceptional large-volume/no-runoff claim was recovered. This case does not establish a comparably strong supernatural Hindu event. Claims that every temple episode was experimentally settled would also exceed this record.

AHMAD–DOWIE PRAYER CHALLENGE, 1902–1907 — NEW PROPHECY COMPARISON
Mirza Ghulam Ahmad's challenge has a genuine pre-outcome press trail: Colonist, 24 September 1903, p.4, reports a prayer duel about which claimant would die first. The article is available as indexed archival OCR; direct-page retrieval failed in this pass, and no new original scan was downloaded. Dowie's 9 March 1907 death and decline are independently described in the 1912 Dictionary of National Biography account. The prediction and subsequent outcome are not merely a modern legend. The movement explicitly presents the outcome as vindication of Islam/Ahmad.
However, death-order prediction without an appointed date differs materially from a predicted public physical anomaly. One of two mortal claimants ordinarily dies first. The broader wording, conditional acceptance, and the companion Pigott prediction require checking against original publications before claiming an extraordinary successful forecasting record. Thus this supplies a meaningful media-age religious prophecy claim, not an established equivalent to Fatima. Do not cite 156 newspaper appearances as 156 independent supernatural observations; the promotional compilation supplies leads to copies/reports of a challenge.

OTHER CANDIDATES RETAINED FROM THE EARLIER REVIEW
Sri Lankan statue rays, 2006: contemporary AFP/PTI report crowds and perceived light; AFP includes an engineer who perceived it and attributed it to optics. PTI describes a boundary effect after gazing. No controlled study establishing every site's mechanism was recovered. This is an observable public religious visual claim, not a documented detached moving figure or appointed sign. Crowds trying to see something are not an audited number seeing it.
The Bab's execution, 1850: the earlier audit recovered a near-contemporary diplomatic report of survival of a first volley followed by execution. Not an equally documented modern apparition.
Sai Baba, Tibetan rainbow-body/tukdam, Itigilov and Sufi piercing: potentially relevant personal/physical comparators remain documented in the earlier reports. None has been upgraded in this pass to a Fatima/Zeitoun counterpart.
Thai solar event: no new evidence was found here warranting reversal of the earlier conclusion that it was not an adequate matched counterexample. That is narrower than proving no reported visual experience occurred.

DEBATE CONSEQUENCE
No equally strong non-Apostolic counterpart to Fatima or Zeitoun is established by this follow-up. This is a bounded evidential result, not an exhaustive proof of global absence. Finding another public claim is not automatically a successful objection to Daniel's three cases. Conversely, successful defenses against selected objections do not by themselves yield numerical odds or establish that every possible ordinary account has been excluded. No debate tally was modified by this research pass.
