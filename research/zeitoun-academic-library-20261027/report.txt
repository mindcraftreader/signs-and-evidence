1. Donald A. Westbrook. Our Lady of Zeitoun (1968-1971): Egyptian Mariophanies in Historical, Interfaith, and Ecumenical Context. Nova Religio 21(2), November 2017, 85-99.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1525/nr.2017.21.2.85
JSTOR: https://www.jstor.org/stable/26418562
Local PDF: sources/Westbrook-LadyZeitoun19681971-2017.pdf
Local extracted text: same basename, .txt
16 PDF pages: JSTOR cover plus 15 journal pages.
Academic background: scholar of religious studies and information studies; PhD, Claremont Graduate University, 2015. His current San Jose State University affiliation is independently documented by the university; this report does not project that affiliation back to 2017.
Background sources:
https://ischool.sjsu.edu/ciri-research-and-innovation-activities
https://catalog.utexas.edu/archive/2023-24/undergraduate/information/faculty/faculty.pdf

Scope: historical, political, interfaith and ecumenical analysis. The article is not a new photographic or optical experiment and does not adjudicate every alleged physical feature.
Relevant findings:
- Printed pp.86-87 summarize the reported moving female figure and luminous birds and emphasize the unusual Muslim first witnesses and publicly accessible character of the events.
- Printed p.89 describes Cairo's wartime curfew and evening electricity shutdowns. Note 13, p.96, attributes confirmation to personal communications with Saad Michael Saad and Hany N. Takla in June 2016, both in Egypt at the time. This is retrospective corroboration of wartime conditions, not an original power-grid log or verification of an exact blackout radius.
- Printed p.87 observes the limited academic treatment of Zeitoun and proposes further research. Do not mistake this lack of scholarly attention for a published scientific refutation or scientific certification.
- Printed pp.89 and 97 note that crowd totals are estimates. He regards Johnson's title When Millions Saw Mary as likely hyperbolic and points to tens of thousands in the Synaxarion and a possible aggregate in the hundreds of thousands. This is his historical assessment, not a deduplicated crowd census, and should be retained alongside the existing crowd-capacity research.
- No first-person quotation from Nasser was found in this article.

2. Maura Hearden. Lessons from Zeitoun: A Marian Proposal for Christian-Muslim Dialogue. Journal of Ecumenical Studies 47(3), Summer 2012, 409-426.
Public record: https://openurl.ebsco.com/results?bquery=IS+0022-0558+AND+VI+47+AND+IP+3+AND+DT+2012&page=1&sid=ebsco:ocu_results:cache
Retrieved through Akron > EBSCO Academic Search Complete.
Local PDF: sources/Hearden-Lessons-from-Zeitoun-2012.pdf
Local extracted text: same basename, .txt
19 PDF pages: 18 journal pages plus rights notice.
Academic background: article p.409 identifies her as assistant professor of theology at DeSales University since 2008, with an MA in theology and a 2008 PhD in religious studies specializing in systematic theology from Marquette University. This is a publication-time affiliation directly verified on the PDF page.
Scope: theological and interreligious interpretation, drawing on earlier historical accounts and an interview with Mary Khair, whose husband witnessed the events. It is not an independent instrumented investigation of the lights.
Relevant findings:
- Printed p.415 explicitly includes President Nasser among reported witnesses. Footnote 26 refers collectively to DeVincenzo, Nelson and Bullivant; no words spoken by Nasser are quoted. This adds a scholarly secondary attestation but does not yet fill the first-person quotation gap.
- Printed p.415 reports removal/disconnection of street lighting, citing Nelson pp.5-6. This is a reuse of Nelson's evidence, not a separate newly discovered blackout observation.
- Printed pp.415-416 summarize the gestures, cross-bowing, luminous birds and prolonged appearances that are central to the user's argument. Most descriptions trace to DeVincenzo; note 32 also identifies the Khair interview.
- Printed p.415 gives an estimated 250,000-500,000 total observers, typical nightly crowds around 10,000 and peaks around 100,000. Note 25 cites DeVincenzo p.4 and Bullivant p.76. These are inherited estimates, not a count conducted by Hearden.
