D115 SHADOWS: DALLEUR, MUSE AND THE COUNTERARGUMENTS
11 November 2026

ASSESSMENT

The ray-convergence method has a legitimate geometrical basis. Dalleur's
principal angular calculation reproduces under his published assumptions.
Known-source examples support the practical method. No reviewed critique
establishes a full ordinary-solar fit to D115's retained features and scene
constraints. The evidence should not be dismissed as an optical impossibility
or given zero weight because it is disputed.

The narrower conclusion is conditional: the selected features and camera
calibration imply a low source. The true angular elevation is not established
merely by rerunning those inputs. Neither the critiques nor this review supply
a complete independent remeasurement of the original 6K source. The strongest
remaining issues are feature correspondence and joint camera calibration,
not a demonstrated arithmetic failure or a completed historical alternative.

The exact proposition tested is whether object-shadow cues in D115 exclude

advance prediction, eyewitness movement reports, agent identification, and

1. WHAT THE PHOTOGRAPH CONTRIBUTES

The crown/brim region contains a visible dark band. The foreground stones
have real occlusion/shadow structure, and small hand, stick and clothing
features are visible. These are not just annotations without photographic
counterparts. The unprocessed 1669x1181 public copy, however, does not let this
reviewer uniquely assign every caster point to its receiving shadow point.
An arbitrary dark contour, material edge or hat silhouette cannot simply be
treated as a projected light ray.

Dalleur's principal hat feature is the crown's shadow on the brim, not merely
the depression inside the crown. Grant explicitly corrects his earlier
misidentification. A critique aimed only at the wrong feature is inadequate.
The published figure is denoised and contrast-adjusted; this is disclosed in
its caption. That does not establish a processing artifact. Measurement on
the raw scan would allow processing robustness to be checked directly.

2. WHY THE GEOMETRY IS LEGITIMATE

Bo Hu, Christopher Brown and Randal Nelson of the University of Rochester
Computer Science Department, technical report TR810 (2004), distinguish the
3D location of a finite light from its direction. Their supporting-line result
supports recovering projected source direction from appropriate object/shadow
contours. Their single-view limitation is not a blanket prohibition on source
direction estimation. Metric conversion requires camera calibration.

Consequently, these categorical criticisms fail: every receiver must be level;
every object's complete 3D shape must be known; a photograph cannot supply
any light-direction information because it loses a dimension. Nor is an
arbitrary tangent sufficient: it must satisfy the physical correspondence
or common-supporting-line condition.

the paper's equation gives:

These are conditional reconstructions, not four independent measurements and
not a statistical confidence interval. The final row differs about 0.01 degree
from the printed table, an immaterial discrepancy already recorded earlier.

New timing sensitivity: a fixed-declination calculation anchored to latitude
39.6317 and Dalleur's 42.72-degree noon elevation gives changes of 0.127 degree
at 15 minutes, 0.507 at 30 minutes and 1.997 at 60 minutes from solar noon.
The actual Sun reaches 28.2386 degrees approximately 172.09 minutes before or
after noon. Reaching the 30-mm estimate takes approximately 146.44 minutes.
This is not an independent ephemeris or a new dating of D115; it establishes
that a modest error around noon cannot alone close the reported gap.

4. VALIDATION: REAL SUPPORT, LIMITED ERROR CALIBRATION

Dalleur's Castel Gandolfo example reports 17.07 versus astronomical 16.59
degrees; another focal assumption gives 15.75, while a zero-roll variant gives
16.93. Those absolute differences are 0.48, 0.84 and 0.34 degree. These are
three configurations of ONE scene, not three independent controls.
Saint Hubert reports 50.246 versus 50.82 degrees, a 0.574-degree difference.

It is false to say the published validation consists only of CGI. The real
examples support the implementation. But the Castel Gandolfo photo has a
directly visible Sun/ray convergence, and the other example uses more rigid
scene cues. These selected demonstrations do not estimate the probability
of correctly identifying D115's faint boundaries. They are not a universal
plus/minus-one-degree error bar or a blind matched historical-image dataset.
An author-controlled demonstration remains useful without being independent
replication. A large blind experiment is one way to strengthen confidence;
defensible worst-case image/camera bounds could also do so.

5. STRONGEST COUNTERARGUMENTS AND THEIR BEST REPLIES

Mark Grant, self-described mathematics graduate student and former software
developer (institution not independently verified): the short-baseline and
uncertain-correspondence concerns are mathematically real. His current Google
document is explicitly tentative, incomplete and partly outdated following
Dalleur's replies. His proposed lapel lines are not a validated replacement
for Dalleur's actual selected cues. His separate ground-shadow demonstration
addresses a supplementary method, not the published main reconstruction.

Grant's later toy rendering, described in Adelstein's debate review and its
comments, is the strongest concrete lighting counterexample located. Bright
overhead fill with an ordinary 42-degree Sun reportedly yields a misleading
hat-boundary estimate displaced by 15-20 degrees. This is a useful failure-mode
demonstration, not a complete D115 reconstruction. This pass did not obtain
its scene file or independently verify the quoted error. The comments correct
the camera pitch to 4 degrees, superseding an earlier recalled 5 degrees.

Muse's section 3, quoting Dalleur's response, argues the simulation loses the
distinct contours retained in D115 and does not fit the hat, stick/hand and
stone cues together. That is substantive. Adding fill illumination changes
contrast; it does not physically relocate an unchanged direct source's
geometric occlusion boundary. It can produce a different apparent boundary
that is mistakenly traced. Resolving which happens in D115 requires feature
identification, rather than simply invoking either clouds or sharpness.

Georgia Ray, independent biosecurity researcher: identifies accessibility and
feature/calibration issues, but explicitly does not replicate the mathematics.
Her requirement of level receivers does not apply generally to this method.
Her alternative interpretations of stone shadows and handle highlights are
possibilities, not measured substitute fits. Her general expectation of enough
error to bridge the discrepancy is not a quantitative demonstration.

James Fodor, whose Melbourne institutional biography records scientific
graduate study: his archived presentation rightly asks for propagation of
uncertainty in the selected lines, rather than only camera-tilt derivatives.
It supplies no new endpoint table or successful solar fit in the reviewed
section. Generic 5-20-degree errors from different automatic estimators are
not a measured error distribution for Dalleur's manual method. Categorical
claims that the paper was not peer reviewed were not established.

Cade Bradley, professional cinematographer: unknown historical calibration
is a legitimate issue. Lost-depth, common-plane and CGI-only objections do
not defeat the actual method. Muse's video response answers those points;
it does not independently authenticate every empirical bound he asserts.
No completed later studio reproduction fitting D115 was located in this pass.

6. CAMERA CALIBRATION AND CONVERGENCE

Earlier documented primary equipment checks show that period Goerz cameras
could have translating fronts. A centered principal point is an assumption,
not a mechanical certainty. The earlier joint-calibration script preserves
the adopted light and vertical vanishing points while fitting a 42.72-degree
source using an ultra-wide lens plus shift and a changed horizon. Its
18.0975-mm-equivalent example needs 13.63 mm image shift in both axes on an
assumed 120-mm-wide plate, and changes the horizon about 82 pixels.

This is genuine mathematical ambiguity, not merely saying unknown camera.
But actual mount/focus/shift compatibility and use by Ruah are not established;
the baseline vertical VP is implied rather than newly measured. Muse's
edge-head perspective and other scene constraints are not answered by
preserving the vertical VP alone. Hence this is not a historically supported
whole-scene ordinary alternative, and no probability for it is defended here.

Multiple feature agreements provide positive internal consistency. However,
the author's ray probe starts with a shared VP; lines drawn through that VP
are not twelve independently fitted measurements. The relevant evidence is
how well independently specified physical cues agree with those rays. No
complete endpoint/tangent inventory with residuals and an admissible joint
camera region was recovered. That is a reproducibility limit, not proof
that the reported agreement is false or meaningless.

An illustrative calculation shows why feature baseline length matters:
two endpoints each uncertain within a one-pixel-radius disk can rotate a
10-pixel line by up to 11.54 degrees, or a 100-pixel line by 1.15 degrees.
These are image-line rotations, NOT source-elevation errors or measured D115
uncertainties. A tangent fitted across many pixels has a different error model.
They must not be assigned wholesale to Dalleur's selected features.

7. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

If the low source direction is reliably established, retinal effects and
expectation cannot explain that physical direction. The agreement with
descent testimony then matters in the advance-announcement context. The
photo alone neither records a trajectory nor identifies Mary. Non-solar
illumination is not identical to supernatural illumination: cloud/sky
illumination is physically ordinary, though no such fit to the full case
was established by the reviewed countermodels.

Sharp near-contact shadows alone do not uniquely prove a compact source;
penumbra also depends on caster-receiver separation. This general fact does
not overturn Dalleur's multiple-cue reconstruction. Similarly, black-and-white
garment tonal patterns do not independently measure water content, duration
of drying or infrared power. The proposed photographic-defect alternative
has not been demonstrated either. Keep the angle reconstruction and drying
interpretation separate, while retaining their possible cumulative relevance.
