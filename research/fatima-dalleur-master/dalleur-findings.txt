HOW TO USE
Before assessing Dalleur, D115, Muse's photo defense or a camera/shadow counterargument, read this register and the current F-04 entry.
A settled defense records the outcome of the tested objection. It is neither an immutable fact nor proof that every conceivable alternative is excluded.
To reopen a settled item, identify its ID, cite substantive new evidence or a materially developed argument, and explain which finding changes. Preserve the previous outcome in change_history.
Open verification is not established counterevidence. A modeled alternative is not a demonstrated historical reconstruction. A reproduced calculation does not independently authenticate its inputs.
Carry positive findings and their strongest existing defenses into the cumulative assessment. Do not repeatedly count the same objection or its micro-components as new tally points.
Update the JSON first, regenerate the DOCX and TXT, check their contents and render, and synchronize the research-library mirror. Do not silently overwrite dated source reports.

STATUS DEFINITIONS
Settled defense: The identified attempted objection was not sustained in the recorded review.
Reproduced: The stated arithmetic or measurement was reproduced with its specified inputs and scope.
Supported: Positive evidence supports the scoped finding; not an independent finding of every stronger claim.
Correction: A prior wording or interpretation is superseded by the stated more precise account.
Open verification: A specific empirical or analytical check remains incomplete; this is not itself an adverse finding.
Modeled only: A conditional calculation or possibility exists without a demonstrated complete historical fit.
Bounded search: The result concerns the documented search, not proof of global absence or expert consensus.

GEOMETRY

G01 | Recovering a projected light direction | Settled defense
Suitable object and cast-shadow correspondences determine projected light-ray lines and their convergence. The basic method has a legitimate projective-geometric basis.
Scope: Metric source elevation also needs camera orientation and angular calibration.
Revisit when: Identify an error in the actual construction or show the chosen physical correspondences fail its conditions.
Evidence: S01, S13, S26

G02 | Physical heights and shadow lengths | Settled defense
The principal ray-convergence construction does not require measuring every object's physical height or comparing physical shadow lengths. Bradley's length demonstration does not refute this construction.
Scope: Longer usable features can improve precision; feature correspondence remains necessary.
Revisit when: Connect a demonstrated failure specifically to the ray-convergence method rather than another shadow-length method.
Evidence: S04, S13, S23

G03 | Sloping and different receiving surfaces | Settled defense
The receivers need not all occupy one horizontal plane. Sloping terrain is not a categorical disproof of appropriate object-to-shadow rays.
Scope: An arbitrary shadow contour on a curved surface is not automatically a valid matched ray.
Revisit when: Show a specific claimed correspondence is invalid because of its actual geometry.
Evidence: S01, S13, S23

G04 | Supporting tangents | Settled defense
An appropriate common supporting tangent to projected object and shadow regions can supply directional information. The attempted categorical objection to tangent-based cues was rejected against primary vision literature.
Scope: A tangent to an arbitrary dark edge is insufficient; its physical supporting-line conditions matter.
Revisit when: Test the precise crown and brim feature against those conditions, retaining its correct identification.
Evidence: S13, S26

G05 | Straightness and angles | Correction
Rectilinear perspective preserves straight-line incidence and convergence, not arbitrary Euclidean angles. Dalleur's calibrated equations supply the necessary conversion.
Scope: Loose spoken language that angles are preserved must not replace the actual mathematical model.
Revisit when: Identify an inference in the actual equations that incorrectly assumes general angle preservation.
Evidence: S01, S05, S23

G06 | Direction and position are different | Supported
Recovering a distant source direction is distinct from locating a finite source in three dimensions. A single photo does not itself establish the emitter's range or height above the landscape.
Scope: Rochester's single-view location limitation is not a blanket impossibility theorem for direction recovery.
Revisit when: Supply additional calibrated views, known geometry or measured bearings that identify a position.
Evidence: S13, S16, S26

G07 | One exposure and movement | Supported
D115 supplies illumination geometry at one exposure. It does not independently photograph a trajectory; the reported descent and repeated motion come from testimony.
Scope: A reliable anomalous direction could corroborate that testimony in the announced-sign context.
Revisit when: Recover synchronized additional exposures or other evidence establishing movement photographically.
Evidence: S01, S15, S17
