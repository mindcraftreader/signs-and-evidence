SHROUD OF TURIN — SCIENTIFIC DATING ASSESSMENT
26 October 2026

JUDGMENT
On the currently reviewed scientific dating evidence, I give greater weight to a
medieval age indicated by the 1988 radiocarbon measurements than to a first-century
age inferred from alternative aging methods plus a repaired-corner explanation.
This is a qualitative judgment about evidential weight, not a calculated probability,
and not a judgment about whether the Resurrection happened.

WHY THE RADIOCARBON RESULT CURRENTLY CARRIES MORE WEIGHT
Radiocarbon dating measures an age-dependent isotopic ratio using an extensively
developed archaeological dating method. In this case, three laboratories used
different preparation procedures and measured comparison textiles with known ages.
All reported medieval measurements, although the Shroud results were heterogeneous.
The entire Shroud allocation came from one corner strip: three laboratories are
not three independent locations on the cloth. That shared sampling weakness matters.
The measurements were made in 1988 and published in 1989, not 1984. [C14-1989]

THE REPAIR ARGUMENT IS A SUBSTANTIVE CHALLENGE, NOT A SETTLED FACT
Rogers reported compositional differences, including cotton and a gum/dye coating,
and interpreted the dating region as replacement cloth. If that interpretation is
correct, a perfectly accurate measurement of the sampled carbon could still be an
inaccurate date for the main cloth. Known fire patches elsewhere do not establish
an invisible repair in this corner. The proportions and distribution of newer fibers
would need to account quantitatively for the measured results. [ROGERS-2005]

The April 2026 study of retained Arizona fragments provides direct counterevidence:
the examined fragments contained flax with matching structural characteristics;
no cotton was observed and the inspected surfaces lacked significant contamination.
This is not a new radiocarbon date and its limited microscopic sampling cannot rule
out every local anomaly. It nevertheless makes treating a mixed-cotton replacement
as established scientifically unjustified. [FRAGMENTS-2026]

Mass-spectrometry criticism and a published response are included together in this
folder; neither should be presented as the last word without reading the other.
[BELLA-2015; ROGERS-REPLY-2016]

WHAT THE NEWER METHODS ADD
Fanti and colleagues reported a combined mechanical/FTIR/Raman estimate of AD 90
plus or minus 200 years at their stated 95% confidence. The individual estimates
were approximately 250 BC, AD 30 and AD 260. Those are positive published findings
supporting antiquity, not merely philosophical objections to the radiocarbon test.
They use calibrated material-aging proxies, however. The spectroscopy calibration
used eight selected FTIR textiles and eleven Raman textiles; the mechanical
calibration used eleven selected textiles. Selection and the effects of temperature,
humidity, treatment and contamination therefore require scrutiny. A correlation in
a small selected calibration set is not the same as broad independent validation of
the date of an unfamiliar, heavily handled archaeological textile. [FTIR-RAMAN-2013;
MECHANICAL-2014; MULTIMETHOD-2015]

The 2022 WAXS study reports degradation consistent with roughly two millennia and
with a first-century Masada specimen. Its authors argue that an ordinary medieval
storage history is difficult to reconcile with their model. That favorable result
must be represented fairly. But the conversion from cellulose degradation to age
requires long-term environmental assumptions. The paper specifies temperature and
humidity conditions for thirteen undocumented centuries. It measures neither
radiocarbon nor a unique elapsed-time clock independent of environment. A new
method is not wrong for being new; its validation and environmental model determine
how much weight it should carry. [WAXS-METHOD-2019; WAXS-2022]

The 2026 Fanti synthesis is saved as well, but it synthesizes existing results and
proposed mechanisms; it is not an additional independent dating experiment.
[FANTI-SYNTHESIS-2026]

STATISTICAL CRITICISM OF THE ORIGINAL DATE
Casabianca and colleagues identify heterogeneity and challenge the original
procedure and aggregation. That can affect confidence in the quoted narrow range
without showing that the true date lies thirteen centuries earlier. Dating a
replacement would be a stronger objection than merely finding excess scatter,
but the replacement itself must be demonstrated. The 2020 reassessment is also
included. [RAW-2019; STATISTICS-2020]

HOW THIS CHANGES THE OVERALL CONCLUSION
The medieval date is currently the better-supported scientific age inference in
my assessment. The quoted AD 1260–1390 interval should not be treated as though
all sampling and statistical questions were resolved. The alternative studies keep
an ancient date under investigation, but do not collectively overturn the medieval
measurements merely because several newer estimates favor antiquity. Several share
researchers, material sources or environmental-aging issues, so they are not simply
independent votes to be counted or averaged together.

WHAT WOULD MOST CHANGE THIS ASSESSMENT
Fresh, well-characterized samples from several original-cloth locations, analyzed
by multiple laboratories with documented handling and explicit repair/coating
checks, would address the main shared vulnerability. Blinded validation of the
alternative methods on representative known-age textiles with varied treatments
and environments would strengthen their comparative weight. An ancient linen date
would still date the material rather than independently establish the identity of
its former occupant or the cause and date of its image.
