CAN DISBELIEF BE SHOWN BETTER SUPPORTED?
A critical test of the cumulative case from Fatima, Zeitoun and Padre Pio
28 October 2026

Verdict

This review identifies substantive evidence for skepticism, but does not establish that the combined ordinary-cause explanation is better supported than Christian supernatural intervention. Its strongest result is a case for withholding a settled causal verdict. That conclusion is narrower than the requested affirmative case for disbelief. It is important not to relabel suspension as an achieved demonstration that no miracle occurred.

The admitted case

The comparison retains Fatima's advance promise, public experience, contemporary reporting, remote testimony and disputed photographic inference. It retains Zeitoun's public luminous phenomena, early articulated-figure testimony and narrower documented local lighting checks. For Padre Pio it uses only stigmata and fragrance. It does not use the discarded temperature, confession, healing or bilocation claims against the user. It does not restore unvalidated Nasser or suburb-wide-blackout assertions in order to knock them down.

Three hypotheses need distinguishing. First, the events had ordinary causes. Second, they involved supernatural Christian action. Third, the presently assessed evidence does not justify ranking those two decisively. The third is a judgment about knowledge, not another proposed physical cause. An incomplete ordinary explanation can still be preferred if positive evidence warrants that preference; however, mere logical availability does not establish it.

The strongest ordinary case for Fatima

The advance announcement really matters. Parish-priest and investigator records predate October 13. The Sanctuary's critical documentary edition, scientifically sponsored by the Portuguese Catholic University's Centre for Religious History Studies, provides the relevant texts. The promise cannot fairly be dismissed as a later invention. [1]

The counterargument is that the promise also organized observation: it brought people together on the announced date, directed attention and supplied the interpretive setting. Avelino de Almeida's contemporary O Seculo report describes a collective turn toward the solar disc, extraordinary perceived motion, and people comparing what they had seen. These are observed conditions under which visual and interpersonal processes could contribute; a skeptic need not suppose a random anomalous spectacle independently landed on a previously selected date. This reduces, but does not eliminate, the predictive force of the announced sign. [2]

There is experimental evidence for an ordinary component. Qasim Zaidi and colleagues at SUNY College of Optometry, the University of Chicago and the Max Planck Institute demonstrated retinal mechanisms producing color afterimages without requiring photopigment-bleaching intensities. This rebuts a universal equation between ordinary color distortion and painfully extreme exposure. Their experiment does not reproduce Fatima's complete reported event. [3]

Auguste Meessen, of the Catholic University of Louvain's Institute of Physics, proposed a broader visual account. His personal tests produced disc/rim and color effects, but his account does not establish an experimental reproduction of the reported solar motion or descent. It must not be advertised as one. [4]

The ordinary explanation therefore has real explanatory content for some colors, perceptual changes and shared interpretation. It has not been demonstrated to explain the strongest environmental-color accounts, extraordinary drying, or a genuine displaced local light source if those physical interpretations are established. The earlier local geometry audit reproduced Dalleur's calculation rather than refuting it. The source-feature correspondences and joint uncertainty remain contested; they cannot be dismissed merely because the conclusion is extraordinary. [5]

Remote witnesses add evidence, but the current map is not completed physical triangulation. Early indirect letters and later interviews have different evidential strengths; they do not provide surveyed bearings and simultaneous instrument readings. That limits a specific geometric reconstruction, not the existence of distant reports. The early accounts and their source chains are preserved in the existing distant-witness audit. [6]

Assessment: an ordinary explanation is a substantive contender at Fatima, rather than an appeal to an unknown future solution. It is not a demonstrated account of the full surviving case. The prediction remains positive evidence for an intentional sign.

The strongest ordinary case for Zeitoun

American University in Cairo anthropologist Cynthia Nelson supplies direct counterevidence to treating every perceived figure as an independently resolved external person. Her 1968 field notes describe a white-shirted person briefly misidentified and then correctly recognized, and a nunlike outline in a real light that depended on how she interpreted it. This is evidence of actual interpretive effects at Zeitoun, not just a generic statement that crowds can err. [7]

Her account also acknowledges disconnected streetlights and an unidentified light source. It does not establish headlights or explain every night. The white-shirted-person episode is said to have been corrected soon; the nunlike-outline passage supplies no measured duration. Her June 1 notes cover a four-hour visit but describe intermittent flashes, not four hours of a continuous resolved human figure. Her own article reproduces the press-conference claim that Bishop Athanasius saw a full-sized figure from 2:45 to 5:00 a.m. on April 30. This is her report of another witness's claim, not her own observation. The early committee and episcopal descriptions of an extended human form and purposeful gestures exceed what her ambiguous-light episodes explain. One corrected misidentification cannot retrospectively turn every other account into a mistake. [7, 8]

The government report adds weight to the case through reported lighting checks. It does not disclose a complete engineering exclusion of every conceivable source, but that limitation is not evidence that a projector or other apparatus existed. Treating an unavailable technical log as proof of fraud would be an argument from missing documentation. [8]

Assessment: the distinction between a real light and its interpreted identity is supported by specific observations. The physical source and the strongest articulated-form testimony remain unresolved. The evidence supports skepticism about particular identifications, not a demonstrated ordinary explanation of the whole event.

The strongest ordinary case for Pio's stigmata and scent

The wound record is not one agreed anatomical measurement maintained for fifty years. Romanelli, Bignami and later observers differed about depth and appearance. Sala's postmortem observation cannot independently establish the earlier tissue destruction, and the Capuchin editor identifies limits in the side-wound photography. These are actual source qualifications, not a diagnosis of Pio's personality. The signed bandaging report and Festa's arguments against artificial maintenance must also remain in the comparison. [9]

For scent, experimental olfaction supplies a narrower positive counterargument. Pamela Dalton and Charles Wysocki of the Monell Chemical Senses Center documented adaptation following prolonged exposure. Charlotte Sinding, Thomas Hummel and colleagues at TU Dresden and collaborating institutions experimentally studied differing habituation to continuous odorants. Casey Trimmer and colleagues at Monell, Rockefeller University, Duke and Pennsylvania linked receptor variation to differences in odor perception. Ordinary odor perception therefore need not be uniform between people or constant over time. Selectivity and intermittency alone do not exclude ordinary odorants. [10-12]

Those studies do not identify the source of Pio's reported fragrance, demonstrate perfume application, explain every transported-object account, or establish deception. Nor does naming chemical access establish the cause of the wounds. A comprehensive ordinary reconstruction remains missing. No diagnosis of psychopathy or coordinated conspiracy has been established by this pass.

Assessment: the source qualifications weaken overly exact claims of an anatomically uniform fifty-year lesion, while olfactory experiments rebut specific impossibility arguments. They do not provide a complete explanation of the admitted stigmata-plus-fragrance case.

Does combining the cases change the result?

Yes, their variety matters. A daytime visual explanation cannot account for Pio's skin lesions. A local explanation at Zeitoun would not erase Fatima's prior announcement. The review found no basis for calling Zeitoun merely a copied Fatima story. Each can add evidence under a Christian-intervention hypothesis.

The correct comparison also does not require one ordinary mechanism for all three. A world containing ordinary visual adaptation, interpretive influence, skin pathology and odor perception predicts different kinds of puzzling reports. That is a legitimate framework if the proposed mechanisms are supported and shown to fit the individual cases. It becomes an evasive framework if unspecified causes are continually inserted solely to preserve a predetermined naturalistic conclusion.

The supernatural account likewise has positive explanatory fit: the announced religious sign, church-associated appearances and Christ-associated wounds make sense within it. It should not be reduced to the bare claim that something unexplained occurred. But the claim must earn its attribution through the evidence; a common religious meaning is also compatible with religious interpretation of ordinary phenomena. The live disagreement is which process best explains the particular observations, not whether the framework can verbally accommodate them.

No calibrated probabilities for the entire record were established. Neither a numerical naturalistic prior nor multiplication of unverified independent miracle probabilities is warranted here. Crowd size is not the same as the number of separately preserved observations of every strongest feature, but the crowds and multiple witness streams must not consequently be erased.

Final comparative judgment

The strongest skeptical case has three real components: ordinary perceptual processes with experimental support; actual source-level ambiguity and a documented misidentification; and gaps between the measured or directly recorded phenomena and their strongest reconstructed physical descriptions. It is more substantial than saying that anything might have happened naturally.

It nevertheless fails to establish the requested overall conclusion. The research does not demonstrate an ordinary cause of the strongest Zeitoun record or Pio's full stigmata-and-fragrance combination, and does not eliminate Fatima's positive prediction-and-event argument. It would be an overstatement to present a favorable skeptical verdict across all three as an established result.

Accordingly: reasoned non-assent remains defensible; disbelief has not been shown to have won the comparative case. Equally, this unsuccessful skeptical demonstration is not independent proof that the Christian explanation is true. The distinction should be maintained without using it to pretend that the user's cumulative evidence has no force.

Method and access notes

This pass reread the completed local Fatima, Zeitoun and Pio source audits; inspected Nelson's original page 6; rechecked public primary documents and experimental articles through web tools; and added the olfactory comparison. No new authenticated university-library session or outside correspondence was used. Newly located C. Newlyn Smith's 1953/1954 medical discussion is a retrieval lead only; its full text was not obtained and no substantive conclusion here relies on its abstract. This is a bounded adversarial assessment, not a claim to have exhausted the whole literature.
