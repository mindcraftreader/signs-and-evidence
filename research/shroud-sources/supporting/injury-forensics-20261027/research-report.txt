SHROUD INJURIES AND FORENSIC DISCRIMINATION
27 October 2026

QUESTION
Do the injuries establish that the cloth contacted a genuinely scourged, thorn-crowned and nail-crucified person, and rule out a statue or uninjured model?

FINDING
Medical and bloodstain analyses add substantive constraints to a manufacturing account. Affirmative researchers interpret the distribution as coherent with severe trauma and crucifixion. They do not establish a measured near-zero probability of manufacture. Some proposed diagnostic details remain disputed among proponents themselves.

The logical distinction matters: medically plausible wounds, natural blood-flow shapes and genuine blood can each be evidence, but none alone demonstrates that the blood emerged from actual injuries in the person/model supplying the body image. Applied-blood experiments show selected patterns can arise on uninjured people. This does not reproduce the complete Shroud and does not settle its origin.

AFFIRMATIVE LITERATURE
Svensson and Heimburger 2012 discuss head injuries, scourging, wrist/arm flows, side wound and blood chemistry. They read these as coherent with a real crucified body and compatible with Jesus. They also acknowledge disagreements. Their broad consensus statement is their characterization of selected literature, not an independently conducted systematic consensus survey.

Bevilacqua et al. 2014 infer shoulder dislocation, nerve damage and other trauma from processed images. University affiliations and abstract are verified. Such fine diagnoses require stronger validation than merely identifying a broad crucifixion depiction; do not present them as an observed autopsy.

Faccini and Fanti 2010 report 196 flagrum-associated lashes/impressions in one count and at least 372 scourge-related bloodstains in another, including marks attributed to different instruments; the latter divides into 159 front and 213 back. These counts are author-dependent image classifications, not a fixed universally established wound count, and multiple marks may arise from a single lash. Their experiment produced comparison shapes by whipping carton, not living human bodies. This supplies morphological comparisons and does not establish that a living injured model was necessary.

EXPERIMENTAL LIMITS ON THE EXCLUSION ARGUMENT
König et al., published online 2023 in a 2024 volume, simulate head bleeding/transfer, side-fluid halos and lower-back staining. They report close visual similarities under selected washing/oiling/body-movement conditions. These are possible pathways; no full-Shroud recreation or historical attribution is established. Sequential serum/blood or pleural-fluid/blood application can make a halo, so a halo by itself does not prove emergence from an actual spear wound.

Heger et al. 2024 apply whole blood without anticoagulants to uninjured volunteers' wrists. Wrist turning can produce a similar wrist pattern. Oiling and movement facilitate branches on forearms, but the well-defined multiple branches remain difficult to explain. Authors explicitly state their findings do not permit reliable conclusions about the actual formation of the Shroud stains. Their results constrain claims of impossibility and do not prove forgery.

Borrini and Garlaschelli, online 2018 / issue 2019, report mismatched arm positions and lower-back patterns under their tested conditions. Their simulations are contested, particularly regarding anticoagulants and body movement between crucifixion and burial. Neither their assertion of unrealistic patterns nor a rebuttal should be treated as a settled whole-object verdict.

Zugibe's primary chapter disputes Barbet's Destot-space/median-nerve story and attributes hidden thumbs to positioning rather than permanent injury-induced flexion. This is a disagreement inside affirmative medical research. It undermines presenting one precise nail-path/hidden-thumb explanation as universally established; it does not show a statue made the image.

STRONG FORMULATION FOR THE ARGUMENT LEDGER
A proposed artificial process must explain the detailed wound and blood-pattern distribution, its anatomical placement, chemistry, and relationship to the body image, rather than merely producing a recognizable crucifixion picture. Existing partial simulations do not account for the complete combination. The stronger assertion that no uninjured model could produce the marks is not established and faces direct experimental counterexamples for selected patterns.

NO FORENSIC PROBABILITY ESTABLISHED
The reviewed studies do not provide a validated classification dataset of genuine-trauma cloths versus artificial wounded-body depictions, with blinded scoring and sensitivity/specificity. Counts of marks cannot be multiplied as independent improbabilities. Details explicitly described in the Passion narratives are also available to a maker and need different weighting from independently verified anatomical features not supplied by those narratives.

REVIEW LIMITS
This pass targeted primary affirmative reviews, scourge mapping, nail-path disagreement and recent controlled blood-flow/transfer experiments. It is not an exhaustive review of all forensic literature. No medical treatment advice, fresh physical tests, or emails were undertaken.
