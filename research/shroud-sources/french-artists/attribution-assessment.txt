FRENCH MEDIEVAL ARTISTS — ATTRIBUTION ASSESSMENT
Reviewed 27 October 2026

Question: if Turin’s image is an artificial medieval work, who could have
made it? This condition is not itself established by this artist survey.

Working guess at the level currently supported: an unnamed artist or
workshop familiar with Passion imagery. The reviewed evidence does not
identify one person, a particular town, or a proven manufacturing method.
An obscure craftsman remains possible; fame is not a necessary condition.

First named research lead in this selected comparison set: Évrard d’Orléans
and his circle. Reason: known painter/sculptor, documented early/mid-1300s
commissions including a cross and commemorative image, and royal/religious patronage.
The 1341 Langres commission is more useful chronological evidence than
uncertain birth dates. The Maubuisson relief illustrated is attributed.
No reviewed record connects him to the Shroud, Charny, Lirey, a cloth-image
experiment, a relevant payment or a surviving technical model. Consequently
there is no justified named-maker probability or basis to call him the maker.

Other relevant early comparisons: Jean Pépin de Huy and collaborators;
Jean Ravy, Jean le Bouteiller and the Notre-Dame workshop; Master of Rieux;
anonymous Adam and Reims sculptors; Jean Pucelle; Jean Le Noir and workshop.

Later comparisons: André Beauneveu (1364 Charles V effigy), Jean de Liège
(1371–72 Charles IV effigy), Sluter/Marville at Champmol, and the Narbonne
Parement painter. These demonstrate later medieval skill, not the making
of an object already displayed under the conventional mid-1350s chronology.
The sitter’s death year is not the sculpture’s production year. The Well
of Moses extends into the 1400s; the Parement is on silk, not linen.

Attribution requires more discriminating evidence than a convincing nude,
recumbent body, Christ’s beard or monochrome shading: a distinctive matched
design, a reproducible process consistent with the cloth’s physical evidence,
and independent documentary links to acquisition, commission or production.
The 1988 radiocarbon range dates sampled linen, not the image separately.

Scope: France and artists working there in the 1200s–1300s. This is a
curated comparison set, not an exhaustive census or objective greatness
ranking. Historical France, the Burgundian court, Hainaut and the Low
Countries are not collapsed into modern national identities.

Local inventory: artists-and-works.json. Each profile gives artwork samples,
original catalogue links, archived metadata/page/summary access, known
attribution qualifiers and specific limitations.
