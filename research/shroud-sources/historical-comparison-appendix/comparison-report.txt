Block18 — concrete historical comparison appendix
==========================================================================
Reviewed: 2026-10-27

READY TO INSERT IN BLOCK18
A concrete comparison inventory places the Shroud alongside eight historical cases: medieval painted linen, painted silk, a linen-reinforced panel, a carved effigy, later groundless cloth paintings and an intentional Shroud copy. The consulted conservation studies document mineral grounds and paint layers in several cases, and through-cloth staining in one thinly painted example. None provides a demonstrated match to the Shroud’s reported combination of a local 0.2 ± 0.2 µm colored fiber region and negative-like body tonality. This is a bounded result: most comparators have no calibrated individual-fiber depth or controlled inversion measurement. The appendix therefore distinguishes demonstrated material differences from unmeasured properties; it supports the claim that no matching historical object has been identified in this inventory, while supplying no numerical rarity probability or universal impossibility proof.

Bounded conclusion: no comparator in this inspected inventory has a demonstrated match to the Shroud’s reported combination of local fiber-surface coloration and negative-like body tonality. Most individual-fiber depths and controlled inversion behaviors remain unmeasured; this supports a documented absence of an identified match in the inventory, not proof that no other image exists or that manufacture was impossible.
Positive evidence of different technique is strongest for Withyham, Spinello and Monreale (grounds/paint systems), and Justus (through-cloth staining). Missing fiber-depth or inversion measurements must not be promoted into demonstrated failures.
Eight historical cases are listed: four painted-textile/panel cases in the medieval group, one medieval sculpture control, two later fifteenth-century painting controls, and one 1516 intentional Shroud copy. Withyham is one group case, not four independent measured thickness observations. None supplies a representative medieval-European mean, minimum, SD or rarity probability.
Measurements must keep four questions separate: paint/ground film thickness; penetration across cloth or yarn; coloration inside an individual fiber wall; ordinary versus inverted tonal response. Overall object thickness, 130 µg sample mass and microscope magnification alone answer none of the other quantities.
Survival and conservation strongly select the sample: fragile groundless cloths are underrepresented, while linings, consolidants, abrasion and replacement areas complicate modern observations. Source-specific caveats belong beside each comparison.
Modern experimental replicas are a separate category, not historical counterparts. Garlaschelli 2010 (DOI 10.2352/J.ImagingSci.Technol.2010.54.4.040301) and subsequent 2011 critique are relevant next-stage experimental comparisons; a fresh full microscopic equivalence audit was not completed in this bounded pass. They are not counted among the eight historical cases.
Sudarium of Oviedo excluded, following user instruction.

R0 — Shroud of Turin body image
Reference object, not a counted comparator
Date: Origin/date disputed; public chain of custody securely enters medieval record at Lirey
Identifier: Turin Cathedral Shroud; body image distinguished from blood, burns, water stains and repairs
Dimensions: MEASURED: Approximately 442 × 113 cm according to CISS institutional description.
Support: Flax linen; herringbone weave.
Production and materials: Image formation is disputed. Fanti 2010 interprets localized coloration in outer fiber material and discusses cell-wall versus impurity-layer possibilities; this is not a settled maker/process identification.
Paint or ground film: NOT APPLICABLE: A conventional deposited paint film is not the quantity estimated in Fig.12. A comprehensive assessment of the disputed pigment literature is outside this appendix.
Numerical paint film thickness: NOT APPLICABLE: The 0.2 ± 0.2 µm local figure estimates colored fiber material, not a conventional applied paint film.
Cloth or yarn penetration: QUALITATIVE OBSERVATION: Fanti 2010 separately describes coloration reaching roughly 2–3 fibers into threads and some colored fibers100 µm beneath a tangential fabric plane; these are different geometrical scales, not 0.2 µm wall penetration.
Individual fiber coloration depth: MEASURED: Fig.12 local estimate 0.2 ± 0.2 µm at an exposed fiber-wall lacuna. No representative all-cloth distribution or population standard deviation established by that caption; retain uncertainty and interpretation.
Tonal behavior: QUALITATIVE OBSERVATION: Institutional photographs compare cloth appearance with photographic negative: light/dark relations make the inverted body image more natural-looking. Negative-like should not be expanded into a mathematically perfect negative of a uniquely known illuminated body.
Controlled inversion test: QUALITATIVE OBSERVATION: Actual photographic inversion is documented; no standardized photometric cross-object test performed for this appendix.
Comparison result: Reference claims are defined at their actual scale. Matching requires an independent object with comparable measurement, not just the adjective superficial.
Sources: FANTI2010, CISS, CISSDIM
