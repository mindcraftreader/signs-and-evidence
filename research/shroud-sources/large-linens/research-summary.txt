15 source records added to the main searchable library. Captures: 3 full PDFs,
6 museum/institution HTML snapshots, 5 official museum API metadata records,
and 1 explicitly labeled researcher source summary. API metadata is not a
full webpage or paper. All source records have provenance and limitations.

13 large/long individual object entries, plus 3 smaller or materially

entries are a selected inventory, not a total surviving-world count.

NEW STRONG COMPARISONS
Brandenburg cathedral, ca.1290: 428 x 206.5 cm (14.04 x 6.78 ft), embroidered
linen. Precise dimensions from the primary 2022 curator catalogue, pp.30–31.
Institutional public descriptions round to 4 x 2 m. About 3% shorter than a
4.4 m Shroud but nearly twice as wide. This is a German, not French object.

Lüne, mid-13th century, LUEKO Hb 001: 369 x 114 cm (12.11 x 3.74 ft), linen
embroidered with linen yarn, curator catalogue pp.18–19. Similar width,
shorter length; original function undocumented. Distinct from other Lüne
cloths described in older surveys.

Met 36.3.140, ca.1491–1473 BCE: greatest length 1990 cm and width 151 cm;
shortest length 1675 cm and smallest width 147 cm. Current museum metadata
is archived. These dimensions exceed the 14–54 ft range of the older group
summary; the discrepancy and overlap with the 76 chest sheets are unresolved.

Kha Suppl.8557/=g, 1425–1353 BCE: linen piece 400 x 65 cm (13.12 x 2.13 ft).
Long but narrow, under a strict 14-foot threshold, not called a complete sheet.

Zittau inv.2844, 1472: 820 x 680 cm, tempera on linen. Damaged/torn in 1945,
reassembled 1994/95. Much larger than Turin, but restored dimensions do not
mean an untouched intact woven sheet. Inv.2843, 1573: 415 x 340 cm, tempera
on linen; near Turin’s length, much wider, but post-medieval.

COUNT AND INTERPRETATION
The 76 sheets in the Hatnefer tomb report plus one separate Kha bed sheet
support the previously stated minimum of 77 ancient examples by approximate
14-foot LENGTH. It is not 77 sheets individually verified for both dimensions,
current completeness, preservation quality, or long above-ground exposure.
Individual objects in the same tomb are not added again. No global census
or strict Shroud-size first-century Rome/Judea / 1300s France survivor was
verified. Broad Egyptian or German examples do not fill those geographic gaps.

Survival alone does not date Turin, prove identical aging behavior, establish
religious identity, or test the French invisible-reweaving hypothesis. Weave,
repair, burial, storage, display and conservation must be compared separately.

EXCLUDED / UNRESOLVED LEADS
Swiss tablecloth LM-16405 (reported 684 x 111 cm), Tristan quilt, Wah’s very
long wrappings and Hammarby cloth remain leads, not counted confirmed additions
here. Swiss dimensions lack a checked holding-museum record; the Tristan is a
layered quilt; Hammarby’s fiber identification may include hemp. Akeldama’s
first-century Jerusalem shroud is wool, not a positive linen example. The
Chartres doublet and Saint Louis shirt are small garments, not 14-foot sheets.
