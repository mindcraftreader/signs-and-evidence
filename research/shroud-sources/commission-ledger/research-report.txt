ÉVRARD: COMMISSION PRICES, PROJECT SCALE AND THE SHROUD
Reviewed 27 October 2026

The strongest controlled price comparison is the two Langres bargains acknowledged on 5 June 1341:140 versus 16 livres tournois, a ratio of 8.75. It controls maker, date and currency, but not scope, materials, number of objects or transport. No price-per-height or price-per-area regression is justified.

Corbeil supplies the best labor-scale evidence: seven painters and an assistant over 136 stated working days. If the entry applies the same days to all, it represents 952 painter-days and 136 assistant-days. It establishes that a royal campaign associated with Évrard could employ a substantial team; it does not measure the labor needed to make the Shroud.

There is no dataset here of independently measured complete artwork areas paired with total prices and actual labor days. The two-foot dimensions concern individual figures inside bundles. Chapel/gallery areas are absent. A statistical size–price correlation would create precision the records do not support.

The Shroud has no established manufacturing method, production duration or medieval invoice. Fourteen feet of cloth is not a labor measure: weaving/procurement, image-making, staging or transfer, chemistry, drying and later alteration could have very different time requirements. A long modern replication effort would not establish the original duration.

An artistic-production hypothesis consistent with these records is a patron hiring a master who procures materials and coordinates specialists. That is a historically documented business structure. Identifying this master as the Shroud’s maker would require a cloth/relic/image commission, a patron chain or a relevant material purchase, not merely an expensive or vaguely described payment.

No retrieved entry is a positive financial match to the Shroud. The large 1,200-tournois item has a named building purpose; the 973-parisis figure aggregates many works; the 480-tournois obligation is not an artwork price. Their size alone does not make them stronger candidates.

KNOWN GAPS
Royal remuneration: Prost p 393 reports 12 deniers/day in 1305 and 1322–1323; unnamed painter at 3 sous/day 1327–1329 is his proposed identification. Keep pensions/payroll separate from project prices.
Royal accounts 1335–1342: Prost p 394 note 1 lists numerous KK5 folios; individual entries have not all been retrieved or classified. This is a verified working ledger, not a complete lifetime cashbook.
July 1323 arrears for Palais de la Cité work and Sainte-Chapelle supervision around 1332–1333: known in Lorentz; no new securely checked amount here.
Conflans May/October 1315 receipts: known dates, amounts not yet read. Original building bargain/devis not retrieved; Richard p 291 reports their loss.
Maubuisson retable attribution and surviving fragment dimensions do not supply a paid contract naming Évrard. Do not turn an attribution into a payment.
The 1314 cross/image receipt does not establish Christ as the figure or linen as its support. No direct Évrard–Charny/Lirey/Shroud transaction has been identified.

C01 | 1304–1305 | Corbeil royal painting campaign
7 painters at 3 sous/day each; assistant at 12 deniers/day; team rate 22 sous/day
Type: Labor account quoted in a documentary study
Size: No surface area stated
Schedule: 136 working days in the printed numeral; 7 painters and 1 assistant
Source: EVRARD-PROST-1896 pp 393
Limit: Évrard is named on the adjoining colors line, not as each painter’s personal employer. Currency standard is not specified in the quotation. Printed wage total appears to be 140 livres 12 sous, inconsistent with 22 sous × 136 = 149 livres 12 sous; original account must resolve this before using its total.

C16 | 5 June 1341 acknowledgment of earlier bargains | Langres Virgin and kneeling Guy Baudet, tabernacles, painted doors, gold, stone supports, gilded copper crozier and delivery
140 livres tournois, acknowledged received
Type: Agreed package price acknowledged fully paid
Size: Bishop: two feet; Virgin size specified by comparison to a Paris model; modern associated Virgin record gives 95 cm
Schedule: Delivery by Saint Rémy, interpreted by Ronot as 1 October 1341; 118 calendar days after acknowledgment, not production time
Source: EVRARD-RONOT-1933 pp 195–196; document 202–204
Limit: Record acknowledges an earlier agreement; work could have begun before June. Price includes materials, several objects and transport. Do not treat 95 cm as dimensions of the entire priced ensemble.

C17 | 5 June 1341 acknowledgment of earlier bargains | Saint Mammès and wooden tabernacle for Jean de Tiercelieue; support and delivery
