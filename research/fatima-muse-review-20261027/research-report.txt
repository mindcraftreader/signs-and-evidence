FINDINGS
Muse's core is a cumulative argument: the children's reported experiences, an advance promise, public observations, distant testimony, and claimed photographic traces. Merely labeling the event mass hallucination does not engage that argument adequately.
Alexander offers a combined account involving visual effects, weather and expectation. He acknowledges important explanatory gaps. His follow-up adds proposed analogies and answers to Muse; his objections are not exhausted by his original post.
Muse's current reply argues for a local emitter, challenges the compatibility of comfortable viewing with the proposed visual effects, and expands the distant-witness discussion. His descriptions of shadows and wet/dry tonal regions are interpreted photographic evidence, not direct measurements of a luminous body or moisture.
The debate does not settle the technical optical dispute: Crummett repeatedly acknowledges not having a detailed response. That is a limitation of the exchange, not independent verification of Muse's calculations. His objections also concern memory, later messages, selection effects and religious interpretation.
Crummett conditionally accepts the cumulative-update principle near the close; he does not concede that Fatima was supernatural. The direct exchange closely matches the user's present argument.

ASSESSMENT / LIMITS
The case merits substantive comparison, not dismissal by a blanket impossibility assumption. The remaining hinge is whether the proposed objective physical traces and witness relationships are securely established. Separate direct observations, reconstructed physical quantities and inferred divine intent. No numerical likelihood ratio has been derived.
For crowd statistics, distinguish attendance, unique people, actual observers of a specified feature, preserved testimony and independent testimony. Repeated attendance is not independent replication. The religious context can be evidential without treating every later message as directly witnessed by the crowd.
