NON-APOSTOLIC HEALING SHRINES: FOLLOW-UP AUDIT
3 November 2026

RESULT
The strongest new case recovered is Vasu Bhardwaj's claimed recovery from multiple myeloma following a May 2002 visit to the Golden Temple, Amritsar. Actual before-and-after laboratory documents and a follow-up radiologist's report have now been recovered from the healing mission's archived website. This is materially stronger than an unsupported testimonial. It does not yet establish a medically inexplicable cure, an abrupt complete cancer reversal within 48 hours, or a Lourdes-equivalent independent medical-review system. No tally is awarded in this report.

PATIENT ACCOUNT AND TREATMENT TIMELINE
Primary first-person statement survives in a SikhNet republication:
https://sikhnet.com/news/story-vasu-bhardwaj
The patient describes six chemotherapy sessions, one every 28 days; a May 2002 temple visit; rapid relief of pain, restored mobility/appetite over a 48-hour recitation; and negative marrow/electrophoresis results on 25 May 2002. Those May test documents were not recovered in the three-image gallery. The exact drugs, treatment dates and response immediately before the visit remain missing. The narrative's claim to be off medicine after 25 April does not disclose the date of last chemotherapy or exclude delayed treatment effects. The mission reports his death sometime in 2006; cause is not established here. Do not assume death proves cancer relapse, or that he was objectively cancer-free until death.

MEDICAL INTERPRETATION LIMIT
International Myeloma Working Group response criteria require more than a single negative serum electrophoresis to establish complete response: serum/urine immunofixation, marrow findings and other disease assessments matter. The recovered scan can be credited as an improved laboratory marker without calling it proof of permanent eradication. The treatment effect cannot be isolated from this selected record. This is a documentation assessment, not an independent medical diagnosis or a finding that chemotherapy definitively explains his recovery.
Primary consensus: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27511158/
Working group's published criteria: https://www.myeloma.org/resource-library/international-myeloma-working-group-imwg-uniform-response-criteria-multiple

SECOND CASE CHECKED: REETA GARG
Mission alleges a pituitary tumor disappeared after a March 1992 healing camp without surgery. Three recovered documents are a handwritten mission/patient form, a neurosurgical evaluation and a radiologist's skull report diagnosing a pituitary tumor with extension into the sphenoid sinus. No objective post-healing scan/report in this three-document gallery. Thus the diagnostic side is stronger than the outcome documentation. Exact handwritten dates are not securely transcribed here and should not be used for a chronology argument. Letterheads name D. R. Gulati, MS Dip Neurosurgery (McGill), FAMS, former PGI neurosurgery professor, and Dr. Sodhi's clinic (J. S. Sodhi, Sanjeev Sodhi, Monica Sodhi). These are printed credentials, not independently checked employment histories or miracle endorsements.

Matched comparison: Lourdes Micheli has actual paired films and Raco has selected medical report findings and two named examining neurologists, although both have limitations already logged (Micheli chemotherapy discrepancy; Raco diagnosis dispute). The Vasu file now has selected lab forms and a written radiology follow-up. Its existence defeats a blanket 'no documentary medical evidence outside Lourdes' statement. It does not yet match the stronger causal/investigative dossier, durable pattern or shrine commission structure. All traditions must be evaluated at the same access level.

OTHER SEARCH RESULTS
The earlier Jatinder Kaur eyesight claim at the Golden Temple remains a named newspaper interview without recovered ophthalmic measurements or paired reports. Hindu Muthuswamy's 31-patient BMJ study is real measured psychiatric improvement, not a demonstrated destructive-lesion reversal. PubMed searches for Buddhist shrine healing chiefly returned historical/iconographic work; Jewish searches returned rabbinic/theological and anthropological studies, not a new clinical shrine case-series. This is bounded screening, not global exhaustion. Buddhist Phakyab Rinpoche's gangrene story has named neuroscience researchers but is individual meditative healing, outside the user's shrine-pattern category and without recovered treating records in this pass.
