1. THE CHALLENGE AS PRESENTED
Dalleur's real photographic controls (V01, V02: Castel Gandolfo, Saint
Hubert) are clear-sun scenes with hard cast shadows, where the method's key
premise holds by construction: the measured dark boundaries are rays from
one direct source. The challenge proposed that D115 was exposed under dim,
hazy or veiled light with a bright cloud (LSb) and bright sky, so that its
contact-type cues (crown on brim, stones on ground; see L04) might be
occlusion or diffuse-shading boundaries rather than direct-source rays.
Proposed test: blind application of the method to dated, located control
photographs taken under hazy or dimmed sun.

2. DANIEL'S REPLY
(a) The burden is on the challenger to substantiate dim or hazy light from
testimony before the shadow argument can be reopened.
(b) "Pale disc" and comfortable viewing describe the luminous object the
crowd watched, which Daniel holds was not the literal Sun. They do not
establish that the scene illumination was weak. The clouds had broken just
before the event.
(c) Dalleur's video shows a sharp principal shadow and a faint second shadow
from high overhead, consistent with a strong low source plus bright cloud.

3. DECISIVE EVIDENCE
José Maria de Almeida Garrett, account dated 18 December 1917, Documentação
Crítica de Fátima selection (2013), Doc. 29, printed p. 151.
Local copy: fatima-distant-witnesses-20261027/sources/dcf-selecao-2013.txt,
approximately lines 5886-5910.

"O sol momentos antes tinha rompido ovante, a densa camada de nuvens que o
tivera escondido, para brilhar clara e intensamente." ... "Também se não
confundia com o sol encarado através de nevoeiro (que aliás não havia
àquele tempo) porque não era opaco, difuso e velado. Em Fátima tinha luz e
calor e desenhava-se nítido e com a borda cortada em aresta como uma tabela
de jogo."

Working translation: moments before, the sun had broken through the dense
layer of clouds that had hidden it, to shine clearly and intensely ... Nor
could it be confused with the sun seen through fog (of which there was none
at that time), because it was not opaque, diffuse or veiled. At Fátima it
had light and heat and was sharply outlined.

The following passage reports light clouds running west to east that did not
dim the light, and clear rents of sky. This supports a strong direct source
with moving bright clouds (compatible with Dalleur's LSb), not a veiled or
diffuse scene.

4. DISPOSITION
The premise (dim or hazy illumination at exposure) is unsubstantiated and
contradicted by the best contemporary weather testimony. With a strong
direct source, hard cast shadows are expected and the clear-sun validation
regime applies. The challenge is withdrawn as presented. What remains is the
already-adjudicated question of which direction the sharp shadows point
(F-04, L02).

Withdrawn with it: the proposed downgrade of core claim CF4 from 3/4 to
1/4. Two of its prongs were mistaken on reading the register (a known
author-run control error exists, V02; peer-reviewed publication, R04), and
its main prong depended on poor lighting. Absence of independent
replication remains the existing bounded-search note R05, not a live
objection.

Scope: one challenge to the method's validity under the actual lighting.
Does not establish that every D115 cue is an authenticated caster/shadow
pair (F03, F05 and the 12 November shadow check remain as stated), nor
exclude every conceivable lighting mechanism.

5. GAZE ELEVATION NOTE (C12)
Daniel proposed that, assuming level vision, the averaged spectator gaze
nearly matches the shadows. Checked against
fatima-gaze-check-20261108/head-directions.csv and report.txt:

  Six-watcher vector-average head axis: about 42° right, 10° up.
  Dalleur nominal lower source:        about 35° right, 28° up.
  Adopted solar elevation:                               42.72°.

Left-right agreement (about 7°) holds and is supporting consistency.
Under level vision the elevation is about 10°, which matches neither 28° nor
43°. Reaching 28° requires upward eye rotation, which equally allows 43°.
Individual head elevations vary widely under perturbation (face C: -1.9° to
43.7° at the 10th-90th percentiles). The original C12 finding stands: head
pose cannot discriminate 28° from 43°. Recorded as a correction note, not a
score against Daniel.

The source's compass bearing has not been computed against the Sun's.
D115 does not fix the camera's compass heading (C11), and Dalleur places the
source near the Sun's southern bearing, so left-right agreement is expected
under either explanation. A recovered camera heading would allow a
consistency check on the source bearing only.
