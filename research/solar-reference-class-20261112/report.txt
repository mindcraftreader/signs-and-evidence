F-09 — OTHER "SOLAR MIRACLES" AND THE SUNGAZING EXPLANATION
12 November 2026

comparative exclusivity tally as case 9: 8–0 → 9–0.

THE CHALLENGE
Crowds at religious sites repeatedly report the sun spinning, changing
colour or approaching, including at unapproved, condemned and non-Christian
sites. Some such reports are ordinary perception of the real sun: Hope-Ross,
Travers and Mooney (1988) treated pilgrims with solar retinopathy, one of
whom reported a dancing sun at Medjugorje (see
../documented-alternatives-audit-20261028/research-report.txt). So either
(a) all are genuine, and solar signs do not identify the true messenger;
(b) all are ordinary, and Fátima needs features the class lacks; or
(c) some are genuine, and a criterion other than Church approval must
sort them.

AGREED NEUTRAL TEST (proposed by the assistant, accepted by Daniel)
1. A specific sign announced in advance by the seers, with a stated purpose.
2. Same-day reporting by an independent, preferably hostile, eyewitness
   journalist.
3. Witnesses kilometres away, outside the crowd and not primed.
4. Physical effects that eye strain cannot produce (e.g. drying).
5. A single bounded event seen across the crowd, not a recurring effect
   whenever people stare.
6. No matching cluster of eye injuries (cf. F-08).
7. Physical corroboration (e.g. D115).

Key findings
- Lubbock: the 15 August gathering answered prayer-group messages ("Look for
  miracles and healings"), not a specific announced sign. UPI (16 Aug 1988)
  quotes believers; "some staring at the sun said they saw it dance and
  pulsate" in the early evening; a skeptic present saw nothing; the Red
  Cross treated several hundred people, at least four hospitalized for heat.
  No distant witnesses or physical effects reported.
- Necedah: the date of an appearance was announced and a very large crowd
  came (New York Times estimate 100,000, as cited in secondary accounts).
  The Associated Press reported that "observers saw nothing unusual".
  Sun reports for 15 August appear only in secondary summaries.
- Palmar de Troya: see ../adversarial-crawl-20261111/comparative/report.txt.
  Named early witnesses report rotation and colours; Cadoret-Abeles (1981)
  separates an April 1970 dusk rotation from the miracle announced for
  15 May 1970, which her fieldwork does not describe as a solar fulfilment.
  The strongest press is a retrospective interview (El Diario Palentino,
  20 Aug 1969).

DISPOSITION
The test separates Fátima from the class on criteria that sungazing and
expectation cannot produce: a fulfilled announced sign seen crowd-wide, a
hostile same-day reporter, distant witnesses, and physical effects. Option
(c) holds without assuming the Church's verdict. The challenge is withdrawn.
Lubbock and Necedah are recorded here as tested cases; they are
unapproved Catholic claims, not rival religions, so they are not added to
the exclusivity tally. Palmar became the Palmarian Church with its own
pope, a rival religious claim, so it is added there.

DISTANT-WITNESS NOTE
Reports of the event from 100 km or more exist only in later recollection,
not same-day documentation; Daniel does not rely on them. The distant
witnesses analysed in the library (Alburitel, Minde, Leiria, São Pedro de
Moel) lie roughly 10–40 km away.

LIMITS
First pass from the UPI wire, Wikipedia and secondary Necedah summaries;
the New York Times and Wisconsin newspaper pages for Necedah were not
retrieved. Medjugorje, Conyers and Bayside were not scored; as recurring
phenomena they fail criteria 1 and 5 by design. Any proposed case can be
scored against the same seven criteria.

REOPEN ONLY IF
A documented solar event scores 3/7 or more on the agreed criteria.
