PADRE PIO: THE BIGNAMI DRESSING EPISODE
Research pass completed 30 October 2026

RESULT
The participant evidence supports an eight-day supervised episode in which medication was withheld and bleeding persisted. That is positive evidence against the need for fresh daily chemical application during that interval, conditional on the described controls. It is not a medical demonstration excluding residual injury or every underlying condition.

An important correction to our earlier corpus review: a contrary allegation concerning broken seals DOES exist in the same published documentary collection. It is not established tampering. It is a later secondhand allegation which must be preserved alongside the favorable evidence. The assistant's earlier statement that no such allegation had been recovered was an incomplete source review.

No complete ordinary explanation of the decades-long stigmata history has been established in this pass. No new debate point is awarded or user concession inferred. P-02 remains open.

PRIMARY DOCUMENTS RECHECKED
Gerardo Di Flumeri, editor, Le stigmate di Padre Pio da Pietrelcina, 2nd edition, 1995. Published transcriptions/reproductions, not a new inspection of the archival manuscripts. PDF pages are one-based and include two printed pages per scan.

1. Document 14, printed p64, PDF34.
Attestation by Paolino da Casacalenda, Basilio da Mirabello Sannitico and Ludovico da San Marco in Lamis. The document is undated; the editor assigns July 1919 and identifies it as the report made at the end of Bignami's experiment. It reports unchanged wound state over eight days, bright-red appearance at the end, blood every day, and more blood on the last day while Pio celebrated Mass. The participants say they applied no medicine during dressing and removed the iodine bottle from his room.
It does not itself give a daily seal log, calibrated wound measurements or a doctor's eight-day bedside chart. Those omissions do not establish noncompliance or false testimony.
The editor says a photograph of this original document was published in Paolino's Memorie, pp171-172. That manuscript photograph has not been newly recovered in this pass.

2. Document 25, printed pp81-84, PDF42-44.
Paolino's memoir supplies removal of medicines, daily supervised dressing changes and intact seals. It was not one dressing left unopened for eight days. His described final bleeding episode follows final dressing removal, during Mass.
The extract is undated, but its reference to stigmata persisting for twenty-eight years places the narrated writing roughly in 1946-47 if the interval is exact. This is an internal dating inference, not an authenticated manuscript date. The bibliography identifies the edited memoir's publication as 1978.
Paolino mentions phenol use as treatment and attributes a phenol-maintenance interpretation to Bignami. Bignami's own report gives iodine as its explicit example. The two should not be silently treated as identical accounts of Bignami's exact words.
Paolino's assertion that phenol cannot cause true lesions is a causal medical argument, not a result demonstrated by his testimony, and is not sound as a universal exclusion. That does not erase his described observations.

3. Document 48, printed pp159-161, PDF81-82.
Romanelli, chief surgeon at Barletta's civil hospital, writes on 26 August 1919. He has already heard about Bignami's eight-day cure expectation and the final bleeding. This is valuable early attestation of the episode's circulation; it is not firsthand observation by Romanelli. He explicitly qualifies its accuracy. His source was a Capuchin who said he heard it in Rome from the provincial.
Romanelli wishes Bignami had stayed to supervise personally. His concern about potential later compliance disputes is not a record that a dispute or tampering actually occurred.

4. Document 51, printed pp173-178, PDF88-91.
Amico Bignami, professor of medical pathology at the Royal University of Rome, reports on 26 July 1919. He proposes an initially pathological process, suggestion-related symmetry and possible chemical maintenance, with old iodine as an example. He withholds a simple accusation of simulation without direct proof.
The signed report as reproduced contains no eight-day protocol or timed healing prediction. Those instructions/expectations are attested through the other records. Therefore do not present the prediction as a recovered verbatim sentence from Bignami's own report.
No signed post-trial Bignami response or retraction was recovered.

5. Document 53, especially printed pp210-211, PDF107.
Giorgio Festa's 1920 report retains the longer-term objection: lesions persist approximately a year after the iodine withdrawal he reports and after longer bandaging than initially advised. The duration of that longer bandaging is not precisely quantified in the recovered passage. It must not be represented as a continuously sealed year-long experiment.
Nevertheless the longer reported withdrawal plus renewed directly observed lesions is positive evidence that an account limited to eight days does not answer. A residual burn healing over weeks is not an explanation of that full history.

NEWLY RECOGNIZED CONTRARY RECORD
6. Document 30, printed pp92-96, particularly p94, PDF48-50.
Author: Giuseppe Maria da Sarezzano, a Capuchin former general-curia official, not a wound examiner. The editor dates the first memorandum to 1952; the document lacks its own signature and date. Its internal references include 1950 and a 1951 book. The editor locates the autograph in APG and a photocopy in APP.
He alleges that seals on Bignami's dressings were broken overnight by Pio or others. He explicitly attributes this to a conversation with his office colleague Pietro d'Ischitella, the provincial superior involved in the 1919 arrangements. He does not say he witnessed the event; the conversation's date is unstated. A late written account is not proof the alleged conversation was equally late.
This is a named-source allegation with institutional proximity, not an anonymous internet accusation. But it remains transmitted testimony, rather than a contemporaneous signed report from Pietro or Bignami.
The editor explicitly notes that the allegation is not confirmed by document14 or Paolino's memoir. Their silence alone does not disprove it; Paolino's express claim of intact seals creates a genuine evidentiary tension.
The memorandum also misplaces Festa's first examination in March/April before Bignami, whereas Festa's dated report and other chronology identify October1919. That weakens confidence in precise recollection, without logically proving every claim in the memo false.
The allegation describes an overnight event around Bignami's dressings. Whether it concerns the entire eight-day protocol, an initial dressing attempt, or a confused sequence cannot be established from these records. Do not invent two separate trials to harmonize the accounts; equally do not assume precise identity of episodes beyond what the texts establish.
It does not identify observed chemical application. Even if a seal was opened, that alone would not demonstrate fraud or a manufactured cause.

7. Document 31, printed pp98-100, PDF51-52.
Sarezzano's second memorandum is dated/signed 23 July1955. It acknowledges the earlier unsigned memorandum submitted three years before and requests that the two be joined in the archive. This supports the provenance/date of the first memo. It does not independently repeat or corroborate the broken-seal episode. Do not count the two memoranda as two witnesses.

WEIGHT OF THE HISTORICAL ACCOUNTS
For the specific eight-day result, the participant attestation and Paolino's claimed personal supervision deserve more weight than the later secondhand contrary account on the recovered evidence. Romanelli's August1919 letter supports the early existence of the failed-cure narrative but is not another eyewitness. Paolino supplies the explicit intact-seal detail much later, so it should not be backdated into the short attestation.
This is a provisional source judgment, not a finding that one friar lied. A mistaken recollection, episode confusion or other transmission issue remains possible, but none is demonstrated.

MEDICAL QUESTION: DOES EIGHT DAYS EXCLUDE ORDINARY INJURY?
Ramesh, Shenoi and Nayak, Department of Dermatology, Venereology and Leprosy, Kasturba Medical College, Manipal, reported a small 2020 chemical-matricectomy comparison (15 people, five per group). After a surgical nail procedure plus a single 88% phenol application, the phenol group had mean postoperative oozing of14 days and re-epithelialization of40.5 days. This demonstrates that stopping chemical exposure does not guarantee closure within eight days. It does not reproduce Pio's unchanged appearance, daily whole-blood bleeding, anatomy, longer persistence or endpoint. Oozing is not interchangeable with whole-blood loss. This was already a known partial comparator in our October28 audit, not a new complete explanation or a new scored argument.

Additional primary clinical lead: Supradeeptha and colleagues, Department of Orthopaedics, G.S.L Medical College, Rajahmundry, 2013, report an antiseptic-related partial-thickness burn under a tourniquet. The published full-text search index describes healing at three weeks; the PubMed abstract verifies the case and affiliation. Full-text retrieval was blocked in this pass. It uses modern povidone-iodine, pressure and surgical conditions, not Pio's old tincture; it is a limited chemical-injury comparator, not a reconstruction. No need to rely on this weaker-access lead when the fully recovered phenol study already establishes the narrow duration point.

CAUSAL ASSESSMENT
A. Fresh daily irritant application during the eight days: the participant evidence argues against this, subject to the unresolved contrary seal account.
B. An ordinary injury that remains unhealed for eight days after the irritant is stopped: not excluded by eight days alone.
C. A demonstrated medical explanation of all five sites across decades, including the final skin observations and supporting fragrance record: not supplied here.
D. Proven fraudulent maintenance: not supplied here.
