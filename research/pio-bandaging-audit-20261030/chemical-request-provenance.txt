PIO: CHEMICAL-REQUEST ALLEGATION AND ITS LIMITS
Checked 30 October 2026

Terminology: carbolic acid = phenol = acido fenico; not carbonic acid.

Sergio Luzzatto, University of Turin historian, French author proofs (2013), printed pp134-139, notes8-20 on printed p447. Local PDF: ../padre-pio-muse-audit-20261028/sources/luzzatto-french-author-proofs.pdf.

Luzzatto quotes a handwritten request attributed to Pio for200-300 grams of pure phenol, expressly for sterilization. He reports seeing the autograph in the Holy Office archive. This research pass examined his reproduction, not the original manuscript or a facsimile of it. Archive reference: ACDF, Santo Offizio, Dev. V.1919,I,Cappuccini,P.Pio da Pietrelcina,fasc.1,doc.14,Voto del P.Lemius,p10 (note18).

Maria De Vito's reported testimony concerns procurement in1919, with a request for secrecy including from fellow friars and an explanation about disinfecting syringes. Her pharmacist cousin Valentini Vista reports supplying phenol and becoming suspicious of wound production/irritation. Their connected procurement chain is not two independent observed wound-production events. No quoted testimony describes observing Pio manufacture his wounds.

A separate request for4 grams of veratrine appears in testimony and a transcribed letter, according to Luzzatto. Do not confuse this amount or substance with the phenol request. The cited hand-written phenol passage itself does not contain an express secrecy instruction: secrecy comes from the witness accounts. Possession/procurement and a suspicious circumstance are not demonstrated causal application to the stigmata.

Relevant defense: Rome examining surgeon Giorgio Festa, report7April1925, Di Flumeri document54, printed pp230-231 (local PDF117). Describes convent purchase of pure phenol subsequently diluted for disinfection connected with injections, with Pio assisting the rector; also crude phenol solutions used for latrines and cleaning the room floor. His historical investigation is affirmative evidence of ordinary uses, not a direct accounting of the ultimate destination of every1919 quantity. Pure procurement does not itself establish undiluted application to skin.

Assessment: stronger provenance than a rumor or receipt, but not a demonstrated chemical explanation for the decades-long lesions. Legitimate uses answer possession-alone inference; they do not by themselves explain the alleged secrecy. The latter warrants investigation without being treated as proof of fraud. No new tally point or user concession.
