FÁTIMA: DRYING TESTIMONY REGISTER
12 November 2026

PURPOSE
At Daniel's request, a complete dated register of witness statements about
clothes or ground drying during the 13 October 1917 event, especially early
ones, plus Muse's and Dalleur's arguments. Two parallel searches: the
library's local sources, and the online DCF transcriptions at
fatima-1917.com (182 Portuguese document pages and the site's 471-passage
dataset), the DCF Seleção PDF, Haffert, Dalleur 2021 and Muse. Key quotes
checked by the assistant against local texts. Full list: register.csv.

RESULT
Named first-hand witnesses who say clothes (or ground) dried: 17.
  1917: 1   Carlos Silva, O Mensageiro, 18 Oct 1917 (DCF III-1 doc 122):
            "estando eu todo molhado, momentos antes, reparei que já estava
            enxuto! Seria milagre? não creio. O que creio é que a mais
            pessôas sucedeu o mesmo." Own clothes; others guessed; doubts a
            miracle.
  1918–30: 1 Inácio António Marques, deposition 23 Nov 1922 (Seleção
            doc 72): "todos que se encontravam molhados aparecem enxutos
            como fui eu um deles."
  Later: 15 named in Haffert's c. 1960 interviews (two are yes/no answers
            to leading questions), plus Pereira Gens and Marques da Cruz via
            Michel, De Marchi's narration, and hearsay (Louis Lopes via
            Guilhermina Lopes da Silva; Antónia Caixerio via her nephew).
Only one witness (Dominic Reis, 1960) mentions the ground drying and wind.

COUNTER-STATEMENTS, 1917–1918
  Maria Augusta Saraiva Vieira de Campos, 19 Oct 1917 (Seleção doc 26):
    soaked "como se saíssemos de um banho"; felt heat "como se entrássemos
    numa estufa aquecida"; afterwards borrowed clothes to replace theirs,
    "que continuavam molhadas" (still wet).
  Leonor Salema Manoel, 16 Oct 1917 (III-1 doc 93): dried her dress in the
    breeze in the car afterwards.
  Fernando Coutinho, 15 Oct 1917 (III-1 doc 84): "molhado até ás
    entranhas" on the walk home.
  Maria Ermelinda Barbosa, 13 Jan 1918 (III-1 doc 359): clothes still
    completely wet at 10 pm.
Detailed 1917 accounts describing the rain and sun without mentioning
drying include Almeida (O Século, 15 Oct), Fr Manuel Pereira da Silva
(13 Oct), Maria Bettina Basto, Maria Magdalena Martel Patrício (O Dia,
19 Oct), Almeida Garrett (18 Dec), and Fr Luís de Andrade e Silva
(30 Dec). The Jornal de Leiria (5 Dec 1918) paraphrased Silva's letter
without the drying line.

WHAT 1917 DOES REPORT: HEAT
Heat is widely reported in 1917, drying almost never: Campos ("estufa
aquecida"), the Reguengo do Fetal inquiry of 25 Oct (a rise in
temperature; the sun "lançou bastante calor"), Garrett ("calor"), Andrade
e Silva ("calor intensíssimo"), Pinto Coelho ("irradiar um forte calor").

MUSE AND DALLEUR
Muse, "It Wasn't the Sun", §4 (11 Oct 2025): evaporation at 15–20 °C and
high humidity would take hours (citing Gluesenkamp 2021); quotes only the
Louis Lopes hearsay; relies mainly on Dalleur's D115 "infrared shadow" on
the young man's trousers. Cites no 1917 drying source.
Dalleur 2021 (pp. 11, 12, 16–17, 28, 40): reads soaked/wet/dry zones and
"IR shadows" on D115 clothing; cites Barthas 1969, Haffert, Chalupsky 2017;
does not cite Silva or Marques. The register (L06, status "supported")
accepts that the tonal boundaries follow clothing regions. Wet cloth
photographs darker than dry cloth, so a monochrome plate can show wet and
dry zones on one garment. What it cannot do alone is tell a wet-dark patch
from shade, a fold, dye or dirt, or say how long drying took. The pattern
is consistent with drying; the testimony has to say that it was drying.

ASSESSMENT
The 1917 record supports heat widely, and drying for at least one person
(Silva), who did not call it a miracle. It also contains explicit
statements that clothes stayed wet. The general claim that everyone dried
appears from 1922 and mainly in 1960 interviews. Criterion 4 of the F-09
test stays at ½. Drying is not a core claim (core-claims tally); "reported
heat" is the better-attested 1917 physical effect.

GAPS
fatima-1917.com hosts about 116 of roughly 360 DCF III-1 documents; the
rest were not searched. Concelho de Ourém and Voz da Verdade for 1917 not
checked. De Marchi (1952 English), Barthas 1969 and the originals behind
Michel's quotations not inspected. Haffert page numbers ±1. Daniel may
search for further same-week diaries and letters.

ADDENDUM — 13 NOVEMBER 2026: GROUND / MUD DRYING
Question (Daniel): are there ~10 reports that the ground or mud dried?
Searched: all 167 documents on fatima-1917.com (DCF III-1, III-2, III-3,
V-4, Seleção) plus OCR of 252 III-1 page scans; Haffert 2006 reprint in
full; De Marchi (Portuguese, 1947 and 1952 English); Walsh 1947 snippets;
Barthas 1947; Michel vol. 1; web in PT/EN/FR/IT. Register: ground-register.csv.

Result: one named first-hand witness says the ground dried — Dominic Reis,
1960 (Haffert p. 11), who attributes it to a strong wind. One hearsay
account at two removes (Caixerio, 2017). One ambiguous (Rainho, 1960).
The Ti Marto puddle (Rambaud 1948 interview, via Jaki 1999) is unverified;
a search summary suggests the puddle may have been in his house. No
Portuguese ground-drying statement 1917–1930 was found. Ti Marto's own
1923 deposition says only "lamaçal enorme".

Every other "the Cova dried" line is authors' narration (Haffert pp. 15,
33, 37, 44, 79; De Marchi 1952 "pools of water"; Johnston 1980; McGrew
2009), not a named witness.

Counter-evidence: five 1917 first-hand writers describe the crowd kneeling
in mud during or after the sun (Pereira da Silva, Patrício, Curado —
explicitly after "Terminára o phenomeno" —, Magalhães e Vasconcelos) or
going home soaked in mud (Maria Rita dos Santos); Paula 1923 likewise.
Dalleur himself (2021, p. 14) reads wet, shiny mud and a puddle in the
photographs, including D115.

Assessment: ground drying is not a decent independent line of evidence on
the present record; it is weaker than clothes drying. Heat remains the
best-attested 1917 physical effect. Criterion 4 unchanged at ½.

Library-only items that could change this: Jaki, God and the Sun at
Fatima (1999), drying section; Rambaud, La Dame toute belle (1949);
Barthas, Fatima, merveille du XXe siècle (1969) pp. 358, 361; Johnston
1980 and McGrew 2009 page numbers. All requestable via UAkron "Get it for
me" (OhioLINK/Rapido). Not searched: Fox, McClure, Nickell, Meessen, Alonso;
~166 unscanned DCF III-1 pages.
