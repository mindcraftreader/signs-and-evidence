What Muse argues
The earlier debate emphasizes sincerity, limited memory, Jacinta's conditional
version and the penance exhortations attributed to Mendes. The later interview
adds the October 13 textual argument and consultations with Portuguese speakers,
including a reportedly Harvard-affiliated professor. Those speakers' full
identities, questions and authenticated answers have not been independently
recovered. Muse's scholarly-consultation claim should remain attributed to him.

His sincerity inference has force: refusing an offered convenient correction
can support honesty. It does not by itself prove accurate recall, make someone
immune to suggestion, or establish supernatural agency. The debate contains
Crummett's reply that even a sincere messenger can materially misremember a
condition. The later interview gives more documentary detail than that exchange.

The October 13 text
The public 1923 printing was acquired and inspected in the earlier audit.
The opening can be read as reported instructions to amend one's ways; the
word se is not automatically a conditional conjunction rather than a reflexive
pronoun. Context may support conditionality without grammar alone proving it.
That distinction is why this review does not endorse an unqualified claim that
the sentence has only one possible translation.
https://www.fatima.pt/files/upload/voz_da_fatima/VF0011_1923-08-13.pdf#page=4

The detailed written defense Muse cites is Veritatis Splendor, not Muse himself:
https://splendoroftruth.substack.com/p/in-defense-of-the-miracle-of-the
Sections 3.2–3.4 and Appendix A distinguish early versions, contextual support
and possible transmission errors. The appendix's manuscript genealogy remains
an attributed account until the full critical apparatus or manuscripts are
inspected. The linked retail listing is not an accessible manuscript facsimile.
Mendes's later recollections should not be described as a contemporaneous
verbatim transcription simply because they recount the day of the event.

What follows, and what does not
There is a supported alternative to treating one extracted sentence as an
unambiguous, unconditionally guaranteed divine deadline. That is sufficient to
retain withdrawal of the decisive disqualification as presented. It is not a
rule that a historical critic must eliminate every logically possible defense:
an adequately supported probabilistic criticism remains possible in principle.
Nor should an inability to falsify a conditional promise be counted as independent
positive verification that the promise came from God.

The world-ending clause cannot be omitted from a complete review. Muse argues
that it lacks the same explicit deadline and admits multiple theological readings.
Those readings answer a claimed immediate logical contradiction; they do not
provide an independently tested timetable. The current recommendation addresses
F-01's recorded unconditional end-of-war challenge, not a new all-prophecies claim.
