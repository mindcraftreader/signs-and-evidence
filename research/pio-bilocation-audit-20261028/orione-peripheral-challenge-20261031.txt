PERIPHERAL REVIEW: ORIONE ATTRIBUTED BILOCATION
31 October2026

Daniel authorizes examination of peripheral claims as well as core claims. This evidence was identified in the28October audit; it is not newly discovered and is not a reopening of the closed stigmata or fragrance objections.

Specific proposition to test: the account that Don Luigi Orione witnessed Padre Pio in Rome and confirmed it to Pius XI should not be retained as corroborating bilocation evidence, because Orione’s own denial contradicts the testimony attributed to him.

Don Flavio Peloso, former superior general of the Sons of Divine Providence, publishes two autograph draft denials from the congregation’s archive: Scritti53-251 and53-250. The article dates the first19September1933, but a footnote dates the second19November1933; this conflict remains. Orione’s wording denies the miraculous episode attributed to him. Peloso also cites Don Angelo Cristiani’s account of a renewed denial16September1937. The1937 report is an additional reported conversation, not a third autograph.

A biographical story depicts Orione as seeing Pio in St Peter’s while Pio remained at his convent, then affirming it to Pius XI. Peloso traces the attribution to Del Fante and its later repetition. The autograph denial is direct contrary evidence to Orione’s alleged testimony. It does not establish that Pio fabricated the story, that every bilocation account is false, or that the observed wounds were manufactured.

The defensible conclusion is exclusion of this particular attributed corroboration. The wider relevance is a demonstrated case of transmission error without requiring the named saint/witness or Pio himself to be complicit. No outcome or score assigned before discussion.

USER SCOPE AND CANONIZATION FOLLOW-UP
Daniel clarifies he never advanced bilocation as an established premise. Accepted: he requested exploratory investigation, not reliance on this episode. No point against Daniel for correcting Orione attribution.
No published case-specific canonization adjudication of the Orione episode recovered. Peloso’s archival correction is not itself a decree from Pio’s cause.
Bilocation testimony was included in the cause: Angelo Battisti, PositioII pp1638–1639, reports asking Pio in March1965 about a Mindszenty prison encounter and receiving an affirmative answer. Battisti was witness to the reported conversation, not the prison appearance. Hegedűs2013 reproduces the Italian deposition extract; Vatican Radio22June2009 separately identifies the same pages. These are transmissions of the same testimony, not independent eyewitnesses.
The Vatican2002 official biography identifies the formally approved beatification/canonization miracles as the healings of Consiglia De Martino and Matteo Pio Colella. This is not a ruling against bilocation or blanket ratification of each circulated story.
