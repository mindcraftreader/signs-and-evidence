SHROUD DATING: DISTRIBUTIONS, CONDITIONAL PROBABILITIES AND CALIBRATION AUDIT
27 October 2026

RESULT
The newer dating results are substantially inconsistent with a medieval age
under their stated dating models. The published information does not establish
the real-world error distributions needed for defensible probabilities that
each method is wrong by about 1300 years, or a combined authenticity probability.
This is not merely a generic concern: reproducing the mechanical calculation
on another included reference textile gives a roughly 1330-year discrepancy.

WHAT THE CITATION COUNTS AND VALIDATION COUNTS ACTUALLY MEAN
The 27 October OpenAlex snapshots give 46 citing records for the joint FTIR/
Raman founding paper, 16 for the mechanical paper, and 23 for WAXS 2019. These
are scholarly uptake, not 46/16/23 independent successful dating experiments.
FTIR and Raman are two techniques sharing a founding paper and some reference
materials, not one technique or two independent lists of 46 citations.

The accessible promising texts among the 68 distinct citing records supplied:
- WAXS: ONE study with FOUR non-anchor C14 reference checks. Absolute central-
  age differences 10, 40, 0, 110 years; mean 40, range 0-110. Average reported
  interval widths approximately 600 years WAXS and 160 years carbon reference.
- Mechanical: ONE published selected withheld-reference check, FII/Masada.
  Known AD65 versus predicted AD147: difference 82 years. This is not an average
  over a validation series. Original C14 lab documentation for this reference
  was not traced, and the papers differ in historical/C14 attribution. There
  are carbon-dated calibration textiles; calibration is not external validation.
- FTIR: TWO modern control pieces from ONE fabric in Barta 2025, estimated at
  AD1598/1626 rather than expected AD2020: differences 422/394, mean 408 years.
  They were not a paired ancient C14-validation series.
Unread full texts remain marked in corpus-screening.csv. These findings are
not an exhaustive assertion about every unavailable paper or all archaeology.

CLAIM: FTIR IS BY FAR THE LEAST ACCURATE
Not established by these comparisons. They mix a four-object WAXS check, one
selected mechanical holdout and two modern portions of one FTIR control fabric.
There is no common large blind validation set comparing all four techniques.
Different reported precisions and different observed errors cannot establish
a population accuracy ranking. The mechanical reanalysis below further changes
the earlier impression produced by the single 82-year result.

CLAIM: A MEDIEVAL DATE IS AT LEAST THREE TIMES OUTSIDE EVERY EXPECTED RANGE
One can compare a discrepancy with a quoted interval HALF-WIDTH, but that is
not a probability. A 95% coverage interval is not a maximum possible error.
The largest observed validation error is also not a population error bound.
For example, 1300/422 = 3.08 is arithmetic, not a three-sigma calculation.

Using AD1300 as a fixed hypothetical true date and the 2015 estimates:
  Method          Estimate       Standard u    Expanded k=2    Gap/u   Gap/(2u)
  FTIR            250 BC             200             400         7.75    3.875
  Raman           AD30               250             500         5.08    2.540
  Mechanical MC   AD278              108             216         9.46    4.731

Thus 'at least three times' is not true across these entries. Raman is 2.54
times its k=2 half-width. WAXS does not supply an unconditional Gaussian
calendar-age error bar suitable for this table. Earlier mechanical AD400
+/-400 (k=2) is 2.25 half-widths from AD1300. These are alternative analyses
of the same method/data family, not additional independent experiments.

Table 10 of MATEC 01001 prints Raman standard u=250 and expanded U=400, while
the accompanying text specifies k=2. This is internally inconsistent. The
calculation above explicitly uses the stated standard u and k=2 (U=500), not
the inconsistent U=400. The updated mechanical MC result replaces, rather
than adds to, the companion regression estimate AD260 with standard u=137.

ACTUAL DISTRIBUTIONS: WHAT IS AND IS NOT AVAILABLE
FTIR/Raman: Gaussian-style GUM standard/expanded uncertainties are reported,
not large empirical distributions of blind prediction errors. Barta 2025
derives standard deviations from eight calibration residuals for individual
ratios and combines them into a weighted uncertainty. All ratios come from
related spectral information, so joint error covariance matters. The newer
heat corrections also differ. None of this supplies a demonstrated far-tail
frequency for a 1300-year error on the Shroud.

Mechanical: the 2015 MC paper explicitly assumes Gaussian input uncertainties
and simulates 100,000 trials. It reports an approximately Gaussian output,
AD278 with standard u=108. Its Gaussian fit assessment depends on binning:
three of seven runs fail its stated 103-bin criterion, and the authors choose
an eighth passing run. Even a passing central-shape test on simulated draws
does not establish real-world prediction-error tails at nine sigma. The
simulation propagates the assigned inputs; it cannot validate omitted model
discrepancy just by producing a narrow histogram.

WAXS: measured aging parameter AP=0.60 +/-0.02 is converted through a nonlinear,
temperature/humidity-dependent degradation model. Under assumed conditions,
the 2022 paper gives a total aging time 20.2 +/-0.8 centuries. That conditional
result is not a universal AD0 +/-80-year Gaussian age distribution. Its 2019
reference intervals are asymmetric. Four checks cannot determine whether
population tails are Gaussian, heavy-tailed or a mixture of sample conditions.
The later temperature paper identifies the AP measurement bounds as +/-one
standard deviation. That still does not measure all uncertainty in historical
environment, calibration applicability or the degradation model.

FORMAL GAUSSIAN CALCULATION: THE NUMBERS REQUESTED, WITH THEIR CONDITIONS
Assume an unbiased normally distributed complete dating error with the quoted
standard u, and a fixed true manufacture date AD1300. The probability of an
estimate at least as OLD as the reported one is Phi((estimate-1300)/u):
  FTIR 2015:        z=7.750; one-sided tail 4.5946e-15
  Raman 2015:       z=5.080; one-sided tail 1.8872e-7
  Mechanical MC:    z=9.463; one-sided tail 1.4967e-21
Two-sided tails are twice these values. They are conditional mathematical
outputs, not empirically validated error frequencies or posterior odds.

Barta 2025 sensitivity to publication/correction choice:
  Table 1 mean heat-corrected date 206 BC with weighted u=274:
    z=5.496; normal one-sided tail 1.9387e-8 under the same assumptions.
  Applying its 216-year correction gives 442 BC. If the same u is carried
  over illustratively, z=6.358; tail 1.0242e-10. A complete uncertainty for
  that revised correction is not established by simply retaining u=274.
These are reanalyses of reused Shroud spectra, not independent datings.

WAXS MEASUREMENT-ONLY ILLUSTRATION, NOT A REAL-WORLD AGE PROBABILITY
The initial 2022 study gives AP about 0.027 for seven centuries in its assumed
European environment. The later temperature follow-up includes monthly
nonlinearity and gives a medieval-history AP range 0.041-0.052 (Figure 9).
Use the larger, more conservative 0.052 against measured 0.60 with measurement
u=0.02. If that model prediction were exact and measurement error wholly
Gaussian, the standardized residual would be (0.60-0.052)/0.02 = 27.4, with
one-sided normal tail about 1.3713e-165. This excludes environmental and model
discrepancy. It does not establish a 27.4-sigma historical dating claim. The
authors' medieval-history scenarios are not a precise fixed-AD1300 test.

JOINT CALCULATIONS
Adding independence to the complete Gaussian assumptions, the directional
joint tail for FTIR2015, Raman2015 and mechanicalMC is 1.2978e-42. This is
the probability of the specified three-coordinate directional event; it is
not automatically a calibrated omnibus p-value. A corresponding sum-of-
squares goodness-of-fit statistic is chi-square=175.4166 with 3 degrees of
freedom, giving conditional p=8.6140e-38. Different tests answer different
questions, which is why these outputs differ.

For the user's three named methods (FTIR, mechanical, WAXS), multiplying the
two calendar tails and the measurement-only WAXS tail gives 9.4298e-201.
That is an illustration of the unsupported assumptions, not a defensible
scientific odds estimate. It cannot be used to say the cloth has that chance
of being medieval. No justified real-world joint probability was identified.

The errors need not be independent: the methods draw on overlapping reference
textiles, related cellulose ageing and overlapping researchers. The actual
covariance has not been estimated. This does not prove a common bias exists
or explain why all results favor older dates. It means their independence
cannot be assumed as a measured fact.

For transparency, assumed-common-bias-sensitivity.csv shows a toy model:
estimate_i = true_date + b + epsilon_i, with b Gaussian of assumed scale tau.
The independent Gaussian weighted mean of the first three is AD142.5, u=88.8.
With tau=0 its one-sided tail under AD1300 is 4.11e-39. With hypothetical
tau=250, 500 and 1000 years the corresponding values are 6.42e-6, 0.0113 and
0.1245. Those tau values are NOT estimates or documented explanations. This
is sensitivity to a missing input, not a replacement authenticity probability.
Posterior odds additionally require rival-model likelihoods and prior odds;
P(data|medieval,model) cannot be substituted for P(medieval|data).

NEW REANALYSIS OF PUBLISHED MECHANICAL REFERENCE DATA
Source: Basso, Fanti and Malfi 2015, MATEC 01003, Table 2, equations (5),(6),
(5'),(6'), Tables 3-7. This is our calculation, not a published validation
series or newly measured samples. The original MATLAB source was not supplied.

Procedure: fit each of the three transformed mechanical readings as m*date+q
on eight references, invert the fit for the omitted reference, then average
the three predicted dates, following the nominal published procedure. Use
the printed rounded data. A second closed-form regression independently
checks every nominal coefficient. Reproduce the published selected FII
holdout before interpreting any other result.

Reproduction check: FII reference AD65. Nominal result AD147.57; our 100,000-
draw reproduction mean AD148.35, standard deviation 107.76, compared with
published AD147, standard u=107. This is close enough to support a substantive
check of the procedure, without claiming bit-identical reproduction.

Other admitted reference D is carbon-dated to AD544-605 in the companion
paper's Table 1; the MC paper uses AD575, standard u=17. It is included in
the calibration, not an excluded chemically bleached modern sample.
Withhold D: three nominal predictions are approximately -997.6, -828.0 and
-446.9 signed years. Their mean is -757.50 (about 758 BC), a discrepancy of
1332.50 years toward excessive antiquity. Our MC reproduction gives mean
-758.90, standard deviation 110.74 and central 95% quantiles -980.61 to
-545.36. The reference AD575 is far outside this simulated interval.

This demonstrates a limitation in treating the narrow propagated uncertainty
as universal predictive accuracy. It does not establish that the Shroud is
medieval, nor that all methods fail together. It does invalidate describing
82 years as an established general mechanical validation error.

All nine nominal omissions, reference => prediction, absolute discrepancy:
  LII  -3250 => -6675.2     3425.2 years [endpoint extrapolation]
  K    -2652 =>  -804.4     1847.6
  HII   -860 =>  -832.4       27.6
  E     -375 =>  -370.7        4.3
  NII   -290 =>  -295.6        5.6
  FII     65 =>   147.6       82.6
  D      575 =>  -757.5     1332.5
  DII   1072 =>  1746.8      674.8
  B     2000 =>  2364.3      364.3 [endpoint extrapolation]

Do not turn these nine into independent laboratory experiments or a reliable
population distribution. They reuse one small selected calibration set;
withholding endpoints requires extrapolation. They nevertheless show that
the single favorable middle-reference check is not representative of all
omissions. A maximum error, MAE or scatter from this set is not a justified
far-tail distribution for every archaeological textile.

ACADEMIC WORDING SUPPORTED BY THIS AUDIT
The FTIR, Raman, mechanical and WAXS results provide convergent evidence for
greater antiquity under their respective calibration and ageing assumptions.
Several reported estimates are substantially inconsistent with a medieval
manufacture date when compared with their quoted model uncertainties.
Available external validation and error-covariance information are insufficient
to assign a reliable combined probability to that discrepancy. In particular,
the mechanical method's selected 82-year holdout cannot be generalized to a
typical prediction error; reanalysis of another included reference produces
an approximately 1330-year discrepancy.
