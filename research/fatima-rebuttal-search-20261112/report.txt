SEARCH FOR THE STRONGEST REBUTTALS TO FÁTIMA
12 November 2026

PURPOSE
Daniel asked the assistant (Claude) to use web search to find the strongest
rebuttals to Fátima and test whether the library's limits hide a reasonable
doubt. Three parallel research passes were told to steelman the case
against: (1) documentary critics (Fina d'Armada and Joaquim Fernandes,
Tomás da Fonseca, Kevin McClure, Torgal, Barreto, Marujo); (2) natural
explanations (Campbell, Meessen, Jaki, Nickell, iridescence, dust, sun
dogs); (3) witness problems (non-seers, Almeida's wording, dating of the
distant and drying reports). Claims below marked VERIFIED were checked by
the assistant against the critical edition in the library:
../fatima-distant-witnesses-20261027/sources/dcf-selecao-2013.txt ("DCF").

OUTCOME
No attack on a core component of Fátima was sustained. One proposed
reopening of D-02 was withdrawn after Daniel's replies. Corrections are
limited to scoring and scope: F-09's Fátima score 7/7 → 6/7; a cirrus
addendum to L08. Main tally 19–0 and comparative 9–0 unchanged.

1. FORMIGÃO'S 1921 REPRINT (VERIFIED; documentary correction)
DCF Doc. 10 (27 Sep 1917, ~line 2124): Lucia says the dress "desce até um
pouco abaixo do meio" [da perna]. In Os episódios maravilhosos de Fátima
(1921, as Visconde de Montelo, ~line 21991) Formigão states "Reproduzo
este interrogatório dos videntes, sem alteração de uma vírgula, exatamente
como o redigi, no dia 29 de setembro de 1917", yet the text reads "desce
quase até aos pés" (~line 22157). His 1917 commentary had called the
short dress the gravest difficulty for a supernatural origin.
Scope: a mark against one investigator's editing. It supports the
children (their original words survived in the 1917 record) and does not
touch D-02's sources: Lucia's 1930 letter (Doc. 125) is her own, and the
1927 custody ran through Aparício and Gonçalves.

2. Q9 "CONVERSION OF SINNERS" (proposed against D-02; WITHDRAWN)
DCF ~line 2532: asked whether the Lady told her to pray for the conversion
of sinners, Lucia answers "Não disse; mandou-me só rezar a Nossa Senhora
do Rosário para que acabasse a guerra." Proposed as a conflict with later
memoirs. Daniel's reply: the decade prayer, documented 27 Sep 1917
(Doc. 10, p. 59), is itself prayer for the salvation of souls ("livrai-nos
do fogo do inferno, levai as alminhas todas para o Céu"). Accepted: no
conflict of substance. With point 1, the proposed D-02 reopening is
withdrawn; D-02 stands.

3. DRYING (dating note; criterion 4 scored ½)
Earliest 1917 source located in the DCF selection: Carlos Silva, O
Mensageiro, 18 Oct 1917 (Doc. 122 in the full DCF): "estando eu todo
molhado, momentos antes, reparei que já estava enxuto! Seria milagre? não
creio." The general claim appears in Inácio António Marques's deposition
of 23 Nov 1922; most drying testimony is from Haffert's c. 1960
interviews. Counter-report: Maria Ermelinda Barbosa, 13 Jan 1918, still
soaked at 10 pm. Limit: the 2013 selection is part of the full critical
edition; more 1917 accounts may exist. Daniel intends to search for
same-week diaries and letters. Drying was not treated as a core claim in
the core-claims tally.

4. DISTANT WITNESSES (dating note; criterion 3 scored ½)
Contemporaneous regional reports (Granja 14 Oct 1917; Torres Novas
24 Nov 1917) are relayed second-hand. The direct Minde and Leiria accounts
come from Haffert's c. 1960 interviews; Alburitel accounts from De Marchi's
1940s interviews, where watchers had prepared smoked glass. Consistent with
the library's existing assessment (CF3, Jenga block 28). Muse's map and
treatment of the sightings were not read in this pass and could raise the
score if they cite earlier documents.

5. OTHER FINDINGS
- Non-seers: Leonor Salema Manoel's letter of 16 Oct 1917 (DCF III-1
  doc. 93, index only) says she saw nothing at first, then saw the sun turn
  green after being prompted. Partial and uneven perception, not "no
  event". Weiss de Oliveira's "nada vira" refers to 13 October 1922, not
  1917 (VERIFIED, ~line 11096).
- Almeida: 15 Oct describes the disc first-hand and attributes the dance
  to the peasants' expression; 29 Oct writes in the first person.
- Recurrence: DCF Doc. 57 (1920) reports a Cova crowd seeing "o fenómeno
  solar tal qual o de 13 de outubro" on 13 June 1920. Not connected to the
  prediction; criterion 5 concerns whether 13 October itself was bounded
  and crowd-wide, which it was.
- Garrett (Doc. 29, VERIFIED ~lines 5899–5916) also reports "cirros leves"
  with blue gaps and two brief interruptions when the "sol bravio" forced
  people to look away. Added to L08's scope; it does not produce a 28°
  shadow and does not reopen PH-19.
- Natural explanations: thin cirrus with cloud iridescence plus sun-gazing
  under expectation is the most coherent account of the visual experience
  (Dalleur 2021 n. 10 concedes possible iridescence). It requires a
  coincidence with the date announced months earlier; Necedah shows that a
  primed crowd does not by itself produce a crowd-wide sign. Campbell's
  stratospheric dust, Saharan dust and sun dogs are weak.
- Already decided: the "war ends today" statement (F-01); Coelho on
  14 October (F-03). Tomás da Fonseca and McClure: polemic or covered.
