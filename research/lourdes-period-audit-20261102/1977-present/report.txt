LOURDES MODERN-PERIOD AUDIT: RECOVERIES 1977-2026
2 November 2026

Scope and result

The current Sanctuary list still contains72 recognized cases. Only four have
recovery dates after 1976: Jean-Pierre Bely 1987, Danila Castelli 1989, Bernadette
Moriau 2008 and Antonia Raco 2009. John Traynor's2024recognition belongs to the
1923 recovery period; Luigina Traverso's2012recognition belongs to 1965. No
additional73rd recognized case was found in this bounded current-source check.

This is not a census of all reported modern recoveries. A newly retrieved 2011
Church dossier documents Serge Francois2002as a medically and ecclesiastically
'remarkable' recovery outside the72. Its accompanying contemporary magazine
also describes Nadine's2004case as remarkable. These must not be silently added
to the recognized-miracle list or erased from the broader evidence queue.

Recovery-decade counts WITHIN THE72 RECOGNIZED CASES:
1977-1979:0;1980-1989:2;1990-1999:0;2000-2009:2;2010-2019:0;2020-2026:0.
These zeros concern recognized recovery dates, not absence of reported healings,
not absence of medical review, and not a calculated rate per ill pilgrim.

The previous November 1audit is the foundation. Re-reading its downloaded
reports is not new independent corroboration. This pass added independent
diagnostic-literature checks, an identified specialist's detailed criticism,
contemporaneous Bely publication leads, and the original Serge Francois dossier.
No complete original clinical dossier, original imaging series or pathology
specimens were obtained for these four modern cases. Published clinical reports
and physician testimony nevertheless contain substantive medical observations.

The user's hypothesis is preserved: ordinary private prayer healings do not by
themselves identify a religion. Lourdes adds the conjunction of a Marian shrine,
its spring and practices, repeated publicly documented recoveries and long medical
scrutiny. It would misdescribe this modern sample to call all four instantaneous
recoveries inside a pool. Their actual connections differ, as recorded below.

1. ANTONIA/ANTONIETTA RACO: strongest accessible modern quantitative report

Recovery: initial substantial change during/after pilgrimage30 July-5 August 2009,
with later progressive normalization. The AMIL report sometimes abbreviates this
asJuly 2009; Silani's account describes progression startingAugust. Do not invent
a single exact day from those descriptions. Ecclesiastical recognition:16 April 2025,
by Bishop Vincenzo-Carmine Orofino of Tursi-Lagonegro. Medical Bureau report 2010,
five Bureau meetings 2010-2017; CMIL discussions 2018,2019 and 2024.

Sources: OLD-S6,OLD-S12,OLD-S13,OLD-S19,N03,N04,N05.
The supplied 22-page2025AMIL report by Dr Sandro de Franciscis, president of the
Lourdes Medical Bureau, reproduces dated excerpts from the University of Turin
neurologist Professor Adriano Chio's25 September 2009report and University of Milan/
Auxologico neurologist Professor Vincenzo Silani's22 July 2013assessment. Those
are stronger than anonymous cure stories but are still excerpts in an involved
investigator's publication, not all raw traces or an independently accessible file.

Positive observations include serial limb-strength findings, spastic gait and
hyperreflexia before improvement, wheelchair attendance with some supported steps
still possible, and respiratory test results. FVC is reported as56%inApril 2007,
62%inJanuary 2009and93%inSeptember 2009; swallowing study normalized. The treating
neurologist documented substantial independent-walking recovery. Silani described
near-complete later normalization and no comparable complete sustained PLS
remission known to him or consulted experts. Partial clinical fluctuations and
responses to treatment were explicitly considered, not denied categorically.

The diagnostic issue is genuine, recorded inside the approving medical report.
Raco had a clinical upper-motor-neuron syndrome but no supporting corticospinal
MEP/MRI abnormality. Silani reports disagreement between experts Eisen and Meininger.
Marseille neurologist Professor Jean Pouget's2018opinion accepted a cure but not
PLS. The2019CMIL ballot was12 yes / 13 no, with no qualifying decision. The publication
describes the division as doubt about the diagnosis versus doubt about cure.
After updated diagnostic consensus and its validation, the2024vote was17 yes / 4 no.
No individual2024dissenting reasons were recovered; do not claim unanimity or
attribute a particular objection to those four anonymous votes.

Independent check of the defence: the2020international PLS consensus led by Oxford
neurologist Martin Turner really does make diagnosis clinical after excluding
mimics. Abnormal MEP/MRIis not a mandatory criterion. It follows that simply
pointing to normal tests does not disprove the PLS diagnosis. DefinitePLSrequires
at least four years without significant active lower-motor-neuron degeneration,
along with the clinical distribution and exclusions. Applying that framework
patient-specifically requires the complete record; the diagnostic consensus alone
does not retroactively authenticate every finding in Raco's case.

Ulm University neurologist Simon Witzel's2024validation abstract reports107
baseline patients and63longitudinal follow-ups. Its qualified positive findings
support the consensus's clinical usefulness; it is not a study of Raco and does
not establish a zero-error diagnostic method. The AMIL wording 'more than120'
should not substitute for the abstract's explicit analysed denominators. Full
publisher text was inaccessible in this pass; no unseen tables are asserted.

A subsequent2025study by Balint de Vries and colleagues at University Medical
Center Utrecht and KU Leuven followed86patients with upper-motor-neuron syndromes.
It documents diagnostic revision and recommends a four-year minimum. Its
reclassifications were predominantly other serious neurological diseases, not
demonstrated complete cures. Therefore these rates CANNOT be used as the odds
that Raco never had PLS, nor as an ordinary reconstruction of her recovery.

Religious connection: her contemporary bishop's2009letter refers to an unusual
experience while bathing. The main functional recovery became evident upon
return home; the2013specialist describes an initial sudden improvement followed
by gradual normalization. This supports a specifically Lourdes-linked claim but
not instantaneous MRI-demonstrated regeneration of destroyed neurons in a pool.

Next decisive records: complete Chio/Silani reports, pre/post MEP/EMGtraces and
imaging, pulmonary/swallowing records with testing conditions, full treatment
history, and CMIL's final patient-specific diagnostic reasoning. The supplied
English PDF ends at printedp57although its header sayspp36-59; completeness
remains unverified. Do not treat every missing page as a missing medical finding.

2. BERNADETTE MORIAU: strong firsthand functional observations; structural claim
not independently established from the accessible material

Recovery:11 July 2008inBresles, after returning from Lourdes. Medical consideration
began 2009; Bureau finding7 July 2016; CMIL18 November 2016; ecclesiastical recognition
11 February 2018by Bishop Jacques Benoit-Gonnin of Beauvais.

Sources: OLD-S7,OLD-S8,N06,N07,N08.
The seven-page 2019AMILpublication preserves firsthand accounts by Moriau and her
family doctor Dr Christophe Fumery of Bresles, with the Bureau president and
bishop. Fumery had known her since 2007, not throughout all forty years. He
describes left-foot weakness requiring a splint, a rigid corset, bladder self-
catheterization, implanted pain stimulation and substantial morphine use. He
personally saw markedly improved unaided function shortly after the reported
recovery. The document reports a26 yes / 1 noCMIL vote. That is substantive medical
testimony; it is not the full neurological, surgical and imaging dossier.

The experience followed baths, sacraments and a Eucharistic blessing during
pilgrimage, but physical recovery occurred at home during/after adoration at the
time of Lourdes's procession. The patient describes abrupt loss of pain, release
of supports, improved foot position and bladder function; a5 km walk followed the
next day. The source does not establish forty years of unvarying total paralysis,
nor an objective before/after MRIshowing disappearance of every structural lesion.

New criticism recovered: Dr Francesco D'Alpa, a neurologist/neurophysiologist
running an SSN-accredited diagnostic practice in Gravina di Catania, published a
detailed2020critique. His institutional practice document supports those clinical
credentials; a University of Catania marine-research namesake must not be used
to invent an academic appointment. His critique relies largely on Moriau's 2018
memoir, its Italian edition and public accounts. He says requests for documents
went unanswered. It is not an examination of Moriau or access to her full file.

Useful patient-specific leads in the critique include quoted dated2008letters
attributed to Fumery and rheumatologists Christophe Alliaume and Allary: restored
function but residual muscle atrophy, reflex abnormalities, physiotherapy and
occasionally recommended corset use. It also quotes the patient's description
of a later EMGresidual and an earlier period of substantial symptom improvement
after charismatic prayer. Those originals/memoir pages were NOT independently
retrieved here; retain the leads as attributed reports, not new authenticated
clinical documents. They materially qualify a claim of perfect anatomical
restoration, without disproving rapid major functional recovery.

D'Alpa proposes pain/postural changes or disc remodeling. His discussion supplies
a clinical question, not a demonstrated patient-specific mechanism: original
comparative scans and neurophysiology are absent. The online patient anecdote
used as one analogue is not a medically controlled comparator. Nor does a residual
operative injury, on its own, explain the reported abrupt improvement or prove
that the illness was imagined. Conversely, demanding that a recovery erase every
old surgical change would need justification; functional recovery can be real
without complete anatomical restitution.

Do not upgrade Paulo Nuno Martins's2021article, styled a 'case report', into
independent clinical confirmation. Its methods say it derives the description
from Moriau's memoir. It names specialists and mentions examinations, but does
not provide their original reports or paired test results. Its speculation about
quantum nonlocality supplies no established mechanism. Its description of Fumery
as treating her since age27conflicts with the primary2019account's2007firstmeeting.

Next decisive records: original2008letters; pre/post urodynamics and examination
scores; pre/post CT/MRIandEMGwith interpretation; surgical history;2016 CMIL
report;2018 AMIL341pp5-6notice. Treating and reviewing specialists' names are useful
leads, not substitutes for their evidence. No particular reason for the lone
anonymous negative vote was recovered.

3. DANILA CASTELLI: directly water-linked; the original endocrine evidence is
the main retrieval priority

Recovery:4 May 1989, immediately associated with leaving the Lourdes baths.
Recognition:20 June 2013by Bishop Giovanni Giudici of Pavia. Bureau meetings are
reported in 1989,1992,1994,1997 and 2010; CMIL decision19 November 2011. There is long
reported follow-up, not merely a bath-side feeling recorded without later checks.

Sources: OLD-S1,N12,N13. The official summary describes serious recurrent
hypertensive crises and a complex surgical/endocrine history. Scintigraphy is
reported to support a catecholamine-producing tumour diagnosis in the pelvic
region; multiple operations through1988had not resolved the syndrome. The medical
assessment is quoted as finding complete lasting recovery unrelated to previous
treatments. This is important favorable evidence, but we do not possess its
underlying metanephrine/catecholamine values, pathology, images or treatment charts.

Fabio Bolzetta's2019OsservatoreRomanoarticle reproduces patient testimony linking
the bath, a prayer concerning forgiveness, reunion with her physician husband,
and the change in health. This improves access to the religious context. It is
a retrospective first-person narrative, not a1989clinical measurement or an
additional independent experiment. Some summaries call this cancer disappearing;
the precise malignancy, multifocality and objective post-recovery tumour status
must be verified from the original records before that stronger phrasing is used.

No adequate published patient-specific ordinary explanation was established in
this pass. The source gap is not proof of diagnostic error. But a generic
'metastatic cancer instantly vanished' narrative currently outruns our actual
medical access. Next records: the endocrine workup, histology, serial secretion
measurements, scintigraphy, operative reports and full2011CMILassessment.

4. JEAN-PIERRE BELY: contemporary recognition evidence and a concrete1988lead

Recovery:9 October 1987during the Rosary pilgrimage, after receiving sacraments.
Recognition:9 February 1999by Bishop Claude Dagens of Angouleme. Reported neurological
symptoms began 1972; a multiple-sclerosis diagnosis was made at Poitiers in 1984.
Sources: OLD-S15,N09,N10,N11 and the specific new bibliographical lead below.

Dagens's actual declaration, archived by the French Bishops Conference, is
accessible. Dr Patrick Theillier, who presented the case to CMIL in 1998, gave a
2005 interview describing review after more than eleven years and a large favorable
majority. Professor Roger Gil's neurology service at Poitiers is identified as
the diagnosing/follow-up service. The account includes regained sensation, use
of limbs and walking. Those are substantive clinical claims with named physicians;
the interview does not itself supply their original examination sheets.

The APIC report published11 February 1999states that Journal de la Grotte announced
the case on18 December 1988, following Bureau examination. That is a precise
near-contemporary retrieval target. Theillier's2005interview dates the positive
CMIL decision15 November 1998. Bely died27 October 2005of another illness according
to the Bureau president. An unrelated later death is not evidence of MSrelapse.

The primary investigation booklet is cited in a canon-law journal as Medical
Office and International Medical Committee of Lourdes, A Medical-Spiritual Report
on the Cure of Mr Jean-Pierre Bely, Sanctuary of Notre-Dame de Lourdes,January 2003,
36pages. This booklet and the1988newspaper were not recovered in this pass.

Claims of paired MRIproof, total anatomical restoration or an exact quantified
diagnostic error probability cannot be extracted from the bishop's declaration
or newspaper interview. The task is to obtain the patient-specific clinical
evidence, not to assume ordinaryMSremission accounts for a severe durable recovery.

5. SERGE FRANCOIS: original dossier outside the72, particularly relevant to the
spring-linked hypothesis

Recovery:12 April 2002. CMILremarkable-healing recognition reported1 December 2008;
bishop's public declaration27 March 2011. Status: 'remarkable healing', NOT one of
the72 recognized miracles. Sources:N02,N15. All eight PDFpages inspected in text
where available; image-only testimonialpp6-8(visually printedmagazine20-22)were
rendered and read. The signed declarationpp2-3says chronic leftS1sciatic pain,
major analgesic treatment and lumbar disability; subsequent recovery was sudden,
functionally complete, durable for eight years and unrelated to a particular
therapy. The bishop explicitly connects spring drinking/face washing to Marian
intercession while maintaining the 'remarkable' classification.

The first-person magazine account adds prior disc operations, postoperative
haematoma/fibrosis, partial leg paralysis and morphine treatment. He had remained
able to do some hospitalier work, so total immobility would be inaccurate. After
prayer at the grotto, touching spring water, drinking and washing, he describes
intense leg pain followed after2-3 minutes by relief/warmth; the next morning he
could push another patient's wheelchair. Reported skin change occurred about ten
days later. He reported to the Bureau in2003and was examined by around12doctors.
The dossier contains no original spinal images or clinical laboratory tables.

Same-page additional lead: a woman named Nadine, described as having an unclassified
myopathy, improved in2004during her sixth pilgrimage following prayer; the article
also calls her case 'remarkable'. No surname or original medical file was recovered.
Do not count her as a recognized miracle or treat her anonymous short account as
equivalent to the four clinical cases. It is a useful lead and an explicit example
of reported medically considered events beyond the official miracle list.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT AND NEXT STEP

This period adds several different medically scrutinized claims and confirms that
the modern case is not just private recollection without any physicians or records.
Raco offers the most accessible quantitative excerpts and explicit diagnostic
debate. Moriau offers strong treating-physician functional testimony plus a genuine
unresolved structural-evidence question. Castelli is especially close to the user's
pool/spring formulation but has weaker public access to the original endocrine file.
Bely provides a named treating-service history with a precise near-contemporary
publication lead. Serge Francoisadds documented spring linkage outside the72.

The records should strengthen or weaken the cumulative argument according to
what they actually establish. No case here was demonstrated fraudulent. No complete
patient-specific ordinary account was established for these recoveries in this
bounded pass. Equally, absence of those accounts cannot be converted into a
calculated probability of divine agency. The medical/public record and the
religious linkage are both part of the inference; neither can be omitted.
