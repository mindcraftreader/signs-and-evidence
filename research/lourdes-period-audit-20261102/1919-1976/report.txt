LOURDES HEALING RECORDS: RECOVERY YEARS 1919–1976
Research pass completed 2 November 2026

Scope and outcome

This pass builds on the1November medical audit. It does not count re-reading
the same report, a translation of it, or a later summary as an independent
medical observation. The official72-case register contains28 recognized cases
with recovery dates in this period:1920s2;1930s4;1940s7;1950s11;1960s2;1970–76two.
No1919 recovery appears in that register. These counts are by reported recovery,
not recognition year. They include Traynor1923/recognized2024 and Traverso1965/
recognized2012. They are not a census of all reports to the Medical Bureau.

The strongest accessible starting cases remain Micheli, Traynor and Cirolli.
The useful new acquisitions are a publicly reproduced Traynor pension card,
specific holdings for the medical monographs, and a VU doctoral thesis whose
physician author reports examining the Micheli/Cirolli/Perrin booklets at Lourdes.
The original military operations files, pathology slides and complete committee
dossiers have not been retrieved. No new causal verdict or tally is assigned.

Medical evidence and religious connection are tracked separately, then assessed
together. A healing after a pilgrimage is relevant to Daniel's shrine-pattern
argument; it must not be automatically excluded as 'private'. Conversely, a
remote later recovery must not be described as an instant cure in a bath.

Vittorio Micheli:1963 recovery, recognized26May1976

The existing retrieved CMAJ1974 page reproductions were visually rechecked:
Fig.1 is captioned1August1962; Fig.2 is captioned23August1963, ten weeks after
return from Lourdes. The second image itself also carries the1963 date. Thus
these are actual published before-and-after radiographs, not merely a claim
that films once existed. They are low-contrast printed reproductions supplied
by the Medical Bureau, not the original films or a new radiologist's reading.
[M2]

The retained record distinguishes an abrupt reported change in pain/appetite
associated with the bath from subsequent restoration of walking and progressive
bone reconstruction. It does not document an entire pelvis regenerating
instantaneously on film. Regional Hematology Oncology author Barbara Neilan's
2013 paper derives from Michel-Marie Salmon's report, with permission. Salmon
was Marseille's professor of anatomy and later pediatric/orthopedic surgical
clinic, and a national correspondent of the Academy of Medicine. [M3,M10]

The major treatment conflict remains unresolved. CMAJ associate editor Jim
Garner explicitly reports a chemotherapy trial stopped after two months without
improvement. Neilan says no radiotherapy or chemotherapy was given. Additional
report-derived summaries also say no anticancer treatment. Counting those as
several independent treatment records would be incorrect. The original treatment
chart and Salmon monograph are needed to settle what drug, dose and interval,
if any, occurred. A chemotherapy mention is not proof that treatment caused the
recovery; omitting it as inconvenient would also be unsound. [M2,M3]

There is a second chronology conflict: Neilan calls1964 the first post-pilgrimage
X-rays, while Garner publishes a film labelled23August1963. The date discrepancy
has not been reconciled from original film envelopes or hospital charts.

New concrete monograph holdings: French1995edition56pages at Hautes-Pyrénées
departmental archives, Tarbes,8°BR1546; English1995edition52pages,8°BR1548. The
French book title/catalogue is newly saved; these are catalogue records, not
retrieved report contents. [N4,N5]

Christian Delourmel, a former general practitioner and Paris Psychoanalytic
Society author, discusses the case in a2014conference/2015web text and2017text.
He quotes Salmon and points to the1971 Marseille bone-tumor meeting account,
Revue de chirurgie orthopédique57(4),pp321–324,especiallyp323. His interpretation
explores psychosomatic mechanisms but does not experimentally demonstrate one.
The original meeting pages were not recovered. His2017note says he can make
Salmon's complete observation available to readers: a useful future retrieval
lead, not evidence obtained by this agent. [N6,N7]

Do not describe this meeting as independently verified blind testing. Secondary
accounts say Lourdes was not mentioned at presentation, while the quoted printed
summary explicitly mentions the pilgrimage. That is a question about oral versus
published disclosure; it cannot be settled without the original record. The
case's clinical significance does not depend on upgrading it into a blind trial.

Comparison: Dissing, Heerfordt, Schiødt and Sneppen of Copenhagen's Finsen
Institute and Rigshospitalet/University of Copenhagen documented a histologically
malignant destructive pelvic tumor that improved without radiotherapy or
chemotherapy. Their1978report includes serial radiographs and histology. The
subtype was uncertain, but malignancy was retained. Deterioration continued into
1973; improvement and sclerosis unfolded over years. This is direct evidence
that spontaneous regression with substantial pelvic-bone changes has occurred
outside a documented shrine context. It does not reproduce Micheli's reported
bath-linked functional turn, and the authors themselves could not identify its
cause. It does refute any categorical claim that documented malignant pelvic
tumor regression has never otherwise occurred. [M17]

Priority next records: Salmon's full monograph; Verona/Trento/Borgo treatment
charts and biopsy reports; original serial films and their dates;1971meeting
abstract. This pass did not resolve the chemotherapy conflict.

John Traynor:25–27July1923 recovery, recognized8December2024

The original published French report signed by Auguste Vallet on2October1926
is available directly from the Sanctuary as a16MBPDF, now saved in this period
folder. Its SHA256 is identical to the earlier library copy: it is the same
evidence, not a second report. Original relevant pages and the2025AMIL English
translation were rechecked. [M4,N1]

The report incorporates examinations attributed to doctors Azurdia, Denis Finn
and James Marley on24July before the recovery,25July after the procession and
27July before departure. The24July certificate was issued subsequently: the
retrieved object is its1926published reproduction, not an original1923clinical
chart. It reports observed seizures, severe right-arm paralysis/atrophy, a skull
opening, lower-limb deficits and incontinence. On25July walking was possible
but difficult, with painful congested feet. On27July walking, arm use and
sensation were improved and the skull opening was smaller. MacConnell's23June
1924certificate, reproduced in the report, states seizures had ceased since
July1923. Vallet's1926assessment records durable function, residual clawing of
the hand and a remaining forearm circumference difference. [M4]

That is stronger than a single late devotional anecdote. It is not direct nerve
histology or paired skull radiographs. The dramatic nerve-regrowth description
is Vallet's inference from reported prior injury and restored function, not a
microscopic observation. The nerve operations themselves still need their
original records. Ordinary labels such as functional paralysis cannot be treated
as a proven explanation of the entire case merely because they are possible.

New documentary acquisition: the Western Front Association's19December2024
article publishes an image of Traynor's pension card. The image has been saved
and visually inspected. It records his name, RNRservice, address at121Grafton
Street,Liverpool, and a right-chest gunshot injury; its listed birth year1882
differs from the article's1883. The WFA reports100%disability. The card is an
administrative routing/index record with handwritten correspondence references,
not the operative or neurological file. It corroborates independent military/
pension documentation; it does not by itself prove every clinical detail or a
post-cure medical reassessment affirming incurability. [N2,N3]

The WFA links National Archives record D8562562 and a Fold3 pension-record item.
The National Archives page returned a robot-verification requirement; the full
record was not obtained or bypassed. No claim that missing original medical
files do not exist is made. The familiar story that officials knowingly kept
paying because a cure was impossible still needs its actual correspondence,
not merely a card showing that a pension was administered.

The religious connection is direct in the medical narrative: baths and the
Eucharistic procession, with examinations immediately around the pilgrimage.
The2024ecclesiastical recognition is not itself the date of the medical report.

Priority next records: the original unsuccessful nerve-operation notes,
neurological/pension-board examinations and correspondence, and the2024canonical
dossier's source list. No direct contact or paid access was attempted.

Delizia Cirolli:December1976 recovery, recognized28June1989

St John Dowling, MB MRCGP, a Much Hadham general practitioner and CMILmember,
published a case analysis in JRSM1984. The original printed pages636–637 were
visually rechecked. University of Catania orthopedic physician Mollica obtained
a biopsy, read by Cordaro as metastatic neuroblastoma. Marseille's Payan, Paris's
Nezelof and Mazabraud/Curie colleagues subsequently favored Ewing's tumor while
not wholly excluding neuroblastoma. This is expert reassessment of actual slides
as reported by a committee member; the slides were not acquired by this agent.
[M9]

Amputation and cobalt irradiation were proposed and declined; no anticancer
treatment was administered in Dowling's narrative. After an August1976pilgrimage
including bathing, SeptemberX-rays reportedly showed extension of the lesion.
Improvement began shortly before Christmas at home in Sicily, where the family
continued Marian prayer and gave her Lourdes water. Later films showed bone
repair; there was residual knee angulation. Thus a four-month delay is part of
the published positive medical narrative, not a newly discovered contradiction
that advocates had necessarily hidden. [M9]

The exact tumor classification matters for natural-history comparisons. Statements
that no spontaneous regression was known at this age are historical literature
judgments and should not be repeated as an exhaustive statement about today's
entire oncology literature without a fresh subtype-specific search. This pass
does not demonstrate an ordinary explanation of her recovery. It does establish
that 'instant tumor disappearance while bathing' is not the retrieved account.

BNF now supplies exact retrievable holdings: André Trifaud and Bernard C.Colvin,
French1989report47pages,8-KPIECE-2500; English1990report47pages,8-KPIECE-2501.
The2005edition cited by François et al. is48pages; these are distinct editions.
Trifaud is the authority-record spelling, rather than 'Triffaut' in some later
citations. The booklets themselves were not downloaded. [N8,N9]

The medical review by Bernard François of Lyon1, Esther Sternberg of NIH/NIMH
and Elizabeth Fee of the National Library of Medicine raises delayed attribution
as a specific concern. That concern weakens a tightly timed bath-effect inference;
it does not erase the explicitly continuing Lourdes-water/prayer association or
prove the event ordinary. Their broader neuroimmune hypothesis is not a
demonstrated Cirolli mechanism. [M18]

Priority next records: Trifaud/Colvin report, original biopsy and radiology, and
independent contemporary pathology review using the best available classification.

Perrin and the value/limits of a later firsthand research account

Physician Dirk Johan Kruijthoff's2023Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam theology PhD
reports personally inspecting the three detailed Lourdes booklets in2012. His
printedpp28–30describe the presence of radiographs/histology and the decision
arguments. This is a concrete firsthand account of documentary access, not an
independent re-examination by us. It adds a useful Serge Perrin lead:1970recovery
after anointing, prior recurrent hemiplegia/visual symptoms, inconclusive
arteriograms, and the committee's attribution to vascular disease. Original
Mouren/Bartoli report32pages remains the decisive document. [N10]

Kruijthoff's brief summaries have date/anatomical simplifications relative to the
other records. For example, his Cirolli pilgrimage month is July rather than
Dowling's August, and his 'X-rays normal' Micheli shorthand omits the displaced
reconstructed acetabulum described by Neilan. Do not silently merge them into a
single flawless medical chronology or count their repetition as new cases.

How to use this period in the cumulative argument

The period supplies multiple serious, medically scrutinized recovery claims and
specific published observations worth investigating. The close bath/procession
timing is strongest in Micheli and Traynor; Cirolli has continuing shrine-linked
practice but a much longer interval. Those distinctions should remain visible.

The28official recognized recoveries cannot by themselves establish an excess
healing rate at the shrine. There is no complete matched denominator here for
diagnosis, treatment, exposure, follow-up and spontaneous-remission expectation.
This is a limit on a rate-based argument, not evidence that the recoveries were
fabricated or that their religious interpretation is automatically false.

Keep the Church-recognized roster separate from François et al.'s25medically
acknowledged1947–76cases: their tables include cases without episcopal recognition
and document some later relapse/adverse follow-up. Dowling also records that
medical acceptance and canonical recognition can diverge. A low recognition ratio
demonstrates filtering, not that every unrecognized claim was medically disproven.
