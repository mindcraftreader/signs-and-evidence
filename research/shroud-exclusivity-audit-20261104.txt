SHROUD: HISTORICAL AND EXPERIMENTAL EXCLUSIVITY
Opened 4 November 2026 at Daniel's request

QUESTION
If the Turin Shroud is a medieval manufacture, what independently documented
objects, craft traditions or modern experiments are genuinely comparable to
its relevant properties? Daniel observes a parallel with our comparative
miracle investigation: an explanation should be tested against its proposed
class, rather than made plausible solely by assigning the object to it.

THREE DIFFERENT COMPARISONS
1. Historical counterparts: independently dated surviving objects with the
   property in question. Record region, date, support, process and what was
   actually measured. The date of a relief or brass is not automatically the
   date of a surviving rubbing taken from it.
2. Experimental counterparts: modern controlled processes reproducing the
   property. Distinguish period-available processes from modern equipment.
3. The complete combination: one documented object or coherent demonstrated
   process matching the relevant optical, chemical, geometrical and stain
   constraints together. Avoid requiring the same subject or religious
   identity when it is irrelevant to the physical comparison.

A complete historical match, a partial mechanism and a complete experimental
replication have different evidential force. None should be mislabeled as
another. Likewise, a missing published measurement on a comparator is not
a measured failure of that comparator.

FOUR STARTING COMPARISON GROUPS
These organize the properties discussed in this conversation; they do not
assert that Daniel has specified exactly these four exclusive claims.

A. Negative-like tonal behavior of a recognizable human image.
B. Spatial/anatomical representation and the reported body-distance/shading
   relationship, rather than merely any software-generated height surface.
C. Very superficial coloration and body-image chemistry without an ordinary
   applied paint medium accounting for the image.
D. Injury/blood-pattern distribution and the proposed relationship between
   those marks and the body image.

PRELIMINARY FINDINGS

HISTORICAL COMBINATION: NO DEMONSTRATED MATCH IN THE INSPECTED INVENTORY
Our earlier concrete comparison appendix contains eight cases: medieval
painted cloth/panel systems, a sculptural comparator, later cloth paintings
and a 1516 intentional Shroud copy. It identifies no demonstrated match to
the combined reported local fiber coloration and negative-like tonality.
Several materials are demonstrably different; other comparator properties
remain unmeasured. This is a bounded result, not a survey of every surviving
world artifact or a numerical probability of manufacture.

NEGATIVE CONTRAST: ANCIENT RUBBING TRADITIONS ARE A PARTIAL ANALOGY
The Field Museum documents Chinese wet-paper ink rubbings as an old craft
tradition producing white impressions on a black field from incised surfaces.
Its collection includes rubbings taken much later from ancient originals.
The British Museum also catalogs a rubbing of a figurative stone relief.
These demonstrate historical contact-copying and negative contrast without
photographic technology. They do not establish a Shroud-like continuous
tonal human image, a linen surface reaction or microscopic equivalence.
Do not score them as a complete Shroud counterpart.
https://www.fieldmuseum.org/project-narrative/science/chinese-rubbings-collection
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/A_OA-0-384

NEGATIVE/RELIEF EXPERIMENT: GARLASCHELLI REMAINS A PARTIAL COMPARATOR
University of Pavia chemist Luigi Garlaschelli's 2010 paper describes negative-
like tones and crude three-dimensional qualities produced by rubbing. Its
full-body experiment is relevant; it is not only a wrist experiment. Its
adequacy at fiber level and in the full combination remains disputed in the
already reviewed Fanti/Heimburger response. This audit preserves that earlier
assessment rather than reopening it as a new successful rebuttal.
https://doi.org/10.2352/J.ImagingSci.Technol.2010.54.4.040301
https://doi.org/10.2352/J.ImagingSci.Technol.2011.55.2.020102

SUPERFICIAL COLORATION: MODERN PARTIAL REPRODUCTION EXISTS
ENEA Frascati physicist Paolo Di Lazzaro and colleagues report superficial,
Shroud-like coloration of linen using short vacuum-ultraviolet laser pulses.
The work demonstrates that selected coloration properties can be reproduced.
The advanced equipment does not establish a medieval production mechanism,
nor does the localized coloration experiment itself reproduce the full
front/back body image and its stain relationships. The statement that every
individual relevant property remains unreproduced today is therefore too
strong. The full-combination question remains open.
https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.51.008567

SPATIAL IMAGE: RELEVANT STUDY, NOT AN EXCLUSIVITY CENSUS
Jackson (Kaman Sciences), Jumper (Air Force Institute of Technology) and
Ercoline (US Air Force Academy) 1984 analyze body-distance/shading correlation
and candidate processes. They identify partial successes and joint failures.
This supports a combination-of-constraints test; it does not provide an
exhaustive inventory proving that all other historical figurative objects
lack every comparable spatial property.
https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.23.002244

FORENSIC COMBINATION: RETAIN THE EARLIER AUDIT
Detailed injuries, red-mark chemistry and sequence constrain manufacture.
Some selected blood-transfer patterns have experimental analogues. Neither
the whole-object artificial replication nor global uniqueness of every
individual forensic interpretation has been established.

INITIAL STATUS
Supported within reviewed sources: no identified historical counterpart or
demonstrated complete production process matching the full relevant suite.
Not established: four independently proved global exclusivities, a measured
number of centuries by which each feature precedes all counterparts, or
inability to reproduce every individual property with modern equipment.

EVIDENTIAL USE
Absence of a relevant prototype, parallel or complete replication can weaken
a specific workshop/process explanation. It does not logically require that
an unusual human product have surviving siblings. Its likelihood weight
depends on the proposed process, expected survival and discovery, how much
of the artifact's properties are measured, and the actual comparison corpus.
This does not make the absence irrelevant or remove the manufacturing burden.

The Marian assessment remains relevant background to the competing relic
explanation. This audit does not assign numerical odds, change existing
tallies, reopen closed challenges without new evidence, or declare that a
global art-history survey has been completed.
