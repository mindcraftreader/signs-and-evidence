SHROUD DATING METHODS: UNCERTAINTY, VALIDATION AND EXCLUDED SAMPLES
27 October 2026


LATER 27 OCTOBER REANALYSIS
The 82-year mechanical holdout is not a general accuracy estimate. Our
reproduction of the published calculation gives an approximately 1330-year
older error when another included C14 reference textile is withheld. This is
our reanalysis, not a new published experiment. See shroud-probability-analysis.txt
and its reproducible data/script before interpreting Gaussian tail probabilities.

RESULT

The newer methods have published calibration and validation work. It would be
incorrect to describe all their evidence as speculation or to say they have no
successful known-age checks. They also exclude some reference materials because
of chemical treatment, signal interference or limits of application. Those are
not automatically examples of a faulty method giving a bad date on an eligible
sample.

I did not identify a large independent series in which these particular linen
clocks repeatedly gave grossly wrong dates on samples meeting their stated
eligibility conditions. Their limited external validation should not be turned
into an invented record of demonstrated failure.

WHAT THE REPORTED ERROR BARS MEAN

Radiocarbon measures an isotope ratio and calibrates it against independently
dated atmospheric records. FTIR/Raman and mechanical methods infer age from
changes in material properties calibrated on known-age textiles. WAXS infers age
from cellulose degradation with an environmental aging model.

All require appropriate samples. Numerical measurement uncertainty does not
automatically include an unknown repair, an unrecognized treatment, a mistaken
fiber attribution or an incorrect environmental model. A small error bar on
the sampled material cannot establish that it represents the main cloth.

The 1988 experiment's published mean was 691 +/-31 conventional radiocarbon years
BP, not calendar AD 1259 +/-31. Its calibrated 95% intervals were AD 1262-1312
and 1353-1384, summarized as AD 1260-1390. The original authors acknowledged
extra scatter. These intervals are conditional on the sample and model.
https://www.shroud.com/nature.htm

Routine contemporary AMS precision can be approximately +/-25 radiocarbon years
for young samples, according to Ottawa. Calendar ranges depend on calibration.
The laboratory also describes reference standards, blanks and participation in
international proficiency tests. This is an established validation infrastructure;
it does not solve the Shroud's disputed sample representativeness.
https://www.uottawa.ca/research-innovation/national-ael-ams-facility/radiocarbon-laboratory/understanding-results

SHROUD ESTIMATES: KEEP THE PUBLICATION VERSIONS SEPARATE

The 2015 methods paper reports FTIR 250 BC +/-400 years; Raman AD 30 with an
uncertainty inconsistency; mechanical AD 260 +/-274 years; combined AD 86 +/-206
years. Rounded approximate spans are respectively 650 BC-AD 150, 370 BC-AD 430
(Raman as printed), about 15 BC-AD 534, and 120 BC-AD 292.

Table 10 lists Raman standard uncertainty 250 years and expanded uncertainty
400 years, while the text specifies expansion by a factor of two. That would be
500 years. Do not silently repair the table or treat the difference as proof of
a false age. Earlier publicized estimates, including Raman 200 BC +/-500 and a
combined 33 BC +/-250, are different versions, not extra independent experiments.
https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/20153601001 (Table 10 and conclusion)

The 2022 WAXS study argues for compatibility with approximately 2000 years. It
does not provide a straightforward universal AD date +/- a single 95% error bar
that can be substituted for its conditional environmental analysis. Its stated
ancient-age interpretation assumes correlated average temperature/humidity
conditions during the undocumented history. These are model conditions, not a
95% probability that the cloth dates to the first century.
https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage5020047

ACADEMIC STATUS

AMS radiocarbon is an established archaeological dating method with independent
calibration, multiple laboratories and proficiency programmes. The analytical
instruments FTIR, Raman and WAXS, and mechanical fiber testing, are also established
tools. That does not establish the specific age-conversion formulas as routine
archaeological clocks.

The relevant linen-dating formulas are published exploratory research, with small
selected reference collections. I did not find a comparable large independent
interlaboratory validation programme for them. A 2025 FTIR paper by another team
explicitly describes lower dating precision than radiocarbon while proposing a
complementary role. Only its abstract and metadata were accessible here.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.microc.2024.112502

Ricci, Di Lazzaro and Sanchez Hermosilla published criticism of applying WAXS to
this cloth's uncertain history, particularly heat, humidity and wet firefighting
conditions. That is identifiable expert criticism, not a blinded failure series
or proof that the entire field formally rejected WAXS dating.
https://cesandalucia.org/en/post/can-the-shroud-be-dated-with-x-rays/

DOCUMENTED EXCLUSIONS AND CONTROLS

The detailed ledger is shroud-dating-audit/method-exclusions.csv. It distinguishes
sample exclusions from corrected controls and statistical outliers. Most examples
come from the developers' calibration/treatment studies; they are not unrelated
museum artifacts independently dated and then rejected.

WAXS: modern sample A (AD 2000) was excluded because oxidative bleaching changed
its degradation signal. No numerical false calendar date is reported. Very old
sample LII (3500-3000 BC) could not supply a useful finite WAXS date because its
signal had saturated. These are scope limits, not evidence that an eligible
artifact was confidently misdated by thousands of years.
https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage2040171 (sections 5-6)

Mechanical: modern sample A was excluded after sodium-hydroxide treatment altered
its properties. Raman: Egyptian sample MII (about 2400 BC) was excluded for high
fluorescence, not a demonstrated wrong age. Mechanical NI was excluded for falling
outside acceptance criteria; an individual cause should not be invented. The
heated A1/A2 controls were used to estimate bias, rather than simply discarded.
https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/20153601001 (sections 3.1, 4.1-4.2)

The 2013 spectroscopy abstract reports selecting 8 of 14 reference textiles for
FTIR and 11 of 14 for Raman after screening for pollution and suitability. Without
the full underlying records, that should not be described as six or three proven
wrong-date experiments.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.vibspec.2013.04.001

POSITIVE CHECKS ALSO BELONG IN THE RECORD

The WAXS method paper reports four checks beyond its two main age-calibration
anchors. The archived comparison, expressed as aging durations, is:

  Sample    Reference age (years)       WAXS result (years)
  DII       950 +/-80                   940 +/-70
  NII       2310 +/-60                  2350, -290/+510
  E         2390 +/-40                  2390, -230/+280
  HII       2880 +/-140                 2770, -360/+600

The paper labels these approximately 95% intervals. They agree within uncertainty.
This is evidence of within-study validity under its selected conditions, not a
large blinded external trial. D and FII were calibration anchors and should not
be counted as additional independent validation successes.
https://doi.org/10.3390/heritage2040171 (section 6, Table 3)

INTERPRETATION

An exclusion is persuasive when the eligibility rule is established independently
of the desired result, the treatment is identified, and excluded samples and their
signals are reported. Removing observations only because they disagree with the
model raises a different concern. The 2015 regression also removes an outlier;
that paragraph does not establish a bleaching or contamination cause for it.

For the Shroud, the relevant question is whether a tested thread satisfies the
method's conditions and whether its historical treatment can be characterized.
Modern industrial bleaching and ancient linen processing must not be conflated.
A September 2026 study investigates distinguishing bleaching processes with FTIR;
its abstract does not establish a new calendar date or an independent dating failure.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s40494-026-02965-y

The appropriate comparison is established radiocarbon measurement with a disputed
sample versus published experimental aging methods with limited external validation
and treatment-dependent applicability. The exclusions alone neither discredit the
newer approaches nor establish that the Shroud is an eligible sample for them.

ADDITIONAL EVIDENTIAL BURDEN: THE CONVERGENCE OF OLDER ESTIMATES

The agreement in direction among FTIR, Raman, mechanical and WAXS estimates must
count as evidence for antiquity. If the original linen is medieval, the age
interpretations of these measurements require an explanation. Relative to an
illustrative AD 1325 origin, the revised FTIR, Raman and mechanical point estimates
are approximately 1575, 1295 and 1065 years earlier, respectively; WAXS argues for
roughly two millennia under its environmental model. These are not four identical
1300-year offsets, but their common direction is a real observation.

Their dependence reduces how much their agreement can be multiplied: several
track cellulose deterioration, use overlapping reference textiles, and could share
environmental or treatment sensitivities. A shared-bias hypothesis is testable,
but identifying a vulnerability does not demonstrate that it actually produced
these particular dates. This review has not established a common treatment or
environmental history that quantitatively reproduces all four reported outcomes
for known medieval linen matching the tested material.

Conversely, an ancient-linen account must explain the medieval radiocarbon
measurements, including the actual composition and distribution of the dated
material. Neither side receives an established reconciliation merely because it
can propose one.

Failure-search scope: not finding a published gross-error example in this review
does not establish a zero failure rate in every domain. General applications of
FTIR, Raman and WAXS are also different from these specific linen-age formulas.
Successful known-age checks, exclusions and gaps in external validation should all
be retained in any later assessment. No numerical likelihood for the four-method
convergence was estimated here, so it cannot honestly be assigned either no weight
or an invented product of four independent tiny probabilities.
