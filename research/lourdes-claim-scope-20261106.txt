Working interpretation for subsequent research:
The Lourdes argument is cumulative: the Marian instruction and discovery of the spring; a continuing, religiously and geographically associated pattern of healings; and Bernadette's bodily preservation as supporting evidence. The user's allowance of private healings across traditions is a premise, not a concession newly obtained by finding another such healing.

An individual recovery outside apostolic Christianity does not by itself rebut this claim. A relevant comparison would address the sustained, religiously associated pattern and its evidential support, or independently challenge an important component of the Lourdes sequence. A rival need not duplicate every incidental feature of Lourdes to bear on exclusivity; specify what it actually parallels and what it does not.

This records the claim under examination. It does not declare every historical, clinical or preservation assertion proved, and it does not change any debate tally. The wording '150 years' is the user's description of the long-running pattern, not a newly calculated duration. Cases associated with Lourdes must retain their actual recovery locations, dates and relation to the water; do not silently describe every accepted case as an instantaneous cure physically inside the spring.

7 November 2026 — Supporting details

Daniel is open to debating supporting details when they are explicitly labeled
as such. Bernadette's bodily preservation is supporting context for Lourdes.
He does not advance it as a core claim and accepts natural preservation as a
live explanation. He likewise treats the survival of relics or other objects
as potentially providential without making preservation itself a core
argument. He distinguished these questions from the role of Padre Pio's
reported scarless endpoint in the separate stigmata argument.

7 November 2026 — The spring

Daniel clarified that the spring itself need not be geologically miraculous.
The historical claim is that Mary directed Bernadette to the spring. The
Lourdes argument then connects that discovery to the history of healings
associated with the site.
