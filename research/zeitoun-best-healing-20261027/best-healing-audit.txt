ZEITOUN: BEST HEALING CASE AUDIT
Research checked 27 October 2026

CHOICE
The best sight-recovery case to pursue is Riyad Naguib Iskandar, as named in
Watani, 5 May 1968, page 3. Its strength is unusually specific contemporary
reporting: a named hospital, named ophthalmologists, a reported pre-recovery
acuity of 2/60, a described fundus examination and a claimed earlier published
appeal. I visually checked these points in the original Arabic newspaper scan,
not only in a modern English translation. This establishes that the detailed
claim was publicly printed in 1968. It does not turn the patient's quotations
of doctors into inspected original medical records.

There is no verified amputated limb regrowth among the retrieved cases. Atwa's
finger story concerns a threatened amputation being avoided; Matta's injured arm
was never removed. Neither should be presented as regrowth.

WHAT THE ORIGINAL 1968 ARTICLE ACTUALLY SAYS
The item is headed 'After medicine had failed...' under the newspaper's healing
coverage. It identifies Riyad Naguib Iskandar, associated with commerce studies
at Alexandria University, in room 209 at the University's Students' Hospital,
Shatby. The article introduces a letter that he personally supplied to Watani.

The newspaper introduction calls the condition retinal detachment. The patient's
own letter describes rupture of a retinal vein, 21 days of bleeding and a clot
in front of the retina. Those are different descriptions, and the precise
ophthalmological diagnosis should remain open until his chart is obtained.

The letter says an appeal about his illness had already appeared in Watani on
7 April 1968, in its complaints-to-the-Minister-of-Health column. This would be
valuable evidence predating the recovery, but I did not retrieve that issue or
verify the appeal itself. Do not describe this as already checked before/after
newspaper documentation.

According to the letter, presidential secretary Mohamed Ahmed became involved,
and a consultation was chaired by Dr Ahmed Kamel, identified in the article as
head of ophthalmology at Alexandria University Faculty of Medicine. The patient
says the consultation offered no effective treatment. That is a contemporary
attribution of the professor's judgment, not his signed report in our hands.

Riyad describes travelling to Zeitoun with Fikry El-Sayed El-Shahawi, seeing the
apparition and returning to Alexandria on Tuesday. Back in the hospital he saw
his roommate Mostafa Kamal Shaarawi waving. Two named nurses, Thania Mahmoud and
Fayeka Abdel Hady, were called; resident Dr Adly Abdel Aziz was informed.

Dr Bahgat Badawi is identified as an ophthalmologist at the hospital. The letter
attributes to him an earlier acuity measurement of 2/60 and a warning of possible
total blindness. The original Arabic explicitly prints 'two over sixty.' Thus
this was a claim of severe visual impairment with residual vision, not a
measurement of no light perception or missing/regenerated eyes.

The same doctor reportedly examined the fundus after the improvement and found
the clot undergoing internal absorption and reduced to a thin membrane. The
patient reports that Badawi called the abrupt development unexpected. A further
consultation reportedly observed improvement and vision returning toward normal.
No numeric post-recovery acuity, fundus photograph or separately signed doctor's
statement is reproduced in this article. The letter's own account includes a
physical change (clot absorption); it does not claim an optic nerve was regrown.

The observed Arabic newspaper text supports the main factual content of the
archive's translation. The disease terminology and identity issues below must
still be resolved rather than harmonized by assumption.

THE 1970 BOOK: PROMISING BUT NOT YET AN INSPECTED CLINICAL DOSSIER
The modern archive describes 'The Light Returned' / وعاد النور, author Riad
Naguib Azer / رياض نجيب عازر. Its scanned cover visibly confirms that author
name. It also names Bishops Gregorios and Shenouda in its introductory credits.
The modern archive dates publication to August 1970 and says the book contains
medical documents and three commissions: 10 April (before), 25 April (after),
and 7 May 1968 (discharge). It connects the commissions to Alexandria Faculty of
Medicine and Health Minister Dr Nabawi El-Mohandes.

This is a strong lead, not three original commission reports already retrieved.
Coptic Treasures links a 22 MB public Google Drive file. That URL currently
returned a 404 in the browser and download route; the canonical file-preview
route returned HTTP 401 in a separate public request. No access restriction was
bypassed. I inspected the cover, archive summary and source download page, but
could not inspect the book's actual report pages, publication colophon or
medical appendices. I did not download or render a PDF because none was obtained.

The contemporary newspaper says ISKANDAR, while the book's cover says AZER. The
modern archive's book narrative has many matching features (commerce student,
Alexandria hospital, late-April visit, eye recovery, Watani 5 May coverage), so
it is a plausible match. It is not yet a resolved identity. The Muslim friend's
surname also differs: El-Shahawi in the newspaper versus Metwally in the modern
book summary. Recovery sequence is not identical either: the newspaper narrates
recognition of restored everyday vision after returning to the hospital; the
modern summary emphasizes regained sight during the apparition and a later
light at the bedside. Keep these accounts visibly separate pending the book.
Do not count them as two independent healed men or as independent corroboration
of one healed man without resolving identity.

SECOND CHOICE: GEORGE LOUIS, THE TODDLER
Watani, 5 April 1970, page 2, reports the mother's public anniversary testimony
about her approximately two-year-old son George Louis. I retrieved the full
original continuation page and visually checked the relevant Arabic columns.
A photograph shows a woman holding a child before a microphone; its caption
identifies the child in the context of the sight recovery. The photo documents
the public presentation, not ophthalmic function.

The mother's reported story describes poor sight in infancy, a glass-like
cloudiness, five months of treatment and a diagnosis attributed to a specialist
of optic-nerve atrophy. It says a consultation at the Ophthalmology Hospital in
Rod El-Farag found no remedy, with surgery discussed but the child judged too
young. After a Zeitoun visit, the mother says the cloudiness disappeared and a
specialist and committee found the eyes healthy.

Important new finding: the original Arabic itself names Dr Michel Ishaq at the
initial examination and Dr Michel Kamel after recovery. This discrepancy is not
merely introduced by the archive's English translation. The article contains no
signed examination, numeric acuity, fundus record or test of optic-nerve function.
The relationship between visible cloudiness, optic-nerve atrophy and proposed
surgery needs original clinical clarification. I would not diagnose the child
from the newspaper or claim documented optic-nerve regeneration.

This is the most striking sight-restoration story located, but Riyad is the
better research lead because of the earlier numeric acuity and much more specific
hospital and examination trail. Neither currently meets the stronger description
'we have inspected the complete original before-and-after ophthalmic records.'

THIRD CHOICE: MATTA, STRONGER DIRECT PHYSICIAN DOCUMENT
Wagih Rizk Matta has a reproduced signed English physician report by Dr Zarif
Bishara Rizk, FRCS, MRCOG, identified on the document as a consultant surgeon and
gynaecologist at Manchiet El Bakry Hospital, Cairo. It describes a severe 1967
elbow injury, prior treatment, very limited movement and unexpectedly restored
flexion/extension in May 1968. It is the strongest inspected direct physician
attestation in this retrieval, but is not the requested case of restored sight,
speech or amputated-limb regrowth. The limb had remained attached, with intact
main artery and median nerve according to the report. The report alone does not
establish the exact apparition/recovery timing or demonstrate muscle regrowth.

MADIHA: NOT THE BEST MATCH TO THE USER'S CRITERION
The 1969 committee booklet reports restored sight and speech in Madiha Mohammed
Said, but explicitly attributes their loss to severe psychic shocks. The report
may be meaningful on its own terms. It should not be advertised as demonstrated
repair of destroyed eyes or vocal structures without further records.

EVIDENCE LEVELS
1. Inspected period publications: Watani 5 May 1968 p.3 and 5 April 1970 p.2.
   Primary evidence that specific named claims were printed then. Doctors'
   examinations are described inside patient/mother testimony, not separate
   inspected clinical documents.
2. Inspected reproduced signed physician statement: Matta. Stronger direct
   clinician attestation, but a different type of healing and incomplete chart.
3. Modern archive summaries/translations: useful finding aids; not independent
   confirmations of the underlying events.
4. Claimed 1970-book medical appendices: not retrieved and not yet gradeable.
No hospital affiliation or academic position was independently verified beyond
what these historical sources state. Titles and institutions are attributed
rather than presented as newly authenticated staff biographies.

CONCRETE NEXT RECORDS, IN ORDER OF VALUE
- Public/readable full scan or physical copy of 'The Light Returned,' especially
  its title/colophon, patient identification and reproduced clinical reports.
- Watani 7 April 1968 complaints-to-health-minister item, to establish whether
  Riyad's illness really was publicly recorded before the visit.
- Alexandria University Students' Hospital ophthalmic chart, exact diagnosis,
  dated measured acuities, treatment record and fundus findings before and after.
- Any original signed 10 April / 25 April / 7 May commission reports, if the
  identity matches the book and newspaper; do not count summaries as three files.
- George Louis's initial and later specialist/committee records, establishing
  doctor identity, diagnosis and objective function rather than mother's report.
No external contact or messages were sent. Lack of retrieval does not establish
that these records never existed.
