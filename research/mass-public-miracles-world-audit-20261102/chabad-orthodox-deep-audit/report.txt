CHABAD-LUBAVITCH / ORTHODOX JEWISH PUBLIC-MIRACLE AUDIT
2 November 2026. Follow-on to Ajmer; no tally change.

FINDING
The strongest recovered Chabad comparison is a combination of documented public wartime reassurance/prediction and a sustained posthumous apparition tradition. It is not an established equivalent of Zeitoun's claimed repeated publicly visible Marian figure, nor a demonstrated equivalent of Fatima's appointed public visual sign. The claims are real, specific enough to investigate, and not exhausted by private healing stories. The current evidence nevertheless falls short in important, documentable ways.

Do not generalize the messianic faction's interpretations to all Chabad, all Hasidim or all Orthodox Jews. Some supporters say the Rebbe did not die; others expect resurrection; other Chabad authorities reject the messianic identification. Distinguish debates over those interpretations from factual questions about particular reported experiences. [C01, C16, C17]

1. THE MASS SATELLITE EVENT, 31 JANUARY 1993
This was a large, well-publicized gathering around the living, physically present Rebbe, who had suffered a stroke. It is NOT a mass posthumous apparition.
Before the gathering, JTA documented an advertised messianic coronation, internal opposition and secretariat disclaimers. Its subsequent eyewitness-style report describes about 7,000 inside, a similarly large outside gathering and international satellite audiences. It describes a ten-minute seated appearance and no unequivocal messianic revelation. The secretariat called it normal attendance at evening services; organizer Shmuel Butman backed away from a human coronation claim. [C16, C17]
A later messianist narrative instead treats his encouragement of the chant as public acceptance of kingship. This is an interpretive dispute about his conduct, not documentation of a supernatural visual transformation. I have not independently watched the full original broadcast, so no frame-level conclusions are offered. [C18]
This case has substantial media documentation, including skeptical reporting. That supports the gathering and expectation, not an extraordinary phenomenon witnessed by everyone there. It cannot be added to a mass-apparition count merely because large crowds saw a revered leader.

2. POST-1994 REBBE SIGHTINGS
The advocate introduction on Chabad.fm says sightings generally concern individuals, awake, occasionally including speech and apparent physical effects. It presents redemption and messianic identity as their meaning. The source itself therefore does not claim universal visibility at gatherings. [C02]
The Hebrew forum includes a poster claiming at least two other students saw the figure at Ramat Aviv, and a husband reporting his wife and children saw/heard him. Other participants question why everyone did not see or why no photograph exists. Names, individual co-witness interviews, exact observation conditions and originals are missing. The page combines 2007 comments with a 2015 lead entry. [C03]
The English testimonial collection includes a named account of two sequential sightings in a kitchen and a later claim that a disposable-camera photograph captured a figure identified from behind. Neither supplies authenticated continuous footage of a public apparition. The supposed photo's Tishrei 5764/2004 labels disagree: Tishrei 5764 was autumn 2003. This is a provenance issue, not a fraud finding. [C04, C21]
These are genuine apparition claims with limited alleged shared observation. They do not currently establish a repeated public sign witnessed by independently identified large mixed crowds. Worshippers at 770 are not automatically people who optically observed a posthumous Rebbe.

3. WHAT THE ACADEMIC LITERATURE ESTABLISHES
University College London psychiatry researcher Simon Dein's open-access 2016 paper reports long-term ethnography, living with a Lubavitch family, participant observation and interviews. It documents storytelling, healing and bibliomancy traditions, including failures sometimes blamed on insufficient faith. This matters to selection of published success stories. It is not a controlled scientific test of apparition claims, and the truth of every narrative was not independently established. [C01]
Hebrew University psychology professor Yoram Bilu explicitly says his book relies heavily on texts, probably edited, while also using social-science explanations. His deliberate experience-near presentation should not be mistaken for independent optical authentication. His credentials establish relevant scholarship, not supernatural detection. [C05, C06]
Neither scholar's skeptical interpretation alone disproves a sighting. Conversely, having an academic paper about a community does not turn its miracle stories into scientifically established events. The missing item is case-level verification, not a higher-ranking credential.

4. THE 1967 SUCCESS ASSURANCE: REAL PRIMARY ARCHIVE, LIMITED SPECIFICITY
JEM dates an archived address to 28 May 1967. Its related text says Jews in Israel will emerge successfully and urges Torah/commandments as assistance. That is a real prewar assurance in an identifiable public-address archive, stronger evidence than an undated anecdote. It does not predict a specified public optical sign or precise battle timetable. I inspected metadata/text, not the original film. [C07]
An important ordinary comparison is the CIA's contemporary 26 May intelligence memorandum: it already expected Israeli air superiority and successful armored operations, even considering an Arab first strike. This does NOT show the Rebbe read secret intelligence. It shows that forecasting Israeli success was within ordinary military assessment, so successful general reassurance alone cannot demonstrate supernatural knowledge. [C08]

5. 1990–1991: BETTER DOCUMENTATION THAN A PURELY RETROSPECTIVE LEGEND
Independent JTA reporting dated 21 August 1990, months before the shooting war, records the Rebbe's reassurance concerning the Gulf crisis/gas masks and his messianic framing. That is genuine pre-event public documentation and must be credited. [C09]
The more specific Purim timing claim needs its original wording preserved. JTA on 28 February 1991 quotes spokesman Yehuda Krinsky reporting a November conversation: the war would be “more than likely over by Purim.” Krinsky declined to call this prophecy, while invoking holy insight. It is contemporary reporting at the outcome, but the November original recording or dated transcript was not recovered here. Later flat formulations are stronger than this qualified report. [C10]
A 1991 publisher collection lists prewar essays and includes later essays after the outcome. It contains adaptations of multiple talks, not simply verbatim transcripts. The current retrospective foreword is not evidence every sentence was printed before the war. [C11–C13]
Successful reassurance and the Purim coincidence are positive evidence for supporters, but forecasting successful war outcomes is a different evidential species from predicting an appointed apparently extraordinary public observation. The amount and precision of advance information must be judged from originals, without adding later certainty.

6. WERE THE LOW SCUD CASUALTIES SCIENTIFICALLY INEXPLICABLE?
This has actual physical/medical literature. Steve Fetter of the University of Maryland, George N. Lewis of MIT's Defense and Arms Control Studies program, and Lisbeth Gronlund of the Union of Concerned Scientists/MIT analyzed warning time, inaccurate missiles, warhead size/reentry breakup, impact sites and reinforced-concrete buildings. Their conclusion is that these factors and large fluctuations from few urban explosions can account for the observed rate. [C14]
The recovered university PDF is stamped 4 January 1993 DRAFT, submitted to Nature, associated with the published Nature article (28 January 1993, 361:293–296). It is not falsely presented as the final publisher PDF.
Israel Defense Forces Medical Corps researcher E. Karsenty and colleagues reported two deaths and numerous injured/anxious patients, while describing the injury count as strikingly low. The abstract distinguishes explosion injuries from anxiety, inappropriate atropine and rushing injuries. Full clinical text was not recovered. [C15]
Therefore “no one was harmed” is an inaccurate literal formulation. Low casualties can still be interpreted providentially, but the literature does not establish an impossible casualty distribution. In particular, one cannot quote the uncorrected London comparison while omitting the authors' later corrections and conclusion. Conversely, this analysis concerns missile damage, not the truth of Judaism or every Rebbe prediction.

7. OTHER ORTHODOX LEADS: BABA SALI AND RACHEL
Baba Sali traditions include publicly shared inexhaustible-liquor stories. The compilation identifies an attendant in one account but lacks a fixed date, vessel measurements, continuous recording or independent adversarial observer. Its hundreds-of-cups account belongs to his brother, Rabbi Yitzchak, not automatically Baba Sali. A translator adds an unnamed friend's claimed firsthand experience. This is a genuine physical-effect lead, but not currently an independently authenticated public demonstration. [C19]
Gal Einai's healing story links a wounded man's restoration to observance, giving it religious meaning rather than a contextless lucky recovery. But the patient, medical record and contemporary press trail are absent in the recovered account. Under the user's distinction, it is not a substitute for a mass apparition. [C20]
The earlier local Jewish audit already traces Rachel's alleged group appearance to Gaza soldiers: a named Hebrew radio claimant months later, variants in other soldiers' accounts, and academic analysis. This remains a closer qualitative apparition analogue than generic healing, but no new pre-event record, filmed apparition or independently verified large witness body was recovered in this follow-on. See prior report; do not count its sources again as fresh independent witnesses. [C22]

8. ADVERSARIAL WITNESSES AND PUBLIC VINDICATION
No new named prior skeptic personally witnessing and confirming a supernatural public Rebbe apparition was established. JTA is independent journalism and the medical/military literature is independent investigation; these do not count as positive adversarial supernatural witnesses simply because they independently verify ordinary surrounding facts.
The proponents' religious meaning is explicit: Torah observance, messiah, redemption. It would be wrong to say the claims have no vindicatory intent. The issue is whether a sufficiently extraordinary public occurrence and its claimed connection are established, not whether followers intended a theological inference.
Current result: modern Jewish public-prediction and small-group apparition claims exist and deserve accurate comparison. No mass apparition comparable in recovered public observational record to the Fatima/Zeitoun bundle was established by this audit. That is not proof that none has ever occurred, nor a verdict on all miracles in Judaism. No comparative tally altered.
