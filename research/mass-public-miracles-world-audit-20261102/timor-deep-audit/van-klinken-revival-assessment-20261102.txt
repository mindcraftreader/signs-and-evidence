VAN KLINKEN ON THE REVIVAL
Source: Gerry van Klinken, The Making of Middle Indonesia (2014), printed pp223-226, PDF pages242-245 in the KNAW copy.
His treatment is social/political and skeptical of treating miracle stories as transparent proof. He connects the movement to town/countryside class tensions, church/PKI competition, inflation, famine, cattle disease, apocalyptic expectations and the anti-communist military crackdown. He quotes Peters about preachers competing to tell the greatest miracle stories. He describes entry into churches partly as refuge from being treated as atheist/communist during the purges. He notes institutional restraints on healing teams by late1967 and cites Cooley on limited subsequent change in indigenous church life.
Limits: no specific physical or optical investigation of Tari's non-consuming church fire appears in this recovered revival section. His contextual explanation is not a demonstrated mechanism for external flames, and he does not establish all accounts deliberate fraud. Political fear after1October cannot simply explain away the beginning of the movement before that date; his discussion also includes earlier tensions and conditions.
