**The modern methods continue to provide positive evidence for antiquity. But the claim that their combined error would be virtually impossible needs substantial narrowing.** Mechanical dating gives us the clearest reason.

**1. Mechanical dating**

Giulio Fanti and Roberto Basso of the University of Padua, with Pierandrea Malfi, published several related analyses yielding approximately **AD 260–400**, with reported uncertainties of a few centuries.

Their approach has substance: it measures strength, stiffness and energy loss. They also investigated whether damage makes those measurements disagree, providing an internal check against misleading aging.

I reproduced our earlier calculation using their published reference data:

| Reference linen | Known date | Prediction when excluded from calibration |
|---|---:|---:|
| Masada, FII | AD 65 | AD 148 |
| Egyptian sample D | AD 575 | **758 BC** |

The first reproduces their successful published check. The second misses by **about 1,332 years toward an older date**.

That second result is **our calculation from their rounded table**, using the three-channel regression underlying their Monte Carlo analysis. It is not an independent laboratory experiment. Nevertheless, it demonstrates that this calibration can produce a large ancient-direction error on an included reference linen. The approximately 82-year successful check cannot serve as the method’s general accuracy. [Original mechanical study](https://doi.org/10.1051/matecconf/20153601003).

[Local source: mechanical-montecarlo-2015.pdf]

Fanti’s spectroscopy team—including Pietro Baraldi of Modena and Reggio Emilia and Anna Tinti of Bologna—obtained an ancient estimate, approximately **250 BC ±400 years** in their 2015 synthesis.
