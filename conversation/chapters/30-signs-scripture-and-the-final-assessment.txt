  > “Otherwise believe for the very works’ sake.”

- **Acts 2:17–18:** sons and daughters prophesying.
- **Acts 8:5–13:** people attend to Philip’s preaching because of the signs.
- **Acts 9:32–35, 36–42:** healings followed by people turning to the Lord.
- **Acts 11:27–30:** Agabus predicts a famine; the narrator records its occurrence.
- **Acts 13:6–12:** the proconsul believes after the encounter involving Elymas.
- **Acts 15:7–12:** apostolic deliberation includes signs among the Gentiles.
- **1 Corinthians 12:4–11, 27–31:** prophecy, miracles, healing and discernment within the Church.
- **1 Corinthians 14:24–25:** prophecy reveals an outsider’s heart, leading him to acknowledge God’s presence.
- **Galatians 3:5:** Paul appeals to the Spirit and miracles experienced among his readers.
- **Revelation 11:3–13:** future prophetic witnesses and their extraordinary powers.

**The qualification concerning “believe future prophets” comes from the New Testament itself:** it treats signs as evidence, while requiring their interpretation alongside teaching and conduct.

- **Matthew 7:15–23:** recognize prophets by their fruits; claims to prophecy and mighty works do not alone establish fidelity to Jesus. “Fruits” here concerns conduct, not simply miracles.
- **Matthew 24:23–26; Mark 13:21–23:** false prophets can display impressive signs.
- **Galatians 1:8–9:** even an angelic appearance does not authorize a contrary gospel.
- **1 John 4:1–6:** test spirits and prophets.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:9–12:** warning about deceptive signs.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:19–22 — Paul:**  
  > “Extinguish not the spirit. Despise not prophecies. But prove all things: hold fast that which is good.”  
  [1 Thessalonians 5](https://ebible.org/engDRA/1TH05.htm)

**The clearest biblical formulation is therefore: works and fulfilled predictions can authenticate a messenger and message; claimed signs must also undergo discernment.** John 10:37–38, John 14:29, Acts 14:3, 2 Corinthians 12:12 and Hebrews 2:3–4 are the most direct texts for the argument you’re developing.
