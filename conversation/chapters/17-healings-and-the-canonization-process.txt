**Yes—1995 and 2000. That mainly reflects how the process works.**

For someone following Pio’s route to sainthood, the Church ordinarily examines three separate questions:

1. **His life:** Did he practice Christian virtues heroically? His conduct, writings and extraordinary experiences can be investigated here. Stigmata alone do not establish holiness.
2. **Beatification:** Was a miracle granted through his intercession **after his death**?
3. **Canonization:** Was another miracle granted through his intercession **after his beatification**? These timing requirements are stated explicitly in the Vatican’s procedural explanation. [Congregation for the Causes of Saints](https://www.vatican.va/news_services/press/documentazione/documents/avvenimenti/canonizzazioni-beatificazioni_nota-procedura_it.html)

Pio’s chronology fits:

- **1968:** died.
- **1995:** De Martino’s recovery.
- **1999:** beatified.
- **2000:** Colella’s recovery.
- **2002:** canonized. [Official Vatican biography](https://www.vatican.va/news_services/liturgy/saints/ns_lit_doc_20020616_padre-pio_en.html)

So **his lifetime stigmata could not substitute for those specifically required later miracles**. The theological purpose is confirmation of his intercession from heaven.

An earlier healing **after 1968** could potentially have served for beatification; I haven’t established why the postulation selected De Martino over other candidates. Selecting these two cases does not itself imply rejection of his earlier miracle claims.
